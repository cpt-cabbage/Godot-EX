## Key question 1: NaturalMotion Euphoria — architecture, behaviors, shipped games, public documentation, runtime cost

### Takeaway
Euphoria is a behavior-based active-ragdoll middleware: a physically simulated body (per-joint stiffness/damping "muscles" with strength scaling) is driven by a library of "tens" of composable behaviors (balance/stepping, catch fall, brace for impact, grab, stagger fall, high fall, point gun, shot reactions...) that are stacked by message, each exposing stiffness/damping/timing parameters; it shipped in GTA IV/V, RDR 1/2, Max Payne 3, The Force Unleashed 1/2, Backbreaker and Clumsy Ninja, and licensing ended in 2017. No primary source publishes its tick rate or per-character CPU cost; the most concrete public technical record is the GTA V behavior/parameter list exposed by the ScriptHookVDotNet docs plus NaturalMotion's patents.

Academic/patent roots (the "Dynamic Motion Synthesis" papers)
- Reil & Husbands, "Evolution of central pattern generators for bipedal walking in a real-time physics environment", IEEE Trans. Evolutionary Computation 6(2), April 2002: biped simulated with MathEngine SDK 1.0.5 ("Fast Dynamics Toolkit"), 13 bodies and 11 joints, 6 DOF total (hip 2 DOF pitch/roll, knee 1 DOF), sphere-plane foot contacts (radius 8 cm), no ankles. CPG: ten fully interconnected neurons, iteration time step 0.02 s; motor neuron outputs 0..1 scaled to joint angle limits. ### Inferences
- Reading the GTA V parameter set as an architecture: every behaviour scales a small number of per-region joint "stiffness" values in a common 6–16 range and "damping" in 0–2, so a shared PD/spring-damper per joint with per-behaviour gain scaling is the runtime primitive; behaviours are pure parameter/target generators layered by message (ConfigureBalance is shared by BodyBalance, StaggerFall, BraceForImpact, HighFall). This matches US9177409B2's "control frame vs target frame with a resilient connection" description.
- The balancer is a stepping controller with explicit failure conditions (lean threshold 0.6, COM height 0.5 m over lowest foot, step budget, time budget), and failure hands off to a fall (ramping stiffness down) rather than a hard switch — exactly the "gate powers by consequences" structure the user wants: fall tolerance can be expressed as raising `MaxSpeedForRecoverableFall`, lowering `Catchfalltime`, enabling `FowardRoll`, and raising `LegStrength`.
- The "asymmetry" parameters (HandsDelayMin/Max, LegAsymmetry, PredictionTimeVariance) show that believability came from deliberate randomization, not from higher fidelity simulation.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives Euphoria's simulation tick rate or per-character CPU/SPU cost. The MCV 2013 piece establishes the PhysX dependency and CPU-heaviness qualitatively only. Backbreaker's Wikipedia entry does not say how many players were simulated concurrently.
- NaturalMotion's own GDC talks and the "Euphoria 3D White Paper — DMS" (Scribd) could not be opened; the Bullet forum DMS thread (pybullet.org t=2194) was down (503) on three attempts.
- The GTA V numbers above come from a community wrapper of NaturalMotion's message schema (ScriptHookVDotNet), not from NaturalMotion documentation; they are exact as shipped in GTA V but the units of "stiffness" are internal (dimensionless 6–16), not N·m/rad.

## Key question 2: Overgrowth (Wolfire) — GDC 2014 talk, open-source code paths, technique

### Takeaway
Overgrowth's shipped approach is a hybrid: keyframe/procedural animation for locomotion and ledge/roll systems, with a Bullet ragdoll that is only switched on for falls, knockouts and injuries; while ragdolled, a per-joint "ragdoll strength" (0..1) drives the body toward a blended animation pose (flail + fetal + catch-fall layers) via soft 6-DOF "fixed joints" whose ERP/CFM are scaled by strength, and the character wakes up by picking stand/roll/flip/fall recoveries once velocity drops. The sim runs at a fixed 120 Hz.

The active-ragdoll drive (how the ragdoll is pulled toward the animation pose)
- Each `PhysicsJoint` carries both the limit joint (`bt_joint`) and a second `fixed_joint` (Source/Graphics/skeleton.h:71–86). The fixed joint is a `btGeneric6DofConstraint` with zero linear and zero angular limits (a weld) between the two bones (`BulletWorld::AddFixedJoint`, bulletworld.cpp:1142–1178) — [skeleton.h (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Graphics/skeleton.h), [bulletworld.cpp (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Physics/bulletworld.cpp)
- Every step while ragdolled, `RiggedObject::Update` evaluates the animation blend tree, then for each joint computes `weight = max(physics_weights[boneA], physics_weights[boneB])` and calls `skeleton_.SetGFStrength(joint, weight * ragdoll_strength)`, then `skeleton_.RefreshFixedJoints(animation_frame_bone_matrices)` (riggedobject.cpp:1275–1296). `RefreshFixedJoints` re-targets each weld's frames from the *animation* bone matrices (skeleton.cpp:1131–1143, using `UpdateFixedJoint` bulletworld.cpp:1123–1140, which sets `frameInA/frameInB` from the animated transforms). So the weld's rest configuration is the animated relative pose, and the physics bones are pulled toward it — [riggedobject.cpp (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Objects/riggedobject.cpp)
- Strength is mapped onto Bullet's Baumgarte parameters, not onto a motor: `SetJointStrength` sets, for the three angular rows, `BT_CONSTRAINT_STOP_ERP = 0.2f * strength`, `BT_CONSTRAINT_STOP_CFM = 1.0f - strength`, `BT_CONSTRAINT_CFM = 1.0f - strength` (bulletworld.cpp:1731–1740). `SetGFStrength` unlinks the weld entirely when strength is 0 and relinks when nonzero (skeleton.cpp:1240–1255). Morph weights are blended with the same `ragdoll_strength` (riggedobject.cpp:1301–1308) — [bulletworld.cpp (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Physics/bulletworld.cpp)
- Entering ragdoll: `RiggedObject::Ragdoll(velocity)` activates bones, seeds each body's `old_transform` from the interpolated animation transform (so limbs inherit animation velocity), links the skeleton into the Bullet world and sets every joint's GF strength to 0 (riggedobject.cpp:2516–2541). Leaving: `UnRagdoll()` resets the anim client, unlinks from Bullet, `UpdateTwistBones(true)` (2561–2578). Script API: `SetRagdollStrength(float)`, `SetRagdollDamping(float)`, `ApplyForceToRagdoll(force, pos)`, `ApplyForceLineToRagdoll(force, pos, line_dir)`, `ApplyForceToBone` (3922–3927) — [riggedobject.cpp (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Objects/riggedobject.cpp)
- Ragdoll damping/sleep: `RiggedObject::SetDamping(amount)` sets Bullet linear+angular damping on every bone and puts bodies to sleep at 1.0 (riggedobject.cpp:2543–2559); `BulletObject::SetDamping` → `body->setDamping(lin, ang)` (bulletobject.cpp:222–228) — [bulletobject.cpp (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Physics/bulletobject.cpp)

- `SetActiveRagdollInjuredPose` (4979–5000): `time_until_death = 12 s`; `strength = clamp(2 - speed*0.3, 0.2, 1) * (time_until_death - injured_time)*0.1`, capped 0.9; goes LIMP after 12 s.
- `UpdateRagdollDamping` (4896–4925): if `|avg_velocity| < 0.4 m/s` accumulate `ragdoll_static_time`; `damping = min(1, static_time * 0.5)`; at 1.0 the ragdoll is frozen/slept (unless `no_freeze`).
- Recovery: `HandleRagdollRecovery` (5002–5027): after `recovery_time` and when `|avg_velocity|^2 < _auto_wake_vel_threshold (20)` → `WakeUp(_wake_stand)` if `CanRoll()` (sphere radius 1 m finds a contact with normal.y > 0.5) else `WakeUp(_wake_fall)`; if the player pushes a direction (`WantsToRollFromRagdoll`) after 0.2 s → `_wake_roll` (or `_wake_flip` if no ground). `WakeUp` (9871–9936) records torso axes, sets `ragdoll_cam_recover_time = 1.0`, repositions the capsule via `HandleBumperCollision/HandleStandingCollision`, then per mode: stand → `PlayStandAnimation`, `ragdoll_cam_recover_speed 2`, `ragdoll_fade_speed 1`; fall → `ApplyIdle(5)`, cam speed 10, fade 4; flip → `jump_info.StartFall(); flip_info.StartFlip(); FlipRecover()`, cam speed 100; roll → `flip_info.StartRoll(dir)` with `roll_dir = normalize(target*0.9 + normalize(flat_vel))`, cam speed 10, fade 2.
- Impact damage while ragdolled comes from Bullet contact impulses via `Collided(posx,posy,posz, impulse, hardness)` (1306–1395): if `impulse*hardness > 5`: `damage = impulse * 0.005 * fall_damage_mult` (×4 if `fall_death` flag set by level "fall_death" message, line 6387; ×mix(0.2,1,difficulty) for the player); knockout shield decays with airtime `ko_shield -= int((time - ragdoll_air_start)*2*fall_damage_mult)`; death → `SetRagdollType(_RGDL_LIMP)`. Body-fall sound tiers by impulse: >1 light, >2 medium, >3 heavy, >4 "bodyfall"; skid particles if impulse > 1.
- Non-ragdoll landing: `Land(vel)` (8387–8455): if a flip is incomplete (`flip_info.ShouldRagdollOnLanding()`) or in attack state → `GoLimp()` (= `Ragdoll(_RGDL_FALL)`) and `ko_shield -= 1`; otherwise `ApplyIdle(10)`, duck to 1.0 with `duck_vel = 3`, sounds scaled by slide amount, and `velocity.y = max(velocity.y, -10)` (hard clamp on residual downward velocity; −1 for AI). Landing is only recognised after `air_time > 0.1 s` on a surface with `normal.y > 0.5` (8960–8975).
- Wall hits at speed in `HandleGroundCollisions` (8895–8925): for wall contacts the velocity component into the wall is removed (`temp_norm.y *= 0.1`), `impact = |old_vel - new_vel|`, `ImpactSound(impact)`, `tuck_override = max(.., min(0.4, impact*0.1))`, and if the contact flagged `ragdoll` → `Ragdoll(_RGDL_FALL)`, `TakeDamage(0.5)` (or lethal `TakeDamage(1)` if flagged `death`), `recovery_time += 1.0`.
- Character-to-character collisions apply ragdoll forces: `ApplyForceToRagdoll(push_force * 500, COM)` (1657) and `ApplyForceLineToRagdoll(rel_vel * -500 * num_frames, pos, up)` (2717); hits pass `HandleRagdollImpactImpulse(impulse, torso_pos, 5.0)` (3505, 3668); item impacts `HandleRagdollImpactImpulse(force*200, point, 0)` (1223).
- Zone/level scripts kill by ragdolling: `fall_timeout.as` hotspot kills a character `Delay` (default 2.46 s, slider 0.01–10) after entering by executing `Ragdoll(_RGDL_INJURED);` — [fall_timeout.as (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/fall_timeout.as)

Ledge grab (Data/Scripts/ledgegrab.as) — kinematic + IK, not physics

Animation interpolation in the engine
- Bone interpolation between network/animation frames uses Catmull-Rom style cubic weights `0.5*(-t³+2t²-t), 0.5*(3t³-5t²+2), 0.5*(-3t³+4t²+t), 0.5*(t³-t²)` (`GetCubicSplineWeights`, riggedobject.cpp:254–261) and quaternion Slerp for two-bone interpolation (329–339) — [riggedobject.cpp (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Objects/riggedobject.cpp)

### Inferences
- Overgrowth's "active ragdoll" is a soft weld to the animation pose, i.e. a position-level Baumgarte/ERP-CFM constraint rather than a torque motor. With Catto's mapping (below) ERP/CFM are exactly implicit-Euler spring/damper coefficients, so `0.2*strength` ERP with `1-strength` CFM is a stiffness/damping ramp that is unconditionally stable at 120 Hz. In Jolt this maps directly onto a SixDOF/SwingTwist constraint motor in Position mode with `SpringSettings` in FrequencyAndDamping mode, or onto a soft "fixed" constraint.
- The three-layer fall pose (flail base + fetal + catch-fall-front, opacities from a proximity probe and torso orientation) is a cheap stand-in for Euphoria's CatchFall/BodyFoetal/BraceForImpact and is reproducible with a few animation poses.
- Fall gating in Overgrowth is entirely event- and speed-based: impulse thresholds (5 for damage, 1/2/3/4 for sound tiers), `0.005` damage per unit impulse, a shield that decays with airtime, a 1 s recovery timer, and a 20 (m/s)² velocity threshold before waking. These are directly tunable "unlocks".

### Gaps
- The GDC 2014 talk's exact keyframe counts and interpolation curves could not be extracted (video-only; secondary summaries were non-technical). The code above documents what shipped, which is the more reliable reference.
- Overgrowth does not implement balance/stepping while ragdolled (it switches back to the kinematic controller via `WakeUp`), so it is not a source for standing active-ragdoll balance.

## Key question 4: Other active-ragdoll games and techniques

### Takeaway
Shipped active-ragdoll games fall into three patterns: (a) full physics bodies balanced with "cheat" forces and animation-curve-driven torques (TABS, Human Fall Flat, Gang Beasts); (b) animation-first with a physics layer blended on top through powered constraints and per-state "ragdoll profiles" (Uncharted 4, Unreal's Physical Animation Component, Unity ConfigurableJoint active ragdolls); (c) bespoke procedural bodies (Rain World's point-chain soft bodies; Boneworks' tracked-rig-driven physics body in VR).

## Key question 5: Core math — PD joint drives, gains, stability, balance feedback, blend weights

### Takeaway
All systems reduce to a per-joint spring-damper (PD) toward a target pose with a torque limit ("muscle budget"); stability at a fixed tick is governed by the spring frequency vs. the step (Catto: at least ~4 steps per oscillation period with semi-implicit Euler; soft constraints/implicit formulations are unconditionally stable), which is why engines expose frequency/damping-ratio parameterisation (Jolt) and why Stable PD predicts the next state; balance is added on top with SIMBICON's swing-hip feedback `θd = θd0 + cd·d + cv·v` from COM position and velocity, with published gains kp=300 N·m/rad, kd=30 N·m·s/rad and torque limits 90–1000 N·m for human-sized bipeds.

### Cited Findings

PD drive formulation and gains
- Standard discrete PD: `τ_n = −kp(q_n − q̄_n) − kd·q̇_n`; when high gains are needed "the control force can become numerically unstable as the simulation progresses" and the fix has been to reduce the time step, so "PD controllers suffer from undesired coupling between the tracking accuracy and simulation efficiency". Stable PD (SPD): `τ_n = −kp(q_n + Δt·q̇_n − q̄_{n+1}) − kd(q̇_n + Δt·q̈_n)` (and with reference velocity `− kd(q̇_n + Δt·q̈_n − q̄̇_{n+1})`); for articulated bodies `τ_int = −Kp(q_n + q̇_nΔt − q̄_{n+1}) − Kd(q̇_n + q̈_nΔt)` solved via `q̈_n = (M + KdΔt)^−1(−C − Kp(q_n + q̇_nΔt − q̄_{n+1}) − Kd·q̇_n + τ_ext)`; stability condition "kd ≥ kp·Δt should be maintained"; demonstrated with 33 ms steps and explicit Euler — [Tan, Liu, Turk, "Stable Proportional-Derivative Controllers", Georgia Tech (IEEE CG&A 2011)](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- SIMBICON: joints "apply torques computed by proportional-derivative control, τ = kp(θd − θ) − kd·θ̇"; with motion-capture tracking the damping term becomes `−kd(θ̇ − θ̇d)`. Numbers: 2D biped (70 kg trunk, 5 kg thighs, 4 kg shanks, 1 kg feet) "PD gain values of kp = 300 Nm/rad, kd = 30 Nms/rad are used for all joints", ground penalty spring kp = 100000 N/m, kd = 6000 Ns/m, friction 0.65, "A time step of 0.0001 s is used. We use torque limits of 1000Nm, which can be lowered to 370 Nm for all motions except the fast run. Balance via center-of-mass feedback
- SIMBICON balance law: `θd = θd0 + cd·d + cv·v` applied to the swing hip, where d is "the horizontal distance from the stance ankle to the center of mass (COM)" and v "the velocity of the center of mass"; "The midpoint of the hips can be used as a simple and effective proxy for estimating the position and velocity of the center of mass"; cd matters for low-speed/in-place stepping (example: with v = 0, COM at +10 cm requires a quick forward step, at −10 cm a backward step); applied in both sagittal and coronal planes for 3D; generalises to `θd = θd0 + F·[d; v]` with F an n×2 gain matrix (used e.g. for stance-ankle feedback in quiet stance). Torso pitch is controlled by a virtual world-frame PD torque, and the stance-hip torque is set to `τA = −τ_torso − τB` so the virtual torque is realisable with internal torques only. Walk = 4 FSM states (two symmetric steps), run = 2 states; transitions on elapsed time or foot contact; robust to a "350N, 0.2s diagonal push to the torso" — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~kkyin/papers/Yin_SIG07.pdf)
- Shipped equivalents: Euphoria's balancer exposes a step "PredictionTime" (0.2 s; "Bigger values = fewer, bigger steps"), abort at lean 0.6 and COM height 0.5 m above the lowest foot, and "OpposeGravityLegs/Ankles" gravity compensation (see Q1) — [SHVDN ConfigureBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_configure_balance_helper.html); TABS uses head-height-to-ground upward force and COM-relative foot forces — [Game Developer, Landfall](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-landfall-games-finds-the-fun-in-physics-engines); EA's locomotion supervisor compares COM to the foot support polygon — [US7403202](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7403202)

Stability at fixed tick rates, why substepping/soft constraints help
- Erin Catto, "Soft Constraints: Reinventing the Spring", GDC 2011: explicit Euler with springs "causes overshoot and the stiffer the spring" the worse ("Stay far, far, away from explicit Euler"); "Semi-implicit Euler will eventually blow up if you take big time steps. A general rule is to take at least 4 time steps per period of oscillation. For example, if the oscillation frequency is 60Hz, then you shouldn't take time steps slower than 15Hz"; implicit Euler is stable at any step ("The larger the time step, the more energy is absorbed"); tuning raw k and c is bad because they "have units that are baffling" and depend on the (effective) mass including rotational inertia; a soft constraint adds Baumgarte position feedback β and force feedback γ to the velocity constraint and is provably identical to implicit-Euler integration of the harmonic oscillator, so it is always stable; "Gamma is CFM and beta is ERP"; when k becomes very stiff "gamma goes to zero and beta goes to one"; for impulse-based solvers divide γ by h; tune via damping ratio ζ and frequency ω with the effective mass — [Catto GDC 2011 slides (PDF)](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_SoftConstraints_GDC2011.pdf)
- Jolt Physics (the user's engine) exposes exactly this: `MotorSettings` drive `force = stiffness × (target_position − current_position) + damping × (target_velocity − current_velocity)`, modes Off/Velocity/Position (`EMotorState`), symmetric limits `mMinForceLimit/mMaxForceLimit` (N) and `mMinTorqueLimit/mMaxTorqueLimit` (N·m) (unlimited = ±FLT_MAX); `ESpringMode` StiffnessAndDamping (N/m, N·m/rad), mass-normalised (1/s², scales by effective mass/inertia), or FrequencyAndDamping "based on 'Soft Constraints: Reinventing The Spring - Erin Catto - GDC 2011'" with frequency valid in `(0, 0.5 × simulation_frequency]`, recommended stiff 20 Hz (reaches target in ~0.0125 s undamped) and soft 2 Hz (~0.125 s), damping ratio <1 oscillates, =1 critical, >1 over-damped, sensible range [0, 1]; "if your motor doesn't seem to do anything, chances are that you have set the value too low", size it as Force = Mass × Acceleration; `SwingTwistConstraint` and `SixDOFConstraint` support orientation motors; `GetTotalLambda*` gives constraint force for breakability — [Jolt Physics docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/)
- Jolt `Ragdoll` class: `SetPose`/`GetPose` (via `SkeletonPose`), `DriveToPoseUsingKinematics(pose, deltaTime)` ("setting velocities on each of the bodies so that it will reach inPose in inDeltaTime"), `DriveToPoseUsingMotors(pose)` and the advanced overload taking previous pose, target pose and Δt that "Drives both to target position and velocity" — [Jolt Ragdoll class](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/class_ragdoll.html)
- Overgrowth's shipped mapping of a 0..1 "strength" onto Bullet ERP/CFM: `STOP_ERP = 0.2*strength`, `STOP_CFM = 1 - strength`, `CFM = 1 - strength` on the angular rows of a zero-limit 6-DOF weld, at a fixed 120 Hz step — [bulletworld.cpp (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Physics/bulletworld.cpp), [engine.cpp (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Main/engine.cpp)

Blend weight between kinematic animation and dynamics
- Unreal: per-bone `PhysicsBlendWeight` 0..1 (1 = physics drives the bone and its children; 0 = animation), plus `strength_multiplyer` 0..1 scaling motor strength; hit reaction = ramp weight 0→1→0 then disable simulation — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/physics-driven-animation-in-unreal-engine), [Epic Python API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/PhysicalAnimationComponent?application_version=5.0)
- Overgrowth: `ragdoll_strength` 0..1 (per-bone `physics_weights` from the animation output × global strength) sets constraint softness every step; strength ramps: fall = min(0.8, 0.1·|v|), decays to 0 within ~1 s of knockout; injured = up to 0.9 decaying over 12 s; morph weights blended with the same factor; velocity continuity on entry by seeding `old_transform` from animation — [aschar.as (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/aschar.as), [riggedobject.cpp (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Objects/riggedobject.cpp)
- Euphoria: `SetCharacterStrength` and `SetCharacterHealth` in [0..1], `BodyRelax` to collapse into a ragdoll-like state, `SetMuscleStiffness`/`SetStiffness`, and `FallType = RampDownStiffness` (balancer failure ramps stiffness down with `FallMult`) — [SHVDN Euphoria](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_euphoria.html), [SHVDN ConfigureBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_configure_balance_helper.html)
- EA US7403202: strength limits cut to ~50% during impacts and minimised on ground contact; get-up uses internal PD forces plus damped external pelvis forces — [US7403202](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7403202)

### Inferences
- Concrete Jolt recipe consistent with the sources: SwingTwist (shoulders/hips/spine) and Hinge (knees/elbows) constraints with Position motors; set springs in FrequencyAndDamping mode (Catto/Jolt) so "strength" is a frequency ramp (e.g. 2 Hz relaxed → ~10–20 Hz stiff) with ζ≈0.7–1.0, and clamp torque per joint (the Euphoria "stiffness 6–16" scale and SIMBICON's 90–1000 N·m give the plausible band); run physics at 120 Hz or use Jolt collision steps so the stiffest spring has ≥4 steps per period (a 20 Hz spring needs ≥80 Hz). Feed `DriveToPoseUsingMotors(prev_pose, target_pose, dt)` so the drive gets a target velocity (Tan et al.'s reference-velocity term; Jolt's advanced overload).
- The Overgrowth ERP/CFM ramp is a soft constraint in Catto's sense; in Jolt the equivalent is a SixDOF constraint with all rotation axes limited to zero and `SpringSettings` (soft limits) whose frequency is scaled by strength — this is worth noting as a cheaper alternative to motors.
- Balance: implement SIMBICON's `θd = θd0 + cd·d + cv·v` at the swing hip and a world-frame torso PD; publish `cd`, `cv`, abort lean and COM-height thresholds as the "power" knobs (Euphoria's 0.6 lean / 0.5 m defaults are a sane starting point).

### Gaps
- SIMBICON's specific cd/cv gain values are given per gait in its Table 1, which did not survive text extraction; the paper says gains are set interactively.
- Reil & Husbands' kp/kd values are in an image; not extracted.
- No shipped game publishes its joint gains in SI units; Euphoria's 6–16 "stiffness" is an internal scale.

## Key question 6: First-person camera on a physically simulated body

### Takeaway
Few shipped titles put the camera on a simulated body: GTA V (2014 update) offers an optional "First Person Ragdoll" mode where the view stays first-person while the Euphoria ragdoll is knocked down; Boneworks builds the whole player from a tracked head/hands rig and a physics rig with waist/torso/head colliders moved by a "frictionized" rigidbody sphere; Overgrowth (third-person) shows the recovery trick of blending the camera from the ragdoll's tracked position back to the controller over ~0.1–1 s.

### Cited Findings
- GTA V's first-person settings include toggles "First Person Ragdoll", "First Person Combat Roll" and "First Person Head Bobbing" (GTA BOOM guide, Matt Gibbs, Nov 22 2014); the guide does not document the camera mechanics — [GTA BOOM](https://www.gtaboom.com/gta-5-first-person-guide-bc4b). A search snippet of the same guide states: "With the 'first person ragdoll' setting on, if you are knocked down while in first-person, you will remain in first-person" (snippet).
- Boneworks: the avatar "is treated as a physical object which properly collides with everything else in the game"; input from head and two controllers (6-DOF) drives the body; SLZ's "full IK body systems built from the ground up" (see Q4 citations) — [Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/boneworks-reveal-stress-level-zero-physics-based-interactions-embodiment/), [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/boneworks-body-vr-game-mechanic/); community wiki (snippet): locomotion "passed through a highly frictionized rigidbody sphere" with head/torso/waist colliders — [BONEWORKS Wiki (snippet)](https://boneworks.fandom.com/wiki/BONEWORKS_(2019))
- Overgrowth (third-person, but the same problem of a camera following a ragdoll and snapping back): while ragdolled the camera tracks `ragdoll_cam_pos` (aschar.as:11192); on wake `ragdoll_cam_recover_time = 1.0` and `cam_pos = mix(cam_pos, ragdoll_cam_pos, ragdoll_cam_recover_time); ragdoll_cam_recover_time -= dt * ragdoll_cam_recover_speed` (11064–11067), with recover speeds 2 (stand), 10 (fall/roll), 100 (flip), 1000 (instant cases) — [aschar.as (raw)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/aschar.as)
- Euphoria's `HeadLook`/`UseHeadLook` behaviours drive the head toward a look target during balance/catch-fall (defaults on in CatchFall) — a natural anchor for a first-person camera — [SHVDN CatchFallHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_catch_fall_helper.html)

### Inferences
- Combining the sources: mount the camera on the head body's transform, drive the head with a HeadLook-style motor toward the intended view direction (so it resists tumbling like Euphoria's head look), and on recovery blend from the head-body position back to the controller-derived camera with an Overgrowth-style exponential (speed 2–10 /s). Boneworks' separation of a "controller rig" (intent) from a "physics rig" (what collides) is the model for keeping input authoritative while the body is simulated.

### Gaps
- No developer-authored description of GTA V's first-person ragdoll camera implementation was found (only the option's existence). Rockstar Newswire (Nov 2014 first-person post) and Digital Foundry's GTA V PC/next-gen analyses are the places to look.
- No Boneworks/Bonelab engineering talk was retrievable; the "hexabody" description rests on a fan wiki snippet.
