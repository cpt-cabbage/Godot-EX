## Key question 1: Shipped destruction systems — how they work and what they cost

### Takeaway
Every shipped large-scale system falls into one of four families: (a) finite-element deformation/fracture (Pixelux DMM in The Force Unleashed; ~2400 tets per showcase scene, multi-core CG solver, Xbox 360/PS3), (b) pre-fragmented connection graphs with a stress or health model (Red Faction: Guerrilla, Havok Destruction, NVIDIA Blast, UE Chaos), (c) runtime procedural cutting of surfaces or convex compounds (Rainbow Six Siege RealBlast, Smash Hit, Müller 2013 VACD), and (d) voxels with connectivity splitting (Teardown). The published budgets are small and concrete: Siege spent roughly 6 ms per wall and 0.3–23 ms per event, Müller's VACD fracture stayed under 50 ms per event, and Crackdown 3 needed twelve Xbox Ones of cloud compute for its lockstep arena destruction.

### Cited Findings

Pixelux DMM / Star Wars: The Force Unleashed (2008) — see Key question 2 for the full treatment.
- "The motion of the gate and resulting debris was simulated with a real-time finite element system running on an Xbox 360"; the system "is currently being sold commercially by Pixelux Entertainment under the trade name DMM and has been successfully used in the Xbox 360 and the PS3 versions of the video game Star Wars: The Force Unleashed" — [Parker & O'Brien, "Real-Time Deformation and Fracture in a Game Environment", SCA 2009, PDF](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Parker-RTD-2009-08/Parker-RTD-2009-08.pdf); [ACM DL entry](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1599470.1599492).
- The game's Ronin engine combined three middlewares: Havok for rigid-body physics, DMM for "dynamically destructible objects", Euphoria for character behaviour; "LucasArts' programmers had to overcome technical hurdles to get Havok-, DMM- and Euphoria-coded components to interact"; the Wii version used none of the three — [Wikipedia, Star Wars: The Force Unleashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_The_Force_Unleashed).
- In the published game "objects fade away once the game engine has determined that they are no longer needed", a decision the game team made "because they did not want the levels to become cluttered with debris that would interfere with the kinematic motion of the player and other characters" — [Parker & O'Brien 2009](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Parker-RTD-2009-08/Parker-RTD-2009-08.pdf).

Red Faction: Guerrilla — Geo-Mod 2.0 (Volition, 2009)
- Eric Arnold (senior developer, Volition), interviewed 30 June 2009: the engine reacts to how a structure is hit — "Swinging a sledge hammer will have a different effect than hitting it with a rocket propelled grenade or driving through it at high speed"; weakening supports collapses bridges; the team filmed real demolitions and added rebar to concrete mid-development; "Artists had to become structural engineers to prevent their creations from crumbling as soon as they are dropped in game"; the load was both "updating the physics in real time" and "having to draw the insides of walls"; development took "5+ years"; the PS3 build needed SPU-specific optimisation — [CBS News, "Red Faction: Guerrilla Q & A", 2009](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/red-faction-guerrilla-q-a/).

- Geo-Mod 2.0 "uses pre-broken meshes that respond in a realistic manner to physical forces such as stress and kinetic impact (typically from player weapons or vehicles)" and moved away from real-time CSG on terrain toward "physics-based destruction of man-made structures" — [Red Faction Wiki, Geo-Mod 2.0](https://www.redfactionwiki.com/wiki/Geo-Mod_2.0) (fan wiki, page marked under construction, no citations).
- Guerrilla "does not allow for the destruction of terrain"; buildings, cover and structures can be destroyed; destroyed buildings drop "salvage" currency — [Wikipedia, Red Faction: Guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Faction:_Guerrilla).
- Red Faction: Armageddon art director Chad Greene: the engine took "7 years of engine development", five of them on Guerrilla; objects contain invisible "shards" that stay dormant until destruction; shard size differs per material (concrete vs fibreglass); an earlier iteration where "shard size was so dense you could write your name into the wall" produced hundreds of thousands of polygons and had to be dialled back; QA had to test "world anchoring" — [Game Developer, "The Destructible World: Building Red Faction: Armageddon"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-destructible-world-building-i-red-faction-armageddon-i-).

Rainbow Six: Siege — RealBlast (Ubisoft Montreal, 2015) — from the GDC 2016 slide deck (text extracted locally from the PDF)
- Definitions: procedural destruction is "A change in the state of an object generated at runtime, where the outcome is unique", as opposed to "pre-fragmented destruction, which is pre-determined and has a fixed outcome" — [L'Heureux, "The Art of Destruction in Rainbow Six: Siege", GDC 2016, PDF](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/LHeureux_Julien_Art_Of_Destruction.pdf).
- Team: "Small team, mostly programmers", "Dedicated to destruction for ~5 years"; RealBlast "First shipped destruction with AC IV: Black Flag" — same source.
- Destruction model: "Objects are separated into different parts based on their physical material"; "Hierarchical decomposition based on fragmentation"; "Connection-based leaf graph"; "Game interacts with connections, leaf graph manages state"; leaf fragments can be flagged procedural and "create new child fragments" — same source.
- Surface procedural destruction ("Developed exclusively for Rainbow 6: Siege"): "Use arbitrary cutting polygons to cut a planar surface", "3D -> 2D projection", intersect surface polygons with cutter polygons ("Simple example: Weiler-Atherton polygon clipping algorithm"), triangulate with ear clipping ("Can handle multiple holes"), extrude back to 3D; cutter classes define a perimeter (random ellipse, spline), inner fragments (Voronoi) or both (glass, texture cutters mapped in UV space) — same source.
- Collision after cuts: "After destruction, very likely concave"; solution is a "Collection of 2D convex shapes from a simplified version of the surface", removing small holes and reducing hole tessellation; actual surface geometry is used only for hi-res queries such as shooting — same source.
- Debris: "No procedurally cut dynamic fragments", "Well-placed replacements", "Instanced", "Recycled aggressively"; "Dynamic fragments don't collide together"; "Vaporize fragments on explosion"; "Simple collision primitives (always boxes)"; Havok FX — same source.
- Budgets: 60 fps target; "Given roughly 6ms for a wall (2 procedural layers + pre-fragmented)"; "GPU memory: 25 MB"; "RAM: 200 MB data + 150 MB engine" — same source.
- Benchmarks (PC / PS4 / XB1, two values each, labelled "To take with a heap of salt"): single bullet hole 0.33/0.36 ms, 1.1/1.4 ms, 1.1/1.5 ms; single explosion on a drywall layer 1.4/1.9 ms, 2.8/4 ms, 3.6/4.9 ms; single explosion through 2 drywall + 2 wood layers 8.1/10.3 ms, 19.5/23.5 ms, 19/23 ms — same source.
- Engineering: destruction runs asynchronously (with an event-forwarding mechanism because it "Introduces delay in game perception vs. actual destruction state") and is time-sliced ("What about a 60ms spike?"), with pre-allocated pools and in-place algorithms; multithreading at object granularity — same source.
- Networking: replicate "Events (messages)" not "States (meshes)"; determinism requires "The exact same inputs / In the same order"; the RNG is seeded "based on impact position"; "Destruction on dynamic objects not replicated"; "All dynamic objects and debris are always small, ignored by gameplay" — same source.
- Systemic consequences: destruction events feed AI navlink updates, AI visibility through partially broken walls, and sound propagation ("Destruction changes the acoustic of the environment drastically"); rendering loses static lighting and occluders — same source.
- Future work listed in 2016: "Plastic deformation", "Stress analysis" — same source.

Teardown (Tuxedo Labs, 2020) and Smash Hit (Mediocre, 2014) — Dennis Gustafsson
- Design: "Gameplay was designed to fit the technology"; because any wall can be torn down, the only true obstacles are "elevation, distance, water and unbreakable objects"; an AMD 3D-texture limitation capped the final chase level at 400 m instead of 800 m — [Voxagon Blog, "Teardown design notes", 2020](https://blog.voxagon.se/2020/11/05/teardown-design-notes.html).
- Objects are "represented and modeled as voxel volumes on a regular grid"; Gustafsson "spent a lot of time optimizing the physics and destruction, making use of both SIMD and multithreading" — [80.lv, "Teardown Developer Breaks Down Multiplayer and Voxel Destruction Tech"](https://80.lv/articles/teardown-developer-breaks-down-multiplayer-and-voxel-destruction-tech).
- The engine uses "several volumes that contain a smaller number of voxels to allow for local translation"; each voxel carries an 8-bit palette index whose material "determines a voxel's colour, roughness, emissiveness, reflectivity, and physical type"; engine in C++ with real-time ray tracing; a noted weakness is that buildings can "remain unrealistically supported by very few voxels" — [Wikipedia, Teardown (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teardown_(video_game)).
- Multiplayer (2026): voxel data could not be sent ("too much data"); instead the server sends deterministic commands ("cut hole in this shape at voxel coord x,y,z", "change ownership of that shape") applied in the same order on all clients; transforms use an unreliable priority-queued stream with a budget of "around one Mbit per client"; full scene files are "30-50 MB is common, often more" — [Voxagon Blog, "The unlikely story of Teardown Multiplayer", 2026](https://blog.voxagon.se/2026/03/13/teardown-multiplayer.html).
- Structural integrity: Gustafsson has publicly shown a prototype structural-integrity/stress system for his next game, confirmed by a Tuxedo Labs developer as "a prototype of the game's structural integrity system", with a solver "inspired by Erin's work" (Erin Catto) — [80.lv, "This Real-Time Voxel Building Demolition Is So Satisfying"](https://80.lv/articles/this-real-time-voxel-building-demolition-is-so-satisfying). A search-result snippet attributed to his X post claimed "one building at 60 fps"; the post itself could not be opened (x.com, xcancel and nitter all failed), so treat that number as unverified.
- Smash Hit fracture: objects are "compounds of convex shapes"; an object breaks when "the exerted impulse is above a certain threshold"; the engine then "carves out a small volume around the point of impact" and "slices all the convex shapes with five bounding planes, slightly randomized around the point of impact"; contact forces of breakable objects are capped and the solver reruns "multiple solves during breakage" (max three) so pre-break motion is preserved — [Voxagon Blog, "Cracking destruction", 2014](https://blog.voxagon.se/2014/05/13/cracking-destruction.html).
- GDC 2015 slides (text extracted locally): the step loop is "1 integrate new velocities / 2 collision detection / 3 limit impulses / 4 run solver / 5 if there are saturated impulses: fracture objects, collision detection on new objects, if ++iterationCount < 3 then goto 3 / 6 integrate new positions"; the engine is "Shape-centric", broad phase is a "Dynamic Bounding Volume Tree" with "World offset shifting", near phase "GJK incremental manifold" with "Speculative contacts", solver "Sequential Impulse, No solver islands, Custom deactivation"; robust convex plane splitting clamps intersection points onto edges and nudges them away from vertices — [Gustafsson, "Physics for Game Programmers: Destruction in Smash Hit", GDC 2015 (Internet Archive PDF)](https://archive.org/download/GDC2015Gustafsson/GDC2015-Gustafsson.pdf).

NVIDIA Blast (open source; part of the PhysX repository)
- Structure: "static data associated with a destructible is stored in an asset"; actors are damaged and fracture into hierarchical chunks; user-designated support chunks "form an exact cover" of the root and are connected by bonds (centroid, normal, area) forming the support graph; damage applies a "user-defined material function" to bond/chunk health, bonds with non-positive health are broken; after damage Blast performs "island detection on the support graph to find all groups of support chunks that are connected by unbroken bonds" and each island becomes a new actor; bonds to "the world" anchor structures — [Blast SDK docs, Introduction](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/blast-sdk/latest/docs/api/introduction.html).
- Stress solver: works "directly with the bond graph", "simulates iteratively spreading forces across bonds"; needs mass, volume, position per node (or `setAllNodesInfoFromLL(density)`); one node must have zero mass as anchor or supporting forces must be applied; "Computation time is linearly proportional to" `maxSolverIterationsPerFrame`; experimental `graphReductionLevel`; independent tension/compression/shear limits; overstressed bonds are turned into fracture commands — [Blast SDK docs, Stress Solver (NvBlastExtStress)](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/blast-sdk/latest/docs/api/extensions/ext_stress.html).
- UE4 authoring parameters (useful as a reproducible parameter set): material Health, Min Damage Threshold (e.g. 0.1 "filters all damage below 10% of health"), Max Damage Threshold (e.g. 0.8); impact damage "Damage = Impulse / Hardness", Max Damage Radius, Velocity Based (ignore mass) vs mass × velocity, Self Collision; stress: Hardness, Bond Iterations Per Frame ("linearly connected with amount of time spent"), Graph Reduction Level (graph "2 ^ GraphReductionLevel times smaller"), Angular vs Linear Stress Fraction, Split Impulse Strength; debris: Debris Depth, Debris Max Separation, Debris Max Size, Valid Bounds, Debris Lifetime Min/Max seconds — [NVIDIA Blast UE4 plugin, Blast Settings](https://archive.docs.nvidia.com/gameworks/content/gameworkslibrary/blast/1.1/authoring_docs/BlastUe4_BlastSettings.html).
- The PhysX GitHub repository is BSD-3-Clause and includes the Blast SDK and Flow SDK alongside PhysX — [GitHub, NVIDIA-Omniverse/PhysX](https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/PhysX).

Unreal Engine 5 Chaos Destruction
- Geometry Collections are built from static meshes ("water-tight with no interpenetration"); six Voronoi fracture modes (Uniform, Clustered, Radial, Planar, Slice, Brick); Clustering groups pieces into Fracture Levels ("Level 0" is the whole object) with a per-level Damage Threshold ("descending thresholds across ascending levels"); Max Cluster Level caps progression; Fields (Anchor, ExternalClusterStrain, InternalClusterStrain, LinearForce, AngularVelocity/Torque, Sleep, Disable, Culling); a connection graph built at construction; `OnChaosBreakEvent` reports location, velocity, angular velocity and mass; performance levers are Collision Particles Fraction, cached (kinematic playback) simulations, per-collection custom solvers, Disable/Sleep thresholds and Cluster Group Index — [Unreal Engine docs, Chaos Destruction Overview (4.27)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/chaos-destruction-overview?application_version=4.27).
- Numbers from the Fields guide: collections with Damage Threshold 200,000 break under a 400,000 strain field while collections at 500,000 do not; "If the strain force is lower than the Damage Threshold for the Geometry Collection, the strain will not cause a fracture"; an internal-strain decay example reduces thresholds by 100,000 per second after a 1 s delay against a 500,000 threshold; Sleep/Disable field thresholds of 2 vs 50 illustrate aggressiveness; strain falloff is 0..1 — [Unreal Engine docs, Chaos Fields User Guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/chaos-fields-user-guide-in-unreal-engine). Units are not stated in either page.
- The "Chaos Destruction Optimization" page returned an empty body for versions 5.0, 5.4 and 5.6; its specific numbers could not be captured.

Runtime convex fracture in research (Müller, Chentanez, Kim 2013)
- "Real Time Dynamic Fracture with Volumetric Approximate Convex Decompositions": visual meshes are represented as volumetric approximate convex decompositions and user-defined (Voronoi-style) fracture patterns are applied locally at the impact; supports partial fracture and island detection "to determine support structures"; the Roman arena demo (1 M vertices, 500 k faces) "runs at over 30 fps including rigid body simulation, dust simulation and rendering" until it is split into "20k separate pieces"; "The time to fracture small to average sized objects is typically negligible, i.e. below 10 ms" and even the largest buildings stay "below 50ms" per fracture event on an Intel Core i7 3.07 GHz with a GTX 680 (GPU rigid bodies); frame time was typically 15/45/40 percent rendering/rigid bodies/dust; below a size threshold no true rigid bodies are created, debris particles are used instead — [Müller, Chentanez, Kim, SIGGRAPH 2013, PDF](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/fractureSG2013.pdf); [ACM DOI 10.1145/2461912.2461934](https://doi.org/10.1145/2461912.2461934).

### Inferences
- Every shipped system separates "what the physics simulates" from "what the player sees": DMM splinters, Red Faction shards, Siege instanced debris, Control's three visual scales, Müller's debris-as-spheres. A Godot design should plan the same split from day one (a few real RigidBody3D chunks plus a MultiMesh/particle debris layer).
- The two cheapest reproducible concepts for a small team are Siege's "connection graph + island detection" (also the core of Blast) and Smash Hit's "impulse threshold → plane-split convex compound", both fully specified in the sources above.
- All the online systems (Siege, Teardown, Crackdown) replicate deterministic events, not meshes; even a single-player game benefits from this for replays and save games.

## Key question 2: Digital Molecular Matter (DMM) and finite-element destruction — status, method, ancestry, building blocks, verdict

### Takeaway
DMM is real, published, and was run on 2005-era consoles, but it is proprietary middleware from a small Geneva company whose public footprint stops around a Maya 2019 plugin; the underlying method (corotational tetrahedral FEM with implicit integration, node-splitting fracture, additive plasticity, sleeping islands, GPU-skinned embedded render meshes) is fully described in Parker & O'Brien 2009 and its ancestors and could be re-implemented, but the paper's own PC numbers — roughly 2400 tetrahedra costing up to ~16 ms of a 4-core 2.4 GHz CPU — and the absence of any open-source real-time FEM *fracture* library mean it is a multi-month engine project, not an addon; for "a super-strong player punches through walls", pre-fractured/connection-graph rigid bodies or voxels deliver the same player-facing reaction at a fraction of the cost.

### Cited Findings

Status of DMM and Pixelux
- DMM is proprietary middleware by Pixelux that uses finite element analysis for destruction and deformation; developed over 6.5 years starting 2004; CEO Mitchell Bunnell; games: Star Wars: The Force Unleashed (exclusive to LucasArts 2005–2008) and The Force Unleashed II; engine integrations were made for Gamebryo, Trinigy Vision, Irrlicht and OGRE; Moving Picture Company built it into its "Kali" pipeline; "The plugin is built into Maya 2012"; last stable release 1.0 (May 2010); platforms Windows, Xbox 360, PS3, Mac OS X, Linux; in 2015 James F. The published method (Parker & O'Brien, SCA 2009 — text extracted from the PDF)
- Core: "a corotational tetrahedral finite element method" assembled "from components published in the graphics and computational physics literatures"; requirements listed: "efficient and predictable" performance, "cannot blow up or crash", memory limits on consoles, authoring accessible to level designers — [Parker & O'Brien 2009](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Parker-RTD-2009-08/Parker-RTD-2009-08.pdf).
- Rendering/embedding: a coarse tet mesh encloses the graphical model; each render vertex stores barycentric coordinates in its owning tet; deformation is applied "within the GPU using shader programs" with nodal positions encoded "into a texture map" read from the vertex shader (bone-matrix limits of "256 or less" made skinning unsuitable); badly shaped elements are handled by clamping singular values of the β matrices [IO06] and the solver tolerates inverted elements — same source.
- Plasticity: additive split ε̃ = εP + εE; when the Frobenius norm of εE "exceeds a material yield threshold, y", plastic strain grows at creep rate c; plastic strain is clamped at a maximum n — same source.
- Islands and sleeping: meshes are broken into persistent islands that are "live, asleep, or kinematic"; nodes are partitioned with METIS; "Islands are initially asleep and are not simulated. They are awoken if they are struck by another object or if a force above some threshold is applied"; islands whose nodes stay below a velocity threshold for multiple timesteps sleep; contacting islands form transient composite islands — same source.
- Integration: "linearized backward Euler"; lumped mass, Rayleigh damping; system (M + Δt C + Δt² K) v⁺ = Δt f + M v − Δt K (x − u), solved by conjugate gradient with 3×3 block-compressed rows, "stop once a relative error of 0.001 has been reached" plus an iteration cap that designers set "as if it were another material parameter"; an island is "large" if it has "at least sixty nodes" and more than a quarter of all live nodes; large islands use a parallel CG, small ones are farmed out serially to threads (Figure 2) — same source.
- Fracture: "a simplified version of the algorithm that appears in [OH99] and [OBH02]" with tet splitting "disabled" because designers could not plan for growing tet counts; per-node separation tensor → eigen-decomposition; "When we find a node with a positive eigenvalue that exceeds the material's toughness parameter, we set the split plane to be the plane normal to the corresponding eigenvector passing through the node, and we replicate the node"; nodes are examined in random order, without residual propagation, with "an arbitrary limit on the number of fracture events that we will allow in any given timestep"; fracture is suppressed if it would leave "fewer than three face-connected tetrahedra"; fracture runs only for materials "tagged as breakable" and not necessarily every step — same source.
- Splinters: artist-authored sub-pieces "significantly smaller than the tetrahedral elements", each associated with the tet containing its centroid, re-associated on fracture, so fracture surfaces look like wood/brick rather than triangular blocks; concrete spawns particles on new fracture faces — same source.
- Collision: fixed timestep; objects moving "more than 1/6 their bounding box diagonal per timestep are considered 'fast'" and are sub-stepped; hybrid friction (implicit dynamic, explicit static) — same source.
- Timings: PC measurements only ("contractual restrictions preclude disclosure of detailed timing information for the Xbox 360 and PS3"); the glass-explosion scene "involved approximately 2400 tetrahedra" on "a PC with Intel 2.4 GHz Core 2 Q6600, 667 MHz DDR2"; Figure 6 plots per-frame simulation time over ~180 frames for 1–4 processors on a 0–25 ms axis; Figure 7's four-processor breakdown (Fracture, Solve, Solver Setup, Collision Detection, Build Islands, Other) is drawn on a 0–16 ms axis; Figure 8 shows live and sleeping tets varying within a 0–2500 range over the run; speed-up is sub-linear "in part because not all parts of our code have been parallelized" and because of Q6600 memory coherence — same source. (The exact per-frame values are only in the plots; the axis ranges are the reproducible bound.)
- Why dynamic FEM rather than quasi-static or jointed rigid bodies: stored elastic energy is released at fracture and "dramatically changes the resulting fracture patterns and motion", whereas "rigid bodies joined by constraints produce deadened motions where the objects look like they simply fell apart" — same source.

### Inferences
- Proprietary status: nothing in the reachable record says DMM was open-sourced or acquired; the company still advertises "evaluation and licensing" but its newest visible product build targets Maya 2019 and its news page is gone. A licence enquiry is possible but a small team should assume no active support.
- Cost model from the paper: ~2400 tets ≈ up to ~16 ms on four 2007-era cores. Modern desktop cores are several times faster per core, but the paper also shows CG solve, solver setup and collision each taking a comparable share, and Jolt's own soft bodies are described as "fairly expensive". A realistic planning figure is "a few thousand simulated tets per frame budget-slice, on a dedicated thread", i.e. one or two DMM-class objects live at once, exactly the pattern the paper's sleeping islands were built for.
- Nothing open-source does real-time FEM *fracture* out of the box: Vega has FEM but no fracture; PhysX 5 has FEM but GPU-only and tearing is left to the application; Jolt and Bullet soft bodies are PBD-class without fracture (Bullet has link cutting). A DMM-style reproduction therefore means writing the fracture (separation tensor, node replication, splinter re-association) and the island manager yourself on top of one of those solvers, plus a tet-meshing pipeline (fTetWild/TetGen) and a GPU barycentric skinning path.
- Verdict for this game: the player-facing fantasy ("I hit the wall hard and it gives way with debris and dust") is delivered equally by (1) a Blast/Siege-style connection graph over pre-fractured convex chunks with impulse-thresholded bond breaking, or (2) voxel connectivity splitting, both of which run on the existing Jolt rigid-body path. DMM's unique selling point — elastic energy storage and bending before break — matters most for wood, glass and metal beams, which could be faked with a plastic-deformation shader or a short XPBD soft body swap for the specific hero objects. Reproducing TFU-style FEM in Godot is a several-month C++ effort with solver-stability, collision and content-pipeline risk; the pre-fractured route is days to weeks with existing addons and V-HACD.

## Key question 3: Fracture algorithms, support graphs, debris, and performance budgets

### Takeaway
The reproducible toolbox is: Voronoi or plane-split fracture (offline or runtime), a connection/support graph with island detection to decide what falls, an impulse or strain threshold to decide when bonds break, and an aggressively pooled cosmetic debris layer; every shipped source above caps real bodies (Siege boxes-only recycled debris, Müller's no-rigid-bodies-below-a-size rule, ShatterKit's 48 physics chunks + 2048-shard pool) and bounds work per event (Siege 6 ms per wall, Müller < 50 ms per event).

### Cited Findings
- Voronoi fracture at authoring time (UE Chaos): Uniform/Clustered/Radial/Planar/Slice/Brick Voronoi, hierarchical clustering with per-level damage thresholds — [Unreal Engine docs, Chaos Destruction Overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/chaos-destruction-overview?application_version=4.27).
- Runtime Voronoi over convex decompositions with local application at the impact and island detection for support (Müller 2013; <10 ms typical, <50 ms worst per event) — [Müller, Chentanez, Kim 2013](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/fractureSG2013.pdf).
- Runtime plane splitting of convex compounds around the impact (Smash Hit: five randomised planes; impulse threshold; ≤3 re-solves per step) — [Voxagon, Cracking destruction](https://blog.voxagon.se/2014/05/13/cracking-destruction.html), [GDC 2015 slides](https://archive.org/download/GDC2015Gustafsson/GDC2015-Gustafsson.pdf).
- Runtime 2D polygon clipping of planar surfaces (Siege: Weiler–Atherton clipping, ear-clipping triangulation, extrusion; collision from simplified 2D convex sets) — [L'Heureux GDC 2016](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/LHeureux_Julien_Art_Of_Destruction.pdf).

- Sleeping is the universal lever: DMM islands start asleep and wake on impact — [Parker & O'Brien 2009](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Parker-RTD-2009-08/Parker-RTD-2009-08.pdf); Jolt sleeps islands "when they have come to rest" via `mTimeBeforeSleep` / `mPointVelocitySleepThreshold` and does not wake bodies on creation/removal so loading does not thrash — [Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/), [Jolt README](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics); Godot exposes these as `sleep_velocity_threshold` 0.03 m/s and `sleep_time_threshold` 0.5 s — [Godot-EX jolt_project_settings.cpp](/home/user/Godot-EX/modules/jolt_physics/jolt_project_settings.cpp).

### Inferences
- A Godot pipeline that matches the shipped systems: Cell Fracture or VoronoiShatter offline → one convex hull per shard (or V-HACD for concave shards) → a bond list generated from shard adjacency → Blast-style `Damage = Impulse / Hardness` at contact → island detection (flood fill over unbroken bonds) → islands not connected to a world anchor become RigidBody3D compounds → shards below a volume threshold are spawned into a MultiMesh debris pool with a lifetime.
- Budget rule of thumb derived from the sources: cap simultaneous awake fracture bodies in the low hundreds, keep debris cosmetic, and spread fracture work over frames (Siege's time-slicing; Müller's proposed multi-step distribution).

### Gaps
- No Godot-specific benchmark (bodies vs ms) was found for RigidBody3D counts under Jolt; only Jolt's own numbers and Godot forum claims exist.
- Blender Cell Fracture's algorithm/implementation is undocumented in the current manual.

## Key question 4: Godot 4 feasibility — RigidBody3D limits, Jolt, addons, soft bodies, voxels, what needs C++

### Takeaway
The fork already contains the two hardest ingredients (Jolt 5.6 with a 10,240-body default ceiling that is user-raisable, and V-HACD), Jolt scales to thousands of bodies across cores, and three open-source Voronoi/pre-fracture addons exist for Godot 4; what is missing and would need C++ (module or GDExtension) is any stress/support-graph solver, runtime mesh cutting at scale, and anything FEM.

### Cited Findings
- Jolt is the built-in 3D engine option since 4.4 (ported from Mikael Hermansson's godot-jolt, sponsored by W4 Games), experimental and not a full drop-in — [GameFromScratch, "Godot 4.4 Gets Native Jolt Physics Support"](https://gamefromscratch.com/godot-4-4-gets-native-jolt-physics-support/) (secondary). The extension's README claims "better performance, mainly, but also generally having a more stable simulation" than Godot Physics, says "Godot Jolt is not able to make such guarantees" about determinism, notes the physics server is not thread-safe, and is in maintenance mode because the module is built in — [GitHub, godot-jolt/godot-jolt](https://github.com/godot-jolt/godot-jolt).
- Official differences/limits: Jolt lacks joint soft limits (bias, damping, softness, relaxation, restitution); uses a convex radius (shrinks shapes) rather than an enlarging margin; Baumgarte stabilisation is position-only; ghost collisions mitigated by active-edge detection and enhanced internal edge removal; `get_contact_impulse()` values "are estimated and only accurate when bodies collide with no other bodies"; kinematic-vs-static contacts are opt-in (`generate_all_kinematic_contacts`); ray-cast face indices cost ~25 % memory — [Godot docs, Using Jolt Physics](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html).
- Body ceiling: `physics/jolt_physics_3d/limits/max_bodies` default 10240 with `or_greater`; exceeding it logs an error per body — [Godot-EX jolt_project_settings.cpp](/home/user/Godot-EX/modules/jolt_physics/jolt_project_settings.cpp), [Godot-EX jolt_space_3d.cpp](/home/user/Godot-EX/modules/jolt_physics/spaces/jolt_space_3d.cpp).
- Jolt performance: 3680-body ragdoll benchmark; 4.9×/5.7× thread scaling; better multi-core scaling than PhysX 4.1 and Bullet 3.21 on the convex-vs-mesh test although "its single threaded performance is worse than that of PhysX" — [Rouwé, Multicore Scaling PDF](https://jrouwe.nl/jolt/JoltPhysicsMulticoreScaling.pdf); PerformanceTest scenes: Ragdoll 3680 bodies, ConvexVsMesh 484 shapes on a 2000-triangle mesh, Pyramid 1240 boxes, LargeMesh 4410 boxes — [JoltPhysics Docs/PerformanceTest.md](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/master/Docs/PerformanceTest.md); Jolt is MIT, deterministic given identical inputs, used by Horizon Forbidden West and Death Stranding 2 — [GitHub, jrouwe/JoltPhysics](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics).
- Known Jolt-in-Godot performance issues (titles from search results; issues not opened): "Changing collision shapes for bodies already in scene tree is slow when using Jolt Physics" — [godotengine/godot #105642](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/105642); "Unexpectedly big performance impact of overlapping non-monitoring Area3Ds" — [godotengine/godot #106482](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/106482). A secondary blog claimed loading thousands of box collisions rose from ~2 s to >30 s under Jolt — [StraySpark blog](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/godot-46-jolt-physics-migration-guide) (unverified vendor blog).
- General RigidBody3D guidance: prefer primitives; few convex shapes; no trimesh on dynamic bodies; raise physics ticks (120/180/240) for stability at CPU cost; enable Continuous CD for fast bodies; beware the "physics spiral of death"; Jolt is more stable for stacking than GodotPhysics — [Godot docs, Troubleshooting physics issues](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/troubleshooting_physics_issues.html). Contact reporting requires `contact_monitor = true` and `max_contacts_reported > 0`; forces are per-tick, impulses are one-shot; `_integrate_forces(state)` exposes `PhysicsDirectBodyState3D` — [Godot docs, RigidBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_rigidbody3d.html).
- Addons:
  - VoronoiShatter (robertvaradan / "woollyone"): Voronoi via Godot's CSG combiner; random points in the bounding box → cells → clipped to the mesh; editor button and runtime `VoronoiGenerator` API; "Create Rigid Bodies" button; requires manifold meshes (non-manifold "will return an empty set of meshes"); no UVs on inner faces (triplanar recommended); runs on the main thread; "experimental"; MIT; Asset Library lists version 0.3 for Godot 4.6 — [GitHub, robertvaradan/voronoishatter](https://github.com/robertvaradan/voronoishatter), [Godot Asset Library 3918](https://godotengine.org/asset-library/asset/3918).
  - Godot-Destruction (the-dunk): consecutive plane slicing of convex meshes (triangle count ≈ original + 2 × cut faces), Voronoi fracturing "still in development", global minimum-volume limit, convex meshes only, MIT; author notes "pre-computed destruction is still the superior option" for performance — [GitHub, the-dunk/Godot-Destruction](https://github.com/the-dunk/Godot-Destruction).
  - Jummit destruction plugin: pre-fractured swap, 5–20 pieces, Godot 3.x/4.x, MIT — [GitHub, Jummit/godot-destruction-plugin](https://github.com/Jummit/godot-destruction-plugin).
  - ShatterKit (commercial, $19.99): Godot 4.7+, pure GDScript, seeded Voronoi with capped interiors, persistent carved holes, anchor-based stress graph, 48 physics chunks + 2048-shard MultiMesh pool, deterministic "bit-identical replays", 36 effects — [ShatterKit itch.io](https://indiedevwilliam.itch.io/shatterkit). No benchmark numbers are published.
- Voxels: Zylann's godot_voxel exists as both a C++ module and a GDExtension; blocky (Minecraft-style) and smooth Transvoxel LOD terrain; "Realtime 3D terrain editable in-game"; chunk paging; "Godot physics integration + alternate fast Minecraft-like collisions"; MIT; multiplayer sync and blocky LOD are work-in-progress — [GitHub, Zylann/godot_voxel](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel); `VoxelTool.do_sphere/do_box/do_point` with `MODE_ADD/REMOVE/SET/TEXTURE_PAINT`; `smooth_sphere` with a large radius called 60×/s can cause "significant performance drops" — [godot_voxel docs, VoxelTool](https://voxel-tools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/VoxelTool/). Note this is terrain-oriented: it does not split disconnected voxel islands into rigid bodies the way Teardown does (no such feature is listed).
- CSG at runtime: CSG nodes are "mainly intended for prototyping"; "complex meshes can take a while to process"; avoid putting too many objects in one tree — [Godot docs, Prototyping levels with CSG](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/3d/csg_tools.html). (VoronoiShatter depends on this combiner, which is why its author recommends editor-time shattering.)
- SoftBody3D for cloth/awnings/banners: see Key question 2 (Jolt more robust; precision > 5; no interpolation) — [Godot docs, Using SoftBody](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/soft_body.html).
- GDExtension vs module: see Key question 2 — [Godot docs, About godot-cpp](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/scripting/cpp/about_godot_cpp.html).

### Inferences
- Cheap tier (GDScript only): pre-fractured shards from Blender/VoronoiShatter, swap on impulse threshold using `contact_monitor` + `get_contact_impulse` (accepting Jolt's caveat that the value is only exact in isolated two-body contacts), sleeping enabled, debris in MultiMesh.
- Mid tier (GDScript or C#): add a bond graph and island flood-fill per destructible (dozens of shards — trivial in script), Blast-style hardness per material.
- Expensive tier (C++): Blast itself compiled as a module/GDExtension (BSD-3, physics-agnostic so it can drive Jolt bodies), runtime cutting à la Siege/Müller, Teardown-style voxel connectivity with per-island bodies, or FEM.
- Determinism for replays is not guaranteed by Godot's Jolt integration even though Jolt itself is deterministic; do not design around lockstep replication.

### Gaps
- No measured Godot+Jolt "bodies per millisecond" figure was found.
- Whether godot_voxel can emit per-island rigid bodies (Teardown-style) is not documented; assume it cannot without custom code.

## Key question 5: Strength-power consequences — thresholds, material data, how TFU and Hulk tie damage to speed and mass

### Takeaway
Shipped systems trigger breakage on an impulse or strain threshold ("Damage = Impulse / Hardness" in Blast, impulse > limit in Smash Hit, strain > Damage Threshold in Chaos), and TFU tuned the fantasy by editing physical parameters rather than scripting (a 5,000 lb stormtrooper); real-world anchors exist — a 73 kg boulder breaks through a single-leaf brick wall at about 500 J of kinetic energy, and normal concrete has 2–5 MPa tensile strength — so a player at 80 kg and 10 m/s (4 kJ) is already in wall-breaking territory, which lets the "power growth" be expressed as thresholds the player's momentum crosses.

### Cited Findings
- Blast: "Damage = Impulse / Hardness"; "Velocity Based: If true, ignores mass; if false, uses mass × velocity"; damage filtered by Min/Max Damage Threshold fractions of health; Max Damage Radius with falloff; per-bond tension/compression/shear stress limits — [Blast UE4 settings](https://archive.docs.nvidia.com/gameworks/content/gameworkslibrary/blast/1.1/authoring_docs/BlastUe4_BlastSettings.html), [Blast Stress Solver](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/blast-sdk/latest/docs/api/extensions/ext_stress.html).
- Smash Hit: break "when the exerted impulse is above a certain threshold"; contact impulses of breakables are capped at the limit so the solver reports saturation, then fracture and re-solve — [Voxagon, Cracking destruction](https://blog.voxagon.se/2014/05/13/cracking-destruction.html), [GDC 2015 slides](https://archive.org/download/GDC2015Gustafsson/GDC2015-Gustafsson.pdf).
- Chaos: strain fields vs per-level Damage Threshold (200k/400k/500k example; 100k/s decay) — [Chaos Fields User Guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/chaos-fields-user-guide-in-unreal-engine).
- DMM: fracture when a node's separation-tensor eigenvalue "exceeds the material's toughness parameter"; yield threshold and creep for plasticity; designers exposed to toughness, plasticity, shape preservation — [Parker & O'Brien 2009](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Parker-RTD-2009-08/Parker-RTD-2009-08.pdf), [Pixelux DMM Plugin](https://www.pixelux.com/DMMplugin.php); TFU tuned gameplay by making "the column a little weaker" and the thrown stormtrooper "weigh 5,000 pounds" with a more rigid body — [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/dmm-fea-for-vfx/).
- Red Faction: response depends on how the structure is hit (sledgehammer vs RPG vs vehicle "at high speed") and on which supports are weakened — [CBS News Q&A 2009](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/red-faction-guerrilla-q-a/).
- Hulk: Ultimate Destruction (Radical, 2005, "Titanium engine"): objects are weaponised ("wielding a lamppost as a club", cars and tanks thrown, "two halves of a car as steel gloves or a flattened car as a shield"); a "Critical Mass" bar fills "whenever the Hulk takes damage or destroys property" and unlocks the most devastating moves; "smash points" for destroying property unlock abilities — [Wikipedia, The Incredible Hulk: Ultimate Destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Incredible_Hulk:_Ultimate_Destruction). A GameSpot review (seen only as a search snippet) describes Critical Mass attacks with slow-motion and visible shockwaves — [GameSpot review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/the-incredible-hulk-ultimate-destruction-review/1900-6131684/) (not opened).
- Real material data: normal-strength Portland cement concrete: density 2240–2400 kg/m³, compressive strength 20–40 MPa, tensile strength 2–5 MPa, flexural strength 3–5 MPa, modulus of elasticity 14–41 GPa — [Engineering ToolBox, Concrete Properties](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/concrete-properties-d_1223.html).
- Real impact data: seven pilot tests on five single-leaf 1000 × 1000 mm brick walls hit by a 72.925 kg granite boulder released from 0.25, 0.50, 1.00, 2.50 and 3.00 m: "wall breakthrough occurs for translational kinetic energies as low as 500 J"; "The prevalent failure mechanism is local shear damage" — Mavrouli, Fan, Li, Wang, Xu, "Experimental Analysis of Rock Boulder Impacts on Brick Walls to Support Numerical Modelling of Building Damage", Geosciences 14(9):226, 2024 — [Semantic Scholar record with abstract](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/geosciences14090226), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences14090226) (MDPI page returned 403 here).
- Demolition-tool scale (secondary, trade blog): an 11 kg jackhammer rated 27 J per blow "can be used for breaking light (thin) concrete and some brick work"; 6 kg at 14 J; 30 kg at 63 J — [Centenary Hire blog](https://centenaryhire.com.au/demolition-you-just-need-a-bigger-hammer-right/) (seen as a search snippet; not opened).
- Godot mechanics: impulses are "time-independent" one-shot quantities, forces are per-tick — [Godot docs, RigidBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_rigidbody3d.html); Jolt's reported contact impulse is exact only for isolated pairs — [Godot docs, Using Jolt Physics](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html).

### Inferences
- Reproducible threshold design: give each destructible material a Hardness (Blast semantics) and compute damage per contact as impulse/Hardness where impulse ≈ effective mass × closing speed along the normal. Arithmetic from the cited data: an 80 kg body at 10 m/s carries 800 N·s and 4 kJ; a 73 kg boulder at ~3.7 m/s (500 J) breaks a single-leaf brick wall. So a "brick wall" Hardness set so that ~270 N·s (73 kg × 3.7 m/s) is the break point makes an un-powered sprinting player (≈ 80 kg × 6 m/s = 480 N·s) marginal and a superhuman 15 m/s player (1200 N·s) decisive — a tuning knob directly tied to the power curve rather than a scripted flag.
- Repeated hits should accumulate (Blast health, Chaos decay) so a weak player can still chip through with effort; that is what the "health" and "decay" parameters in the shipped systems are for.
- Propagation: use the bond graph so impulse damage spreads with a radius and falloff (Blast Max Damage Radius / Chaos falloff) and gravity/stress reclaims unsupported islands afterwards.

### Gaps
- No primary source for Hulk: Ultimate Destruction's actual damage formula (momentum-scaled or not) was found; Wikipedia describes systems, not physics.
- Chaos's strain units are undocumented, so its numbers cannot be mapped to SI.
- No paper gives fracture energy for a full reinforced-concrete wall under body impact; the brick-wall test is the closest primary experiment found.

## Key question 6: Non-destruction reactivity — cheap wins

### Takeaway
Shipped games surround the few real destruction events with cheap multi-scale feedback — Control's three visual scales, Siege's audio/nav updates, Crysis's per-object vegetation bending, Prototype/Saints Row's landing shockwaves and pedestrian panic — and Godot 4 has built-in nodes for most of it (Decal with a 512-element Forward+ budget, GPU particle collision via SDF/heightfield, Area3D-driven reverb, SoftBody3D cloth, RigidBody3D clutter).

### Cited Findings
- Multi-scale feedback (Control): rigid chunks, mid-scale mesh particles and material decals, and sprite particles form "a rich gradient across all those different scales"; material rules spawn dust for concrete, leaves for grass, water jets for pipes — [Game Developer, Control destruction](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/using-procedural-destruction-to-unleash-chaos-in-i-control-i-).
- Sound and AI reactivity (Siege): destruction updates navlinks, AI visibility and sound propagation; "Destruction changes the acoustic of the environment drastically" — [L'Heureux GDC 2016](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/LHeureux_Julien_Art_Of_Destruction.pdf).
- Foliage bending (Crysis, GPU Gems 3 ch. 16): main bending displaces XY by height with `fBF = vPos.z * fBendScale; fBF = fBF*fBF - fBF; vNewPos.xy += vWind.xy * fBF`, then rescales to keep a "spherical limited movement"; detail bending uses vertex colours (red edge stiffness, green per-leaf phase, blue overall stiffness) and four triangle waves; interaction is done by "summing up the wind forces affecting the instance" so helicopters, explosions and weapon fire bend vegetation at constant per-vertex cost — [NVIDIA GPU Gems 3, Chapter 16](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis). The same per-instance force vector can carry a fast player's wake.
- Landing shockwaves and pedestrian reactions: in Prototype, "Falls from sufficient heights will create shockwaves at the point of impact which will kill most humans nearby and send objects as large as cars flying" — [Prototype Wiki (fan wiki), Alex Mercer](https://prototype.fandom.com/wiki/Alex_Mercer); Saints Row IV powers "greatly increase their jump height and running speed, such that the player can hop over buildings and outrun vehicles", with shockwaves "upon landing jumps" and telekinesis — [Wikipedia, Saints Row IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saints_Row_IV); Hulk: UD Critical Mass shockwaves — see Key question 5.
- Crowd reactions (GTA): the GTA fan wiki (fetch returned HTTP 402; content seen only as a search snippet) describes pedestrians who, when a gun is aimed at them, "may put their hands up in a surrender position, run away or duck, often begging or yelling insults" — [GTA Wiki, Pedestrians](https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Pedestrians) (unverified fan source; no Rockstar primary source found).
- Godot Decals: projected textures for "bullet impacts and explosion scorches", fadeable; cost scales with screen coverage; Forward+ default limit of 512 clustered elements (lights + decals + probes), Mobile "Only 8 decals ... on each individual Mesh resource", Compatibility unsupported (use Sprite3D) — [Godot docs, Using decals](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/3d/using_decals.html).
- Godot GPU particle collision: box/sphere colliders, runtime heightfield ("When Moved" or "Always" update, can follow the camera), baked SDF (resolution 16–256, `Thickness`, Forward+/Mobile only); raise `Fixed FPS` for accuracy at cost — [Godot docs, 3D particle collisions](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/3d/particles/collision.html).
- Godot 3D audio: attenuation models (inverse, inverse-square, logarithmic, disabled), `max_distance` cutoff saves mixing CPU, Doppler tracking on player and listener, `area_mask` so Area3D layers apply reverb and bus effects (e.g. underwater) — [Godot docs, AudioStreamPlayer3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_audiostreamplayer3d.html).
- Cloth: SoftBody3D (Jolt more robust; pin via BoneAttachment3D) — [Godot docs, Using SoftBody](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/soft_body.html); Jolt soft bodies collide only with rigid bodies and are "fairly expensive" — [Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/).
- Physics props/clutter: Jolt's ragdoll benchmark of 3680 bodies and thread scaling suggest thousands of awake bodies are feasible on multi-core PCs — [Jolt Multicore Scaling](https://jrouwe.nl/jolt/JoltPhysicsMulticoreScaling.pdf); Godot's guidance: primitives, few hulls, sleeping — [Godot docs, Troubleshooting physics](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/troubleshooting_physics_issues.html).

### Inferences
- The "pressure wave from speed" the brief asks about can be implemented without new physics: feed the player velocity into the Crysis-style per-instance bending vector for foliage/cloth, apply `apply_impulse` to nearby sleeping props proportional to speed, and spawn dust particles with a heightfield collider. No shipped game source describing an explicit aerodynamic wake was found (see Gaps).
- A sound-propagation approximation à la Siege (occlusion changes when walls break) can be approximated in Godot by toggling Area3D reverb/bus overrides per room and by re-baking NavigationRegion3D after destruction.

### Gaps
- No primary source for camera-shake practice (e.g. the GDC 2016 "Juicing Your Cameras With Math" talk) was reachable; the GDC Vault ID tried was a different talk.
- No primary design documentation of GTA pedestrian AI or of Superhot/Prototype wind/pressure effects was found; only fan wikis.
- Godot has no built-in foliage-bending or footstep-decal system; the above are shader/script patterns, not engine features.
