# Megaton Rainfall (Pentadimensional Games, Alfonso del Cerro, 2017) as the end-state reference: capability set, scale engineering, first-person speed and comfort, Godot 4 + Jolt mapping

Research date: 2026-09-27. Scope per coordinator refocus: what the player can do and how the game handles it at scale; the mission loop, city health bar and casualty scoring are only summarised (the progression note covers them).

Access notes: GameSpot, TechRaptor, TV Tropes, PlayStation Universe, Rock Paper Shotgun (404 on the archived URL), VRFocus (DNS failure), IndieDB and the Fandom wiki HTML all refused WebFetch and curl (HTTP 403 / Cloudflare challenge / 402), including through the r.jina.ai reader. ## Key question 1: The full capability set and its numbers (flight envelope, braking, hover, precision, steering, dash, telekinesis, largest attacks, interaction with buildings and terrain)

### Takeaway
The player is an indestructible first-person flyer whose speed regime spans hover to Mach 8 in about five seconds, then (after story unlocks) superluminal travel; flight is stick-thrust along the look direction with automatic context-dependent speed scaling that del Cerro called the hardest thing to build, and reviewers consistently reported overshoot and a lack of lock-on as the price of that envelope. Attacks scale from building-toppling bolts to a charged beam that (per the fan wiki) can destroy the whole planet.

### Inferences
- The "automatic speed adjustment" is most plausibly altitude- or proximity-driven (max speed rising with distance from the nearest surface), because del Cerro's three regimes are described by context (short distances, city-to-city, out of the stratosphere) and the player workaround "go way up in earth orbit" only makes sense if speed caps rise with altitude or drag falls with it. No developer statement confirms the variable.
- Reviewer reports of "flying ice skates", overshoot and stop/start in mid-air together describe a velocity-damped controller with strong braking when thrust is released (you can stop in mid-air) but with inertia during turns at speed. Whether the body has mass-based inertia or is a kinematic velocity integrator is not stated anywhere.
- Collision with buildings while flying: the marketing "fly through buildings" plus "fly through the ground" powers and the "stuck in terrain" complaint suggest the flyer does not destroy buildings by body contact at ordinary speed (destruction is attributed to shots in every source), while the Dash/drill is the explicit "pass through solids" tool. This is unconfirmed.
- Projectile (not hitscan) attacks with no lock-on are the game's precision pressure at speed; every negative review names this, which is the design lesson rather than a bug.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives the top cruising speed inside the atmosphere in m/s, the acceleration curve, or the braking distance; "Mach 8 in 5 s" (Wikipedia) vs "0 to Mach 10 in 5 s" (IndieDB snippet) is unresolved.
- No source explains what the automatic speed scaling depends on, or how the VR head-aim interface merges with stick control in the shipped build (the Road to VR article says six interfaces exist but does not list them).
- Whether flying into a building at speed damages it, damages nothing, or blocks the player is not documented in any opened source.

## Key question 2: Scale engineering (real-size Earth, seamless street-to-orbit-to-interstellar, precision, LOD and streaming, semi-procedural cities, destruction technology and memory strategy, persistence, platform budgets)

### Takeaway
The game runs on a proprietary engine del Cerro inherited from a previous employer and extended with "planet rendering" for seamless space-to-ground transitions, semi-procedural cities up to 200 square miles, CPU physics plus GPU particles, and a two-mesh ("paper" and "inflated") procedural destruction scheme chosen explicitly because pre-fractured meshes would exhaust PS4 memory at that city scale; nothing in the public record explains the coordinate-precision strategy, atmosphere rendering or destruction persistence, so those are inferred.

### Cited Findings

Engine and planet-scale rendering
- "The engine hasn't been totally built for MR. I inherited it from the company where I worked previously in Spain", and it received "a lot of features during the development of the game" — [Megaton Rainfall Interview: A Real Sized Earth to Save, Alessio Palumbo, Wccftech, 10 Mar 2016](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-interview-real-sized-earth-save/).
- The engine includes "planet rendering, which allows seamless transitions from outer space to ground level", letting the player "fly around a real size Earth"; it renders "cities as big as the real Los Angeles (more than 200 square miles)" with proprietary destruction using "CPU-based physics simulation, GPU-accelerated particles" — [Wccftech interview, 2016](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-interview-real-sized-earth-save/).
- Wikipedia summarises the same as a "Universe-scale rendering engine" and "Earth represented at full scale" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaton_Rainfall) (wiki-only paraphrase).
- Cities are "semi-procedurally generated"; del Cerro: "You won't find your home or lots of recognizable places" — [Road to VR, 2017](https://roadtovr.com/battle-aliens-map-big-earth-first-person-superhero-game-megaton-rainfall-coming-psvr/). The Steam and PlayStation store text also says "semi-procedurally generated cities" — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/430210/Megaton_Rainfall/).
- The 2015 announcement says the Earth features "procedurally-generated cities and terrain" — [PlayStation.Blog, 2015](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2015/10/28/first-person-superhero-sim-megaton-rainfall-coming-to-ps4-and-ps-vr/).
- The 2015 demo generated a random region of Murcia when visited, and atmospheric entry was smooth — [Real o Virtual, 2015](https://www.realovirtual.com/articulos/27/megaton-rainfall-madrid-games-week) (Spanish, paraphrased).
- Solo scope was possible because "everything is procedural. The amount of work is not proportional to the size of the cities" — [AP2HYC interview, 2015](https://ap2hyc.com/2015/04/interview-alfonso-del-cerro-lead-developer-of-megaton-rainfall/).
- Install footprint: 1536 MB storage on PC, minimum GPU GTX 750 2 GB / Radeon HD 7770 2 GB, 4 GB RAM, DirectX 11 — [Steam system requirements](https://store.steampowered.com/app/430210/Megaton_Rainfall/).
- No dynamic day/night cycle; the player crosses from the day side to the night side by flying, and lighting adjusts accordingly — [Megaton Rainfall Dev Would Love To Release On Xbox One, Alessio Palumbo, Wccftech, 29 Dec 2015](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-dev-would-love-to-release-on-xbox-one/).
- Reviewer on transitions: "there's a bit of pop-in and judder as the title transitions between the various planes of the universe" — [Push Square, 2017](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps4/megaton_rainfall); "The ease of navigating this enormous universe, especially with no loading times, is pretty impressive" and space-to-surface transitions are "such a simple, organic feeling process" — [Review: Megaton Rainfall (PSVR), Virtuanaut, A Most Agreeable Pastime, 28 Oct 2017](https://amostagreeablepastime.com/2017/10/28/review-megaton-rainfall-ps4/); "pop-in for numerous things, including buildings, cars and clouds" on PC — [Worthplaying, 2018](https://worthplaying.com/article/2018/2/22/reviews/107352-pc-review-megaton-rainfall/).
- Universe content per the fan wiki (wiki-only): black holes "pull in the light from nearby stars", nebulae, ringed planets, storm eyes on gas giants, oceanic planets you can swim in, asteroid belts; the solar system's eight planets are visitable — [SuperLuminal Flight](https://megaton-rainfall.fandom.com/wiki/SuperLuminal_Flight), [Universe](https://megaton-rainfall.fandom.com/wiki/Universe) and [Places](https://megaton-rainfall.fandom.com/wiki/Places), Fandom.

Level of detail, streaming and the GPU budget
- The city is "a massive amount of relatively simple objects" and is the GPU's main load; platforms are scaled by "setting the density of city stuff and the render distance of those details"; the game is "super-GPU bound", already uses extensive instancing and shader texture arrays to minimise draw calls, leaving the render thread "several milliseconds idle every frame", so DX12/Vulkan would not help — [Megaton Rainfall Targeting 1080p@60fps at Medium Settings on PS4, Alessio Palumbo, Wccftech, 9 Mar 2016](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-targeting-1080p60fps-medium-settings-ps4-release-q3q4/).
- PSVR is the platform where CPU limitations are most pronounced — same source.

Destruction technology and memory strategy (developer's own posts)
- Two-mesh scheme: "we discard the inflated version, we remove the triangles of the 'paper' mesh near the explosion (updating the paper version), and we regenerate the inflated version based on the paper version". The "paper" version has zero-thickness walls and is never shown; the "inflated" version has thick walls and is what the player sees — [How procedurally destructable cities work in PS VR superhero sim Megaton Rainfall, Alfonso del Cerro Aguilar, PlayStation.Blog (EU), 17 Oct 2017](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2017/10/17/how-procedurally-destructable-cities-work-in-ps-vr-superhero-sim-megaton-rainfall-out-today/).
- Motivation: the approach had to handle "massive explosions of massive skyscrapers in massive cities 200 squared miles in size" at 60 fps in stereo on PS4, and pre-fractured meshes would have exhausted memory — same source.
- Explosion continuity trick: "at the time of the explosion, the engine takes a snapshot of the house just as it's seen from the camera. Yes, a snapshot. A bitmap." The bitmap is applied to GPU billboard particles so "the first frame of the explosion seems exactly the same. Continuity achieved!"; the particles are "GPU-accelerated 2D billboards. The PS4 can handle bazillions easily"; "no other game handles the explosions this way as far as I know" — [The Tech That Powers Megaton Rainfall, Alfonso del Cerro Aguilar, PlayStation.Blog (US), 17 Oct 2017](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/10/17/the-tech-that-powers-megaton-rainfall-launching-today-on-ps-vr/).
- The destruction experiments began in "Holiday 2012" as a personal experiment, and del Cerro spent months on the "rendering techniques necessary to display the procedural destruction of buildings" — [PS Blog EU, 2017](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2017/10/17/how-procedurally-destructable-cities-work-in-ps-vr-superhero-sim-megaton-rainfall-out-today/); [AP2HYC, 2015](https://ap2hyc.com/2015/04/interview-alfonso-del-cerro-lead-developer-of-megaton-rainfall/).
- Physics is CPU-side, particles GPU-side — [Wccftech interview, 2016](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-interview-real-sized-earth-save/).

Platform budgets and visual cost
- PS4 target "1080p 60fps at medium settings"; del Cerro's high-end PC ran "4K 60 fps with high settings" — [Wccftech, 9 Mar 2016](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-targeting-1080p60fps-medium-settings-ps4-release-q3q4/).
- PS4 Pro: 4K (3840x2160) at 60 fps in all play modes — [PlayStation.Blog, 9 Aug 2017](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/08/09/first-person-superhero-sim-megaton-rainfall-flies-to-ps4-ps-vr-september-26/).
- PSVR: 60 fps rendered, reprojected to 120 — [Wccftech interview, 2016](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-interview-real-sized-earth-save/).
- Switch (review-only): "I have never seen a game run worse on the Switch than this", "some of the worst pop-in and graphical hiccups", muddy visuals making enemies indistinguishable from buildings, "second-long freezes" several times per seven-minute mission — [GamingTrend, 2018](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/the-quest-for-score-megaton-rainfall-review/). Nintendo Life scored it 6/10 without quantifying resolution — [Nintendo Life, 2018](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/megaton_rainfall). No developer statement on Switch resolution or frame rate was found.
- PC VR (Rift/Vive) arrived as a free update on 9 Aug 2018 with "gamepad or new gesture controls" — [Steam Community announcements list](https://steamcommunity.com/app/430210/allnews/).
- Godot-relevant benchmark for what "acceptable" precision means at these distances is in Key question 4.

### Inferences
- Coordinate precision: no developer statement on floating origin or double precision was found in any opened source or search. Given a 1:1 Earth (radius ~6,371 km) and superluminal travel, the engine must use either double-precision world positions, an origin-relative (camera-relative) coordinate frame with periodic rebasing, or nested reference frames; all three are standard for this class (see the Star Citizen, Elite and KSP citations in Key question 4). Which one Megaton Rainfall uses is unknown.
- Streaming: "semi-procedural" plus a 1.5 GB install and no loading screens implies cities are generated on demand from seeds and cached, with density and draw distance as the platform knobs (del Cerro names those two knobs). Whether generation is deterministic per city so that returning players see the same city is unconfirmed; the 2015 Murcia demo was described as "randomly generated".
- Destruction persistence: the paper/inflated scheme edits the building's own mesh, so destruction can persist as long as the building stays resident; whether damage survives leaving and returning to a city (unloading) is undocumented. Free-roam mods that let players "destroy everything" without a mission reset exist, which implies the mission system, not the world, normally resets damage.
- The "paper mesh" reads like a boolean-subtraction (CSG-style) footprint of the building from which walls are re-extruded; that is my reading, not the developer's words.
- Atmosphere/horizon: reviewers describe a smooth atmospheric entry and god rays, and the fan wiki describes atmospheric scattering-like visuals on exoplanets, but no developer statement on the atmosphere model exists.

### Gaps
- Floating origin / precision method: not documented anywhere I could reach (searched "Megaton Rainfall" with "floating point", "double precision", "floating origin"; no hits).
- No numbers for LOD distances, city cell size, generation time per city, tri counts, or memory budget per city.
- No developer statement on how terrain (as opposed to cities) is generated, or on atmosphere rendering.
- IndieDB dev-news posts (which may contain devlog detail) could not be opened (HTTP 403).
- No GDC/Gamelab talk or postmortem by del Cerro was found (searched English and Spanish "charla/ponencia").

## Key question 3: First-person speed and comfort (FOV, vignette, speed cues, smoothing, motion blur, PSVR comfort choices, reviewer reports on disorientation and sickness)

### Takeaway
Comfort in Megaton Rainfall is handled by control-scheme choice rather than by slowing the game: six VR control interfaces, a default snap-turn scheme for the most sensitive players, peripheral vignetting ("huge wedges of blackness"), 45-degree turning, and quarter-turn or teleport alternatives; reviewers who switched these off reported nausea, and one reviewer explicitly warned strong sim-sickness sufferers away. No source documents FOV values, motion blur or camera smoothing settings.

### Cited Findings
- Six control interfaces, default right-stick quick turns for the players "most prone to VR sickness", because the game is "flying around at supersonic speeds in first person without a cockpit" (no reference frame) — [Road to VR, 26 Sep 2017](https://roadtovr.com/battle-aliens-map-big-earth-first-person-superhero-game-megaton-rainfall-coming-psvr/).
- "Huge wedges of blackness would cover up my peripheral vision" and "45 degree turning felt unnatural"; both can be disabled for better visuals, but doing so "may cause you problems" — [PlayStation Country, 18 Oct 2017](https://www.playstationcountry.com/megaton-rainfall-psvrps4-review/).
- "More anti-motion-sickness options than I've seen in any other VR game, actually", including quarter-turning and teleporting alternatives; the reviewer felt minor nausea after disabling them — [A Most Agreeable Pastime, 28 Oct 2017](https://amostagreeablepastime.com/2017/10/28/review-megaton-rainfall-ps4/).

## Key question 4: Mapping to Godot 4 (4.8-dev fork) with Jolt: what is built in, what needs custom engine work, and comparison titles with a similar capability ceiling

### Takeaway
Godot supplies the precision foundation (a `precision=double` engine build with emulated double precision in rendering) and Jolt as the default 3D physics since 4.4, but everything that makes Megaton Rainfall's ceiling possible above that (planet renderer, origin rebasing or camera-relative frames, procedural city generation and streaming, the paper/inflated destruction scheme, snapshot-billboard explosions) is project-level custom work; the comparison titles show the two standard answers to scale (64-bit world positions vs. floating origin) and one deliberate alternative (Outer Wilds' miniature system).

### Cited Findings

Godot 4 facts
- Godot's "large world coordinates" option raises Vector types from 32-bit to 64-bit to prevent objects snapping to representable values far from the origin; it requires recompiling with the `precision=double` SCons option, produces binaries with a `.double` suffix, and needs custom export templates — [Large world coordinates, Godot Engine documentation (stable)](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/large_world_coordinates.html).
- Practical single-precision limits given in that page: first-person 3D about 2,048–4,096 m from the origin before artifacts, third-person 4,096–8,192 m, top-down 16,384–32,768 m, "any 3D" up to 32,768–65,536 m; double precision remains acceptable beyond 262,144 units — same source.
- Rendering does not use true double floats in shaders for performance reasons; the engine uses an emulated approach; known limitations include triplanar-mapping jitter far from the origin, GPU particle snapping when Local Coords is off, some render modes bypassing the benefit, and no benefit to 2D — same source.
- Jolt is built into Godot since 4.4 and is the default physics engine for new projects; listed differences from GodotPhysics include unsupported soft-limit joint properties (warning emitted), different single-body joint semantics (compatibility project setting exists), convex-radius collision margins that shrink small shapes, ray-cast `face_index` returning -1 unless a project setting is enabled (about 25% more ConcavePolygonShape3D memory), kinematic RigidBody3D not reporting static/kinematic contacts by default, and two ghost-collision mitigations (active edge detection, enhanced internal edge removal) that do not apply between separate bodies — [Using Jolt Physics, Godot Engine documentation (stable)](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html).

Megaton Rainfall technique to Godot mapping (inference unless cited)
- Real-size Earth and interstellar travel: Godot's `precision=double` build covers the world-position precision problem at planetary scale (radius 6.4e6 m is well beyond the single-precision 4 km first-person limit above) but the documentation frames it as "acceptable" beyond 262,144 units rather than at light-year distances, so interstellar scale still needs an origin-rebasing or nested-frame scheme on top (custom). The docs describe no automatic floating-origin feature (inference from the page's content).
- Seamless street-to-orbit rendering (planet LOD, horizon, atmosphere, clouds): no Godot planet renderer exists in the docs read; this is custom shader and mesh work, and the docs' note that rendering precision is emulated means a camera-relative rendering path must be validated at orbital distances (custom).
- Semi-procedural cities and streaming: custom generation; Godot's instancing (MultiMesh) and texture arrays correspond to the instancing and "shader texture arrays" del Cerro used to keep draw calls low — [Wccftech, 9 Mar 2016](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-targeting-1080p60fps-medium-settings-ps4-release-q3q4/) — but the density/draw-distance knobs and cell streaming are project code.
- Destruction: the paper/inflated re-extrusion and the snapshot-to-billboard explosion ([PS Blog US](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/10/17/the-tech-that-powers-megaton-rainfall-launching-today-on-ps-vr/), [PS Blog EU](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2017/10/17/how-procedurally-destructable-cities-work-in-ps-vr-superhero-sim-megaton-rainfall-out-today/)) are engine-agnostic ideas: runtime mesh editing (ArrayMesh/SurfaceTool), a viewport capture at the explosion frame, and GPU particles with that texture. Jolt's documented feature list (above) mentions no fracture system, so debris bodies and collision-shape rebuilds after damage are custom. Given the user's Euphoria-like active ragdoll goal, note del Cerro kept physics on the CPU and pushed only visual debris to the GPU as billboards ([Wccftech interview](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-interview-real-sized-earth-save/)).
- Flight controller: look-direction thrust with context-scaled max speed and mid-air stop ([Gamecritics](https://gamecritics.com/daniel-weissenberger/megaton-rainfall/), [Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2018/2/22/reviews/107352-pc-review-megaton-rainfall/), [AP2HYC](https://ap2hyc.com/2015/04/interview-alfonso-del-cerro-lead-developer-of-megaton-rainfall/)) maps to a CharacterBody3D or a force-driven RigidBody3D in Jolt; the "vsync-coupled speed" bug in the original ([Steam announcement, Nov 2017](https://steamcommunity.com/app/430210/allnews/)) is a reminder to integrate in `_physics_process` with fixed delta.
- VR comfort: vignette, snap/quarter turns, teleport fallback and head-aligned thrust ([Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/battle-aliens-map-big-earth-first-person-superhero-game-megaton-rainfall-coming-psvr/), [PlayStation Country](https://www.playstationcountry.com/megaton-rainfall-psvrps4-review/), [A Most Agreeable Pastime](https://amostagreeablepastime.com/2017/10/28/review-megaton-rainfall-ps4/)) are all post-process or input-layer features implementable in Godot without engine changes (inference).

Comparison titles with a similar ceiling (brief, one or two facts each)
- Star Citizen: CIG refactored CryEngine so physics and positioning use 64-bit world space; Technical Director Sean Tracy stressed that not every module (e.g., AI) needed rewriting to run in 64-bit world space — [Star Citizen's Sean Tracy on 64-bit Engine Tech & Procedural Edge Blending, GamersNexus](https://gamersnexus.net/gg/2622-star-citizen-sean-tracy-64bit-engine-tech-edge-blending) (search-snippet-only; page not opened).
- Kerbal Space Program: the "Deep Space Kraken" bug (craft parts misaligning far from the origin from float rounding) was fixed by making the active craft the coordinate origin so "the entire universe moves around it"; the 1.2 changelog notes work on "Krakensbane and FloatingOrigin to improve precision and performance" and that particle systems can register with FloatingOrigin — [KSP 1.2 changelog, Kerbal Space Program Wiki](https://wiki.kerbalspaceprogram.com/wiki/1.2) (wiki changelog) and a secondary summary at [prokopetz.tumblr.com](https://prokopetz.tumblr.com/post/723012983570268160/kerbal-space-program-used-to-have-a-bug-the-fans) (search-snippet-only).
- Floating origin definition and the precision math: accuracy "halves for every power of two units away from the origin" and single precision cannot represent fractions beyond 8,388,608 units; alternatives are double precision, half precision, or origin shifting where "the world and camera snap to origin after reaching a specific distance threshold" — [Floating origin, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_origin) (wiki-only).
- Elite Dangerous (Frontier, 2014, Cobra engine): 1:1-scale Milky Way with ~400 billion procedurally generated systems ("Stellar Forge"); two speed regimes, supercruise "up to 2000+ times the speed of light" in-system and hyperspace jumps between systems; Horizons (15 Dec 2015) added seamless landings on airless planets — [Elite Dangerous, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Dangerous) (wiki-only). Relevant because it splits "extreme speed" into discrete regimes exactly as Megaton Rainfall's supersonic/superluminal/dash tiers do.
- Outer Wilds (Mobius Digital, 2019): a small solar system whose bodies "swiftly orbit the sun" under exaggerated Newtonian physics; players must "counteract their own momentum to slow down while flying" — [Outer Wilds, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Wilds) (wiki-only). Relevant as the deliberate alternative: shrink the world so single precision and full physics simulation suffice.
- Superflight (GrizzlyGames, 8 Nov 2017): wingsuit flight through procedurally generated terrain scored by proximity; 96% positive from 7,932 Steam reviews — [Superflight on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/732430/Superflight/). Relevant as the minimal "speed feel from near-miss proximity" reference.
- Superman Returns (EA Tiburon, 2006): "Metropolis has a damage meter" and the game ends when it is depleted while Superman is invincible; flight "up to 800 mph" with motion blur intensifying at speed; Metacritic 51 (Xbox 360), 46 (PS2) — [Superman Returns (video game), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman_Returns_(video_game)) (wiki-only). The direct ancestor of Megaton Rainfall's body-to-world consequence transfer.
- Superman (Titus, 1999, N64): "the only way to stop flying is to crash into a solid object" (N64 Magazine, quoted); distance fog dressed as "Kryptonite fog" to hide draw distance; the licensor demanded removal of destructible architecture; Guinness record for lowest-rated superhero game — [Superman (1999 video game), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman_(1999_video_game)) (wiki-only). A catalogue of what not to do: no braking, hidden horizon, no destruction.
- Saints Row IV (Volition, 2013): super sprint, super jump, telekinesis, elemental blasts, unlocked via collected "data clusters"; destruction is framed as happening inside a simulation; Metacritic 86 (PC) — [Saints Row IV, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saints_Row_IV) (wiki-only).
- Crackdown 3 (Sumo Digital, 2019): orbs raise agility (double jump) and strength (ground pound); Azure cloud-computed destruction was limited to the multiplayer "Wrecking Zone" while the campaign got "limited" destruction; the mode was later described as "dead"; Metacritic 60 (Xbox One) — [Crackdown 3, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crackdown_3) (wiki-only). A warning about destruction budgets that do not fit the main mode.
- Prototype (Radical, 2009): scale any wall, jump great heights, sprint "at extreme speed indefinitely" in an open Manhattan; Metacritic 79 — [Prototype (video game), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prototype_(video_game)) (wiki-only).
- Gravity Rush (Japan Studio, 2012): gravity-shift "falling" as flight, tied to an energy gauge; orientation is kept readable through architecture with clear tops and bottoms and the character's clothing behaviour; Metacritic 83 (Vita) — [Gravity Rush, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_Rush) (wiki-only). Relevant for orientation cues when "down" changes.

### Inferences
- For the user's ladder, Megaton Rainfall's top tier maps as: (a) sustained flight with context-scaled max speed and hard stop, (b) a Dash tier that both multiplies acceleration and permits passing through solids, (c) mass-manipulation (telekinesis of vehicles and bombs, levitation of whole buildings), (d) area attacks whose blast radius exceeds a city. Each is gated in the original by a new enemy that requires it (levitation vs. building-droppers, telekinesis vs. nukes, dash vs. yellow-weak-point enemies), which is the same "power arrives with the thing that needs it" gating the user is already planning, but the original delivers it as scripted unlocks rather than emergent physics.
- The engineering order of operations implied by del Cerro's history (destruction rendering first, 2012; flight and planet transitions next; comfort last, 2015–2017) is a reasonable template: the expensive systems (destruction, scale) were prototyped before the game existed.
- Reproducible in Godot with no engine changes: comfort layer, flight controller, GPU billboard debris, runtime mesh editing. Needs a custom engine build: double-precision (`precision=double`). Needs custom engine-level or deep project work: origin rebasing beyond ~262k units, planet-scale terrain/atmosphere LOD, city streaming, damage-aware collision rebuilds in Jolt.
