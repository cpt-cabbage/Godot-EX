## Key question 1: What do the four user-supplied sources contain, and what is reusable?

### Takeaway
Source 1 is Reil & Massey (2001), a CPG + genetic-algorithm controller for a 3D rigid-body biped written by the founders of NaturalMotion (the Euphoria company); source 2 is Ronald Van Ham's VUB PhD on the MACCEPA compliant actuator (thesis PDF unreachable; the canonical journal version is Van Ham et al. 2007); source 3 is Saputra et al. (2016, IEEE TSMC: Systems), a neural-oscillator/muscle-synergy controller for a 12-DoF-leg humanoid robot tuned by multi-objective evolution; source 4 is Yilei Gao's 2024 Manchester PhD, which builds reflex torque and reflex muscle controllers for a Simscape biped from human gait data. Reusable content: Reil's CPG-plus-reflex architecture and its Euphoria lineage; MACCEPA's idea of a joint with independently settable equilibrium angle and stiffness (a direct model for "PD target + PD gain as two separately animated channels"); Saputra's "gain neurons" that scale joint energy and "command neurons" that set step length/direction; Gao's progression torque -> reflex torque -> muscle -> reflex muscle with perturbation tests.

### Inferences
- The user's four sources are all "biologically inspired" in the robotics/biomechanics sense (CPGs, reflexes, muscles, compliant actuators); none of them is a game-oriented controller, and none provides published PD gains or tick rates that a Godot programmer can copy. Their value for the game is conceptual: (a) Reil's CPG+reflex+GA pipeline is the documented ancestor of Euphoria's "motor nervous system" simulation; (b) MACCEPA suggests treating each ragdoll joint as an actuator with two independently animated channels, equilibrium angle and stiffness, which maps directly onto Jolt/Godot joint motors (target angle + spring stiffness/damping); (c) Saputra's gain neurons and command neurons are a clean abstraction for "power level" and "intended step" that the game's progression system could scale as powers unlock; (d) Gao's staged torque -> reflex -> muscle progression is a sensible development order for the project.
- For engineering reproduction, the SIMBICON/Coros family (Key question 2) is a far better starting point than any of the four supplied sources; the supplied sources should be cited for the biological framing and for the CPG/reflex concept.

## Key question 2: Control laws, state machines, gains and disturbance handling per controller family

### Takeaway
Every real-time classical biped controller in graphics decomposes into (1) a small finite state machine driven by time and foot contact, (2) joint PD servos toward per-state target poses with a few joints (torso, swing hip) servoed in the world frame, and (3) a balance feedback that moves the swing-foot target as a linear function of COM offset and COM velocity (SIMBICON) or by an inverted-pendulum/capture-point prediction (Coros 2010, Pratt 2006). Gravity compensation and virtual forces via Jacobian-transpose (Pratt's virtual model control) let the PD gains be low and the motion compliant. Falls and impacts are handled by separate controllers with explicit pre-conditions (Faloutsos 2001), by gain-scheduling around impacts (Zordan & Hodgins 2002; Zordan 2005), and by momentum-shaping landing/rolling controllers (Ha, Ye, Liu 2012); no classical paper in this set covers ledge catching or running into walls.

### Cited Findings

#### SIMBICON (Yin, Loken, van de Panne, ACM TOG 26(3), SIGGRAPH 2007)
- Three elements: "a finite state machine, torso and swing-hip control, and balance feedback". States each have a target pose; "Transitions between states happen after an elapsed time, e.g., state transition 0 -> 1, or after foot contact, e.g., 1 -> 2"; joints apply PD torques tau = kp(theta_d - theta) - kd*theta_dot. — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [DOI 10.1145/1276377.1276509](https://doi.org/10.1145/1276377.1276509)
- Torso and swing hip are servoed in the world frame with "virtual PD controllers"; the stance hip torque is made physically realizable as tau_A = -tau_torso - tau_B (tau_B = swing hip torque), so the torso sees the net torque -tau_A - tau_B it was asked for. — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Balance feedback law on the swing hip target: theta_d = theta_d0 + c_d*d + c_v*v, where d is the horizontal distance from the stance ankle to the COM and v the COM velocity; "The midpoint of the hips can be used as a simple and effective proxy" for the COM; the same law is applied in sagittal and coronal planes for 3D; general form theta_d = theta_d0 + F*[d v]^T with F an n x 2 matrix (used to add stance-ankle feedback for quiescent stance). — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Walking uses 4 states (two symmetric steps; state 1 of each step lifts the swing foot for a fixed duration, state 2 drives it down until contact); running uses 2 states padded with two zero-duration dummy states so walk<->run transitions can jump "from state n of controller A to state n+1 of controller B"; skipping uses 8 states; controllers are started "from a double stance state with a moderate forward velocity (1 m/s)". — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- 2D model and gains: 7-link planar biped, 70 kg trunk, 5 kg upper legs, 4 kg lower legs, 1 kg feet; "PD gain values of kp = 300 Nm/rad, kd = 30 Nms/rad are used for all joints"; penalty ground contact kp = 100000 N/m, kd = 6000 Ns/m, Coulomb friction 0.65; "A time step of 0.0001 s is used"; torque limits 1000 Nm (370 Nm suffices for all but the fast run; the basic walk survives down to 90 Nm); 5x faster than real time on a 1.8 GHz Core Duo. — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- 3D model and gains: simulated in Open Dynamics Engine 0.6 with "simulation time step is 0.005 s", LCP contacts with friction 0.8 and zero restitution; "The largest kp value is 1000, used for the waist joints. All other joints use kp = 300 or less. We use kd = 0.1 kp"; torque magnitudes limited to kp; 1.2x real time on a 3.2 GHz Pentium 4. — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Table 1 (columns: state, dwell time [s], c_d, c_v, then target angles in radians for torso, swing hip, swing knee, swing ankle, stance knee, stance ankle). 3D walk: states 0,2: dt 0.3, c_d 0.5, c_v 0.2, torso 0, swing hip 0.5, swing knee -1.1, swing ankle 0.6, stance knee -0.05, stance ankle 0; lateral c_d 0.5, c_v 0.2 with zero lateral targets; states 1,3: transition on foot contact, c_d 0.5, c_v 0.2, torso 0, swing hip -0.1, swing knee -0.05, swing ankle 0.15, stance knee -0.1, stance ankle 0. 3D run: states 0,1: dt 0.3, c_d 0.5, c_v 0.2, targets 0 / 0.5 / -1.1 / 0.6 / -0.05 / 0. 2D "fast run": one state, dt 0.15, c_d 0, c_v 0.2, torso -0.2, swing hip 1.08, swing knee -2.18, swing ankle 0.2, stance knee -0.05, stance ankle 0.27. 2D "crouch walk": states 0,2: dt 0.30, c_d 0, c_v 0.20, torso -0.2, hip 1.10, knee -2.17, ankle 0.62, stance knee -0.97, stance ankle 0.44; states 1,3: fc, c_d 2.20, c_v 0.00, torso -0.3, hip -0.70, knee -0.05, ankle 0.20, stance knee -0.92, stance ankle 0.44. — [SIMBICON PDF, Table 1](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Robustness numbers: terrain with "unanticipated downward steps of 20cm and slopes of +-6 degrees"; pushes tested as 10 pushes at 5 s intervals; "The walking controller can withstand 0.1s duration pushes of up to 600N forwards and 500N backwards at all 10 sampled points"; the skipping gait only 40 N forwards / 50 N backwards; larger impulses F*dt survivable "by increasing dt and decreasing F". — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Feedback-error learning (anticipatory torques): feed-forward torque learned as a function of phase tau = f(phi), phi = t/T_hat, phase split into N = 20-1000 bins, per-bin low-pass update v_ff' = (1-alpha) v_ff + alpha (v_ff + v_fb) with alpha = 0.1; the applied torque is v_ff + v_fb; FEL reduced 2D torso oscillation from 5 deg to 0.5 deg. Motivation: mocap tracking "requires high-gains in order to track well. At the instant of an unexpected impact, the gain is decreased ... and then increased again to resume tracking"; with feed-forward torques the controller can run low gains and "optionally increase the gains for some time after impact after a reaction-time delay (150ms)". — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Motion-capture-driven variant: Fourier-filter 3-7 gait cycles into a periodic trajectory Theta(phi), keep two states (left/right stance) switching on foot contact at phi ~ 0.5 and 1, track theta_d0 = Theta(phi) with tau = kp(theta_d - theta) - kd(theta_dot - theta_dot_d), keep world-frame torso/swing-hip servos and balance feedback on swing hip (and stance ankle for slow walks). — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Released controller files (SIMBICON 1.5 "Controller Editor Framework", GPL v3, used for Coros et al. 2009 "Robust Task-based Control Policies for Physics-based Characters"): for the humanoid character bipV2, PDParams are root kp 3000 / kd 300; pelvis_torso 200/30; hips 300/30; knees 300/30; torso_head 200/20; shoulders 20/5; elbows 5/1; ankles 75/10; toe joints 10/0.5; MaxTorque 10000 on all; extra "stanceHipDamping 25" and "stanceHipMaxVelocity 4"; swing-hip balance feedback c_d 0.55, c_v 0.30 (sagittal) and c_d -0.30, c_v -0.30 (coronal projection); a state lasts 0.7 s and transitions on "footDown". Character link masses in bipV2.rbs include 12.9, 22.5, 5.2, 2.2, 1.7, 6.6, 3.2, 1.0, 0.2 kg links. — [SIMBICON-CEF page and source zip](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/); [README.txt](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/README.txt)

#### Generalized Biped Walking Control (Coros, Beaudoin, van de Panne, ACM TOG 29(4), SIGGRAPH 2010) and Cartwheel-3D
- Architecture: motion generator (spline joint trajectories over step phase) tracked by low-gain PD; "a gravity compensation model adds computed torques"; foot placement from "a predictive inverted pendulum model"; "continuous balance adjustments ... using Jacobian transpose control" (virtual force on the COM). — [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [DOI 10.1145/1778765.1781156](https://doi.org/10.1145/1778765.1781156)
- Inverted-pendulum foot placement: constant-leg-length IPM (after Pratt & Tedrake 2006); equate energies 1/2 m v^2 + m g h = m g L with L = sqrt(h^2 + d^2) and solve: d = v * sqrt(h/g + v^2/(4 g^2)) is the step that brings the COM to rest; for a desired speed V_d use d' = d - alpha*V_d with alpha = 0.05; applied independently in sagittal (x_d) and coronal (z_d) planes using the true COM; recomputed every time step; step length capped at d = 0.6 L, "leading to the character possibly having to take multiple steps to recover". — [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Swing foot trajectory: height y(phi) from a three-segment spline; x(phi) = (1-phi) x0 + phi x_d, z(phi) = (1-phi) z0 + phi z_d; IK for swing hip/knee, tracked by PD plus gravity compensation; a "twist angle" parameter sets knock-kneed/bow-legged style. — [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Velocity tuning by virtual force: F_V = k_V (V_d - V) in the sagittal plane; coronal plane uses a PD on a desired lateral COM position moving linearly to step width W by next footstrike (W = 0 gives a catwalk); tau_V = J_V^T F_V with J_V the COM Jacobian of the chain rooted at the stance ankle (joints stance foot -> head); "we zero the ankle component of the torque if we detect that the stance foot is not well planted" (toe off ground or foot rotational velocity above a threshold); in double stance each leg is treated independently and results summed. — [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Gravity compensation: virtual force F_i = -m_i g at each link COM, tau_i = J_i^T F_i summed along the chain to the pelvis root, applied to all links except the stance leg; "allows simple local control methods, such as low-gain PD-control, to succeed". — [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Implementation numbers: ODE, "simulation time step of 0.0005 s"; "All balance control parameters are kept fixed across all our simulations"; kp scaled by character mass ratio M'/M and "kd = 2*sqrt(kp) for all joints"; ground friction 0.8; no torque limits in results, but limits of |tau| <= 200 Nm (hips, knees) and 100 Nm (others) "did not have any visible impact"; 37 DOF humanoid (incl. 6 root); real time single-threaded on a 2.4 GHz 2-core machine; no self-collision. — [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Robustness: "disturbance forces to the torso for a duration of 0.1 s. During a forward walk of 0.6 m/s, the character is robust to omni directional pushes of 600 N ... The mass of the character is 70.4kg"; removing IPM "results in an inability to recover from any significant perturbations"; skills include stepping over/ducking under obstacles, climbing steps, lifting and pushing crates. — [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Code: released as Google Code project cartwheel-3d, C++ on ODE 0.9 with Python bindings, Apache-2.0, mirrored on GitHub (Banbury/cartwheel-3d, RomkoSI/cartwheel-3d); contains Core, Physics, MathLib, Python, GLUtils, data directories and a Visual Studio solution. — [GitHub mirror Banbury/cartwheel-3d](https://github.com/Banbury/cartwheel-3d); [LocoTest PDF, Section 3](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~kkyin/papers/locotest.pdf)

#### Feature-based locomotion controllers (de Lasa, Mordatch, Hertzmann, ACM TOG 29(4), 2010)
- Abstract: control "based on high-level features of movement, such as center-of-mass, angular momentum, and end-effectors. Objective terms are used to control each feature, and are combined by a prioritization algorithm"; controllers for "human balancing, standing jump, and walking"; "arm-swing, heel-off, and hip-shoulder counter-rotation emerge automatically"; "controllers are robust to changes in body parameters"; "No motion capture or off-line optimization process is used". — [DOI 10.1145/1778765.1781157](https://doi.org/10.1145/1778765.1781157)
- Survey characterization: they "construct robust balancing, walking and jumping controllers by combining several coordinated and state-driven low-level objectives, using prioritized optimization"; the survey lists this family among methods whose "real-time performance ... is still limited to around a single character at a time on modern hardware". — [Geijtenbeek & Pronost 2012 survey, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03189.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03189.x); [open copy](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/handle/1874/257862)
- Follow-up with online planning: Mordatch, de Lasa, Hertzmann 2010 model stance with a SLIP model and flight with projectile equations; "Different types of gaits, including walking, running, and jumping, emerge automatically ... The controllers can traverse challenging terrain and withstand large external disturbances, while following high-level user commands at interactive rates." — [DOI 10.1145/1778765.1778808](https://doi.org/10.1145/1778765.1778808)

#### Flexible muscle-based locomotion (Geijtenbeek, van de Panne, van der Stappen, ACM TOG 32(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2013) and SCONE
- Per-leg FSM with four states "stance, lift-off, swing, and stance preparation"; contact from GRF threshold F_contact; lift-off when the signed foot-to-COM distance d_S < d_lift; stance preparation when d_S > d_prep; thresholds optimized. Target features: world-aligned trunk orientation (sagittal lean from v_COM minus desired speed), head position/orientation, swing-leg segment orientations. Upper-leg target uses a SIMBICON-like law with gains k^theta_p, k^theta_d, k^phi_p, k^phi_d on foot-to-COM distances d_S, d_C and velocities v_S, v_C (Eq. 13); knee target is a fixed sagittal angle relative to the upper leg. — [Geijtenbeek 2013 PDF](https://www.goatstream.com/research/papers/SA2013/SA2013.pdf); [DOI 10.1145/2508363.2508399](https://doi.org/10.1145/2508363.2508399)
- Actuation: Hill-type muscles (Geyer & Herr 2010 model) with activation dynamics rate c_a = 100 s^-1 (after Wang et al. 2012); muscle-based Jacobian-transpose feature control with PD feedback force/torque on target features; neural delays "dt = 20ms for muscles attached to the foot, dt = 10ms for ... lower leg, and dt = 5ms for ... upper leg", 5 ms otherwise. — [Geijtenbeek 2013 PDF](https://www.goatstream.com/research/papers/SA2013/SA2013.pdf)
- Optimization: all parameters K optimized with CMA-ES; objective E = E_speed + E_headori + E_headvel + E_slide + E_effort with thresholds; 500-3000 generations; "optimization time takes between 2 and 12 hours" on a standard PC. — [Geijtenbeek 2013 PDF](https://www.goatstream.com/research/papers/SA2013/SA2013.pdf)
- Simulation: ODE 0.8.2, "integration time step of 0.0003s", Wang 2009 ground contact model via ODE CFM/ERP; results (Table 4): Human 2.0 m/s controller tolerated +-5 deg slopes, 100 N pushes, 12 deg/s turning after 197 generations; Human 4.0 m/s: +-3 deg, 50 N, 760 generations; controllers "can locomote in real time". — [Geijtenbeek 2013 PDF](https://www.goatstream.com/research/papers/SA2013/SA2013.pdf)
- SCONE (successor tooling by the same author): "free and open-source software for predictive simulation of human and animal motion"; supports OpenSim 3, OpenSim 4 and Hyfydy ("a 100x speedup over OpenSim"); reflex controllers referencing Geyer & Herr 2010; Windows/Linux/macOS; Python API; version 2.4.4 (2026-06-04). JOSS paper: Geijtenbeek, "SCONE: Open Source Software for Predictive Simulation of Biological Motion", J. Open Source Software 4(38):1421, 2019, DOI 10.21105/joss.01421. Code split: scone-core under Apache 2.0, scone-studio (GUI) under GPL 3. — [scone.software](https://scone.software/doku.php?id=start); [JOSS DOI](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.01421); [opensim-org/SCONE README pointing to new repos](https://github.com/opensim-org/SCONE)

#### Optimizing walking controllers (Wang, Fleet, Hertzmann, SIGGRAPH Asia 2009; and 2010, 2012 follow-ups)
- Controller is a SIMBICON-style 4-state FSM on a 30-internal-DOF character (28 in the skeleton plus toe joints for landing and push-off); per-DOF PD with tau = kp(theta_d - theta) + kd*theta_dot (sign convention as printed), hip and ankle in world-frame coordinates with balance feedback (c_d, c_v) after Yin et al.; state 0 starts at foot strike, 0->1 when the swing foot passes the stance foot ("approximately"), 1->2 on foot contact; arm swing is coupled to legs by a scale alpha_arm. — [Wang 2009 PDF](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fleet/research/Papers/optwalkACMTransGraph.pdf)
- Optimization: 184-dimensional search space (controller + start state), 10 s simulations, thresholded-quadratic objective Q(d; eps) = d^2 if |d| > eps else 0; terms for user speed/step length, straightness, landing, failure, symmetry, angular momentum, head stability, power, hip/knee/ankle power ratio; weights w_head = 100, w_power = 1e-5*(70/mass), w_ratio = 1, eps_vel = 0.025 m/s, eps_step = 0.025 m, eps_head = 0.1 m/s; CMA-ES, "1000 CMA iterations take approximately 3 hours" on 19 Xeons, stop by 3000 iterations; initial coronal feedback c_d = c_v = 0.2, sagittal zero; alpha_arm = 1.0. — [Wang 2009 PDF](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fleet/research/Papers/optwalkACMTransGraph.pdf)
- Simulation: ODE 0.9 at "a simulation frequency of 2400Hz"; contact as spring-damper "kp = 75000, kd = 2000"; friction mu = 10 ("higher than physically realistic, but necessary to prevent lateral slipping during toe-off"). — [Wang 2009 PDF](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fleet/research/Papers/optwalkACMTransGraph.pdf)
- Wang, Fleet, Hertzmann 2010: optimize the expected return under distributions over "external forces, control torques, and user control inputs" by Monte Carlo, which "increases robustness and produces natural variations in style". — [DOI 10.1145/1778765.1778810](https://doi.org/10.1145/1778765.1778810)
- Wang, Hamner, Delp, Koltun 2012: sagittal hip/knee/ankle driven by eight Hill-type musculotendon units per leg with "biologically-motivated control laws", parameters optimized against metabolic energy; produces walking and running; open copy in PubMed Central. — [DOI 10.1145/2185520.2185521](https://doi.org/10.1145/2185520.2185521); [PMC4523558](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/4523558)

#### Animating human athletics (Hodgins, Wooten, Brogan, O'Brien, SIGGRAPH 1995) and running controllers
- Abstract: "control algorithms that cause a physically realistic model to perform" running, bicycling and a handspring vault; "control algorithms allow the simulated humans to maintain balance while moving their arms, to run or bicycle at a variety of speeds"; results compared against biomechanical data. DOI 10.1145/218380.218414 (Proc. SIGGRAPH 95, pp. 71-78). Full text was not retrievable here (ACM 403; three mirrors 404). — [DOI 10.1145/218380.218414](https://doi.org/10.1145/218380.218414)
- SIMBICON positions its running against Hodgins: "our framework uses no explicitly-computed injection of energy to maintain a given flight time during running or skipping" and yields running "that, qualitatively speaking, looks less like hopping than previous work [Hodgins et al. 1995; Hodgins and Pollard 1997]"; Kwon & Hodgins report Hodgins 1995 "demonstrated control algorithms for a human runner at speeds between 2.5 m/s and 5 m/s". — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [Kwon & Hodgins 2010 PDF](https://diglib.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/SCA.SCA10.129-138/129-138.pdf)
- Kwon & Hodgins, "Control Systems for Human Running using an Inverted Pendulum Model and a Reference Motion Capture Sequence", SCA 2010, DOI 10.2312/SCA/SCA10/129-138: balance from an inverted pendulum on a cart (IPC) whose predicted trajectory re-plans the desired motion every frame; runs "at speeds ranging from 1.5 to 5 m/s and turn at speeds up to 1.1 rad/s, run up and down slopes of +-5 deg and withstand pushes up to 230 N having a duration of 0.4 s"; tracking via a hybrid-dynamics solver plus low-gain PD tau_PD = kp(Theta_d - Theta) - kd(Theta_dot_d - Theta_dot) with kp = 100, kd = 10 for rotational joints (and kp = 10000, kd = 1000 for sliding joints), a 5-frame tracking delay; planning at 120 Hz while "the forward dynamics simulation uses a much higher frame rate (3000hz)". — [Kwon & Hodgins 2010 PDF](https://diglib.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/SCA.SCA10.129-138/129-138.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2312/SCA/SCA10/129-138)

#### Raibert hopping/running control (MIT Leg Lab, 1983-1986)
- Three-part decomposition: "one part that controls forward running velocity, one part that controls attitude of the body, and a third part that controls hopping height"; the parts "worked independently, treating any coupling as disturbances"; the 3D hopper "hopped in place, travelled at a specified rate, followed simple paths, and maintained balance when disturbed". — [Raibert 1983 CMU report "Dynamically Stable Legged Locomotion"](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub3/raibert_marc_h_1983_1/raibert_marc_h_1983_1.pdf); [Raibert, Brown, Chepponis 1984, IJRR 3(2):75-92, DOI 10.1177/027836498400300207](https://doi.org/10.1177/027836498400300207)
- Forward velocity by foot placement: during flight place the foot at the "CG-print" center plus a velocity-error correction. "The length of the CG-print is the product of the average forward velocity and the duration of stance. The desired symmetry is obtained when the foot is placed in the center of the CG-print"; "The algorithm implemented here uses a linear function of velocity errors to calculate displacement of the foot". The equation as printed (OCR-degraded) is x_f,d = x_dot*T_st/2 + K_x_dot*(x_dot - x_dot_d), i.e. nominal placement = half the stance-time travel, plus gain times velocity error; then hip angle from foot position by leg kinematics. Stance duration T_st "is governed by the springiness of the leg ... nearly constant for a given leg". — [Raibert 1983 report, Section 2.4.1](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub3/raibert_marc_h_1983_1/raibert_marc_h_1983_1.pdf)
- Body attitude: "a control system can manipulate the body attitude only during stance, when there is traction"; stance servo tau = -K_p (theta - theta_d) - K_v theta_dot on hip torque (3D: separate pitch and roll servos on the two hip actuators). Yaw could not be controlled on the physical 3D hopper (umbilical torque dominated). — [Raibert 1983 report, Section 2.4.2](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub3/raibert_marc_h_1983_1/raibert_marc_h_1983_1.pdf)
- Hopping height: "Hopping height is regulated by providing a fixed thrust on each hop. Equilibrium occurs when the energy lost in one hopping cycle equals the energy introduced through the leg actuator"; passive bouncing lost "about a 35% loss on each bounce"; the leg is a spring-mass resonant system excited each cycle. Velocity control experiments held speed "within about 0.2 m/sec of the desired value" with ~0.5 s response delay. — [Raibert 1983 report, Sections 2.4.3-2.5](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub3/raibert_marc_h_1983_1/raibert_marc_h_1983_1.pdf)

#### Virtual model control (Pratt et al.) and Jacobian-transpose virtual forces
- Pratt, Chew, Torres, Dilworth, Pratt, "Virtual Model Control: An Intuitive Approach for Bipedal Locomotion", IJRR 20(2):129-143, 2001, DOI 10.1177/02783640122067309: "uses virtual components to create virtual forces generated when the virtual components interact with a robot system"; "a simple set of virtual components that allows the robot to walk successfully over level terrain"; extended to rough terrain "based on geometric consideration"; "The ground is detected using foot contact switches"; a simulated seven-link planar biped walked "blindly up and down slopes and over rolling terrain". — [DOI 10.1177/02783640122067309](https://doi.org/10.1177/02783640122067309)
- Survey formulation: virtual forces are realized by "Jacobian transpose: tau = J^T F. This process is described in detail as part of the virtual model control strategy of Pratt ..." and is used by Coros et al. 2010/2011. — [Geijtenbeek & Pronost 2012 survey](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03189.x)

#### Capture point and linear inverted pendulum balance
- Kajita, Kanehiro, Kaneko, Yokoi, Hirukawa, "The 3D linear inverted pendulum mode: a simple modeling for a biped walking pattern generation", IROS 2001, pp. 239-246, DOI 10.1109/iros.2001.973365 (journal version Advanced Robotics 2003, DOI 10.1163/156855303321165097). — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Kajita+Kanehiro+Kaneko+Yokoi+Hirukawa+2001+The+3D+linear+inverted+pendulum+mode+a+simple+modeling+for+a+biped+walking+pattern+generation&rows=2)
- LIPM equation: horizontal COM displacement x from the pivot obeys x_ddot = C^2 x with "Typically, a value of C = sqrt(g/h) ... where g = 9.81 m/s^2 ... and h is the assumed constant height of the center of mass"; closed-form solution for future (x, x_dot); "The pendulum base, the pivot point of the pendulum, the ZMP, and the Center of Pressure (CoP) are all different names for the same concept." — [Missura, Bennewitz, Behnke, "Capture Steps: Robust Walking for Humanoid Robots", arXiv:2011.02793 (Int. J. Humanoid Robotics)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02793)
- Pratt, Carff, Drakunov, Goswami, "Capture Point: A Step toward Humanoid Push Recovery", IEEE-RAS Humanoids 2006, pp. 200-207, DOI 10.1109/ICHR.2006.321385: "for a large magnitude push a human or a humanoid robot must take a step to avoid a fall"; the capture region is "the region on the ground where a humanoid must step to in order to come to a complete stop"; "The intersection between the capture region and the base of support determines which strategy the robot should adopt"; the LIP is extended "to include a flywheel body" so that "Adding rotational inertia enables the humanoid to control its centroidal angular momentum, much like the way human beings do, significantly enlarging the capture region"; validated on "a simple planar biped that can recover balance after a push by stepping to the capture region and using internal angular momentum". — [DOI 10.1109/ICHR.2006.321385](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICHR.2006.321385); [ERAU record](https://portfolio.erau.edu/en/publications/capture-point-a-step-toward-humanoid-push-recovery/)
- Koolen, de Boer, Rebula, Goswami, Pratt, "Capturability-based analysis and control of legged locomotion, Part 1", IJRR 31(9):1094-1113, 2012, DOI 10.1177/0278364912452673: N-step capturability = "the ability of a legged system to come to a stop without falling by taking N or fewer steps"; three 3D-LIPM-based models: point foot, finite foot, and reaction mass (centroidal angular momentum). — [DOI 10.1177/0278364912452673](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364912452673)
- Capture Steps (robot practice): "The most effective way of controlling balance are well timed and placed recovery steps--capture steps--that absorb the expense momentum gained from a push or a stumble"; the capture point (Pratt 2006) is "much simpler and faster to compute than ZMP preview control"; their main control loop period is rho = 0.01 s; they fit C experimentally to the robot instead of using sqrt(g/h). — [arXiv:2011.02793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02793)
- In graphics, Coros 2010's IPM step d = v sqrt(h/g + v^2/(4g^2)) (see above) is the constant-leg-length analogue of the capture point; Faloutsos 2001's balance controller "is based on an inverted pendulum model [12], using the ankles to control the body sway"; Tsai et al. 2010 (IEEE TVCG 16(2):325-337, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2009.76) use an inverted pendulum "to online adjust the desired motion trajectory from the input motion capture data" in real time and replace PD with "a velocity-driven method which computes joint torques based on the desired joint angular velocities". — [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [Faloutsos 2001 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2001-siggraph-composable.pdf); [Tsai 2010 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2009.76)

#### Composable controllers (Faloutsos, van de Panne, Terzopoulos, SIGGRAPH 2001): falls, protective steps, getting up
- Framework: each controller declares pre-conditions (region of state space plus balance condition C_b(S, g, c, c_dot) on support polygon S, gravity g and COM c) and post-conditions; "At every time step" each controller reports whether it can act; transitions "occur implicitly in response to the evolution of the motion over time"; a typical chain is "balance -> fall -> default -> rollover -> prone-to-standing -> balance"; pre-conditions can be learned with SVMs. — [Faloutsos 2001 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2001-siggraph-composable.pdf); [DOI 10.1145/383259.383287](https://doi.org/10.1145/383259.383287)
- Balance controller: inverted pendulum on the ankles; "can tolerate small perturbations of the posture and the velocity/acceleration of the center of mass by stiffening the ankle joints. For larger accelerations of the center of mass, the controller actively actuates the ankle joint"; beyond that the character "maintains balance by taking a protective step" (Terminator example knocked back by a ball to the head). — [Faloutsos 2001 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2001-siggraph-composable.pdf)
- Fall controller: "designed with the average adult in mind. Its main action is thus to absorb the shock of the impact using mostly the hands"; pre-conditions are the complement of the balance controller's ("Situations that are beyond the capabilities of the latter should be handled by the fall controller"); "can handle falls in any direction, responding in different ways to falls in different directions"; roll-over requires a supine pose; prone-to-standing and a "kip" get-up exist; "Getting up off the ground is a surprisingly difficult motion to simulate". — [Faloutsos 2001 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2001-siggraph-composable.pdf)
- Simulation: DANCE framework, SD/FAST-generated dynamics (Kane's method) with "a fourth order explicit Runge-Kutta time integrator"; penalty-method collisions with spring-dampers ("it can make the system stiff"); the 2D character ran in real time while "the 3D model runs between 5 and 9 times slower than real time on a 733 MHz Pentium III". — [Faloutsos 2001 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2001-siggraph-composable.pdf)

#### Zordan & Hodgins 2002 and Zordan et al. 2005: impacts on mocap-driven characters
- Zordan & Hodgins, "Motion capture-driven simulations that hit and react", SCA 2002, pp. 89-96, DOI 10.1145/545261.545276: "trajectory tracking to follow motion capture data and a balance controller to keep the character upright"; "The system reacts to forces computed from a physical collision model by changing stiffness and damping terms. The freestanding, simulated humans respond automatically to impacts and smoothly return to tracking." — [DOI 10.1145/545261.545276](https://doi.org/10.1145/545261.545276)
- Survey summary of the 2002 gain rule: they "scale controller gains in accordance to an estimate of the moment of inertia of the chain of bodies controlled by a joint. They also increase responsiveness by temporarily lowering controller gains for significant body parts when a perturbation is detected." Tan et al. add that Zordan & Hodgins had to use "small time steps (0.67ms)" because high tracking gains were unstable. — [Geijtenbeek & Pronost 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03189.x); [Tan, Liu, Turk 2011 PDF](https://www.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- Zordan, Majkowska, Chiu, Fast, "Dynamic response for motion capture animation", ACM TOG 24(3):697-701, SIGGRAPH 2005, DOI 10.1145/1073204.1073249: pipeline = on impact run a physical simulation, search a reaction motion library for the best matching clip, then re-simulate with a joint controller informed of the target clip and blend; per-joint inertia-scaled PD tau = I (kp (theta_d(t) - theta) - kv theta_dot) with I the outboard inertia and a single hand-tuned kp, kv "held fixed for the entire transition"; theta_d(t) is a slerp/ease-in-out blend between the two mocap clips; a deliberate reaction delay of "0.1-0.2 s, which falls in the range of real human response delays" before the active response, otherwise the character "appears to be anticipating the contact" (too short) or "responds more slowly, as if stunned" (too long). — [DOI 10.1145/1073204.1073249](https://doi.org/10.1145/1073204.1073249); [PDF mirror](http://strangepaths.com/forum/files/Zordan2005.pdf)
- Zordan 2005 implementation: 51-DOF character (3 DOF at ankles, knees, hips, waist, back, shoulders, elbows, wrists, neck + 6 root), ODE with ball-and-socket joints, penalty foot-ground contact with Coulomb friction plus rotational friction, "simulation time step of about 0.0005 s", a 110-clip reaction library; friction lowered to 0.5 in the second pass "to allow foot slippage". — [Zordan 2005 PDF mirror](http://strangepaths.com/forum/files/Zordan2005.pdf)

#### Landing, falling, tripping and momentum balance (hybrid/optimization-era, still non-learning)
- Ha, Ye, Liu, "Falling and landing motion control for character animation", ACM TOG 31(6), 2012, DOI 10.1145/2366145.2366174: "fall from a wide range of heights and initial speeds, continuously roll on the ground, and get back on its feet, without inducing large stress on joints"; airborne phase "optimizes its moment of inertia to meet the ideal relation between the landing velocity and the angle of attack, under the laws of conservation of momentum"; landing phase = "impact, rolling, and getting-up"; "the character leverages contact forces to control linear momentum and angular momentum, resulting in a rolling motion which distributes impact over multiple body parts"; compared to "real world footage of experienced freerunners"; runs in real time. — [DOI 10.1145/2366145.2366174](https://doi.org/10.1145/2366145.2366174)
- Shiratori, Coley, Cham, Hodgins, "Simulating balance recovery responses to trips based on biomechanical principles", SCA 2009, DOI 10.1145/1599470.1599475: "impact from an obstacle during early swing leads to an elevating strategy, in which the swing leg is lifted over the obstacle and impact during late swing leads to a lowering strategy, in which a swing leg is positioned immediately in front of the obstacle and then the other leg is swung forward". — [DOI 10.1145/1599470.1599475](https://doi.org/10.1145/1599470.1599475)
- Macchietto, Zordan, Shelton, "Momentum control for balance", ACM TOG 28(3), 2009, DOI 10.1145/1531326.1531386: real-time standing balance that controls linear and angular momenta, relates them to COM and center of pressure, reconciles with tracking via optimization producing joint accelerations, then hybrid inverse/forward dynamics for torques; yields "wind-milling". — [DOI 10.1145/1531326.1531386](https://doi.org/10.1145/1531326.1531386)
- Lee, Kim, Lee, "Data-driven biped control", ACM TOG 29(4), 2010, DOI 10.1145/1778765.1781155: "modulating the reference trajectory continuously and seamlessly such that even a simple dynamic tracking controller can follow the reference trajectory while maintaining its balance"; realtime; turns, spins, steering. — [DOI 10.1145/1778765.1781155](https://doi.org/10.1145/1778765.1781155)
- Spring-mass leg mechanics (why running/landing legs should be compliant): Geyer, Seyfarth, Blickhan 2006, Proc. R. Soc. B 273:2861-2867, DOI 10.1098/rspb.2006.3637: "not stiff but compliant legs are essential to obtain the basic walking mechanics"; one spring-mass model "combines the basic dynamics of walking and running". — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2006.3637)

### Inferences
- Reproduction recipe distilled from the cited findings: (1) 4-state FSM (swing-lift for fixed dt ~0.3 s, then swing-down until contact; mirrored), per-joint PD with kp ~ 300 Nm/rad for legs/waist, kd = 0.1 kp (SIMBICON) or kd = 2 sqrt(kp) (Coros), torque clamp ~ 200-370 Nm; (2) torso and swing hip PD in world frame, stance hip torque = -(tau_torso + tau_swinghip); (3) swing-hip target += c_d*d + c_v*v with c_d ~ 0.5, c_v ~ 0.2 (or Coros's IPM step d = v sqrt(h/g + v^2/4g^2) minus 0.05*V_d, capped at 0.6 L); (4) gravity compensation via J^T(-m g) on all non-stance links, and a COM virtual force k_V(V_d - V) via the stance-leg Jacobian; (5) for impacts, drop gains for ~0.1-0.2 s then ramp back (Zordan) or keep low gains and rely on feed-forward torques (SIMBICON FEL); (6) wrap everything in Faloutsos-style controllers with pre-conditions so balance -> protective step -> fall -> roll -> get-up is a legal chain.
- Mapping to the game's power gating: "fall tolerance" is literally the Ha/Ye/Liu airborne-inertia + rolling controller being unlocked; "self-arrest" before a wall corresponds to enabling the capture-step/IPM stop (d = v sqrt(h/g + ...)) and the velocity-tuning virtual force with a large k_V; "impact tolerance" corresponds to the Zordan gain-schedule and joint torque limits (the 90-370 Nm SIMBICON figures give a scale for what "human strength" means); superhuman strength = raising kp, torque limits and the IPM step cap 0.6 L.
- Nothing in this literature addresses ledge catching; the closest primitives are Coros 2010's IK-driven reaching/picking-up and step climbing, and de Lasa's end-effector features. Ledge catch would need a new controller: end-effector reach with pre-condition on hand-ledge distance and COM velocity.

## Key question 3: Real-time feasibility and open-source implementations

### Takeaway
SIMBICON-family controllers (PD + FSM + IPM/virtual forces) run at or above real time for one character even on 2007-2010 hardware, but only with 0.3-0.5 ms physics steps; optimization-based controllers (de Lasa, Mordatch) were limited to about one character; muscle/reflex controllers (Geijtenbeek 2013) run in real time after hours of offline CMA-ES. Open source: cartwheel-3d (Apache-2.0, ODE), SIMBICON-CEF (GPLv3, ODE), LocoTest (ports of cartwheel to ODE/PhysX/Bullet/Vortex), SCONE (scone-core Apache 2.0, GUI GPL3), plus a 2D Java SIMBICON applet.

### Cited Findings
- SIMBICON 2D ran 5x real time (1.8 GHz Core Duo, dt 0.0001 s); 3D in ODE ran 1.2x real time (3.2 GHz P4, dt 0.005 s). — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Coros 2010: "all the simulations run in real time, written as a single-threaded application" on a 2.4 GHz 2-core machine, at dt 0.0005 s. — [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- LocoTest timing (one 0.5 ms step, Xeon X5680 3.33 GHz): ODE 0.099 ms/step for 1 character (9.9 ms for 100), ODE-quick 0.064 ms, PhysX 0.300 ms, Bullet 0.107 ms, Vortex 0.120 ms; PhysX's character controllers and Bullet's "do not use 'real' physics"; the controller "controls the walking style through explicit PD torques at every simulation time step ... the disadvantage is that the simulation has to take smaller time steps"; the authors planned to explore stable PD. — [LocoTest PDF](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~kkyin/papers/locotest.pdf); [DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-25090-3_20](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-25090-3_20)

### Inferences

## Key question 4: Physics tick rate and integration stability for PD-driven ragdoll joints

### Takeaway
Classical PD-torque controllers used 0.1-0.5 ms steps (2-10 kHz) in ODE, and LocoTest measured that a SIMBICON/cartwheel walker falls at steps above ~0.9-1.6 ms and the simulation diverges above 18-150 ms depending on engine; the governing constraint is explicit PD with high kp. Tan, Liu, Turk's Stable PD (implicit-in-the-controller) removes this coupling and tracks with arbitrarily high gains at 33 ms steps under forward Euler, provided kd >= kp*dt; the survey's rule of thumb kv = 2 sqrt(kp) (critical damping) and SIMBICON's kd = 0.1 kp bracket practical damping choices.

### Cited Findings
- Time steps used: SIMBICON 2D 0.0001 s, 3D (ODE) 0.005 s; Coros 2010 (ODE) 0.0005 s; cartwheel-3d default 0.5 ms; Wang 2009 (ODE 0.9) 2400 Hz; Geijtenbeek 2013 (ODE 0.8.2) 0.0003 s; Zordan 2005 (ODE) ~0.0005 s; Zordan & Hodgins 2002 0.67 ms; Kwon & Hodgins 2010 3000 Hz dynamics with 120 Hz control. — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [Wang 2009 PDF](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fleet/research/Papers/optwalkACMTransGraph.pdf); [Geijtenbeek 2013 PDF](https://www.goatstream.com/research/papers/SA2013/SA2013.pdf); [Zordan 2005 PDF mirror](http://strangepaths.com/forum/files/Zordan2005.pdf); [Tan 2011 PDF](https://www.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf); [Kwon & Hodgins 2010 PDF](https://diglib.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/SCA.SCA10.129-138/129-138.pdf)
- LocoTest Table 6 (largest stable step in ms for the cartwheel normal walk; bound1 = simulation unstable, bound2 = character falls, bound3 = motion deviates > 0.1 from the 0.5 ms baseline): ODE 18 / 1.6 / 1.0; ODEquick 18 / 1.4 / 1.0; PhysX 150 / 0.9 / -; Bullet 100 / 1.3 / 1.0; Vortex 35 / 1.6 / 1.2. "simulations in PhysX and Bullet are much more stable at large time steps than in ODE and Vortex, but they do not differ much in terms of the stability of the walking controller once the simulation moves into the stable region." A (250,150) N push for 200 ms was absorbed on all engines with the limit cycle recovered within ~4 cycles (Table 7). — [LocoTest PDF](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~kkyin/papers/locotest.pdf)
- LocoTest joint modelling: ball-and-socket joints need an added angular motor constraint (ODE dJointCreateAMotor) to impose swing/twist limits; PhysX and Bullet can "motorize certain joints, where users can specify the desired joint angles and velocities"; ODE separates collision detection (dSpaceCollide) from stepping (dWorldStep/dWorldQuickStep, the latter PGS iterative, also used in PhysX and Bullet). — [LocoTest PDF](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~kkyin/papers/locotest.pdf)
- Stable PD (Tan, Liu, Turk, IEEE CG&A 31(4):34-44, 2011, DOI 10.1109/MCG.2011.30): conventional tau_n = -kp(q_n - qbar_n) - kd qdot_n; SPD uses the predicted next state, tau_n = -kp(q_n + dt qdot_n - qbar_{n+1}) - kd(qdot_n + dt qddot_n) (with reference velocity: subtract qbar_dot_{n+1} inside the kd term); for articulated bodies qddot_n = (M + K_d dt)^-1 (-C - K_p(q_n + qdot_n dt - qbar_{n+1}) - K_d qdot_n + tau_ext), then explicit Euler; stability condition "K_d >= K_p dt"; "tracking motion capture data can be stably simulated with large time steps (33 ms) using first-order explicit Euler integration"; in a two-link test, PD "crashes the simulation" at kp = 3000-30000 while SPD tracks; they used kp up to 1e9 for a volleyball catch; "With 50 times larger time steps" than Zordan & Hodgins' 0.67 ms, gain tuning becomes interactive; SPD is not overly damped like a fully implicit integrator. — [Tan 2011 PDF](https://www.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf); [DOI 10.1109/MCG.2011.30](https://doi.org/10.1109/MCG.2011.30)
- Survey on gains: PD tau = kp(theta_d - theta) + kv(theta_dot_d - theta_dot); "When controller gains are set too low, a joint may not be able to track its target and lag behind. When set too high, a joint may follow a target trajectory too rigidly"; "If the desired state and dynamics characteristics are fixed, the optimal relation ... is: kv = 2 sqrt(kp)" (critically damped) used "as an estimate or a starting point"; PID is of "very limited use" in character animation "because any change in desired state invalidates the current integral error"; "a larger time step will decrease simulation stability ... while a smaller time step will decrease simulation performance". — [Geijtenbeek & Pronost 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03189.x)
- Gain/limit scales used in practice: SIMBICON kp 300 (waist 1000), kd = 0.1 kp, torque limits 90-1000 Nm; Coros kd = 2 sqrt(kp), limits 200/100 Nm optional; SIMBICON-CEF humanoid: root 3000/300, hips/knees 300/30, ankles 75/10, shoulders 20/5, elbows 5/1; Kwon & Hodgins kp 100 / kd 10 with a hybrid dynamics solver doing the heavy lifting. — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [simbicon_cef source](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/); [Kwon & Hodgins 2010 PDF](https://diglib.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/SCA.SCA10.129-138/129-138.pdf)
- Ground/contact stiffness values that went with those steps: SIMBICON 2D penalty ground kp = 1e5 N/m, kd = 6000 Ns/m; Wang 2009 contact spring-damper kp = 75000, kd = 2000 via ODE CFM/ERP; Faloutsos's penalty method "can make the system stiff". — [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [Wang 2009 PDF](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fleet/research/Papers/optwalkACMTransGraph.pdf); [Faloutsos 2001 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2001-siggraph-composable.pdf)

### Inferences
- The explicit-PD stability envelope for a 70 kg humanoid with kp ~ 300 and kd ~ 30 is roughly 1 ms per step (LocoTest bound2 0.9-1.6 ms) regardless of engine; Godot's default 60 Hz physics tick (16.7 ms) is an order of magnitude too coarse for explicit PD torques of that stiffness. Options consistent with the sources: (a) substep Jolt to 1-2 kHz for the player ragdoll only; (b) implement Stable PD (tau from predicted next state, kd >= kp dt) and run 120-240 Hz; (c) use the engine's implicit joint motors (Jolt's motor/spring settings solve the spring inside the constraint solver, which is the same implicit idea Tan et al. describe) with the PD targets fed as motor targets, and reserve explicit torques for virtual forces and gravity compensation.
- Because kd >= kp*dt is the SPD stability bound, at 120 Hz (dt = 8.3 ms) a kp of 300 needs kd >= 2.5, and kd = 2 sqrt(kp) = 34.6 already satisfies it; the more restrictive choice is SIMBICON's kd = 0.1 kp = 30, also fine. At 60 Hz (dt 16.7 ms) kp = 3000 (root) needs kd >= 50, satisfied by the CEF root value 300.

### Gaps
- No source in this set measured Jolt specifically; the LocoTest data are for ODE/PhysX 2.8/Bullet 2.78/Vortex 5 in 2011. Jolt's constraint-solver motor behaviour under substepping should be measured empirically.
- Stable PD's interaction with engine-side constraint solvers (rather than a custom generalized-coordinate integrator) is not documented in these sources.
