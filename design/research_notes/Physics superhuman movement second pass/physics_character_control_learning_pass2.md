# Learning-based physics character control — second pass: verification, gap filling and extension

Research date: 2026-09-27. Scope: re-check every number in the pass-one note (`../Physics based superhuman movement design/physics_character_control_learning.md`) and in the report section "Hand-author the controller first; a learned policy is a later residual", re-verify the shipped-game and Godot-inference claims, fill the named gaps, and chain citations forward from DReCon, SuperTrack, PHC and MaskedMimic.

Method note. Every paper below was re-downloaded as a PDF with curl (arXiv, Ubisoft, theorangeduck.com) and its text extracted locally with PyMuPDF; numbers were then located by regex and read in context. Tool availability this session: WebSearch worked for 12 queries and then the session budget (200/200) was exhausted; the arXiv API returned HTTP 406 on every attempt, so arXiv's HTML search (`arxiv.org/search`) was used instead; OpenAlex refused every request (shared daily budget exhausted, needs an API key); Semantic Scholar's `/citations` endpoint worked (440 unique 2024+ citing papers across the four seed papers) but its `/search` endpoint returned 429 on every attempt; GitHub commit pages were read through WebFetch because the GitHub MCP tools are restricted to the project repository and api.github.com returns 403 from this container. Items that could not be opened are named in §5 with what was used instead.

### 2b. ARC Raiders — primary-source detail (closes the "no primary Embark source" gap)
Two first-hand reports of Embark's own talks now exist. Both are journalism (4Gamer editors attending the talks), not Embark documents, but they quote the talks directly.

From the GDC 2026 talk (11 March 2026, Martin Singh-Blom, ML Research Lead) as reported by 4Gamer ([4Gamer, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.4gamer.net/games/609/G060942/20260312064/)):
- Hand-keyed animation, mocap ("you can't put a capture suit on a 12-metre, six-legged robot") and procedural animation (a Unity "Stickman" demo) were all evaluated and rejected; the team then combined physics simulation with deep RL, citing 2018 robotics results.
- Early tests used "MuJoCo and NVIDIA Isaac Gym"; observations were joint angles plus distance and direction to the target, **actions were target angles for PD controllers**, reward 1 point on reaching the cube; natural hip-swing on sharp turns emerged "for free".
- Algorithms named: PPO and SAC. Tuned variables included network depth, learning rate, "robot leg length and motor strength", reward balance and terrain generation; "how cool the gait looks" could not be quantified, so the animation director judged videos; **finishing one robot's walk took "years"**.
- Multi-task interference ("behavior bleed"): a network trained to both walk and stop became timid; the fix was **one independent policy ("brain") per task, switched by the game engine's behaviour tree**, which also let designers tune behaviour without ML engineers.
- Style was solved with **adversarial imitation learning** (GAIL, Ho & Ermon 2016; AMP, Peng et al.): animators author a reference walk cycle in their usual curve editor and a discriminator rewards the robot for being indistinguishable from it. When gait and look still clashed, the robot's 3D armour was thinned to match the gait.
- A "cheat" from a sibling project: when the robot is about to fall, apply a non-physical "magic force" to the head and penalise its use.
- Perception: a top-down height-map grid failed in tunnels (ceilings read as obstacles) and let the grid "see" through walls; replaced by egocentric point-cloud raycasts with hits cached in world space and re-projected each frame, processed with PointNet.
- Tooling: a cloud parallel-training tool "Hive RL" and TensorBoard dashboards over terrain sets (PatrolBridge, Slopes, UnevenRocks).

From the NDC26 talk (June 2026, Nexon Developers Conference) as reported by 4Gamer ([4Gamer, 16 Jun 2026](https://www.4gamer.net/games/652/G065211/20260616016/)):
- "The initial demo was done in a Unity test environment with game assets"; reward terms were distance-to-target, cosine of body-up vs world-up, and facing angle; later the reward grew to "10–15 reward models" (speed to target, uprightness, energy, torque use, non-foot contacts, facing) that "do not scale": a new robot meant rewriting everything, and an art note like "more bouncy / heavier" took "weeks to months" of re-tuning.
- Two brains shipped for the walker: an "always walk, never stop" model and a "walk and stop" model, selected by the behaviour tree.
- AMP with an animator-authored walk cycle as discriminator data put the artist back in the loop; a new gait means a new animation plus retraining.
- Falls are part of the look: the Leaper "misjudges a landing, tumbles, gets up again", and these small failures "make the robot look real".

The GDC session description states the team "trained and deployed these agents in Unreal Engine" ([GDC schedule](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/learning-to-move-physics-based-enemy-locomotion-in-arc-raiders/917319), read via search snippet). Combined with the 4Gamer reports (early tests in MuJoCo/Isaac Gym and a Unity test bed), the production training simulator is still not unambiguously stated in any source I could open. Two follow-ups confirm the "no online learning" point: [GamesRadar, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/third-person-shooter/arc-raiders-killer-robots-arent-learning-in-real-time-but-embark-will-sneak-in-ai-upgrades-based-on-player-behavior-we-keep-trying-to-make-the-flying-drones-better-and-better/) and [FRVR, 16 Apr 2026](https://frvr.com/blog/news/embark-says-arc-raiders-robots-dont-learn-on-player-data/) ("only really applies to the fluid movement animations that help the virtual machines stably walk, jump, and fly"). A further Embark talk, "How We Taught the Robots in Arc Raiders to Move (Eventually)", is the day-one keynote of the AI and Games Conference, 10–11 Nov 2026 — [aiandgames.com](https://www.aiandgames.com/p/fenris-creations-embark-warhorse). An academic benchmark "inspired by the bestiary of ARC Raiders" (four MuJoCo hexapod/quadruped morphologies) exists but is by University of Florence authors and says nothing about Embark's pipeline — [ARC-RL, arXiv 2605.19503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19503).

## 3. New sources and findings

### Takeaway
Nothing in the second pass overturns the pass-one verdict (hand-author first, learned residual later), but four things move: (1) per-skill training cost has collapsed on GPU-native simulators (seconds on one RTX 5090 for DeepMimic-style skills; 15–45 min for locomotion/tracking on one RTX 4090; ~5 h for a fall-safety policy), so the remaining obstacle is the Jolt transfer step, not compute; (2) a maintained native ONNX Runtime GDExtension appeared in late August 2026 alongside the ncnn one, so the ".NET or nothing" framing is out of date; (3) Embark's own talks show the production recipe — PD-target actions, PPO/SAC, one policy per task switched by a behaviour tree, AMP against animator-authored cycles, egocentric point-cloud perception, a penalised assistive force — and confirm that reward tuning, not learning, consumed the years; (4) two 2025–2026 papers (AMOR, Neural Assistive Impulses) map directly onto the game's competence scalar and superhuman tiers.

### Cited findings that add to the report
- Training-cost floor, 2026: DeepMimic-style skills train in 1.5–4.5 s each and a 37.4 h AMASS tracker reaches 98.6 % in 29.1 min on one RTX 5090 with MuJoCo Warp — [InstantMimic](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09821); humanoid locomotion in 15 min on one RTX 4090 with pushes and rough terrain — [Seo et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01996); whole-body tracking in ~45 min vs ~6 h PPO — [LooperMuscle](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.00820); a fall-safety policy in ~5 h on one RTX 4090 — [Xu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07407); a 3 M-parameter motion prior in <5 h on one RTX 4090 — [SMP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.03028); exaggerated-motion assistive impulses in ~8 h on one RTX 4090 — [Wang & Benes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05394).
- Training-cost ceiling, 2026: 24 A100s for GPC — [GPC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29148); 21k GPU-hours for SONIC — [SONIC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07820); four V100s for parkour vaulting — [HIL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.12619); one A6000 for 30 days for a football low-level policy — [PhysicsFC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.21216).
- Effective sample throughput of the cited controllers (computed from their own numbers, for comparison with Godot RL Agents' "12k interactions per second … on 4 CPU cores" — [Godot RL Agents](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.03636)): DeepMimic ≈ 60 M / 48 h ≈ 350 samples/s; AMP ≈ 100–300 M / 30–140 h ≈ 600–900/s; DReCon 2,500/s; SuperTrack ~5,000/s; ASE ≈ 10 B / 10 days ≈ 11,600/s; PhysicsFC low-level ≈ 5 B / 30 days ≈ 1,900/s. The raw simulator gap (Isaac Gym's "2-3 orders of magnitude" — [Isaac Gym](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10470)) is real, but end-to-end training pipelines of the cited papers ran at 10^2–10^4 samples/s, so a headless Jolt harness at a few thousand samples/s is in the DReCon/SuperTrack regime rather than hopeless.
- Runtime-adjustable behaviour without retraining: AMOR conditions the policy on reward weights and adjusts them live — [AMOR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23708) — a learned analogue of the report's competence scalar; its 50 Hz policy and 4×1024 network are larger than DReCon's but still MLPs.
- Falls are a design feature, not a bug, in the one shipped example: Embark shows a Leaper failing a landing, tumbling and getting up as the thing that "makes the robot look real" — [4Gamer NDC26](https://www.4gamer.net/games/652/G065211/20260616016/).
- Embark's "behavior bleed" and per-task brains — [4Gamer GDC](https://www.4gamer.net/games/609/G060942/20260312064/) — match the report's plan to keep intent in a state machine/behaviour tree; the same BT+RL pattern is now packaged for Unreal by AMD Schola — [arXiv 2510.14154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14154).
- Fall-recovery methods for single trackers now exist that need no separate recovery policy at deployment (StableMimic, Stubborn) — [StableMimic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02385); [Stubborn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12814) — the "tracker or ragdoll" dichotomy in pass one is softer in 2026.
- Godot native inference options as of Sep 2026: godot-onnx-loader (ORT 1.20.1, Godot 4.6+, C++) — [repo](https://github.com/DynamicDevices/godot-onnx-loader); godot-native-rl (ncnn, Godot 4.5+, web/console-friendly static link) — [repo](https://github.com/minigraphx/godot-native-rl); godot-infer (compute-shader ONNX, Godot 4.5+) — [repo](https://github.com/FrancoYudica/godot-infer); stock godot_rl_agents still .NET-only — [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/edbeeching/godot_rl_agents/main/README.md). None publishes a latency number.

### Inferences
- The report's cost argument should be re-based: "training is the obstacle" is true for the research-scale generalists (GPC, SONIC, MaskedMimic) but no longer for a DReCon-sized residual, whose bottleneck is now (a) writing the headless Jolt harness or (b) accepting a MuJoCo-Warp → Jolt transfer with domain randomisation over gains, friction and contact — the PolySim and issue-7750 evidence says a *single* low-level tracker transfers, a hierarchy on top does not.
- For the superhuman tiers, Neural Assistive Impulses is the closest published mechanism: a learned or hand-authored root wrench whose allowed magnitude is the tier variable, penalised in the reward so the body uses joints first. It fits the report's "pelvis authority spring" exactly and gives it a citation.
- AMOR-style weight conditioning is the right shape if the game ever wants one learned policy across competence levels rather than one per tier; the 5-day-per-character training cost is the price.
- The Godot inference recommendation should read: prefer a hand-written MLP forward pass or ncnn for a CPU-side 30 Hz policy; godot-onnx-loader is now a viable ORT path for iteration but is one week old; godot-infer is GPU-bound and wrong for a physics-tick policy.

## 4. Contradictions found in the report or other notes

1. **Report, learned-control section: "only DReCon and SuperTrack were ever trained in a game-class engine (Bullet, PhysX/Havok)".** Contradicted by the primary sources: DeepMimic and AMP both use "the Bullet physics engine" at 1.2 kHz — [DeepMimic](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02717); [AMP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.02180) — and Won 2022 uses PyBullet — [PhysicsVAE README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/PhysicsVAE); ControlVAE uses ODE — [ControlVAE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.06063).
