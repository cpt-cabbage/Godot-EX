## 2. Gaps closed

### Takeaway
The December 2015 Cobra TV interview is the missing developer text on engine choice and scale (terrain "is a sphere of radius 6370 kilometers! Where is the check box to do that?"), the Spanish Gamescom 2017 coverage shows the peripheral blinders were an Eagle-Flight-style, platform-driven comfort requirement rather than a free design choice, and four Spanish reviews add option names — but no source anywhere enumerates the six VR interfaces, states the FOV, motion-blur, LOD or coordinate-precision scheme, and no postmortem or technical talk by del Cerro exists in anything reachable.

### Cited findings

Engine choice, scale, and development history (developer's own words)
- Why a custom engine: "Unreal Engine, at the time I started the development (January 2012), was not cheap for Indies. And Unity, I think I have an unreasonable dislike for it… the game features a lot of visual effects that are not standard. Terrain rendering, for example. The common engines start asking the size for the terrain: 512x512? 1024x1024? But I want to make the terrain of the entire Earth, that is a sphere of radius 6370 kilometers! Where is the check box to do that? The same happens with everything: the size of the cities (the game features cities as big as the real Los Angeles), the destructibility of every building… I need access to every layer of the engine to optimize them… I always have been the engine guy in my former company" — [Cobra TV GNN, 21 Dec 2015](http://www.cobratvgnn.com/2015/12/megaton-rainfall-developer-interview.html). This is the same interview Wikipedia cites for the January 2012 start date.
- The two headline capabilities as an engineering problem: "Both things (the real scale of the Earth and the supersonic speed) have never been done in a First Person game, although we have more than enough technology to achieve it (but it's an engineering nightmare)"; his interest is "how could those effects (collapsing of buildings, giant clouds of debris and smoke, etc.) be implemented in real time, in a non-scripted GPU-friendly way?" — same source.
- Timeline: "2 years of free time + 2 years full time" as of Dec 2015; "In December 2013, the building-collapsing technology looked so promising to me that I decided to go Indie, and finish the game"; the build was "playable from start to finish, and runs on PC, PS4 and PSVR" with "at least 6 more months polishing" planned — same source.
- Procedural cities and terrain: "both the cities and the terrain are generated procedurally. Different cities will have different recognizable monuments"; street level is populated: "You'll see pedestrians, vehicles, etc., and they will react to the action and the explosions (with panic basically)" — same source.
- Space as reuse of the planet renderer: "the game features procedurally-generated solar systems and galaxies. It's the natural development of the 'absolute freedom of movement' idea, and it's a way of making profit of the planet rendering technology developed for the Earth" — same source.
- Speed regime evidence: "in any moment you can fly out of the atmosphere, and in half a minute go from the day side of the Earth to the night side" — same source. Half the Earth's circumference (~20,000 km) in 30 s is ~670 km/s, roughly Mach 1,900 (my arithmetic), i.e. the exo-atmospheric regime is three orders of magnitude above the Mach 8 in-atmosphere figure.
- 2015 preview corroboration: "no load times whatsoever" between orbit and surface; del Cerro "cites procedural content as one of the big ways to make up for the lack of manpower" — [Destructoid, Alessandro Fillari, 2 Mar 2015](https://www.destructoid.com/megaton-rainfall-is-superhero-action-on-an-epic-scale/).

## 3. New sources and findings: the primary literature for extreme speed and planet scale, and what Godot 4 + Jolt can do without engine work

### Takeaway
Every shipped planet-scale first-person system in the record uses the same two-part split — 64-bit (or reference-frame-relative) positions on the CPU and camera- or patch-relative single precision on the GPU — and Godot 4 already ships both halves (`precision=double` and PR #66178's emulated-double renderer) while Jolt's own documentation adds the third constraint the pass-one note missed: velocities, rotations and the broadphase stay single-precision even in a double build, so a constrained body (a ragdoll) moving at Mach speeds must live in a co-moving physics space, exactly what KSP's Krakensbane and X4's multi-`PhysicsSystem` design do. Planet-scale terrain LOD (cube-sphere quadtrees, clipmaps, chunked LOD) is all published and engine-agnostic; speed regimes as a function of gravity-well distance are documented only at wiki level for Elite; and the comfort layer (dynamic FOV restriction, peripheral blinders, fog-contrast effects on perceived speed) has a peer-reviewed basis.

### 3.1 Floating origin, camera-relative rendering and 64-bit positions

- **Thorne 2005.** "Using a floating origin to improve fidelity and performance of large, distributed virtual worlds", Chris Thorne, 2005 International Conference on Cyberworlds, IEEE Xplore document 1587542 — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1587542) (record-only: the page returned a 202 challenge; ResearchGate and dblp blocked; Semantic Scholar 429). It is the paper Wikipedia's floating-origin article cites — [Floating origin, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_origin). Search-result summary of its claims (not verified against the text): jitter arises far from the origin because large matrix values lose 32-bit precision on the GPU; keeping the camera at the origin and moving the objects "eliminates spatial jitter effects and lowers design and processing overheads" and "the continuous floating origin algorithm is capable of scaling the simulation to Solar system scale". Later Thorne work found only as records: the UWA PhD thesis "Origin-centric techniques for optimising scalability and the fidelity of motion, interaction and rendering" and "Rotation Floating Origin" (ResearchGate, 2021), plus a commercial "Relative Spacetime Framework" ("Play at any scale without jitter and precision problems") — [floatingorigin.com/framework](https://www.floatingorigin.com/framework). Contribution: the canonical citation and the vocabulary (floating origin = camera stays at 0; origin shift = periodic snap). Godot: no engine feature; doable in project code (see the proposals below for what breaks).
- **Wikipedia's precision arithmetic** (wiki-only): single precision "halves for every power of two units away from the origin" and cannot represent fractions beyond 8,388,608 units; Minecraft's "Far Lands" as the classic failure — [Floating origin, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_origin).
- **Star Citizen, primary CIG texts.** Foundry 42 graphics programming, 3 Oct 2014: "We're moving towards 64 bit physics to handle enormous maps, however rendering doesn't require such precision because from the cameras perspective everything is actually relatively nearby"; also "most games actually use just 24 bit depth buffers and this isn't enough for large space scenes and so we've just completed the transition to 32 bit rendering"; Austin: "Large World 'Phase 1'… galaxies… that will literally take days to traverse without quantum travel" — [Monthly Report: September 2014, RSI](https://robertsspaceindustries.com/en/comm-link/transmission/14191-Monthly-Report-September-2014). Chris Roberts, 20 Jul 2015: "we spent eight months converting the engine to 64 bit precision… the Zone system and local grids… fundamentally change how the engine organizes, streams, updates and renders objects" — [Letter from the Chairman, RSI](https://robertsspaceindustries.com/en/comm-link/transmission/14839-Letter-From-The-Chairman). Tracy 2016 (verified above) adds that only physics and positioning needed the change and the cost was "marginal". No GDC talk on the procedural planets exists in the GDC Vault results; the planet tech was shown at CitizenCon and in GamersNexus interviews ([GamersNexus, Roberts on procedural planets, 2016](https://gamersnexus.net/gg/2613-chris-roberts-star-citizen-procedural-planets-alpha3-citizencon), not opened). Contribution: the shipped architecture in its own words — 64-bit positions, camera-relative rendering, spatial zones with local physics grids. Godot: 64-bit positions = `precision=double`; camera-relative rendering = PR #66178's emulation (below); "local physics grids" = separate physics spaces (`PhysicsServer3D.space_create`, `World3D.space`, fork `doc/classes/PhysicsServer3D.xml:1401`, `World3D.xml:31`) — expressible in project code, but object hand-off between spaces is manual (inference).
- **Frontier, Elite Dangerous.** Doc Ross (Lead Render Programmer), 5 Apr 2018: "To ensure consistent visuals and gameplay on the screen and between users we need millimeter precision, but the input values for the noise functions depend on the point on the surface of the world relative to the planet's center, which can be of the scale of tens of billions of millimeters"; "We have written alternate libraries to create the functions in 64 bit – i.e. double precision and dual-float precision"; landable surfaces "start as a cube with square sub-dividing faces which behave as quadtrees… uniform tri-meshes which, as you get closer… further sub-divided into four sub-patches"; "Using compute shaders on your GPU, the patches undergo spherification"; "Above a certain LOD level of the planet surface patches, flat geometry is used with textures generated for the look, normals, and height"; generation is hierarchical "galactic → star-system → planet → smallest details" — [Generating The Universe in Elite: Dangerous, 80.lv, Kirill Tokarev interviewing Doc Ross, 5 Apr 2018](https://80.lv/articles/generating-the-universe-in-elite-dangerous). No Frontier GDC/Develop "Stellar Forge" talk was found; this interview is the closest primary. Contribution: dual-float ("double-float") noise on the GPU and a cube-sphere quadtree — the same emulation family as Godot's renderer and UE5's `FDFVector3`. Godot: cube-sphere quadtree with compute-shader displacement is project code (`ArrayMesh`, compute shaders via `RenderingDevice`); dual-float noise must be written in the shader by hand (inference).
- **Outerra** (Brano Kemen's engine blog). 2008: LOD "from thousands of kilometers down to centimeters"; Earth elevation "3arc sec dataset remapped to cube faces with roughly 150m resolution", fractally refined "down to under-foot resolution"; on precision: tile geometry is "generated in its 'local space', what is a coordinate system centered in the middle of the terrain tile" and "the camera is expressed in the local space too", so there are no "large static numbers in coordinates that ruin the precision" — [Outerra: Intro, 31 Jul 2008](https://outerra.blogspot.com/2008/07/intro.html). 2009: standard depth values "converge towards value of 1.0… resolution… approximately 1e-7"; logarithmic depth with `z = (exp(depth*farcoef) - 1)/C`, and the alternative of swapping near/far with a "greater" depth test so precision lands at distance — [Outerra: Floating Point Depth Buffer, 31 Dec 2009](https://outerra.blogspot.com/2009/12/floating-point-depth-buffer.html). Contribution: per-tile local origins (a floating origin per patch, not per camera) and the depth-precision half of planet rendering. Godot: reverse-Z landed in 4.3 as "a massive improvement to depth buffer precision at no performance or memory cost" — [Introducing Reverse Z, Clay John, Godot blog, 29 Apr 2024](https://godotengine.org/article/introducing-reverse-z/) — so the depth half is free; per-tile local space is project code.
- **KSP** (verified above): floating origin plus Krakensbane (velocity subtraction) as two separate "astro-scale physics helpers" with engagement thresholds, a floating origin also for the map-view scaled scene, and double precision for orbit splines — [Squad devnote, Sept 2016](https://kerbaldevteam.tumblr.com/post/150703936094/devnote-tuesday-12-pre-release-the-bug-hunt-and); [KSP Fandom Version History](https://kerbalspaceprogram.fandom.com/wiki/Version_History) (wiki-only). Contribution: the velocity half of the problem (Krakensbane) is distinct from the position half (floating origin), and thresholds that are too high let error accumulate.
- **Unreal Engine 5 LWC.** UE4 `WORLD_MAX` was 21 km; UE5's default is "88 million kilometers"; core types moved to double; "Chaos Destruction Physics continues to function unabridged using the double type"; rendering uses translated world space ("convert from absolute world space to a translated world space, such as Camera Relative World Space"); two shader emulations — DoubleFloat (`FDFVector3`, high/low float pairs) and the older tile types (`FLWCVector3`, tile × 256k + offset, "inferior precision"); Niagara keeps floats and "a sufficiently large world will be divided into grid cells"; still flagged Beta ("use caution when shipping with it") — [Large World Coordinates in UE5](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/large-world-coordinates-in-unreal-engine-5); [LWC Rendering in UE5](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/large-world-coordinates-rendering-in-unreal-engine-5). Contribution: the same CPU-double/GPU-emulated split, with particles as the known weak point — matching Godot's GPUParticles3D limitation.
- **Unity.** HDRP's camera-relative rendering exists to "render distant GameObjects (with large world space coordinates) in a more robust and numerically stable way", is on by default in `ShaderConfig.cs`, and `_WorldSpaceCameraPos` "is never Camera-relative" — [Camera-relative rendering, HDRP 17.0.4 manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/Camera-Relative-Rendering.html). Nothing in that page covers physics or origin shifting, which in Unity is asset-store or project code (the Wikipedia article cites a "Visual Design Cafe – Origin Shifting" asset doc; wiki-only).
- **Godot's own history and open issues.**
  - Proposal [#892 "Add support for double precision floats", aaronfranke, 25 May 2020](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/892): doubles give "29 additional significant binary digits", usable areas of "approximately 2 billion kilometers"; explicitly proposed that "GPU rendering would still use single-precision floats; CPU-side calculations would convert coordinates relative to the camera before passing to graphics hardware"; milestoned 4.0.
  - [PR #66178 "Emulate double precision for regular rendering operation when REAL_T_IS_DOUBLE", clayjohn, merged 30 Sep 2022, 4.0](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/66178): "We calculate the lost precision on the CPU and pass it into the GPU so that it can calculate an error-corrected version of the vertex position" (compensated-arithmetic split of the transform into high and low parts); `MODEL_MATRIX` became read-only; does not work with `skip_vertex_transform` or `world_vertex_coords`; particles and multimeshes need separate handling and the approach degrades beyond roughly 10,000 km.
  - [Tracker #98655 "Rendering issues at very long distances (numerical precision)", opened 29 Oct 2024](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/98655): scene rendering fails when zfar > ~1e6 × znear (fixed by PR #99986), camera far was limited to 1,000,000 in the editor (PR #100896), directional shadows failing in large worlds (#81877), shadow NaNs with very long light ranges (PR #100319), `VIEW` NaN at galactic distances, SSAO banding above zfar 5000 (#42390), DOF/SSS/SSR failing at extreme far/near ratios (PRs #99755, #99693). The fork's `Camera3D.far` default is 4000 m with an editor hint of 0.01–4000 "or_greater" (`doc/classes/Camera3D.xml:183`, `scene/3d/camera_3d.cpp:758`).
  - [Proposal #15350 "Add SceneTree.rebase_world_origin() for floating-origin support", 9 Aug 2026, closed as not planned](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/15350): asked for `rebase_world_origin(delta, group)` shifting every grouped `Node3D` by `-delta` and emitting `world_origin_rebased`; rationale "every project that needs it hand-rolls the same pattern". Closure comments could not be retrieved.
  - [Proposal #15436 "Provide functions for teleporting VFX (particles and trails) to support floating-origin worlds", JimmyCushnie, 26 Aug 2026, open, "Needs consensus"](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/15436): lists what already teleports cleanly (`Node3D.global_position`, physics bodies via `RigidBody3D._integrate_forces` or `PhysicsServer3D.body_set_state`, render instances via `RenderingServer.instance_teleport`) and what does not: "there is currently no way to teleport a `GPUParticles3D` or `Trail3D`. Thus there are unavoidable visual issues when these effects are used with floating origin." Requested: `GPUParticles3D.teleport(offset)`, `RenderingServer.particles_teleport`, `Trail3D.teleport`.
  - Contribution: an origin-shift in Godot 4.8-dev is project code and works for nodes, bodies and mesh instances, with GPU particles and trails as the one open engine gap (Megaton's whole destruction look is GPU billboard particles, so this gap is not cosmetic for this design — inference).
- **Jolt's own guidance (the part pass one missed).** From the Jolt documentation, sections "Big Worlds" and "Space Simulations": "If all simulation takes place within roughly 5 km from the origin, floating point precision is accurate enough. If you have a bigger world, you may want to compile the library using the JPH_DOUBLE_PRECISION define. When you do this, all positions will be stored as doubles, which will make the simulation accurate even at thousands of kilometers away from the origin"; a naive all-double build "has been measured to run more than 2x slower", so Jolt "will only use doubles where necessary" and collision queries take a "base offset"; the net cost is "5-10% slower in double precision mode". Then the caveats: "The broadphase uses floats internally so will become less accurate at large distances from the origin"; "Jolt stores velocities in floats, so the large velocities that are common in space will become inaccurate. This will especially be visible if you create an object with constraints (e.g. a ragdoll) and make it move at high speeds. The relative velocities between the bodies will be too low for a float to represent accurately, which means that the constraints will not be solved properly"; "Rotations (Quat) are tracked in floats… it is not possible to rotate a RVec3 by a Quat"; the recommended pattern (as in X4: Foundations): "split the universe into multiple PhysicsSystems and keep objects in each PhysicsSystem close to the origin and with low velocities. E.g.: A ship that is traveling near light speed can happen in a PhysicsSystem that is traveling at near light speed. The ship would be near static in this PhysicsSystem"; planets are static in their own system with rotation "applied as an additional matrix transform when rendering". Jolt's recommended envelope is bodies of "[0.1, 10] meters", speeds "[0, 500] m/s", statics "[0.1, 2000] meter" — [Jolt Physics documentation, Jorrit Rouwé](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html). The fork wires this correctly: `if env["precision"] == "double": env_jolt.Append(CPPDEFINES=["JPH_DOUBLE_PRECISION"])` (`modules/jolt_physics/SCsub:156-157`, engine version 4.8.0 dev per `version.py`). Godot's `using_jolt_physics.rst` mentions precision only for the bounded `WorldBoundaryShape3D`. Contribution and consequence for this design: the double build fixes positions but not velocities; an active ragdoll flown at Mach 8 (≈2,744 m/s, my arithmetic) is 5.5× outside Jolt's speed envelope and Godot's default 500 m/s cap, and Jolt says constraint solving degrades exactly for that case. The engine-free remedy is the Krakensbane/X4 one: keep the ragdoll's physics space at rest and move the world (or subtract the player's bulk velocity from everything in the space each tick), using Godot's existing multi-space API; raising `max_linear_velocity` alone is the wrong fix (inference from the cited Jolt text).

### 3.2 Planet-scale terrain LOD and streaming (academic and studio sources)

- **Geometry clipmaps.** "Rendering throughput has reached a level that enables a novel approach to level-of-detail (LOD) control in terrain rendering. We introduce the geometry clipmap, which caches the terrain in a set of nested regular grids centered about the viewer. The grids are stored as vertex buffers in fast video memory, and are incrementally refilled as the viewpoint moves… Our main dataset is a 40GB height map of the United States… compressed… by a remarkable factor of 100… As the viewer approaches the surface, we synthesize grid levels finer than the stored terrain using fractal noise displacement… interactive flight at 60 frames/sec" — Losasso & Hoppe, "Geometry Clipmaps: Terrain Rendering Using Nested Regular Grids", ACM TOG 23(3):769–776, SIGGRAPH 2004, DOI 10.1145/1015706.1015799 — [PDF](https://hhoppe.com/geomclipmap.pdf). Contribution: a viewer-centred structure that is inherently floating-origin friendly and GPU-resident; flat-plane, so it needs a spherical variant for planets.
- **Spherical/ellipsoidal clipmaps.** Clasen & Hege 2006 "Terrain Rendering using Spherical Clipmaps" (EuroVis 2006) — record could not be retrieved (dblp/S2 blocked); listed as a known title only. A later record: Dimitrijević & Rančić, "Ellipsoidal Clipmaps – A planet-sized terrain rendering algorithm", Computers & Graphics 51:43–61, 2015, DOI 10.1016/j.cag.2015.06.006 (Crossref record-only).
- **BDAM / P-BDAM.** "We describe an efficient technique for out-of-core management and interactive rendering of planet sized textured terrain surfaces. The technique, called P-Batched Dynamic Adaptive Meshes (P-BDAM), extends the BDAM approach by using as basic primitive a general triangulation of points on a displaced triangle… we guarantee overall geometric continuity, exploiting programmable graphics hardware to cope with the accuracy issues introduced by single precision floating points; we exploit a compressed out of core representation and speculative prefetching for hiding disk latency" — Cignoni, Ganovelli, Gobbetti, Marton, Ponchio, Scopigno, IEEE Visualization 2003, DOI 10.1109/VISUAL.2003.1250366 — [PDF](http://www.crs4.it/vic/data/papers/ieeeviz03-pbdam.pdf); demonstrated on Mars MOLA data at 128 samples/degree, 253K triangles at 1-pixel screen tolerance. Companion: BDAM, Computer Graphics Forum 22(3), 2003, DOI 10.1111/1467-8659.00698 (PDF link 404; record-only). Contribution: the earliest explicit treatment of single-precision accuracy on a planet-sized mesh, solved on the GPU with per-patch local frames.
- **Chunked LOD.** Thatcher Ulrich, "Rendering Massive Terrains using Chunked Level of Detail Control", SIGGRAPH 2002 course "Super-size it! Scaling up to Massive Virtual Worlds": a quadtree of independent static chunks, geomorphs between levels, streaming from disk, ~2-pixel screen-space error at runtime; Puget Sound 16K×16K at 4 m tolerance → ~214 MB chunk file, ~30 fps on a 1 GHz P3 with GeForce2Go — [tulrich.com/geekstuff/chunklod.html](http://tulrich.com/geekstuff/chunklod.html) (the course-notes PDF at tulrich.com/geekstuff/sig-notes.pdf downloaded but its font encoding defeated text extraction). Contribution: the simplest production pattern; static chunks are exactly what a floating origin can translate wholesale.
- **Planetary composition on the GPU.** Kooima, Leigh, Johnson, Roberts, Subbarao, DeFanti, "Planetary-Scale Terrain Composition", IEEE TVCG 2009, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2009.43: "a GPGPU process to tessellate spherical height fields… a render-to-vertex-buffer technique to operate upon polygonal surface meshes in image space" (abstract via Semantic Scholar; thesis version open access on figshare per the record).
- **Studio talks (description-only).** Asobo's Lionel Fuentes, "Designing the Terrain System of 'Flight Simulator': Representing the Earth", GDC 2022 Advanced Graphics Summit, members-only: "more than 2 petabytes of data are streamed in real-time to the game client" from Bing Maps and Azure under "flexibility, scalability and real-world fidelity constraints" — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027581/Advanced-Graphics-Summit-Designing-the); [80.lv preview, Ana Kessler, 15 Mar 2022](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-designing-the-terrain-system-of-flight-simulator) ("hybrid procedural/real-world data methods"). Hello Games: Innes McKendrick, "Continuous World Generation in 'No Man's Sky'", GDC 2017, Programming track, free: pipeline "from voxel-based world generation, through polygonization and texturing, to eventual population and simulation", generating planets "continuously in real-time" — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024265/Continuous_World_Generation_in__No_Man_s_Sky_), [Game Developer video post, 9 Aug 2017](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/video-how-continuous-world-generation-works-in-i-no-man-s-sky-i-); Sean Murray, "Building Worlds Using Math(s)", GDC 2017, free — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024514/Building-Worlds-Using); Grant Duncan, "Art Direction Bootcamp: How I Learned to Love Procedural Art", GDC 2015, free — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1021805/Art-Direction-Bootcamp-How-I). None of the descriptions state precision or LOD numbers; the videos would have to be watched.
- **Godot mapping (inference unless cited).** None of these structures exist in Godot 4.8-dev; all are implementable without engine changes as project code: cube-sphere quadtree patches as `ArrayMesh`/`MeshInstance3D` with per-patch local origins (Outerra/P-BDAM pattern), or a clipmap ring set displaced in the vertex shader. What the engine supplies: double CPU transforms and the emulated-double instance transform path (PR #66178), reverse-Z depth (4.3), and the far-plane fixes tracked in #98655. What the engine does not supply and the design must budget: far-distance shadow behaviour (#81877 open), particle precision, and any streaming/prefetch layer (all custom).

### 3.3 Speed regimes: Elite's gravity-well rule, Megaton's automatic scaling, KSP's frames

- Elite (wiki-only): supercruise spans "29.9 kilometers-per-second to 2001c"; "The speeds which are possible in Supercruise are dependent on the distance of your ship from all nearby gravity wells"; players are taught a 75% throttle "blue sector" and a "7 seconds" countdown rule, and "flying next to a planet will slow you down considerably. The closer you are to the planet, the greater the effect" — [Supercruise, Elite Dangerous Wiki (Fandom), via API](https://elite-dangerous.fandom.com/wiki/Supercruise); Wikipedia: "supercruise up to 2000+ times the speed of light in a star system" and hyperspace between systems — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Dangerous). No Frontier designer statement was found before the search budget ran out (candidate venues in §6).
- Megaton: speed "adjusted automatically depending on a number of factors" ([AP2HYC 2015](https://ap2hyc.com/2015/04/interview-alfonso-del-cerro-lead-developer-of-megaton-rainfall/)); day side to night side "in half a minute" outside the atmosphere ([Cobra TV 2015](http://www.cobratvgnn.com/2015/12/megaton-rainfall-developer-interview.html)); a player workaround of climbing to orbit because "There will be no friction in space" ([Steam discussion, 2018](https://steamcommunity.com/app/430210/discussions/0/3051633726593106690/), player claim). Together these are consistent with, but do not prove, an altitude- or proximity-scaled cap plus atmospheric drag.
- KSP: two thresholds, one for position (floating origin) and one for velocity (Krakensbane) ([Squad devnote 2016](https://kerbaldevteam.tumblr.com/post/150703936094/devnote-tuesday-12-pre-release-the-bug-hunt-and)).
- Jolt: the regime *is* a reference frame — "A ship that is traveling near light speed can happen in a PhysicsSystem that is traveling at near light speed" ([Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html)).
- Godot mapping (inference): a max-speed curve keyed to distance from the nearest surface or gravity well is trivially GDScript; the engineering content is that each regime boundary should also be a *frame* boundary (switch the body's physics space and subtract bulk velocity) once speeds leave Jolt's 500 m/s envelope. Godot's fixed tick (`physics/common/physics_ticks_per_second`, default 60; "High tick rates give more accurate physics simulation, particularly for fast moving objects", fork `ProjectSettings.xml:2734-2736`) and `physics/common/physics_interpolation` (`:2722`) are the correct answer to the Megaton vsync-coupled speed bug.

### 3.4 Comfort at speed and the perception of speed

- **Dynamic FOV restriction has a peer-reviewed basis.** Fernandes & Feiner, "Combating VR sickness through subtle dynamic field-of-view modification", IEEE 3DUI 2016, DOI 10.1109/3DUI.2016.7460053, 450 Crossref citations (record-only). Wu & Suma Rosenberg, "Adaptive Field-of-view Restriction: Limiting Optical Flow to Mitigate Cybersickness in Virtual Reality", ACM VRST 2022, DOI 10.1145/3562939.3565611, and "Asymmetric Lateral Field-of-View Restriction to Mitigate Cybersickness During Virtual Turns", IEEE VR 2022, DOI 10.1109/VR51125.2022.00028 (record-only). Ubisoft's Eagle Flight talk (above) is the industry version. Megaton's blinders (2017) sit squarely in this lineage and were, per Real o Virtual, a Sony comfort requirement.
- **Perceived speed depends on where contrast is lost, not how much.** "Visual speed is believed to be underestimated at low contrast… we confirm that speed is underestimated when contrast is reduced uniformly for all objects… However, we show that when contrast is reduced more for distant objects, as is the case in real fog, visual speed is actually overestimated, prompting drivers to decelerate… perceived speed depends on the spatial distribution of contrast over the visual scene rather than the global level of contrast per se" — Pretto, Bresciani, Rainer, Bülthoff, "Foggy perception slows us down", eLife 2012, DOI 10.7554/eLife.00031 (abstract via Europe PMC). Contribution: distance-graded haze (atmospheric scattering, altitude fog) raises perceived speed for free; uniform desaturation or blur lowers it. Godot: `Environment` fog/volumetric fog is project configuration (inference).
- **Field of view and perceived speed.** Pretto, Ogier, Bülthoff, "Influence of the size of the field of view on motion perception", Computers & Graphics 33(2), 2009, DOI 10.1016/j.cag.2009.01.003 (record-only; abstract not retrievable this pass); Banton, Stefanucci, Durgin, "The Perception of Walking Speed in a Virtual Environment", Presence 14(4), 2005, DOI 10.1162/105474605774785262 (record-only). These are the standard citations for FOV-dependent speed underestimation in HMDs; the numbers must be taken from the papers.
- **Industry sources on speed feel.** Matthew Harris (Criterion), "Vehicle Feel Masterclass: Balancing Arcade Accessibility with Simulation Depth", GDC 2018, free: "a solid physical simulation with a whole lot of goodness on top", covering "how the camera moves, but also handling assists and more" across Need for Speed and Battlefront II X-wings — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025295/Vehicle-Feel-Masterclass-Balancing-Arcade) (description-only). Superflight: "Small nudges of the stick will result in precise and tiny movements, but there's a point on the pad where that gives way to large, sweeping turns"; "the whooshing of the wind make for a hectic descent"; developer aim: "a simple game that gives you the feeling of intense speed, high adrenaline moments and beautiful vistas" — [Kotaku, Luke Plunkett, 9 Nov 2017](https://kotaku.com/superflight-is-a-fast-game-about-dying-a-lot-1820280557). The Star Wars: Squadrons FOV figures that surfaced in a search summary (55° at rest to ~90° at boost) were **not** present in the cited Steam thread when opened — [thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1222730/discussions/0/2940245647595904292/) — and should not be used. General framework: Pichlmair & Johansen, "Designing Game Feel. A Survey", 2020 — [arXiv 2011.09201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09201) (abstract-only; no speed specifics).
- Godot mapping (inference): peripheral blinders and dynamic FOV are a full-screen shader plus per-frame `Camera3D.fov`; snap/quarter turns are input code; none touch the engine.

## 4. Contradictions found in the report or other pass-one notes

1. **Wrong citation for the velocity cap (report, Milestone 6).** "raise `limits/max_linear_velocity` ([Godot docs, Large world coordinates])" — the LWC page contains no such setting. The setting is `physics/jolt_physics_3d/limits/max_linear_velocity` (default 500 m/s, fork `doc/classes/ProjectSettings.xml:2772`). More importantly, Jolt's documentation says raising speed is the wrong lever for constrained bodies: velocities stay float, and ragdolls at high speed "will not be solved properly" — [Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html). Milestone 6 should read: co-moving physics space (Krakensbane pattern) plus a raised cap for unconstrained debris only.
2. **Mis-attributed speed figure (report, Megaton paragraph).** "reaching Mach 8 in about five seconds and later superluminal speeds ([AP2HYC]; [Gamecritics]; [UploadVR])" — none of the three contains the figure. Correct provenance: Mach 8 from [Cobra TV 2015](http://www.cobratvgnn.com/2015/12/megaton-rainfall-developer-interview.html) (developer), "5 seconds" from Wikipedia quoting the IGF 2015 entry (unopened), superluminal from the Fandom wiki (wiki-only). `progression_and_gating_design.md:305` has the same figure correctly attributed to Wikipedia.
3. **Loose paraphrase of the Godot table (report and pass-one note).** "double remains acceptable beyond 262,144 units" is not what the page says; it says double "remains far more precise than single-precision past this value", and its acceptability statement is the 8,192 × 8,192 m on-foot area for single precision. Pass one's "the docs describe no automatic floating-origin feature (inference)" should become "the docs name origin shifting as the low-end alternative and decline to detail it".
