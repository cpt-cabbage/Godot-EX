### 2.7 GDC talks on jump math and rigid-body or swept character controllers

## 4. Contradictions found in the report or other pass-one notes

1. **Jolt `CharacterVirtual` numbers applied to Godot's `CharacterBody3D`.** The pass-one note's inference (lines 150-151) recommends driving the player with "`CharacterVirtual`-style kinematic movement (Godot `CharacterBody3D` under Jolt)" and calls Jolt's 0.4 m step-up, 0.5 m stick-to-floor, 75° cone and 2 cm padding "already the step handling answer". The pass-one Godot/Jolt note states the opposite fact: "**CharacterVirtual is not exposed.** The only hit for `CharacterVirtual` in the module is the build list entry" (`godot_jolt_implementation.md:45`), which this pass re-confirmed (`grep -rn CharacterVirtual modules/jolt_physics/` matches only `SCsub:55`). `CharacterBody3D::move_and_slide` (`scene/3d/physics/character_body_3d.cpp:43`) runs Godot's own algorithm on `body_test_motion` with Godot defaults (45° floor limit, 0.1 m snap, 0.001 margin, 6 slides, 15° wall slide angle, no stair step). The report does not repeat the wrong sentence, but it lists `CharacterVirtual.h` in the bibliography (line 294) as if it were applicable. Fix: either bind `CharacterVirtual` in the fork (then the Jolt defaults apply) or state Godot's defaults.
2. **Report line 105, view punch wording.** "rolls the view by `0.013° per u/s` capped at 8° pitch" conflates two statements: Source sets `PunchAngle[ROLL] = fallVelocity × 0.013` with no cap, then clamps `PunchAngle[PITCH]` to 8 (a value the landing code never sets). Same in GoldSrc (`pm_shared.c:2735-2739`). The roll for a fatal 922.5 u/s landing is 12° and is not clamped.
3. **Report line 145, "Source's defaults are ... `sv_maxspeed 320` (8.1 m/s ...)".** True as a movevar, but HL2's character runs at `hl2_normspeed 190` (4.8 m/s) and only sprints at 320 (`hl2_player.cpp:86-88`). Presenting 320 as "the" Source speed overstates baseline Source locomotion by 68 %; the report's H0 human sprint (7.35 m/s) is close to HL2's sprint, not to its run.
4. **Report line 143, Human: Fall Flat citation.** "each hand on a mouse button, arms raised by the look direction ([Human: Fall Flat, Wikipedia])" — the Wikipedia article says only "using both his arms and looking with his head"; it does not mention mouse buttons. The claim is correct per Worthplaying ("Using your mouse buttons or triggers, you can independently stick out your arms ... so long as those buttons are pressed"; "your right stick is responsible for your camera and where your arms are pointing") but is mis-cited.
5. **Pass-one note, Quake III landing inference (KQ4 Inferences, line 182).** "ordinary jumps make a footstep, not a 'short fall'" is wrong; 270 u/s gives `delta = 7.29 > 7`, so EV_FALL_SHORT fires on a flat jump (section 1a, last row).
6. **Pass-one note, Source fall constants presented as universal (KQ4 Cited Findings, line 167).** The 922.5/526.5/173/303 set is HL2-only (`#ifdef HL2_DLL`); other Source games use 1024/580/200/350. The report's "safe about 20 ft and fatal about 60 ft" is correct for both.
7. **Brief's presumptions corrected by sources.** Pittman's talk is GDC 2016, not 2014; the Mirror's Edge GDC 2009 speakers were Jonas Åberg and Tobias Dahl, not Erik Odeldahl or Tom Farrer; no Boneworks GDC 2020 talk exists in the Vault listing; the "Physics for Game Programmers: Character Controllers" content lives inside Catto's 2014 "Understanding Constraints" session.
8. **Titanfall Haggerty slide quote.** The pass-one sentence "A slide is mostly just regular ground-walking movement except you can't stop or change direction as easily, and it gives you a little boost when you start it" could only be partially re-verified this pass (the re-fetch summary rendered the passage as "It's all the same code, plus some minor tweaks").
