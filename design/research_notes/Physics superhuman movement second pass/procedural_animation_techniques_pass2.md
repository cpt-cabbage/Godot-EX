In this thesis the performance aspects for procedurally generating animation of locomotion within real-time constraints is evaluated, for bipeds and quadrupeds, and for simulations of several characters. A general pose-driven feedback algorithm for physics-driven character locomotion is implemented for this purpose. Methods. The execution time of the locomotion algorithm is evaluated using an automated experiment process, in which real-time gait simulations of incrementing character population count are instantiated and measured, for the bipedal and quadrupedal gaits. The simulations are measured for both serial and parallel executions of the locomotion algorithm. Results. Simulations of up to and including 100 characters are performance measured providing an overview of the slowdown rate when increasing the character count in the simulations, as well as the performance relations between bipeds and quadrupeds. Conclusions. The experiment concludes that the evaluated algorithm on its own exhibits a relatively small performance impact that scales almost linearly for the evaluated population sizes. Due to the relatively low performance impacts it is thus also concluded that for future experiments a broader measurement of the locomotion algorithm that includes and compares different physics solvers is of interest" — [CORE](https://api.core.ac.uk/v3/search/works?q=title:%22Performance%20of%20Physics-Driven%20Procedural%20Animation%22&limit=3).

- Citation: A. Aristidou, J. Lasenby, "FABRIK: A fast, iterative solver for the Inverse Kinematics problem", Graphical Models 73(5):243–260, 2011; received 12 Feb 2010, accepted 9 May 2011 — [PDF via r.jina.ai](http://www.andreasaristidou.com/publications/papers/FABRIK.pdf) (18 pages).

The pass-one "standard formulation" of the algorithm is therefore confirmed against the paper.

### 2.3 Buss 2004/2009 IK survey — formulas and timings (retrieved in full)
- Citation: Samuel R. Buss, "Introduction to Inverse Kinematics with Jacobian Transpose, Pseudoinverse and Damped Least Squares methods", UCSD, dated "October 7, 2009" in the current PDF, with the note "originally written for a paper by Buss and Kim [7]" (the April 2004 SDLS manuscript) — [PDF via r.jina.ai](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/ikmethods/iksurvey.pdf) (19 pages). So the report's "April 2004" date belongs to the SDLS draft; the survey PDF itself is dated 2009.
- Formulas verbatim: update θ := θ + Δθ (3); Jacobian transpose Δθ = α Jᵀ e; pseudoinverse Δθ = J† e (6), and when J has full row rank Δθ = Jᵀ(JJᵀ)⁻¹ e (9); damped least squares minimises ‖JΔθ − e‖² + λ²‖Δθ‖², giving Δθ = (JᵀJ + λ²I)⁻¹Jᵀ e (10) = Jᵀ(JJᵀ + λ²I)⁻¹ e (11); SVD form τᵢ = σᵢ/(σᵢ² + λ²) so DLS "tends to act similarly to the pseudoinverse method away from singularities and effectively smooths out the performance of pseudoinverse method in the neighborhood of singularities" (eqs 13–14). On λ: "should [be] large enough so that the solutions for Δθ are well-behaved near singularities, but if it is chosen too large, then the convergence rate is too slow."
- Practical recommendations: clamp each component of e to a maximum length D_max ("ClampMag"), which "allows the use of a smaller damping constant"; "it is almost always a good idea to clamp the maximum angle change in a single update"; Jacobian transpose "fast, but of poor quality" for multiple effectors yet "work[s] well for a system with a single end effector"; pure pseudoinverse "worked very poorly whenever the target positions were out of reach".
- Timings (custom C++, 2.8 GHz Pentium, one update per step): Y-shape (J is 6×7) Jacobian transpose 1.1 µs, DLS 2.2 µs; double-Y (12×16) 6.5 µs vs 18.5 µs.
- Relevance: Godot's `JacobianIK3D` is Jacobian-transpose based (local doc), i.e. the cheapest and least accurate member of this family; its `angular_delta_limit` is exactly Buss's "clamp the maximum angle change" recommendation.

### 2.6 Sprint cadence and stride at top speed (closed)
- Usain Bolt, three fastest 100 m races (9.69, 9.58, 9.63 s): mean velocity 10.38 m/s, "Stride frequency [Hz]" 4.24/4.23/4.29 (mean 4.25), "Number of strides" 41.1/40.92/41.4 (mean 41.13), "Stride length [m]" 2.43/2.47/2.41 (mean 2.44) — the paper's "stride" is a single step; other finalists 44.45–45.69 steps at 4.38–4.55 Hz and 2.19–2.25 m; Bolt's peak 10-m-section speeds "over 12 m/s (12.05 – 12.34 m/s)"; in the 60–80 m sections step length 2.63–2.77 m at 4.39–4.63 Hz — [Krzysztof & Mero 2013, J Hum Kinet 36:149–160, DOI 10.2478/hukin-2013-0015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3661886/).
- Spring–mass characteristics, Berlin 2009 60–80 m split: Bolt 12.3 m/s, contact time 0.091 s, step frequency 4.49 Hz, vertical stiffness 355.8 kN/m, leg stiffness 21.0 kN/m, both "significantly lower than his competitors" — [Taylor & Beneke 2012, Int J Sports Med 33(8):667–670, DOI 10.1055/s-0032-1306283](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Taylor%20Beneke%202012%20spring%20mass%20fastest%20men&resultType=core&format=json).
- Combined with Leacox (2.97 Hz cadence ≈ 1.48 steps/s per leg at 3.83 m/s) and Weyand (swing time constant), the human envelope is: cadence rises from ~2.8 Hz (jog) to ~4.3–4.5 Hz (elite sprint) while step length rises from ~1.3 m to ~2.4–2.8 m and contact time falls from 0.24 s to 0.09 s. Above ~4.5 Hz there is no human data; the extrapolation rule in the report (hold swing time, shorten contact, lengthen flight) is an inference, not a measurement.

### 2.7 Walk–run transition determinants (closed)
- Hreljac 1993: 20 adults; preferred transition speed (PTS) 2.06 m/s, energetically optimal transition speed 2.24 m/s; RPE higher walking at PTS (13.5) than running (10.0), "suggesting that the gait transition ... does not take place in order to minimize metabolic energy consumption" — [Hreljac 1993, Med Sci Sports Exerc, DOI 10.1249/00005768-199310000-00012](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=AUTH:%22Hreljac%20A%22%20AND%20PUB_YEAR:1993&resultType=core&format=json).
- Hreljac 1995: of four candidate kinematic determinants only maximum ankle angular velocity met all four criteria (maximum ankle angular acceleration close); hypothesis "gait transitions are effected to prevent overexertion of the dorsiflexor muscles" — [Hreljac 1995, J Biomech 28(6):669–677, DOI 10.1016/0021-9290(94)00120-S](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=AUTH:%22Hreljac%20A%22%20AND%20PUB_YEAR:1995&resultType=core&format=json).
- Segers 2007: 13 women, treadmill accelerating at ±0.1 m/s²; walk-to-run 2.16 ± 0.12 m/s, run-to-walk 2.19 ± 0.12 m/s; a distinct "transition step" plus a preparatory process in the last swing before WRT; spatiotemporal (flight phase), kinematic (knee flexion) and energetic (in-phase vs out-of-phase energy fluctuation) criteria coincide — [Segers et al. 2007, Gait & Posture 26(3):349–361, DOI 10.1016/j.gaitpost.2006.10.013](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Segers%202007%20transition%20walking%20running%20gradually%20changing%20speed&resultType=core&format=json).
- Abe 2019 (body-weight support): transition speeds are faster under 30 % BWS; "not triggered solely by the minimization of whole-body energy expenditure"; associated with reduced tibialis anterior and soleus activity — [Abe, Fukuoka & Horiuchi 2019, PeerJ 7:e8290](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6924320/).
- Cluster for the design: PTS 1.98 (Kram), 2.06 (Hreljac), 2.14 (Hansen), 2.16 (Segers) m/s; Fr 0.45–0.48 measured. The report's "near 2.1 m/s" is inside the cluster.

### 2.8 Alexander's relative-stride-length relation (closed indirectly)
- Alexander's trackway formula as restated: U = 0.25 g^0.5 l_stride^1.67 h^−1.17, and "gaits will shift from walking to a bouncing gait (e.g. trotting) when l_stride/h reaches 2.0, and the transition from trotting to running (or galloping) at an l_stride/h of 2.9" — [Marmol-Guijarro et al. 2020, Frontiers in Zoology 17:17, DOI 10.1186/s12983-020-00363-z](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7254686/). Rearranging that formula (my algebra) gives l_stride/h = 4^0.6 (U²/(g h))^0.3 ≈ 2.3 Fr^0.3, the usual statement of the dynamic-similarity stride relation; the paper also reports species-specific deviations of up to 55 % when Fr is used to predict speed, so treat it as a first-order design curve.
- Raichlen et al. 2013 confirm the DSH statement and its known violations (non-cursorial quadrupeds and primates take longer relative strides at the same Fr) and attribute deviations to swing-phase limb inertia — [Raichlen, Pontzer & Shapiro 2013, Biology Open 2:1032–1036](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3798186/).

## 3. New sources and findings

### 3.1 Hypogravity locomotion review — support (and limits) for the "effective gravity" scaling idea
- Lacquaniti et al. 2017 (Front Physiol 8:893, PMC5682019) restate dynamic similarity (Fr = v²/gL), note Fr ≈ 0.5 for the walk–run transition and Fr ≈ 0.25 for optimal walking, and summarise the hypogravity evidence: the preferred transition Fr rises as g falls (Kram 1997 data), which is explained "if one replaced the term g in the Froude equation with the summed contribution of gravity and downward acceleration due to swinging limbs" (citing Kram 1997 and Raichlen 2013); parabolic flights at 0.38 g (Mars) showed smaller energy fluctuations and longer step periods, in agreement with the inverted-pendulum model; Moon 0.16 g, Mars 0.38 g — [Lacquaniti et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5682019/).
- Contribution: the report's rule "blend gaits on Fr = v²/(g_eff·L) with g_eff = s·g" has an empirical analogue only in the g < 1 direction (Kram's Table 1: the Froude rule predicts transition speed within 11 % down to 0.4 g and fails at 0.2–0.1 g). For g_eff > g (a stronger character) there is no human or animal measurement; the rule should be labelled as an extrapolation of dynamic similarity.

### 3.2 Daniel Holden's 2021–2026 articles (all first-party, theorangeduck.com; dates from the archive page)
- **"Inverse Kinematics and Foot Locking" (30 Jul 2026)** — [article](https://theorangeduck.com/page/inverse-kinematics-foot-locking). Leg solve: (1) heel target = toe target + (heel − toe) of the input pose; (2) two-bone IK for hip/knee with a soft clamp on maximum extension (`saturation = 1 − exp(−max(targetLength − maxExtension + softening, 0)/...)`); (3) rotate the heel joint to orient the toe at the toe target; (4) optionally rotate the toe-end out of ground collisions. Runtime lock: per-contact state {position, velocity, inputPosition, inputVelocity, offsetPosition, offsetVelocity, time, contact, locked}; when `inputContact && distance < lockDistance` lock to the toe position snapped to `contactHeight` and start a **cubic** inertialization (`InertializeCubicTransition`, Hermite weights over `blendTime`); unlock when `!inputContact || distance > unlockDistance`. Contact labelling: threshold toe **velocity** ("between 0.5 m/s and 0.1 m/s tends to be a pretty reasonable velocity threshold") with a height sanity check ("0.1 m is a pretty reasonable height threshold"), then a majority-vote/median filter "a width of 5 frames at 60Hz", optional Gaussian smoothing; at 30 Hz running contacts "can often be only 1 or 2 frames", so keep data at 60 Hz. Philosophy: "Foot sliding is about velocity" and "The toe is what needs locking" ("90% of the time it's the area of the foot attached to the toe joint which is actually in contact"). Contribution: replaces the 2021 half-life inertializer in the report's foot-lock paragraph with a fixed-blend-time cubic version and gives the missing numeric thresholds.
- **"Perfect Tracking with Springs" (25 Jan 2023)** — [article](https://theorangeduck.com/page/perfect-tracking-springs). A tracking spring blending acceleration, velocity and position of the input: `v = lerp(v, v + a_goal·dt, a_gain); v = lerp(v, v_goal, v_gain); v = lerp(v, (x_goal − x)/dt, x_gain); x += dt·v`, with gains 1.0 / 0.2 / 0.01 at 60 fps; skip the acceleration/velocity terms on frames known to contain a discontinuity, or clamp them; half-life variant 0.0 / 0.05 / 1.0 s; "not in any way robust to varying timesteps"; the appendix shows it is "equivalent to our normal spring-damper with specifically tuned stiffness, damping, goal position, and goal velocity". Contribution: a filter that removes discontinuities in a physics-derived target signal while reproducing it exactly elsewhere — directly applicable to feeding a physics-rig pose into visual bones.
- **"Cubic Interpolation of Quaternions" (25 Apr 2023)** — [article](https://theorangeduck.com/page/cubic-interpolation-quaternions). Hermite basis w0 = 2x³ − 3x² + 1, w1 = 3x² − 2x³, w2 = x³ − 2x² + x, w3 = x³ − x²; Catmull-Rom velocities by central difference; quaternion version works on the scaled-angle-axis of the quaternion difference to p0 and mixes it with angular velocities. Contribution: the concrete math for Rosen's "bicubic interpolation" between sparse poses with continuous angular velocity.
- **"Dead Blending" (25 Feb 2023) and "Dead Blending Node in Unreal Engine" (13 Aug 2023)** — [article](https://theorangeduck.com/page/dead-blending). Extrapolate the source pose forward (positions + dt·v, rotations by `quat_from_scaled_angle_axis(dt·ω)`) and cross-fade to the destination with smoothstep over `blendtime`; `quat_slerp_via` keeps the blend from "switching sides"; only needs the current pose and velocity ("something which is not always easy in all existing animation systems"), but "end[s] up damping out some of the details of the destination clip". Contribution: the transition method to use when the ragdoll hands control back and the source pose's velocity is the only thing known.
- **"Inertial Easing" (22 Mar 2026)** — [article](https://theorangeduck.com/page/inertial-easing). Re-fits a smoothstep easing function (with negative section) by solving for time t and scale s on each target change so velocity stays continuous and the goal is reached in a fixed time even when the target keeps moving; attributed to the Japanese programmer/artist Baku. Contribution: a fixed-duration alternative to half-life springs for UI-like camera/arm offsets.
- **"New Movement Model" (7 Jan 2026)** — [article](https://theorangeduck.com/page/new-movement-model). Holden, Caleb Longmire and Jack Potter's "Smooth Walking Mode" for Unreal's Mover (UE 5.7, used by the Game Animation Sample Project): constant acceleration/deceleration toward a desired velocity, a `directional_acceleration` mix that reproduces the old Character Movement Component behaviour, a `turn_strength` term, and an optional spring stage (`simple_spring_damper_exact(state.vel, state.acc, track_vel, vel_halflife, dt)`) tracking ahead in time; the author warns it "is in no way delta-time invariant". Contribution: a 2026 production reference for the intent capsule's velocity model (outside this note's scope, flagged for the movement-controller note).
- **"Joint Limits" (6 Nov 2021)** — [article](https://theorangeduck.com/page/joint-limits): data-driven kinematic joint limits as projection functions (rectangular, elliptical, k-DOP), swing–twist decomposition, softening, use inside IK and look-at. Contribution: how to keep procedural IK targets inside anatomical ranges before the physics rig tries to reach them.
- **"Let's talk about Animation Quality" (2 Oct 2024)** — [article](https://theorangeduck.com/page/animation-quality): AAA reference data is "sampled at 120 Hz, with finger and toe motions, captured with an optical system which can triangulate marker positions with sub-mm accuracy". Contribution: sets the bar the procedural system is judged against.
- **"Control Operators for Interactive Character Animation" (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025)** — [page](https://theorangeduck.com/page/control-operators-interactive-character-animation): designer-composable control inputs for neural controllers, demonstrated on a flow-matching model and a Learned Motion Matching variant. Contribution: current state of learned controllers at Epic; not needed for the procedural path.

## 4. Contradictions found in the report or other notes

1. **Report misattributes Spore's 0.2 ms to IK.** The report says "Spore's whole 25-body particle IK at ~0.2 ms on a 2004 laptop". Hecker 2008 says 0.2 ms per frame is the cost of "a procedurally animating character with 25 bodies" with "Approximately 35% of the time ... spent in the IK solver" (≈0.07 ms), on a "1.7Ghz Pentium-M"; the year "2004" appears nowhere in the paper — [Hecker et al. 2008 §6](https://www.chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf). The pass-one note had it right.
2. **Pass-one note wrongly accuses Bollo's supplemental of a dropped factor.** The supplemental's eq (7) carries the ÷2 over the polynomial part; A, B, C are defined without it.
