## 2. Gaps closed

### 2.1 Hodgins, Wooten, Brogan, O'Brien 1995, "Animating human athletics" (SIGGRAPH 95; [arXiv 2302.06108](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.06108); [Berkeley PDF](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Hodgins-AHA-1995-08/Hodgins-AHA-1995-08.pdf); [DOI 10.1145/218380.218414](https://doi.org/10.1145/218380.218414))
- Models: runner 17 body segments, 30 controlled DOF (two-part feet); gymnast 15 segments, 30 DOF; bicyclist 15 segments, 22 DOF; mass and inertia from polygonal models with cadaver density data; equations of motion generated by a commercial package (SD/FAST); contacts modelled as constraints with Baumgarte stabilization (lines 215-275).
- Running control: state machine on flight/loading/heel contact/heel-and-toe/toe contact/unloading; "the active leg is swung forward in anticipation of touchdown"; foot placement eq. (1)-(2) quoted in §1.4 (Raibert law with heel-to-ball term l_f and speed-error gain k); ground speed matching by swinging the hip further forward in flight and back before touchdown; "During stance, the knee acts as a passive spring"; vertical thrust from the ankle after 30-50 % of expected stance duration; stance-hip PD servo controls body roll/pitch/yaw ("desired angle for pitch is inclined slightly forward"); idle-leg mirroring α_d = α_lo − (β_d − β_lo) to cancel net torque; per-joint PD τ = k(θd − θ) + kv(θ̇d − θ̇) with desired values fed as trajectories from the current value (lines 296-446). No numeric gains are printed.
- Balance for the gymnast landing: the COM passes over the centre of the support polygon; COM height 0.9 m used (line 533, 572-580).
- Use for the game: this is the canonical hand-authored running controller (foot placement + stance-hip attitude servo + ankle thrust); the l_f term is the correction to add to the Coros IPM when the stance foot is not a point.

### 2.2 Ha, Ye, Liu 2012, "Falling and landing motion control for character animation" (ACM TOG 31(6); [PDF via Ha's page](https://www.dropbox.com/s/6qh29mlcty5xqqb/2012_landing.pdf) linked from [faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~sha9](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~sha9/); [DOI 10.1145/2366145.2366174](https://doi.org/10.1145/2366145.2366174))
- Architecture: airborne phase + landing phase (impact, rolling, getting-up); two landing strategies, hands-first (aesthetic, planar momentum only) and feet-first (robust to arbitrary momentum, has an extra "thrusting" step).
- Landing condition (learned by sampling the landing controller): θ(T) = a·v_y(T) + b·v_z(T) + c·ω_x(T) + d; feet-first a = −0.01, b = −0.06, c = −0.03, d = 0.45; hands-first same a, b, c with d = 3.08 (angles in rad, velocities m/s, rad/s).
- Airborne control: PD-track a sequence current pose → intermediate pose q* held Δt* → landing pose, chosen by minimising the landing-condition error with a 6-DOF rigid proxy whose inertia interpolates over Δt_C = 0.1 s; 16 candidate poses picked from a range-of-motion mocap sequence to maximise inertia diversity; 1-D Fibonacci search over Δt; replanning stops 0.3 s before ground contact.
- Landing control: virtual force f_v = m·c̈ applied through Jᵀ at contact points (opposite sign on contacting bodies), total virtual force capped at 3000 N; vertical law c̈_y = ½(ċ̄_y² − ċ_y²)/(c̄_y − c_y) (constant deceleration to a COM height target), horizontal law c̈_x/z = k_v(ċ̄_x/z − ċ_x/z), rolling law c̈_x/z = k_p(c̄_x/z − c_x/z) toward the left foot; PID joint tracking with contacting limbs as "viscous dampers (PID control with a zero spring coefficient)", k_i = 50 on contacting limbs, 0 elsewhere; chin tucked by PID.
- Table 1 parameters: kp/kd hip 90/20, lower spine 300/60, upper spine 180/40, neck 10/2, knee 60/13, ankle 15/6, clavicle 180/40, shoulder 120/27, elbow 60/13.5, wrist 9/4; c̄_y = 0.4 m, ċ̄_y = 0 m/s, ċ̄_x/z = 4.0 m/s, k_v = 500, k_p (eq. 8) = 800, ω_MAX = 3.3 rad/s; right-knee target during roll θ_R = max((1 − ω_x/ω_MAX)π, 0).
- Model and performance: 164 cm, 59 kg, 49 DOF; ODE with a **0.2 ms** time step; airborne optimisation at 50 Hz; 550 frames per second on one 3.20 GHz core; tested from heights up to 10.6 m with velocities up to 6 m/s and 8.7 rad/s (Table 2); on ice the character rolls but cannot stand; elastic floors produce tumbling; a passive ragdoll "caused much more damage on the neck and the spine" than the controlled landing (joint stress = joint constraint force).
- Use for the game: this is the complete "fall tolerance" verb: the airborne inertia planner, the landing condition, the constant-deceleration virtual force and the roll-to-feet sequence, with concrete gains. Its 0.2 ms step is another explicit-torque data point for the tick-rate debate.

### 2.3 Ha & Liu 2015, "Multiple contact planning for minimizing damage of humanoid falls" (IROS 2015; [PDF via Ha's page](https://www.dropbox.com/s/s50almtargco17c/2015_falling.pdf))
- Plans an optimal sequence of contact points that dissipates the initial momentum with minimal impact, from "a single step to recover a gentle nudge, to a rolling motion to break a high-speed fall", by transforming the fall into a sequence of inverted-pendulum problems solved by dynamic programming; validated in simulation and on a BioloidGP robot. Cites Ukemi-inspired fall strategies. Use: the unified "when do I step, when do I roll" decision the report's ladders need.

### 2.4 Reil & Husbands 2002 (the open companion of Reil & Massey 2001; [Sussex PDF](https://users.sussex.ac.uk/~philh/pubs/reilhusbandsIEEE.pdf); [DOI 10.1109/4235.996015](https://doi.org/10.1109/4235.996015))
- The PDF cited in the report's bibliography is reachable (10 pages). Biped: MathEngine SDK 1.0.5, "Thirteen MathEngine bodies and 11 joints", hips 2 DOF (pitch/roll), knees 1 DOF, six DOF total, no feet or ankles, sphere-plane contacts of radius 8 cm; actuators are PD controllers "essentially equivalent to damped torsional springs" whose desired angle is what the network outputs; spring and damping constants "found to be appropriate" by manual experimentation (the numeric values are inside an equation image that neither PyMuPDF nor pypdf extracts); joint limits enforced by constraining the desired-angle range (Table I). Controller: 10 fully interconnected CTRNN neurons, time step 0.02 s, weights [−16, 16], time constants [0.5, 5], biases [−4, 4]; population 50, rank selection with fittest fraction 0.5, no crossover; fitness = distance from origin plus a penalty for lowering the centre of gravity below a threshold; stable walkers among the top individuals of 100 runs. Reil & Massey 2001 itself remains unopened (Springer redirects to an authorisation endpoint; the publisher elides the abstract from Semantic Scholar).

### 2.5 Pratt, Carff, Drakunov, Goswami 2006, "Capture Point" (Humanoids 2006; [PDF, Atkeson's CMU mirror](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cga/legs/Pratt_Goswami_Humanoids2006.pdf); [DOI 10.1109/ICHR.2006.321385](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICHR.2006.321385))
- Closes the "exact capture-point equation" gap that pass one deferred to Missura et al.: for the Linear Inverted Pendulum the capture point is the footstep that zeroes the orbital energy, **x_capture = ẋ·sqrt(z_0/g)** (eq. 10), measured from the COM ground projection, with z_0 the constant COM height; with a flywheel body the unique point becomes a capture region bounded by the flywheel's torque/angle limits. This is the constant-height counterpart of Coros's constant-leg-length step d = v·sqrt(h/g + v²/4g²) (the two agree to first order in v). Koolen 2012 (Part 1) still could not be opened; its N-step capturability definitions were only seen via the pass-one abstract.

## 3. New sources and findings

Each entry states what it contributes to balance, landing, push recovery, ledge catch or wall collision for a hand-authored Godot/Jolt controller.

### 3.1 Sampling-based model-predictive control without training (Aalto lineage)
- **Hämäläinen, Eriksson, Tanskanen, Kyrki, Lehtinen 2014, "Online motion synthesis using sequential Monte Carlo"**, ACM TOG 33(4) (SIGGRAPH 2014), [preprint](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf), [DOI 10.1145/2601097.2601218](https://doi.org/10.1145/2601097.2601218). A 36-DOF humanoid (30 actuated + 6 root, 15 rigid bodies, ball joints plus hinge elbows/knees/ankles) balances in a ready stance, dodges balls and improvises get-up strategies "all without precomputation or training data". Control strategies are 4-control-point splines of target joint angles plus torque limits (136 variables); each sample is evaluated by forward-simulating ODE 0.12 with the direct "big matrix" LCP solver at **Δt = 1/30 s** (CFM 1e-5, ERP 0.2); joints are driven by ODE velocity motors with target velocity (q_target − q_current)/Δt and sampled torque limits 50-150 Nm (light model) or 50-200 Nm (heavy); planning horizon 2 s default (up to 4 s); N = 25 samples per frame gives about 20 fps on an i7-4930K (12 logical cores) and 6-10 fps on a 2012 MacBook Pro. Objective = damage avoidance (soft threshold on head/pelvis contact speed, t_d = 2.2, c = 2.5) × smoothness × max(balance, 1e-4 × get-up). Key engine note (Appendix): "the iterative solvers in ODE, Bullet Physics or PhysX engines were not stable enough for active characters except at very small time steps"; with the iterative solver "approximately similar quality" needed 1/120 s. Contribution: balance, push recovery and get-up in one cost function; evidence that engine-side velocity motors with force limits are the way to run an active character at game-sized steps.
- **Hämäläinen, Rajamäki, Liu 2015, "Online control of simulated humanoids using particle belief propagation"** (C-PBP), ACM TOG 34(4), [preprint](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf), [DOI 10.1145/2767002](https://doi.org/10.1145/2767002). Same 15-capsule 1.75 m, 70 kg model in ODE at 1/30 s with the direct solver ("the iterative solver ... requires a time step < 1/100s for stability in our case"), integrated with Unity 3D; control u = [30 motor target angular velocities, 5 force-limit categories], sampling bounds ω ∈ [−1.3π, 1.3π] rad/s and force limits [0, 300]; N = 32 samples per frame, planning horizon K = 1.2 s, 20-30 fps on a 6-core desktop (30 fps at N ≈ 24). Behaviours emerge from cost functions: balancing, "recovery from both small and extreme disturbances, taking emergent footsteps and flipping around in air to avoid damage", reaching with steps and stepping onto obstacles, and, directly relevant to wall collision, "the character places hands on the walls to stabilize" when balancing on a ball; a damage cost s_damage = 10000 when the head touches the environment (a proportional version made the character lean on the wall "using its forehead"). Contribution: the only classical-era source found that shows hand-on-wall support emerging from a balance cost; a practical recipe for a Jolt character where the physics engine's motors do the low-level torque work ("balancing can be implemented in many cases by simply setting ω_k = 0").
- **Rajamäki & Hämäläinen 2017, "Augmenting sampling based controllers with machine learning"** and **Eom, Han, Shin et al. 2019, "Model predictive control with a visuomotor system for physics-based character animation"** (ACM TOG, [DOI 10.1145/3360905](https://doi.org/10.1145/3360905)) continue this line; only bibliographic records were reached (Semantic Scholar), not full texts.
- **Khoshsiyar, Gou, Zhou, Andrews, van de Panne 2024, "PartwiseMPC: Interactive Control of Contact-Guided Motions"**, Computer Graphics Forum 43(8), [PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2024-partwiseMPC/2024-partwiseMPC.pdf), [DOI 10.1111/cgf.15174](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.15174). Predictive-sampling MPC on MuJoCo MPC with horizon H = 0.35 s and N = 45 or 90 parallel rollouts, driven by sparse "contact keyframes" (which body part touches what); demonstrates climbing on and off a table, into bed, sliding on a sofa; runs at 25-50 % of real time (slowdown G = 0.25-0.5). Contribution: the current hand-specified (no-training) route to ledge-catch-like whole-body contact tasks; shows the CPU cost of sampling MPC in 2024.

### 3.2 Sampling-based reconstruction and linear feedback (UBC/NUS lineage)
- **Liu, Yin, van de Panne, Shao, Xu 2010, "Sampling-based contact-rich motion control"** (SAMCON), ACM TOG 29(4), [PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf), [DOI 10.1145/1778765.1778865](https://doi.org/10.1145/1778765.1778865). Reconstructs open-loop control for a 50-DoF, 1.7 m character from mocap of walking, running, rolls and kip-up jumps "within minutes on small-scale clusters"; a forward roll is transformed into a dive roll. Contribution: authoring path for roll/landing clips without a learned policy.
- **Liu, Yin, van de Panne, Guo 2012, "Terrain Runner"**, ACM TOG 31(6), [PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2012-TOG-TerrainRunner/2012-TOG-TerrainRunner.pdf), [DOI 10.1145/2366145.2366173](https://doi.org/10.1145/2366145.2366173). Real-time physics-based parkour: running, jumping, speed-vaulting and drop-rolling from one mocap clip each, with reduced-order linear feedback laws, RBF continuation for parameterisation, transition controllers and "a simple online step-based planning algorithm". Contribution: the drop-roll and vault verbs as offline-authored, runtime-cheap linear feedback.
- **Liu, van de Panne, Yin 2016, "Guided learning of control graphs for physics-based characters"**, ACM TOG 35(3), [PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf), [DOI 10.1145/2893476](https://doi.org/10.1145/2893476). Control fragments "each typically 0.1s in length" with a linear feedback policy per fragment learned by regression on SAMCON state-action pairs; skills include walking, running, sharp turns, cartwheels, spin-kicks, flips and "getting up after falls"; multiple characters interact in real time. Contribution: the classical (regression, not RL) way to get robust get-up and acrobatic controllers.
- **Coros, Beaudoin, van de Panne 2009, "Robust task-based control policies for physics-based characters"**, ACM TOG 28(5), [PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2009-TOG-taskControl.pdf) (the paper behind the SIMBICON-CEF release): value-iteration policies over step-based SIMBICON controllers, real time, "can respond to significant physical interaction". Contribution: the step-level action vocabulary the CEF controller files encode.

### 3.3 Push recovery, stepping and balance decision rules (robotics and graphics, no training)
- **Stephens 2007, "Humanoid push recovery"**, IEEE-RAS Humanoids, [PDF](http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bstephe1/papers/humanoids07.pdf). Unifies ankle (CoP), hip (CMP, centroidal moment pivot) and stepping strategies with "analytic decision surfaces that are functions of reference points, such as the center of mass and center of pressure"; ω² = g/L; the boundaries are cosh/sinh expressions in the LIPM state with maximum ankle torque and hip-torque duration limits. Contribution: closed-form thresholds for "stiffen ankles / bend at the hips / take a step", exactly the switching logic the report's balancer needs before it calls the Coros step law.
- **Popovic, Goswami, Herr 2005, "Ground reference points in legged locomotion"**, IJRR 24(12), [PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cga/legs/Popovic_Goswami_Herr_IJRR_Dec_2005.pdf). Defines the CMP and shows that in human walking the CMP stays inside the support base with a mean CMP-ZMP separation of 14 % of foot length. Contribution: the hip-strategy quantity (CMP-ZMP distance) to monitor.
- **Wu & Zordan 2010, "Goal-directed stepping with momentum control"**, SCA 2010, [PDF](https://diglib.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/SCA.SCA10.113-118/113-118.pdf). Steps defined by position and duration, momentum objectives solved by multiobjective optimisation and inverse dynamics; "A novel supervisory routine automatically chooses when and where to step based on an analysis of the momentum conditions"; contrasted with inverted-pendulum step recovery. Contribution: protective-step trigger from momentum.
- **Yin, Pai, van de Panne 2005, "Data-driven interactive balancing behaviors"**, Pacific Graphics, [PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2005-pg-balance.pdf). Push modelled as an impulse, a momentum-based motion index selects a captured hip/arm/stepping response, then adapts it. Contribution: a cheap kinematic fallback for push responses.
- **Shiratori, Coley, Cham, Hodgins 2009, "Simulating balance recovery responses to trips"**, SCA 2009, [PDF](http://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/2009/8/sca2009_trip.pdf), [DOI 10.1145/1599470.1599475](https://doi.org/10.1145/1599470.1599475). Now read in full: elevating strategy for obstacle contact in early swing (5-25 % of stride), lowering strategy in late swing (55-75 %), either in mid swing (30-50 %); both begin with a passive reaction (swing ankle plantar-flexes, then knee flexes) before the active FSM+PD response; both use swing and support legs "to arrest the forward angular momentum". Contribution: the trip-over-a-ledge reaction rule and its phase thresholds.
- **Kenwright, "Watch Your Step: Real-time adaptive character stepping"** (2013 communication article, posted as [arXiv 2210.14730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.14730)). Inverted pendulum plus ankle-feedback torque plus variable leg length; "handle both small and large push forces even during terrain height variations" in real time "without key-framed data, any offline preprocessing, or on-line optimizations"; explicitly game-oriented and critical of the plain IP model (point feet force constant stepping, cannot resist small pushes without a step). Contribution: a minimal stepping controller written for games.
- **Kudoh, Komura, Ikeuchi 2006, "Stepping motion for a human-like character to maintain balance against large perturbations"**, ICRA 2006 (the QP push-recovery work SIMBICON compares against; not opened, cited in SIMBICON §7.2 and in the survey reference list).

### 3.4 Optimisation-era controllers (offline or precomputed, still no learning)
- **Muico, Lee, Popović, Popović 2009, "Contact-aware nonlinear control of dynamic characters"**, ACM TOG 28(3), [PDF](http://www.cs.washington.edu/homes/jovan/papers/muico-2009-cnc.pdf), [DOI 10.1145/1531326.1531387](https://doi.org/10.1145/1531326.1531387). Spacetime-optimised reference motions and controls, an online nonlinear quadratic regulator from Riccati equations, and an LCP that adapts predicted joint and ground forces to the current contacts. Contribution: high-fidelity tracking with anticipated contacts, at the price of per-motion offline optimisation.
- **Al Borno, de Lasa, Hertzmann 2013, "Trajectory optimization for full-body movements with complex contacts"**, IEEE TVCG 19(8), [PDF](http://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/spacetime/spacetime.pdf), [DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2012.325](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2012.325). Full-body trajectory optimisation without mocap, objectives applied over 0.5 s spacetime windows, contact locations not pre-specified. Contribution: offline authoring of get-up/landing references; not a runtime controller.
- **Sok, Kim, Lee 2007, "Simulating biped behaviors from human motion data"**, ACM TOG 26(3), [PDF](http://mrl.snu.ac.kr/research/ProjectSimulBiped/SimulBiped.pdf), [DOI 10.1145/1276377.1276511](https://doi.org/10.1145/1276377.1276511). Planar 12-link, 11-joint biped simulated in real time; ground spring-damper k_s = 27000 N/m, k_d = 675 Ns/m; off-line displacement-map optimisation makes mocap trackable by PD, with push-forward/push-backward examples. Contribution: 2D only; useful mainly as the origin of the "fix the reference so PD can track it" idea that Lee/Kim/Lee 2010 generalised (the 2010 PDF at mrl.snu.ac.kr timed out).
- **Kwon & Hodgins 2017, "Momentum-mapped inverted pendulum models for controlling dynamic human motions"**, ACM TOG 36(1):1-14, [DOI 10.1145/2983616](https://doi.org/10.1145/2983616). Crossref abstract: a "momentum-mapped IPM (MMIPM)" extracted from reference mocap in preprocessing; "the controller plans a desired motion, frame by frame, based on the current pendulum state and a predicted pendulum trajectory", yielding walking, running, turning, jumping and gymnastic manoeuvres from one framework. Full text not reached (Hanyang and CMU URL guesses 500/404).

## 4. Contradictions found in the report or other notes

1. **Report "Tick rate": "Classical PD controllers only worked at 0.1-0.5 ms steps"** contradicts two sources in this note. SIMBICON's own 3D biped ran in ODE at **0.005 s** with kp ≤ 1000 and kd = 0.1 kp ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf), 3D model paragraph), and Hämäläinen 2014/2015 ran 36-DOF active humanoids at **1/30 s** using ODE's solver-side velocity motors with force limits and the direct LCP solver ([2014 preprint](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf) §4.1; [2015 preprint](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf) §4). The 0.9-1.6 ms LocoTest bound is specific to the cartwheel controller's explicit torques at kd = 2·sqrt(kp). The report's conclusion (use Jolt's solver-side motors, 120 Hz) is supported by these sources; its premise should read "explicit script-side torque PD at cartwheel/SIMBICON-2D gains needs ~1 ms steps; solver-side motor drives have run active characters at 5-33 ms".
2. **Report "Control laws and gains" uses SIMBICON's "600 N forwards and 500 N backwards" 0.1 s pushes as milestone-one acceptance numbers for the 3D character.** Those figures are the 2D planar biped's (§7.1). The 3D controller's published tolerance is 0.4 s pushes at chest height of 340 N forward, 270 N backward, 330 N lateral one way and 240 N the other, with diagonals of (230,230), (220,−220), (−190,−190), (−190,190) N ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf) §7.2). In impulse terms: 3D 136 Ns forward / 108 Ns backward versus 2D 60 / 50 Ns. Coros 2010's 600 N × 0.1 s omnidirectional at 0.6 m/s is a 3D result and stands. The pass-one note has the same omission.
3. **Report "behaviour layer": "Impacts follow Zordan's recipe: on contact, drop the tracking gains for a 0.1-0.2 s reaction delay ... then ramp back" citing Zordan et al. 2005.** Zordan 2005 holds kp and kv fixed "for the entire transition" and applies the 0.1-0.2 s delay to the *switch of the desired pose* to the reaction clip ([Zordan 2005 PDF](http://strangepaths.com/forum/files/Zordan2005.pdf) lines 280-298). The temporary gain reduction is Zordan & Hodgins 2002 as summarised by the survey ([Geijtenbeek & Pronost 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03189.x)) and by SIMBICON's FEL section ("the gain is decreased ... and then increased again ... after a reaction-time delay (150ms)"). The design recipe is fine; the attribution should be split, and the report should note that Zordan 2005 is an offline pipeline (~1 minute per reaction, search = 70 % of compute), so only the delay and blend ideas transfer to runtime.
4. **"Fast run (one state, dwell 0.15 s ...)" in both the report and the pass-one note.** Table 1 lists the fast run under states "0,1" and the caption states that runs have two states ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf) Table 1).
5. **Report "Tick rate" proposes Stable PD for "virtual forces, gravity compensation, the balance law's feed-forward".** SPD is a reformulation of the joint PD tracking term (it needs the next-step target q̄_{n+1} and, for articulated bodies, q̈ from (M + K_d Δt) q̈ = ... using the mass matrix) ([Tan, Liu, Turk 2011](https://www.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf) §3); Jacobian-transpose virtual forces and gravity compensation are feed-forward terms with no PD structure, so SPD does not apply to them, and Tan et al. only demonstrate SPD inside a custom generalized-coordinate simulator, not as script torques on top of an engine's constraint solver (a gap pass one already flagged). The sourced engine-side equivalent is Hämäläinen's velocity-motor-with-force-limit parameterisation.
6. **Report: "The released CEF controller uses cd 0.55 / cv 0.30 sagittally and −0.30 / −0.30 coronally with 0.7 s states transitioning on footDown".** True for `fWalk.sbc` only; `bWalk.sbc` uses 0.50/0.20 and −0.50/−0.20 (the paper's values) and `iWalk.sbc` 0.30/0.30 and −0.30/−0.30 with stance-ankle feedback 0.15/0.20; "0.7 s" is a maximum dwell, not the step period ([SimbiconSourceCode.zip](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/SimbiconSourceCode.zip)).
7. **Omission that changes a design number:** the report writes F_V = k_V(V_d − V) without the value; Coros 2010 uses **k_V = 100 Ns/m** (and ω_max = 2 rad/s), fixed across all results ([Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf) "Parameter settings"). Ladder H's "self-arrest ... with raised k_V" therefore has a baseline to scale from.
8. **Pass-one note, Tan 2011:** "used kp up to 1e9 for a volleyball catch" should be "kp = 10^9 to enforce joint limits in the volleyball-hitting example".
9. **Pass-one note vs report bibliography:** the note lists the Sussex figshare record for Reil & Husbands 2002 as "metadata only, no file"; the report's link to the Sussex author-page PDF works (verified 200, 10 pages). Not a contradiction in content, but the note should point at the working PDF.
10. **Pass-one note, Kwon & Hodgins 2010 speed range:** the note's "1.5 to 5 m/s" matches the paper's introduction; the paper's own abstract says 3-5 m/s. Cite the body figure with that caveat.
