# Learning to Walk as the First Ability: Second-Pass Verification and Extension

Second pass over `design/research_notes/Physics based superhuman movement design/learning_to_walk_onboarding.md` (pass one) and the report section "A single competence scalar turns a toddler into a walker in the opening" in `design/reports/Physics based superhuman movement design.md`. Research date 2026-09-27.

**Access notes.** WebSearch worked for six queries, then the session budget ran out (200/200); everything after that came from direct fetches. PubMed abstracts were pulled through NCBI E-utilities (esearch/efetch), full texts through `efetch db=pmc` XML (Adolph 2012, Gimunová 2021, Kroneberg 2024, Sci Rep 2022, Rasman 2021, Tsuyuki 2023, Han/Ossmy/Cole/Lee/Hoch/Adolph papers, Taggart 2017, Marciniak 2026). arXiv PDFs (Learning to Get Up, Reda 2020, Heess 2017, Yu 2018, Rudin 2022, Haarnoja 2018, Smith 2022, Tan 2018, Hwangbo 2019, Lee 2020) and the SIMBICON PDF were downloaded with curl and text-extracted with PyMuPDF. The eLife PDF endpoint returned an empty file and the HTML returned 406; the Rasman full text was obtained from PMC8480973 instead. Both Sutherland 1980 PDF mirrors (analisedemarcha.com and the orthobullets free PDF surfaced by search) are the same 3-page text-layer scan; the age tables are images and no OCR tool (tesseract, ocrmypdf, easyocr) exists in this container, so they remain unrecovered. Fandom wikis were read through their MediaWiki `api.php` (Kingdom Come, Metal Gear, Sea of Thieves), which succeeded where the HTML pages returned 402/403; seaofthieves.wiki.gg returned 403 twice. web.archive.org and pybullet.org/Bullet forum returned no bytes through the proxy; archive.org/stream (Game Developer Magazine Sept 2007 OCR) worked. Semantic Scholar's search API returned empty results for every query (silent failure, probably rate limiting); OpenAlex and Crossref were used instead. Pages that returned only boilerplate (PC Gamer review roundup, Steam I Am Bread control thread, Polygon and GodisaGeek reviews, JBJS abstract page) are marked as not opened. "Wiki-only" marks facts whose only opened source is a community or Wikipedia page; "review-only" marks facts sourced solely from game journalism.

## 2. Gaps closed

**2.1 GIRP input map (primary source found).** Foddy's own release post: "GIRP is the sequel to QWOP. It's a game about climbing a cliff using all the letter keys on your keyboard. Press and hold the letter keys to reach for the rings, and use either SHIFT, CONTROL or the mouse button to haul yourself up... It's not as hard as QWOP" — [foddy.net blog, March 2011](https://www.foddy.net/blog/2011/03/girp/). Wired's 2011 piece adds Foddy's framing: QWOP makes "a deliberate disconnect between your intentions and the character's actions" whereas GIRP "maximizes the feeling of embodiment... You have to grip the keyboard just like you would cling to the cliff"; the score shows distance until you summit, then switches to time; Foddy summits in about 20 minutes — [Wired, Mark Brown, 29 Mar 2011](https://www.wired.com/2011/03/qwop-girp/). Jay is Games (review-only) confirms holding a letter grips, releasing lets go, Shift flexes whichever gripping arm, flexing is used to swing for horizontal reach, and far grips change their letters — [jayisgames.com](https://jayisgames.com/review/girp.php). GIRP is 2011, not 2012 as Kill Screen states ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIRP), wiki-only, consistent with the foddy.net post date).

**2.2 Sumotori Dreams' controller and controls.** No developer statement on the controller math was found (the Bullet forum thread could not be opened through the proxy; WebSearch confirmed no published interview exists). Two primary-adjacent sources were added. (a) The developer's site lists the actual inputs: "Player1: arrows, backspace, enter; Player2: wsad, shift, control", and the free version has a "time limit (about 7 secs)" — [gravitysensation.com/sumotori](http://www.gravitysensation.com/sumotori/). (b) Steve Theodore's "Pixel Pusher" column in Game Developer magazine, September 2007, is the best contemporary technical description: "all the motion is generated by a physics simulation, not by conventional keyed animation or motion capture"; "The wrestlers are rarely able to respond precisely enough to stay on their feet for long once the match begins — they are always just a tad too slow to compensate for uneven footing or an awkward pose"; "un-damped physics reactions occasionally send a wrestler floating into the air" — [Game Developer, Sept 2007, archive.org OCR](https://archive.org/stream/GDM_September2007/GDM_September2007_djvu.txt). That "a tad too slow to compensate" is a designer-grade description of delay-induced clumsiness and lines up with the Rasman delay endpoint. Wikipedia (wiki-only) adds the release history: Windows April 2007, iOS/Android 20 Mar 2014, Steam 3 May 2021 under the Gravitysensation label — [Wikipedia, Sumotori Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumotori_Dreams). Pouet lists the prod under the group "Archee" with the original t-online.hu download — [pouet.net search](https://www.pouet.net/search.php?what=sumotori&type=prod).

**2.3 Kojima's stated MGSV prologue intent (wiki-only, secondary).** The Metal Gear Wiki's "Hospital Escape" article, read through the MediaWiki API, attributes to Kojima's notes in the Piggyback Collector's Edition Guide art gallery that the prologue "serves to remind the player of the gaming experience in previous Metal Gear Solid episodes, and quickly have it evolve into something new, comparing Snake's crawling, crouching, and eventual standing to the theory of Man's evolution", tying it to Sahelanthropus' upright-stance theme; it also records a playable hospital-escape demo at GDC 2013 — [Metal Gear Wiki, Hospital Escape (API)](https://metalgear.fandom.com/wiki/Hospital_Escape). The Piggyback guide itself was not accessible; no interview quote was found in the six searches available.

**2.4 Quantified drunk-gait variability.** PubMed queries ("alcohol AND gait AND variability AND stride", "ethanol AND walking AND (step time OR stride time) AND variability", "alcohol intoxication AND gait AND spatiotemporal AND (BAC OR breath alcohol)", "alcohol AND (tandem gait OR walking) AND accelerometer AND intoxication") returned **no adult acute-intoxication study reporting stride-time CoV against BAC**. ## 3. New sources and findings

### 3.1 Infant locomotion after Adolph 2012 (what each contributes)

- **Falls are cheap and do not deter (Han & Adolph 2021).** 563 falls from 138 infants aged 13–19 months during free play: parent-reported notable falls limited to 33% of infants, medical attention to 2%; infants fussed after 4% of falls, caregivers showed concern after 8%, and infants were "back at play within seconds"; impact was mitigated by "effective reactive behaviors, quick arrest of the fall before torso or head impact, and small body size"; falling "did not alter infants' subsequent behavior" — [Dev Sci 2021;24:e13069, PubMed 33278863](https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.13069). Contribution: the sourced justification for zero-cost, instant-recovery falls in the opening.
- **Zero fall penalty trains the best walker (Ossmy et al. 2024).** A Nao-like simulated robot (57 cm) in SimSpark was trained on infant free-play paths in 12,500 regimens (5 path clusters × 5 penalty levels from 0 to 100× standard × 5 tests × 100 runs); lower fall penalties produced a robot that "learned to walk farther and better" and generalized better to untrained paths; "zero penalty for errors led to the best performance for both learning and generalization", with the benefit stronger for generalization. The paper also restates that infants "return to play within two seconds" and that an infant fall "generates 18 times less potential energy than if infants were adult-sized and walked at adult speeds" — [Dev Sci 2024;27:e13449, PMC10922068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10922068/). Contribution: direct RL evidence for the brief's "agent early in training" metaphor: penalising falls hurts the learned gait.
- **Bouts are stochastic (Cole, Robinson & Adolph 2016).** 30 13-month-olds and 30 19-month-olds: half of 13-month bouts and 41% of 19-month bouts had three or fewer steps; "infants were just as likely to stop walking after five steps as after 50 steps"; most bouts "ended in the middle of the floor, not at a recognizable goal"; 13-month-olds showed elevated stopping after 1–3 steps — [Dev Psychobiol 2016;58:341-54, PubMed 26497472](https://doi.org/10.1002/dev.21374). Contribution: a stop-probability model for novice locomotion that is memoryless in bout length.
- **Non-canonical walking does not go away (Lee, Cole, Golenia & Adolph 2018).** 97 infants 10.75–19.99 months; free-play gait correlates with straight-path gait and both improve with walking age, but "a large proportion of free-play bouts were too short to permit standard gait measures, and most bouts were curved with omnidirectional steps. The high prevalence of these 'non-canonical' bouts was constant over development" — [Dev Sci 2018;21:e12615, PMC5911424](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5911424/). Adolph, Hoch & Cole 2018 summarise the same corpus: 73% of paths curved, 82% of bouts contain backward or sideways steps, and short/curved/omnidirectional bouts are "equally present in novice and experienced walkers" — [Trends Cogn Sci 2018;22:699-711, PMC6145857](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6145857/). Contribution: competence should change step geometry and fall rate, not the stop-start, curved, omnidirectional texture of movement.
- **Skills "stutter" in, and natural input beats treadmill input (Adolph, Hoch & Cole 2018).** "Skill acquisition is not like turning on a switch... skills stutter into infants' repertoires, with variable trajectories that oscillate between skill expression and non-expression over several days, weeks, or months"; "The average toddler takes 2400 steps, travels the distance of 8 American football fields, and falls 17 times per hour"; "Simulated robots trained on infant-like paths or in varied environments perform better in new, untrained settings" — same PMC6145857. Contribution: argues for a competence scalar that is noisy/non-monotone at the day scale rather than a smooth ramp.
- **Idiosyncratic first-step strategies and body-state effects (Adolph & Hoch 2019).** "'Steppers' take small, conservative steps to minimize disruptions to upright posture; 'fallers' lurch forward while their feet hurry to catch up; 'twisters' swing each leg around like a compass by twisting their trunk"; at onset "the side-to-side distance between steps often exceeds the distance from front to back"; a cloth diaper costs "2 months of walking experience" (disposable: 5 weeks); a load of 15% body weight makes infants "take slower, smaller steps", asymmetric loads make steps shorter and wider with more double support; experienced infants treat a 6° slope as "barely an impediment" but modify gait for 24°, and the most skilled traverse 40° slopes, 28-cm drop-offs, 6-cm ledges and 12-cm bridges — [Annu Rev Psychol 2019;70:141-164, PMC6320716](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6320716/). Contribution: three authored "novice archetypes" (stepper/faller/twister) and sourced load and slope thresholds for the consequence-gating layer.
- **Most bouts have no destination (Hoch, Rachwani & Adolph 2020).** 60–70% of walking bouts do not end near a discernible destination; infants "mostly take steps in place while fixating something within reach"; when they do go somewhere they take "straight, short paths to a target fixated while stationary" — [Child Dev 2020;91:1001-1020, PMC6893075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6893075/).
- **Emergentist coordination (Ossmy & Adolph 2020).** In 11-month cruisers, "the least experienced infants generated multiple coordination patterns inconsistently regardless of body size and handrail distance, whereas the most experienced infants tailored their coordination patterns to body-environment relations and switched solutions only when necessary" — [Curr Biol 2020;30:4553, PMC8722405](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8722405/). Contribution: early competence = many inconsistent solutions, late competence = selection; a plausible way to schedule policy blending.
- **Upper-body stabilisation comes first (Ledebt & Bril 2000).** 7 infants followed 46–80 weeks from onset: "during the first weeks of walking head and trunk oscillations significantly decreased", with "dramatic changes in global gait parameters" in the same window, then slower change — [Dev Psychobiol 2000;36:311-24, PubMed 10797252](https://doi.org/10.1002/(sici)1098-2302(200005)36:4<311::aid-dev6>3.0.co;2-v). Ledebt 2000 on arm posture (high guard → low guard → alternating swing) is located but not opened — [Infant Behav Dev 23:79-89](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0163-6383(00)00027-8) (OpenAlex record; Tsuyuki 2023 paraphrases it).
- **Joint kinetics of toddlers (Hallemans et al. 2005).** 10 toddlers with under 6 months of experience: joint moments small; "Balance problems in toddlers lead to a dominance of hip and knee extending moments throughout stance"; "virtually no power is generated at the ankle joint prior to foot-off (no active push-off)"; profiles of "reduced complexity"; evolution toward mature gait "after 4 months of independent walking" — [Gait Posture 2005;22:107-18, PubMed 16139745](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2004.07.010). Contribution: at s = 0 disable ankle push-off and run hip/knee extensors as the balance actuators.

### 3.2 Gait-maturation metrics beyond step width

- **Dimensionless velocity as a competence curve (Vaughan, Langerak & O'Malley 2003).** From 200 children: dimensionless velocity β (dimensionless step length × dimensionless step frequency) "increases from the age of 18 months and reaches a plateau between 50 and 90 months, attaining the adult value of 0.45", following β(t) = 0.45·(1 − e^(−0.05t)) with t in months — [Exp Brain Res 2003;153:123-7, PubMed 14566445](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-003-1635-x). The companion review defines Fr = v²/gL and β = √Fr — [Gait Posture 2005;21:350-62, PubMed 15760752](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2004.01.011). Contribution: a published exponential neuromaturation curve that can be reparameterised from months to minutes as the competence scalar's shape.
- **Pendulum mechanics are learned (Ivanenko et al. 2004).** 8 toddlers at their first unsupported steps vs 18 children and 10 adults: pendulum parameters "deviated significantly (P<10⁻⁵)", so "the pendulum mechanism is not implemented at the onset of unsupported locomotion"; after Froude normalisation, children over 2 recover mechanical energy within 5% of adults — [J Exp Biol 2004;207:3797-810, PubMed 15371487](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.01214). Contribution: with Bisi & Stagni's "present in all after one month", the s = 0 → s ≈ 0.3 window is where the inverted-pendulum COM exchange should switch on.
- **Walk ratio and guard posture by toddler age (Tsuyuki et al. 2023).** 97 toddlers, groups A 13–21 mo (n = 34), B 22–29 (n = 31), C 30–37 (n = 32): walk ratio (step length / cadence) 0.16 → 0.19 → 0.21 cm/(steps/min); hand height 12.66 → 6.11 → lower %height; waist height 39.97 → 42.43 → 43.27 %height; knee-to-knee distance 29.34 → 25.63 → 23.65 %height (strongest age correlation, r = −0.78) and the only parameter still changing between B and C; Group A differs from B and C on all parameters — [Sci Rep 2023;13:2958, PMC9941512](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9941512/). Walk ratio as a speed-invariant gait index traces to Sekiya & Nagasaki 1998 — [Gait & Posture, DOI 10.1016/s0966-6362(98)00009-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0966-6362(98)00009-5) (Crossref record only). Contribution: knee-to-knee distance as the last-maturing "base width" signal, and hand height as an arm-lowering metric.
- **Stride-time variability keeps maturing past 3 years (Hausdorff et al. 1999).** 50 children aged 3–14: stride-to-stride variability "significantly larger both in the 3- and 4-yr-old children, compared with the 6- and 7-yr-old children, and in the 6- and 7-yr-old children, compared with the 11- to 14-yr-old children", persisting after height adjustment — [J Appl Physiol 1999;86:1040-7, PubMed 10066721](https://doi.org/10.1152/jappl.1999.86.3.1040). Contribution: a developmental (rather than pathological) source for a timing-variability endpoint.
- **Age-banded norms 1–10 years (Dusing & Thorpe 2007)**, §2.5.

### 3.3 Motor-learning science for scheduling the ramp

- **Krakauer et al. 2019, "Motor Learning".** Defines skill as "the incremental improvement in our ability to rapidly select and then precisely execute appropriate actions"; argues adaptation and sequence-learning paradigms "fall short" as models of skill acquisition and points to de novo visuomotor-mapping learning and acuity learning as better paradigms — [Compr Physiol 2019;9:613-663, PubMed 30873583](https://doi.org/10.1002/cphy.c170043). Contribution: two separable competence axes (selection speed, execution precision) that map onto the report's "aim your step early, steady your step late".
- **Shadmehr & Krakauer 2008.** The cerebellum's role is "system identification: to build internal models that predict sensory outcome of motor commands and correct motor commands through internal feedback"; parietal cortex does state estimation — [Exp Brain Res 2008;185:359-81, PMC2553854](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2553854/). Contribution: a forward-model view in which "learning to walk" is learning to predict delayed feedback, the same mechanism Rasman's participants used to beat a 400 ms delay; it justifies modelling competence as a shrinking prediction error rather than only a shrinking delay.
- **Power law vs exponential law of practice.** Heathcote, Brown & Mewhort 2000, "The power law repealed: The case for an exponential law of practice", Psychon Bull Rev — [DOI 10.3758/bf03212979](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03212979); Newell & Rosenbloom 1981 chapter "Mechanisms of skill acquisition and the law of practice" — [reprint DOI 10.4324/9780203728178-6](https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203728178-6). Both located via Crossref only; abstracts were not read. Contribution: two candidate shapes for s(t) (power vs exponential); Vaughan's β(t) is empirically exponential in age.

### 3.5 RL locomotion papers: what they say about early training

None of the opened papers describes early-training gait phenomenology in prose beyond falling. What they do say:
- **Hwangbo et al. 2019 (Sci Robotics, arXiv 1901.08652).** Baselines trained without their learned actuator model "could not make a single step without falling... We observed violent shaking of the limbs, probably due to not accounting for various delays properly"; "Low penalty on joint torque and velocity results in unnatural motions whereas high penalty on them results in a standing behavior"; a multiplicative curriculum factor kc starts at k0 = 0.3 with advance rate kd = 0.997, k0 chosen "to prevent the initial tendency to stand still" by "observing the first one hundred iterations" — [arXiv 1901.08652](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08652). Contribution: shaking from unmodelled delay is a documented RL failure mode; standing-still is the documented over-penalised mode — two authored "incompetence looks" with a cause.
- **Rudin et al. 2022, "Learning to Walk in Minutes".** 4096 simulated ANYmals, terrain curriculum, robots reset "whenever they fall", 1500 updates in under 20 minutes; by 1000 iterations robots reach the hardest terrain level — [arXiv 2109.11978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11978).
- **Smith, Kostrikov & Levine 2022.** A real Unitree A1 learns to walk "from scratch with deep RL in under 20 minutes" on five terrains, with learned resets — [arXiv 2208.07860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07860). **Haarnoja et al. 2018**: a real Minitaur acquires a stable gait "in about two hours" (~400 rollouts), with humans resetting the robot "if it falls or runs out of free space" — [arXiv 1812.11103](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.11103). **Tan et al. 2018**: "inaccurate actuator models and lack of latency modeling are two major causes of the reality gap"; control loop 150–200 Hz — [arXiv 1804.10332](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10332). Contribution: training-time budgets (minutes to hours) are a useful yardstick if the fiction wants the opening's learning to feel like a training run; and latency handling is the recurring culprit, consistent with using delay as the primary detune knob.

## 4. Contradictions found in the report or other notes

4.1 **Variability endpoint is mislabelled.** The report's table row "Step-time coefficient of variation (seeded jitter): 0.07 → 0.03, Kroneberg 2024" uses Kroneberg's CoV of stride **velocity** (0.069 vs 0.028); stride-**length** CoV is 0.046 vs 0.019, and stride-**time** CoV is not reported in that paper ([PMC11174553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11174553/)). Either relabel the row (stride-velocity CoV) or source a timing-variability endpoint from Hausdorff 1999 (§3.2) or Ilg 2007's temporal intra-limb variability.

4.2 **The "walking speed" row mixes populations.** "1.10 m/s (bouts of 1–3 steps) → 1.43 m/s continuous" pairs the comfortable speed of ten SCA14 patients with the bout statistics of 12-month-olds, and the 1.43 m/s comparator is healthy **elderly** (69 ± 5 y) ([PMC11174553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11174553/)). A toddler-consistent novice speed can be derived from Adolph 2012: 296.9 m/h while in motion 33.1% of the time is about 0.25 m/s of travel while moving, with 1456.1 steps/h implying a step length of about 0.20 m (consistent with Gimunová's 0.18 m) and an effective in-motion cadence of about 73 steps/min against a within-bout cadence of about 118 steps/min from a 1.02 s stride ([PMC3591461](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3591461/); [PMC8744759](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8744759/)). Derived figures, not reported ones; say so if used. Dusing & Thorpe's youngest-group velocity (82 cm/s) is the open substitute for a continuous-walking toddler speed (§2.5).

4.3 **Arm-pose endpoint compares different metrics.** "arms abducted ≈ 80° → reciprocal swing ≈ 30° range" joins Gimunová's upper-limb **elevation** (78–87° falling to 47–49° after 15 weeks) to Kroneberg's sagittal arm-swing **RoM** in elderly adults (32°). After 15 weeks the sourced toddler value is 47–49°, not 30°; Sutherland says reciprocal arm swing is present "in most children by the age of eighteen months" ([PDF](http://www.analisedemarcha.com/papers/historia/The%20development%20of%20mature%20gait%20-%201980.pdf)).

4.4 **Hip-flexion endpoint is one-sided.** 42.5° → 29.1° is the left hip (p = 0.037); the right hip is 40.5° → 33.4° (n.s.) ([PMC8744759](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8744759/)). Use ~41° → ~31° as a bilateral reading, or cite the left-only figure explicitly.

4.5 **Fall-rate denominators.** The report writes "novice 12-month walkers fall 31.5 times per hour, about one fall per 69 steps". 69.2 is the per-infant mean of steps before a fall for those novices; the ratio of group means is 46 steps per fall, and the pooled 12–19-month ratio is ~136 steps per fall ([PMC3591461](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3591461/)). "Tapering to near zero" is a design choice; the sourced experienced-infant figure is ~1 fall per 136 steps, and Han & Adolph 2021 argue the rate stays high precisely because falls are cheap (§3.1).

4.6 **Sensory delay: added vs inherent, and the classical note's reaction delay.** The report's row "Sensory delay on balance-state input: 200–300 ms → 0 ms" is consistent with Rasman only if read as **added** delay: Rasman's imposed delays sit "in addition to the ~100–160 ms sensorimotor feedback delays inherent to standing balance" ([PMC8480973](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8480973/)). The classical-control note records Zordan et al. 2005's deliberate 0.1–0.2 s reaction delay before an active hit response, without which the character "appears to be anticipating the contact", and SIMBICON's 150 ms "reaction-time delay" before gains are raised after impact (biped_control_classical.md, lines 50 and 115). Geijtenbeek 2013's 5–20 ms neural delays are a different layer (muscle reflex loops). So at s = 1 the balance controller should still carry a ~100–160 ms reaction latency for perturbation responses (or Zordan's 0.1–0.2 s for hit reactions) while the **extra** ring-buffer delay goes to zero; the report should say "added delay", and the total at s = 0 is then 300–460 ms, inside Rasman's learnable band (up to 560 ms total) but above the ≥200 ms added delay where everyone falls.

4.7 **Torque endpoint inverts the source's finding.** The report maps 40–50% of human torque to s = 0 (incompetent) and 100% to s = 1 (competent), citing Learning to Get Up. That paper's finding is the opposite in look: weak limits (40–60%) are what observers judge **natural**, while full strength yields "highly dynamic and erratic" motion ([arXiv 2205.00307](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00307)). The report acknowledges the tension ("pick one signature") but the table row as written will make the competent walker look more erratic, not less. Also the source's range is 40–60%, not 40–50%, and its "human" torque limits belong to a 1.5 m, 38.3 kg character. If the toddler look is the goal, the defensible reading is "weak and slow", i.e. low torque **and** the κ retiming (0.2–0.8), with strength arriving later as a power rather than as competence.

4.8 **Source-vs-source: what drives velocity after the first months.** Bril & Brenière 1992: after 5 months of walking "increase in velocity is due primarily to increased cadence" ([PubMed 14766502](https://doi.org/10.1080/00222895.1992.9941606)). Bisi & Stagni 2015: after the first 2 months "the increased velocity is more due to an increase in step length than to an increase in cadence" ([PubMed 25636708](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2014.11.017)). Different cohorts (5 vs 20 infants), windows (24 vs 6 months) and instruments (video vs IMU). The report's "phase two unlocks speed via cadence" rests on Bril alone; present it as contested.

4.9 **Kroneberg's healthy comparator is elderly.** The report's "1.10 vs 1.43 m/s" and "20.6 vs 32.0°" describe SCA14 patients versus 58–86-year-olds ([PMC11174553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11174553/)); young-adult sober values from Sci Rep 2022 are 1.15–1.20 m/s at 102–108 steps/min ([PMC9637089](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9637089/)).

4.10 **Baby Steps stick assignment differs between sources.** Engadget: "Press forward with the left stick to give Nate just the right amount of momentum" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/baby-steps-preview-serious-gameplay-in-a-silly-walking-sim-150008737.html)); Reverse Shot: "pressing the left or right trigger to raise the corresponding foot, using the right stick to position that foot, then releasing the trigger to plant it" ([Reverse Shot](https://reverseshot.org/features/3411/baby_steps)); Eurogamer: "pushing a thumbstick forward to shift the leg and move your weight" ([Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/baby-steps-review)). Likely both sticks do something (weight vs foot placement); verify in-game before quoting a single mapping.

4.11 **Sumotori size.** 87 KB (Fun-Motion's download) vs 96 KB (Wikipedia, Game Developer Magazine). The compo category was 96k; the shipped executable is 87 KB.

4.12 **Attribution errors in cited pages.** TechCrunch calls Torsten Reil "Rockstar Games CEO" (he co-founded NaturalMotion, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NaturalMotion)); Kill Screen dates GIRP to 2012 (2011, [foddy.net](https://www.foddy.net/blog/2011/03/girp/)).

4.13 **Kingdom Come "sprint disabled while drunk"** is not in the wiki page the pass-one note cites ([KCD wiki via API](https://kingdom-come-deliverance.fandom.com/wiki/Drunkenness)); drop it or re-source.

4.14 **Report table units.** Gimunová's Table 2 header says "Step width (cm)" for values that are metres; the report's "0.19 m → 0.12 m" is the correct reading, but anyone checking the source will hit the typo.
