# Progression and Gating Design: Second-Pass Verification and Extension

Compiled 2026-09-27. This note re-opens every source behind the pass-one note (`design/research_notes/Physics based superhuman movement design/progression_and_gating_design.md`) and the report sections "Each power arrives one tier before the body can survive it" and "The three ladders" (`design/reports/Physics based superhuman movement design.md`), records a verdict for each claim, closes the gaps pass one listed, adds sources pass one missed, and lists the contradictions found between the report's ladders and the sources or the sibling biomechanics and movement-controller notes.

Method and access notes. One WebSearch succeeded at the start, so the session's search budget was not yet exhausted; it ran out (200/200) during the last batch of gap-filling searches, after which everything was fetched directly. Fandom wikis were read through their public MediaWiki API (`https://<wiki>.fandom.com/api.php?action=parse&page=<Page>&prop=wikitext&format=json`) because direct page fetches return 402/403; every fact that rests only on such a page is marked "(wiki-only)". PDFs (Dormans 2011, Chen's thesis, Koster's GDC Online 2012 slides) were downloaded with curl and text-extracted with pypdf. Celeste's published `Player.cs` was read from raw.githubusercontent.com. OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar both returned HTTP 429 (shared free budget exhausted for this network); DBLP sat behind an Anubis anti-bot page; arXiv's API redirected; Crossref's REST API worked without a key and is the academic source used below. Sonic Retro, the KSP wiki and zeldamods.org returned Anubis challenges or 404; YouTube redirected to a Google consent page and youtubetranscript.com reported YouTube blocking; Medium, GamesBeat, Twinfinite, Zelda Dungeon, zeldawiki.wiki, GameFAQs and Giant Bomb returned 403; Red Bull's Just Cause 3 article returned 404 in both regions tried. Each of these is named again where it matters.

Citation convention: [Title — Author, Year, Venue](URL). Quotations are verbatim from the fetched text unless marked "paraphrase".

**Death Stranding balance controls: closed with an official source plus guides.**

### Daniel Cook: mastery, burnout, loops and arcs
- Chemistry of Game Design (verbatim): "The player only experiences the joy of mastery for an atom only once. After the moment of mastery, a biological feedback system kicks in that dampens the pleasure response to exercising those same pathways again. What was once exciting becomes boring."; burnout: "They master a new skill, they play with it but fail to find any interesting use for it. This is known as burnout."; skill chains are directed graphs where "The skill from one atom feeds into the actions of another atom further down the chain." Contribution: a tier that adds only a multiplier risks burnout unless a downstream atom uses the new capability (the report's "+5 ft with no new verb" warning now has a primary text). — [The Chemistry of Game Design — Daniel Cook, 19 Jul 2007, Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-chemistry-of-game-design)
- Loops and Arcs: a loop is "The player starts with a mental model that prompts them to… Apply an action to… The game system and in return… Receives feedback that… Updates their mental model"; "Loops are fractal and occur at multiple levels and frequencies throughout a game"; "An arc is a broken loop you exit immediately"; loops "are well suited for mastery tasks that involve trial and error or repeated exposure"; "One of the common issues with arcs is that people burn out on them rapidly, rarely desiring to experience them more than once." Contribution: consequence-gated powers are loops (repeatable, mastery-building); story unlocks are arcs; the ladder should be loop-driven with arcs only as punctuation. — [Loops and Arcs — Daniel Cook, 30 Apr 2012, Lostgarden](https://lostgarden.com/2012/04/30/loops-and-arcs/)

## 4. Contradictions found in the report or other notes

1. **Crackdown 3 "Resilience" is not impact tolerance.** The report's Ladder H5 ("Immovable: impact tolerance ... Crackdown 3 Resilience") and Ladder S4 ("Resilience ... impact tolerance for strength moves") cite Strength L4 Resilience as a durability precedent. Shacknews' table reads "An upgrade to the Agent's suit that improves resistance to effects of Fire, Cold, Electricity and Chimera" (elemental status resistance). The "capability before tolerance" ordering claim (Ground Pound L2 before Resilience L4) therefore does not hold for Crackdown 3; the ordering example should be replaced (Prototype's Health/Regen prices arriving above the movement tier prices, or Saints Row IV's cluster-priced "Sprint - Reduced Damage" at rank 1, which is the opposite order). — [Shacknews, 2019](https://www.shacknews.com/article/109880/skills-abilities-and-leveling-fast-in-crackdown-3)
2. **Saints Row IV "can't be knocked over" is marked Disabled.** The wiki row for "Sprint - Shove" carries "Disabled / 0" in the acquisition column, the same marking as other cut entries, so the report's H5 precedent ("SR4 'you can't be knocked over'") probably describes an upgrade that is not obtainable in the shipped game. The obtainable relative is the Challenge reward "Super Jump - Immovable Object". (wiki-only) — [Saints Row Wiki](https://saintsrow.fandom.com/wiki/Super_Powers_in_Saints_Row_IV)
3. **Saints Row IV parameter upgrades are not part of the cluster economy.** The report says the game "sells stamina cost in 75 % → 50 % → unlimited steps with its tuning parameters printed in the upgrade text (`super_lateral_velocity` 130 %, ...)". The efficiency steps are cluster purchases (10/20/30), but the three parameter-line upgrades are Platforming Rift and Speed Rift medal rewards. The "printed in the upgrade text" claim is also unconfirmed: the values come from `unlockables.xtbl` per the modding forum and may only be a wiki transcription. — [saintsrowmods.com, 2013](https://www.saintsrowmods.com/forum/threads/changing-glide-terminal-velocity.3816/)
4. **Sucker Punch quote wording.** The report quotes Wikipedia's paraphrase "immediately after they got it"; the primary wording is "Make sure the player immediately has a chance to use a new power and understands what makes it fun and useful." Substantively the same, but the report should quote Bridges via Graft 2009. — [Game Developer, 2009](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-the-meticulous-pacing-of-i-infamous-i-)
5. **Titanfall as the H4 (30 m/s) wall-run precedent.** Titanfall's ceiling is "up to 30 mph" (13.4 m/s), which sits at the report's H1 speed tier, not H4 (30 m/s). Citing Titanfall in the H4 row risks a mph/m-per-second conflation; it is a valid precedent for the wall-run verb and its speed reward, but not for the speed regime. — [Game Developer, 2017](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designer-interview-getting-i-titanfall-i-s-controls-just-right)
6. **Ladder V mixes two landing-force models and gets the same force for opposite verdicts.** V0 calls "≤ 2 m drops: 6.3 m/s, ~7 BW" routine using Niu's pooled fit extrapolated to 2 m (7.3 BW ≈ 5.0 kN for 70 kg, which the biomechanics note itself flags as extrapolation beyond the 1.03 m data range), while V1 calls "3 m drop = 7.7 m/s, 6 g, 4.8 kN" fracture territory using the rigid-stop model a = g·h/s with s = 0.5 m. A 2 m "routine" landing thus scores a higher force than a 3 m "fracture" landing. Two further mismatches: Niu's vGRF is the two-leg total on a force plate, whereas Funk's 3.7–8.3 kN 50 % fracture band is an axial load on one foot/ankle, so per-leg landing force should be halved before comparison; and Puddle & Maulder's 5.2 BW at 0.75 m is itself above Niu's fit (0.49·sqrt(75)+0.37 = 4.6 BW). The ladder should adopt one model per row (recommend Niu for ≤ 1 m, the g·h/s deceleration model above, both divided over two legs) and restate V0–V2 accordingly. — [human_biomechanics_limits.md lines 90, 100–102, 115–119]; [Niu 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4160626/); [Funk 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12596644/)
7. **Report's braking figure vs its own table.** The report says an adult "brakes at −4.4 m/s² on average" but its stopping distances (7.0 m from 7.35 m/s) reproduce only with a = 5 m/s², the value the biomechanics note uses ("a = 5 m/s^2 (average braking) or 8.5 m/s^2 (peak)"). With 4.4 m/s² the distance is 7.75 m. Minor, but the two numbers should agree. — [human_biomechanics_limits.md line 59]
8. **Control Ground Slam prerequisite.** Pass one (PC Gamer snippet): "Upgrading Levitate to level three gives you the option of unlocking the Ground Slam"; the Control Wiki table: Ground Slam, 3 AP, prerequisite "Levitate 2". Snippet vs wiki-only; unresolved. — [Control Wiki](https://control.fandom.com/wiki/Abilities)
9. **Saints Row IV Speed cost.** SegmentNext "Rank 2: Speed (15)" vs wiki "Sprint - Speed | 10". Unresolved (neither is primary).
