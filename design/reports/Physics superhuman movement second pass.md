# Drive One Ragdoll From Toddler to Titan

Second edition, 2026-09-28. Supersedes "Learn to Walk, Then Break the World" (first edition, 2026-09-27) as the single current design and technical plan.

The joint-drive chain the whole design rests on now runs: a headless build of this Godot 4.8-dev fork (commit `efc1d2ea9`, Jolt 5.6.0) tracks a 45° swing through `Generic6DOFJoint3D.set_angular_target_rotation()` with **1.12° mean error** (0.38° with velocity feed-forward), honours a 2 N·m torque cap to within 1° of the analytic equilibrium, reaches the identical drive through `PhysicalBone3D.get_joint_rid()` and the `PhysicsServer3D` API, and costs about **200 µs per step for a 15-body, 14-drive chain** on four worker threads (2.5 % of a 120 Hz tick), with the same tracking error at 60, 120 and 240 Hz (`design/research_notes/Physics superhuman movement second pass/godot_runtime_verification.md`). So the first edition's architecture stands: an active ragdoll of about fifteen `PhysicalBone3D` bodies driven by Jolt's implicit joint motors, an intent capsule the pelvis is pulled toward by a force-limited spring, a SIMBICON/Coros balance controller writing target poses, and a three-layer camera that keeps body motion off the eye. What does not stand is a set of numbers and precedents the second pass falsified against primary sources: the damage model read contact impulses that do not exist on `PhysicalBone3D` and are inexact on any ragdoll, so it is rebuilt on per-tick velocity change and joint constraint forces; the fall thresholds copied Source's 6 m safe height while the biomechanics put fracture onset near 3 m, so the landing table is rebuilt per leg with one model per row; two maximal grips (≈ 0.96 kN) catch a body arriving at about **2 m/s, not 3–4**, so the grab becomes a slipping, energy-absorbing constraint and a level rule rather than a rescue for 3 m drops; the competence table made the competent walker the erratic one by inverting its source, so the toddler is now "weak and slow" and strength arrives later as a power; the Megaton Rainfall mission loop the brief excluded is removed and the world-consequence endgame stands on its own precedents; and the 120 Hz tick is kept as a measured default rather than a derived necessity. The plan's first artifact is delivered: `design/prototype/phase0_joint_drive/` builds, runs headless in about three seconds and prints the API-presence table, the tracking errors and the per-step costs quoted here. Two runtime caveats every implementer must know: under Jolt a `PhysicalBone3D` joint leaves its two bodies colliding unless the script calls `PhysicsServer3D.joint_disable_collisions_between_bodies(rid, true)` (without it the drive error rises to 7.1° and a torque cap is defeated by contact friction), and the simulated pose is readable from script only inside the `skeleton_updated` signal.

## Executive summary: what to build, in what order, and what the second pass changed

The design still resolves into one controller architecture, one opening scalar with a schedule of secondary knobs, three power ladders and a destruction plan graded by cost, but every quantitative claim has been re-verified against its primary source, thirteen verification notes and a red-team review have been applied, and the fork has been built and tested. The controller is an intent rig (a kinematic capsule integrating input into a desired root trajectory and a hidden intent skeleton carrying the procedural target pose) plus a physics rig (a Jolt ragdoll whose joints are pulled toward that pose by solver-side `Position` motors and whose pelvis is pulled toward the capsule by a force-limited spring). The genuine precedents for the pelvis spring are not Boneworks or Ubisoft's motion matching, as the first edition claimed, but the cheat forces every shipped physics body uses: Totally Accurate Battle Simulator's animation-curve torques and standing handler, the EA ragdoll patent's damped external pelvis forces, Coros's velocity-tuning virtual force with **k_V = 100 N·s/m**, and Euphoria's `ResistAcc` ([Game Developer on TABS](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-landfall-games-finds-the-fun-in-physics-engines); [US7403202B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7403202B1/en); [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [SHVDN ConfigureBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_configure_balance_helper.html)). The 2026 learned-control literature supplies the same mechanism under a citable name: Neural Assistive Impulses learns a non-physical root wrench whose allowed magnitude is the knob and penalises its use, and Embark's ARC Raiders talks describe a penalised "magic force" on the head as a fall-prevention cheat ([Neural Assistive Impulses, arXiv 2604.05394](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.05394); [4Gamer GDC 2026 report](https://www.4gamer.net/games/609/G060942/20260312064/)). Balance is not faked: the SIMBICON finite-state machine and swing-hip feedback `θd = θd0 + cd·d + cv·v` (cd 0.5, cv 0.2) write the leg targets, Coros's inverted-pendulum step `d = v·sqrt(h/g + v²/4g²)` places the foot, and Stephens' analytic ankle/hip/step decision surfaces decide which strategy fires ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [Stephens 2007 PDF](http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bstephe1/papers/humanoids07.pdf)).

The opening turns one competence scalar s from a toddler into a walker, and the second pass changes its shape. Learning to Get Up finds that torque limits of **40–60 %** of nominal look natural while full strength looks "highly dynamic and erratic", so the first edition's mapping (40–50 % at s = 0, 100 % at s = 1) would have made the competent walker the erratic one; the toddler is now weak *and slow* (a low torque cap plus the paper's retiming factor κ ∈ [0.2, 0.8]), the competent walker sits in the natural band, and strength above it is the S ladder ([Learning to Get Up PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00307)). The sensory delay is an *added* delay of 200–300 ms on top of the inherent 100–160 ms loop, because Rasman's participants all lost balance at ≥ 200 ms added and still learned to stand at 400 ms ([Rasman 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8480973/)). The table now draws one signature, the toddler, from Adolph, Gimunová, Tsuyuki, Hallemans and Ivanenko; drunk sway and fatigue are separate channels for later use.

Every power is a change to a physical budget with a consequence the same physics produces, and the second pass makes the consequence measurable on the recommended rig. Head and chest deceleration are scored from each body's per-tick velocity change over the contact window against the duration-dependent Eiband tolerance (45 g for 0.044 s, 40 g at 0.1 s, 25 g at 0.2 s, 13 g at 0.6 s), not a flat "46 g"; limb loads and grip failure come from joint constraint forces, which upstream PR #123875 exposes as `get_applied_force/torque` for all five 3D joint types; contact impulses locate the hit ([Eiband 1959, NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19980228043/downloads/19980228043.pdf); [godot PR #123875](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/123875)). The landing table is one model per row and per leg: Niu's per-foot fit to 1 m (3.6 kN at 1 m), the rigid-stop model above it with the 1.5–2.5× peak band, so fracture risk begins at about 3 m (3.6–6.0 kN per leg against Funk's 3.7–8.3 kN band), becomes likely by 5–6 m and exceeds the tibia by 10 m ([Niu 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4160626/); [Funk 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12596644/)). A wall stop is scored by `a = v²/(2s)` over a compliance distance and its duration `2s/v`, with the Rosén–Sander pedestrian curve kept only as a km/h sanity check because its variable is car speed and cannot respond to bracing ([Rosén & Sander 2009](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/pedestrian_fatality_risk_function_car_impact_speed_rosen.pdf)). The grab is the hardest correction: two maximal grips total 0.96 kN, which arrests a 70 kg body arriving at 2 m/s over 0.3–0.5 m of shoulder give, so a hard catch is possible only within about 0.2 m of the apex; the verb is therefore a force-limited, slipping constraint that yields at the grip budget and decelerates the body at (F/m − g) ≈ 4.5 m/s² while the hands stay on a graspable face, catching 3 m/s after a 1.0 m slide, and level design supplies the face ([Bohannon & Magasi 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24729356/)). Brick does not break for an unpowered sprinter: Mavrouli's 500 J threshold belongs to a rigid 73 kg boulder failing a single-leaf wall by local shear, so the bond graph's hardness test reads the impulse of the rigid part in contact (a few kg for a fist, 5–10 kg for a shoulder, game parameters), and masonry stays at S3 ([Mavrouli 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences14090226); [Walilko 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16183766/)).

The world reacts on the same graded budget as before (free dressing; pre-fractured swap; a Blast-style bond graph with `Damage = Impulse / Hardness` and island flood-fill in script; runtime cutting, voxels and FEM in C++), with the tier-three recipe sharpened by The Finals' GDC 2024 slides: decompose the constraint impulse into tension/compression, shear and bending with per-material multipliers and break only the excess over the resting baseline load ([Isaksson, GDC 2024 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/Slides/GDC+slide+presentations/Isaksson_Mans_Engineering+Mayham+Technical.pdf)). The DMM verdict is unchanged and stronger: Quantum Break used the DMM *Playback* System (baked simulations), so the last runtime DMM title is The Force Unleashed II (2010), and every admired 2023–2025 destruction shooter ships pre-fractured parts plus a connection graph ([Pixelux press release 2013](https://www.pixelux.com/Remedy-QuantumBreak2013.html); [fxguide on Quantum Break](https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/time-for-destruction-the-tech-of-quantum-break/)). Megaton Rainfall is cited for capabilities and engineering only; the endgame's world consequence stands on Superman Returns' Metropolis damage meter and Hulk: Ultimate Destruction, and its engine lesson is that Jolt stores velocities in floats even in a double build, so a Mach-8 ragdoll must live in a co-moving physics space (the Krakensbane pattern), not behind a raised `max_linear_velocity` ([Jolt Physics documentation](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html); [Squad devnote 2016](https://kerbaldevteam.tumblr.com/post/150703936094/devnote-tuesday-12-pre-release-the-bug-hunt-and)). Houdini remains offline authoring through glTF, with one new version gate: the glTF extras and path-hierarchy export the pipeline relies on exist only in the **Houdini 22.0** ROP (released 2026-06-23), and skinned characters need a Post-Write Script or JSON sidecar because the KineFX character ROP has no extras option ([glTF ROP, Houdini 22.0 docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/out/gltf.html); [ROP GLTF Character Output docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/kinefx--rop_gltfcharacteroutput.html)). Learned control stays a later residual, but the cost argument is re-based: per-skill training is now seconds on a GPU-native simulator, so the obstacle is the Jolt transfer step, not compute ([InstantMimic, arXiv 2609.09821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09821)). The plan replaces the first edition's unsupported durations with exit criteria, treats Phase 0 as delivered, and adds six requirements the red team ranked high for round one: a death and respawn loop, a per-frame budget measured against the fork's ray-tracing renderer, a determinism and replay policy, impact audio and haptics, a ledge-detection algorithm, and an input-latency chain. The section "What changed since the first edition" lists every correction with its source.

## The joint-drive chain runs: measured engine facts replace source-read inference

### What the fork exposes, with the API names that exist

The repository is upstream Godot master as of 2026-09-22 (`version.py:3-6` reports 4.8.0 dev) vendoring **Jolt 5.6.0** with one build-only alignment patch (`thirdparty/README.md:553-567`). The second pass closed the first edition's Phase 0 item "add an upstream remote and diff": `git log --no-merges` filtered to the fork's authors over `modules/jolt_physics`, `scene/3d`, `servers/physics_3d`, `scene/animation`, `modules/godot_physics_3d` and `thirdparty/jolt_physics` returns only the shallow-graft boundary commit, so the fork carries **no author-attributable physics or animation change**, and upstream master had no commits after 2026-09-22 in `modules/jolt_physics` (latest 2026-09-17), `scene/3d/physics` (2026-09-07) or `servers/physics_3d` (2026-09-22, PR #113970, already merged here) ([godot commits, modules/jolt_physics](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/commits/master/modules/jolt_physics); [scene/3d/physics](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/commits/master/scene/3d/physics); [servers/physics_3d](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/commits/master/servers/physics_3d)). The clone is shallow (`.git/shallow` exists), which is why per-file "first added" dates collapse onto the graft commit; class ages come from the release pages, not local git.

The joint API is two upstream pull requests, not one. The body-space quaternion target is [PR #118997](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/118997) (merged 2026-06-23, milestone 4.8): `Generic6DOFJoint3D.set_angular_target_rotation(Quaternion)`, `get_angular_target_rotation()`, **`has_target_rotation()`** (the first edition's `has_angular_target_rotation` is a private member and does not exist as a bound method, `scene/3d/physics/joints/generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:56-59`, `doc/classes/Generic6DOFJoint3D.xml:61`) and `clear_angular_target_rotation()`, with the server pair `PhysicsServer3D.generic_6dof_joint_set/get_angular_target_rotation(rid, q)` (`servers/physics_3d/physics_server_3d.cpp:435-436`). The drive limits `PARAM_LINEAR_DRIVE_FORCE_LIMIT = 22` and `PARAM_ANGULAR_DRIVE_TORQUE_LIMIT = 23`, which apply in both spring and motor modes and deprecate `PARAM_ANGULAR_MOTOR_FORCE_LIMIT`, are [PR #119332](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/119332) (merged 2026-06-26, closing proposal #14745; `servers/physics_3d/physics_server_3d_enums.h:231-232`). The motor-state mapping the design leans on for velocity feed-forward, motor + spring → Jolt `PositionAndVelocity`, spring-only → `Position`, motor-only → `Velocity` (`modules/jolt_physics/joints/jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:112-124`), is [PR #122996](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/122996) (merged 2026-08-31, milestone 4.8): before it, enabling both selected `Velocity` and the spring settings "had no effect", so anyone on 4.7 or earlier does not have this path. New code should use the renamed flags `FLAG_ENABLE_ANGULAR_MOTOR` / `G6DOF_JOINT_FLAG_ENABLE_ANGULAR_MOTOR` from [PR #96458](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/96458) (merged 2026-07-09); `FLAG_ENABLE_MOTOR` is a deprecated alias (`doc/classes/Generic6DOFJoint3D.xml:460`). `PhysicalBone3D.get_joint_rid()` is [PR #112002](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/112002) (merged 2026-06-22), whose author needed it "for procedurally animated skeletons, where I … apply motor params in PhysicsServer3D". Inside Jolt the `Position` motor is `force = stiffness × (target − current) − damping × current_velocity`, solved implicitly in the constraint solver (`thirdparty/jolt_physics/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/MotorSettings.h:18-21`), the soft-constraint-as-implicit-spring identity Catto proved for Box2D ([Catto, Soft Constraints, GDC 2011](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_SoftConstraints_GDC2011.pdf)).

Four engine facts that shape the rig were found in source during the second pass and one was confirmed at runtime. First, any write of `G6DOF_JOINT_ANGULAR_SPRING_EQUILIBRIUM_POINT` **clears the quaternion target** in both node and module (`generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:228-230, 250-252, 271-273`; `jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:464-468`), and `PhysicalBone3D::_reload_joint()` rewrites that parameter on tree entry, joint-type change and bone-parent change (`scene/3d/physics/physical_bone_3d.cpp:757-767, 1036, 1044-1046, 1124`); a per-tick target hides this, a set-once pattern silently reverts to the Euler equilibrium, and any drive limit set through the server must be re-sent after those events. Second, in `Position` mode Jolt activates the rotation motor only if `SpringSettings::HasStiffness()` (`thirdparty/jolt_physics/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/SixDOFConstraint.cpp:571-577`; `SpringSettings.h:41`), so damping may be zero but stiffness may not, and `PhysicalBone3D`'s default `angular_spring_stiffness` is 0.0 (`physical_bone_3d.h`): a spring enabled with default stiffness drives nothing, which is the real trap behind the first edition's "does zero damping count as active" question. Third, the 6DOF wrapper hard-codes a **pyramid** swing limit (`jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:80`, `ESwingType::Pyramid`) while `ConeTwistJoint3D` now inherits Jolt's default cone after [PR #119982](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/119982) (fixing [issue #119926](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/119926)), so "cone-type limits … all implemented as 6DOF joints" is not what ships: a pyramid lets a shoulder reach swing-space corners a cone forbids. Fourth, Jolt's performance monitors are stubbed: `Performance.PHYSICS_3D_ACTIVE_OBJECTS`, `COLLISION_PAIRS` and `ISLAND_COUNT` return 0 under Jolt (`jolt_physics_server_3d.cpp:1687-1689`; [issue #123514](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/123514)), so the first edition's advice to profile with "Jolt's body-pair counters" cannot work; only the "Physics Process" time monitor and direct `Time.get_ticks_usec()` gaps do, and `TIME_PHYSICS_PROCESS` is a one-second maximum, not a per-step time. The runtime-confirmed fact is the collision default: `JoltJoint3D::collision_disabled = false` (`modules/jolt_physics/joints/jolt_joint_3d.h:47`) whereas GodotPhysics constraints default to disabled collisions (`modules/godot_physics_3d/godot_constraint_3d.h:54`); `Joint3D` nodes call `joint_disable_collisions_between_bodies` themselves (`scene/3d/physics/joints/joint_3d.cpp:124`) but `PhysicalBone3D::_reload_joint()` never does (`physical_bone_3d.cpp:950-1043`), and the flag survives a reload because the new `JoltJoint3D` copies it from the old one (`jolt_joint_3d.cpp:114`).

Character-control primitives are unchanged upstream behaviour, with one correction to the first edition's framing. Jolt's `CharacterVirtual` is compiled (`modules/jolt_physics/SCsub:53-55`) but not wrapped anywhere (`grep -rn CharacterVirtual modules/jolt_physics/` matches only the build list), and no proposal asks for it; the nearest open work is [PR #114447](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/114447) (step handling for `CharacterBody3D`, "needs work"). So the numbers that govern the intent capsule are Godot's own, not Jolt's: `CharacterBody3D` defaults are `safe_margin 0.001`, `max_slides 6`, `floor_snap_length 0.1`, `floor_max_angle 45°`, `wall_min_slide_angle 15°`, `floor_stop_on_slope true`, `floor_block_on_wall true`, `slide_on_ceiling true` and no stair step (`scene/3d/physics/character_body_3d.h:117-151`; `doc/classes/CharacterBody3D.xml`); Jolt's `CharacterVirtual` defaults (70 kg, 100 N `mMaxStrength`, 0.4 m step-up, 0.5 m stick-to-floor, 75° forward-contact cone, 50° `mMaxSlopeAngle`) apply only if the fork binds it (`thirdparty/jolt_physics/Jolt/Physics/Character/CharacterVirtual.h:36-68, 431-435`; `CharacterBase.h:34-45`). The non-allocating typed queries from [PR #113970](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/113970) (`intersect_ray_into`, `cast_motion_into`, `collide_shape_into`; `doc/classes/PhysicsDirectSpaceState3D.xml:119-126`) matter because a Dying-Light-scale ledge scan of 200 traces per frame would otherwise allocate every tick ([Speaker Q&A: Bartosz Kulon, GDC](https://gdconf.com/news/speaker-qa-bartosz-kulon-explains-turning-nba-dreams-game-dev-expertise)). Applied-force readback exists in the module for all five joint types (`jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:667-698` returns `GetTotalLambda… / last_step`; `jolt_physics_server_3d.h:465-501`) and is being bound upstream by [PR #123875](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/123875) (opened 2026-09-26, approved by jrouwe 2026-09-27), reviving the stale [PR #111842](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/111842); cherry-pick it rather than writing a fork-local binding that would later conflict on `Joint3D`/`PhysicsServer3D`.

The costs read three ways. Per-step cost is flat across tick rates, so per-second cost is linear in the tick rate, as the documentation says; at 120 Hz the driven chain uses **2.51 %** of the tick budget on four threads and 0.93 % on one, at 240 Hz 4.83 % and 1.94 %. At fifteen bodies the multi-threaded job dispatch dominates, one worker thread being 2.5× faster per step than four (the module sizes its job system from `WorkerThreadPool::get_thread_count()`, `modules/jolt_physics/spaces/jolt_job_system.cpp:161-165`), so small-scene figures must be quoted with the thread count and a whole scene must be re-measured. The fourteen drives add 15–50 µs per step over the passive chain (1–3.5 µs per driven joint). Two more observations belong in the implementation notes: batching four or more ticks in one frame triggers Jolt's `WARN_PRINT_ONCE` "job system exceeded the maximum number of jobs" (`jolt_job_system.cpp:124`), which never appeared in real-time runs but will appear whenever `max_physics_steps_per_frame` catches up after a hitch; and `Skeleton3D.get_bone_global_pose()` read from `_physics_process` returned 0.000° while the body sat at 26.55°, because `Skeleton3D` saves bone poses before running modifiers and restores them afterwards (`scene/3d/skeleton_3d.cpp:363-457`), while the same call inside a `skeleton_updated` handler returned 26.50°. Scripts that need the simulated pose read it there or from the `PhysicalBone3D` nodes. Still unverified at runtime: a full humanoid with realistic limits, self-contact and ground contact; cone-twist and hinge `Position` motors; whether `_integrate_forces` on a `PhysicalBone3D` receives contact impulses; the `_reload_joint()` parameter-loss inference; targets under enabled angular limits (Jolt clamps `SetTargetOrientationCS` to the swing/twist limits, `SixDOFConstraint.cpp:343-348`); and anything rendering-side (`godot_runtime_verification.md` §7).

### The recommended rig: intent capsule, intent skeleton, physics rig

`PhysicalBone3D` is strictly kinematic-or-simulated: simulating, it is `BODY_MODE_RIGID` with `set_as_top_level(true)` (`physical_bone_3d.cpp:1341-1353`); otherwise kinematic if the simulator is active, else static with layer and mask 0 (`:1355-1375`); it writes its transform back through `_body_state_changed` → `set_bone_global_pose` (`:805-836`). It stores a 6DOF joint with spring stiffness, damping and an Euler equilibrium point but no quaternion target (`physical_bone_3d.h:127-160`), so the target is supplied each physics tick through `get_joint_rid()` and `PhysicsServer3D.generic_6dof_joint_set_angular_target_rotation(rid, q)`, the cap through `generic_6dof_joint_set_param(rid, axis, G6DOF_JOINT_ANGULAR_DRIVE_TORQUE_LIMIT, v)`, and the collision exception through `joint_disable_collisions_between_bodies(rid, true)` per bone (or `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D.physical_bones_add_collision_exception()`). The target `q` is the desired child-relative-to-parent rotation in body A's frame: Jolt applies `SetTargetOrientationBS` as `R_B = R_A · q` (`SixDOFConstraint.h:169-192`, "Rotation motors work in constraint space of body 2 (!)"), the module passes the quaternion through `to_jolt()` unmodified while negating Euler equilibria and motor velocities for GodotPhysics' legacy handedness (`jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:127-144, 190-211, 214-226`), and the runtime test confirmed the convention (target −45° gives −44.8°, no sign flip). This reproduces Jolt's unexposed `Ragdoll::DriveToPoseUsingMotors` one joint at a time (`Ragdoll.cpp:647-680` for `Position`, `:682-720` for the 5.6.0 `PositionAndVelocity` variant), which the samples use each step after sampling an animation ([Jolt Docs/Samples.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/master/Docs/Samples.md); [JoltPhysics v5.6.0 release](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/releases/tag/v5.6.0)). Jolt's maintainer names the same three routes for Godot active ragdolls, motors ("Great for dying as you are applying the target pose in local space"), velocities (`body_velocity = (desired_position − current_position) / step_delta_time`) and manual forces, and points at `KinematicRigTest.cpp`, `PoweredRigTest.cpp` and `SoftKeyframedRigTest.cpp` ([JoltPhysics discussion #1764](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/discussions/1764)). The same local-space-motor versus world-space-velocity split is Havok's `hkaRagdollPoweredConstraintController` versus `hkaRagdollRigidBodyController` ("hit reaction or following animation precisely"), Respawn's motors-versus-velocities-versus-constraints choice for Jedi: Fallen Order (they used motors and switch the hip to free ragdoll when its distance from the animation target exceeds a threshold), and Epic's `WorldSpace`/`ParentSpace` Physics Control sets ([hkaRagdollRigidBodyController.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nitaigao/engine-showcase/master/etc/vendor/havok/Source/Animation/Ragdoll/Controller/RigidBody/hkaRagdollRigidBodyController.h); [Game Developer on Fallen Order](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/leveraging-physical-animation-to-sell-force-powers-in-i-jedi-fallen-order-i-); [Epic API, UPhysicsControlComponent](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/PhysicsControl/UPhysicsControlComponent)).

The architecture needs **two skeletons**, which the first edition's single "15-body `PhysicalBone3D` rig" glossed over. `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D` and every IK node are `SkeletonModifier3D`s that apply 100 % of their result after `AnimationMixer` (`scene/3d/physics/physical_bone_simulator_3d.h:37`; `doc/classes/SkeletonModifier3D.xml`), so on one `Skeleton3D` the simulator overwrites the IK output, and the runtime test shows the simulated pose is not even script-visible outside `skeleton_updated`. The intent skeleton is a hidden `Skeleton3D` with `AnimationMixer.callback_mode_process = PHYSICS` and `modifier_callback_mode_process = PHYSICS` (`doc/classes/AnimationMixer.xml:305,323-325`; `doc/classes/Skeleton3D.xml:395,445-453`) carrying the procedural pose generator and the IK modifiers; the script reads its bone poses each physics tick, forms `q` per joint and pushes targets to the visible skeleton's `PhysicalBone3D` joints; the visible skin follows the simulator. The intent capsule is a `CharacterBody3D` running Quake-lineage acceleration purely to produce a desired root position and velocity; it never renders, does not collide with the ragdoll, and **must stop at obstacles** (`move_and_slide` does), otherwise the authority spring drags the body into a wall indefinitely. The pelvis is tied to it by a linear spring drive on a 6DOF joint to a kinematic anchor with `PARAM_LINEAR_DRIVE_FORCE_LIMIT` as the **authority** scalar, or equivalently a `custom_integrator` virtual force `F = k_V(V_d − V)` clamped by that limit, with Coros's **k_V = 100 N·s/m** and ω_max = 2 rad/s as the human-nominal baseline ([Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)). At zero authority the body must balance on its own legs; at nominal it steadies like Euphoria's balancer; at superhuman values it carries the body through anything. Euphoria's `ResistAcc` (0.5, "cheat force added to resist floorAcceleration") is a moving-floor term, not a velocity spring, and Boneworks' rig is a VR player whose "Controller Rig" is the tracked head and hands, six rigidbodies in Boneworks, eleven in Bonelab and eighteen in Marrow 2, so neither is a precedent for a capsule-chased pelvis; the honest statement is that this design is "in the spirit of" the cheat forces every shipped physics body uses, and the Milestone 1 question no source answers is whether a spring-chased ragdoll feels like a body or a puppet on an elastic ([SHVDN ConfigureBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_configure_balance_helper.html); [Laatsch on X, via the fxtwitter mirror](https://x.com/BrandonJLa/status/2009741997897044046)).

Body layout: cone-type limits at shoulders, hips and neck (as `ConeTwistJoint3D` if the Phase 0 `Position` motor patch lands, else 6DOF with the pyramid caveat), hinges at elbows and knees, collision layers so the capsule never collides with its ragdoll, joint collisions disabled per bone ([Ragdoll system, Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/ragdoll_system.html); [godot-docs PR #11392](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/pull/11392)). Masses follow the SIMBICON-CEF humanoid (pelvis 12.9, torso 22.5, head 5.2, upper arm 2.2 ×2, lower arm 1.7 ×2, upper leg 6.6 ×2, lower leg 3.2 ×2, foot 1.0 ×2, toes 0.2 ×2 = **70.4 kg**, `bipV2.rbs`), which is Coros's "70.4 kg" character; the SIMBICON 2D model the first edition offered as an alternative sums to **90 kg** (70 + 2×5 + 2×4 + 2×1), not 70 ([SimbiconSourceCode.zip](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/SimbiconSourceCode.zip); [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)). Every force and energy in this report uses **70 kg**; at the DReCon ragdoll's 90 kg or Boneworks' 82 kg they rise 29 % or 17 % ([DReCon PDF](https://static-wordpress.ubisoft.com/montreal.ubisoft.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/13214229/DReCon.pdf); [Game Developer on Boneworks](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-classical-game-mechanics-and-physics-converge-in-vr-hit-i-boneworks-i-)).

### Control laws and gains, transcribed from the re-opened primaries

The joint layer is the PD servo `τ = kp(θd − θ) − kd·θ̇`, realised as Jolt spring stiffness and damping in `StiffnessAndDamping` mode (the only mode reachable from script, `jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:169-186`) with `PARAM_ANGULAR_DRIVE_TORQUE_LIMIT` as the cap. SIMBICON's 3D biped in ODE uses kp = 300 N·m/rad or less on all joints except the waist (1000), kd = 0.1 kp, torques limited to kp, at a 0.005 s step; its 2D biped uses kp 300 / kd 30 everywhere with a 1000 N·m limit that can drop to 370 N·m for all motions except the fast run, the basic walk surviving down to **90 N·m** before it "becomes weak-kneed and falls" ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)). The released CEF `fWalk.sbc` gives per-joint kp/kd with per-axis scales the first edition omitted: root 3000/300 (scale 1.0/0.2/1.0), pelvis–torso 200/30 (1/0.2/1), hips 300/30 (1/0.66/1), knees 300/30 (1/0.2/1), torso–head 200/20 (1/0.2/1), shoulders 20/5 (left 0.5/1/1, right 0.3/1/1), elbows 5/1 (0.2/1/1), ankles 75/10 (1/0.2/0.2), toes 10/0.5, `MaxTorque 10000`, `stanceHipDamping 25`, `stanceHipMaxVelocity 4` ([SimbiconSourceCode.zip](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/SimbiconSourceCode.zip)). Coros scales kp by the mass ratio and uses kd = 2·sqrt(kp) with optional limits of 200 N·m at hips and knees and 100 N·m elsewhere that "did not have any visible impact"; the survey's rule of thumb is the same critical damping ([Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [Geijtenbeek & Pronost 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03189.x)). Jolt's own tuning guidance is in frequency terms: valid frequencies lie in (0, 0.5 × simulation frequency], 20 Hz is "a good value for a stiff spring" at 60 Hz, 2 Hz a soft one, damping ratio in [0, 1] with 1 critical, and "if your motor doesn't seem to do anything, chances are that you have set the value too low"; `MotorSettings` default to a 2 Hz critically damped spring with ±FLT_MAX limits, and `SpringSettings.h` sizes stiffness as mass × g / deflection ([Jolt Physics documentation](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html); [MotorSettings.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/master/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/MotorSettings.h); [SpringSettings.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/master/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/SpringSettings.h)). The mode the design really wants for a mass-independent strength scalar, `MassNormalizedStiffnessAndDamping` (`T = i_eff·(−k·θ − c·ω)`), is not mapped by the module and is a Phase 0 binding; Epic's Physics Control defaults to exactly that parameterisation ("the strength values are interpreted as accelerations") ([Epic API, FPhysicsControlData](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/PhysicsControl/FPhysicsControlData)). Euphoria's dimensionless 6–16 stiffness scale (balance legs 12, catch-fall arms 15, torso 9) gives the ratio pattern to keep, legs stiff, reaching arms stiffest, torso softest ([SHVDN CatchFallHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_catch_fall_helper.html)).

The balance layer is SIMBICON's four-state walk (state 1 lifts the swing foot for a fixed **0.3 s**, state 2 drives it down until contact; runs have two states, including the fast run, which the first edition wrongly called one-state) with the swing-hip law `θd = θd0 + cd·d + cv·v`, cd = 0.5 and cv = 0.2 in both planes, the hip midpoint as COM proxy, Table 1's 3D walk targets (states 0,2: torso 0, swing hip 0.5, swing knee −1.1, swing ankle 0.6, stance knee −0.05; states 1,3 on foot contact: −0.1, −0.05, 0.15, −0.1) and its feedback-error-learning option (bins of 0.5–25 ms, feed-forward capped at 0.2 rad × kp, gains raised after a 150 ms reaction delay) ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)). The released controllers differ from the paper: `fWalk.sbc` uses cd 0.55 / cv 0.30 sagittal and −0.30 / −0.30 coronal with stance-ankle feedback 0.2 / 0.2 and a *maximum* dwell of 0.7 s transitioning on `footDown`; `bWalk.sbc` uses the paper's 0.50 / 0.20; `iWalk.sbc` 0.30 / 0.30 ([SimbiconSourceCode.zip](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/SimbiconSourceCode.zip)). Coros's predictive pendulum places the foot at `d = v·sqrt(h/g + v²/4g²)`, shortened by `d′ = d − α·V_d` with α = 0.05 (0.2 when pushing an 80 kg crate), in each plane, recomputed every step, capped at 0.6 L; gravity compensation `J_iᵀ(−m_i g)` on every link except the stance leg and the velocity force `F_V = k_V(V_d − V)` through the stance-leg Jacobian transpose make low gains sufficient ([Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)). The capture point is the constant-height counterpart, `x_capture = ẋ·sqrt(z₀/g)` for the linear inverted pendulum (Pratt 2006 eq. 10), and Stephens 2007 turns ankle, hip and stepping into analytic decision surfaces on the LIPM state with ω² = g/L, which is the switching logic that decides whether to stiffen the ankles, bend at the hips or step before the Coros law is called ([Pratt et al. 2006 PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cga/legs/Pratt_Goswami_Humanoids2006.pdf); [Stephens 2007 PDF](http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bstephe1/papers/humanoids07.pdf)). The running controller is Hodgins 1995: a flight/loading/heel/heel-and-toe/toe/unloading state machine with Raibert foot placement including a heel-to-ball correction `x_hh = ½(t_s·ẋ − cos θ·l_f) + k(ẋ − ẋ_d)`, stance-hip attitude servo, passive knee spring in stance and ankle thrust after 30–50 % of expected stance ([Hodgins et al. 1995, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.06108); [Raibert 1983 CMU report](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub3/raibert_marc_h_1983_1/raibert_marc_h_1983_1.pdf)).

The push numbers that serve as acceptance tests were mislabelled in the first edition. SIMBICON's "600 N forwards and 500 N backwards for 0.1 s" are the *2D planar biped's* (§7.1); the 3D controller's published tolerance is 0.4 s chest-height pushes at phase 0.1 of **340 N forward, 270 N backward, 330 N and 240 N lateral**, with diagonals (230, 230), (220, −220), (−190, −190), (−190, 190) N, i.e. 136 N·s forward and 108 N·s backward ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf) §7.2). Coros's 70.4 kg character survives omnidirectional 600 N × 0.1 s pushes at 0.6 m/s, a 3D result that stands ([Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)). DeepMimic's protocol (0.2 s pelvis pushes raised in 10 N steps: walk 240 N forward / 300 N sideways, run 720 / 300) and SuperTrack's 5 m/s cube once per second remain the secondary tests ([DeepMimic PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02717); [SuperTrack PDF](https://theorangeduck.com/media/uploads/other_stuff/SuperTrack.pdf)).

### Tick rate: 120 Hz as a measured default, not a derived necessity

The first edition derived 120 Hz from "Jolt's stiff 20 Hz drive needs ≥ 80 Hz by Catto's four-steps rule" and "classical PD only worked at 0.1–0.5 ms steps". Both premises fail. Catto's four-steps-per-period rule is for semi-implicit Euler *force* springs, and the same slides show soft constraints are implicit-Euler springs that are "always stable"; Jolt's motors are soft constraints, and Jolt's own bound is a validity range of (0, 0.5 × simulation frequency] with 20 Hz described as good *at 60 Hz* ([Catto GDC 2011](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_SoftConstraints_GDC2011.pdf); [Jolt Physics documentation](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html)). SIMBICON's own 3D biped ran explicit PD at 0.005 s, and Hämäläinen's 36-DOF active humanoids ran at **1/30 s** using ODE's velocity motors with sampled force limits of 50–300 N·m and the direct solver, noting that iterative solvers needed about 1/120 s for similar quality ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [Hämäläinen et al. 2014 preprint](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf); [Hämäläinen et al. 2015 preprint](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf)). The LocoTest bound of 0.9–1.6 ms is specific to the cartwheel controller's explicit torques at kd = 2·sqrt(kp) ([LocoTest PDF](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~kkyin/papers/locotest.pdf)). The correct premise is: explicit script-side torque PD at those gains needs about 1 ms steps; solver-side drives have run active characters at 5–33 ms. The runtime test then answers the question the derivation tried to: for k = 2000, c = 50 the drive error is **1.126 / 1.124 / 1.123°** at 60 / 120 / 240 Hz, and k = 10⁶ with zero damping is stable at 60 Hz, so this soft drive is tick-rate independent and stability is not the constraint. Stable PD, which the first edition proposed for "virtual forces, gravity compensation, the balance law's feed-forward", is a reformulation of the joint PD term that needs the next-step target and, for articulated bodies, `q̈` from `(M + K_d Δt) q̈ = …` in generalised coordinates; it does not apply to Jacobian-transpose feed-forward terms, and it was demonstrated only inside a custom generalised-coordinate simulator, so from GDScript over Jolt's maximal-coordinate solver "kd ≥ kp·Δt" does not transfer unmodified ([Tan, Liu, Turk 2011 PDF](https://www.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)). Under solver-side drives the swing-hip balance law needs no script torque at all: it is written into the target angle.

What remains of the tick-rate argument is contact fidelity and rendering. Jolt in Godot runs exactly one collision step per tick with no sub-stepping (`physics_system->Update(p_step, 1, …)`, `modules/jolt_physics/spaces/jolt_space_3d.cpp:196`); the project settings say high tick rates give "more accurate physics simulation, particularly for fast moving objects" and that CPU usage scales roughly with the rate (`doc/classes/ProjectSettings.xml:2734-2741`; [Fixing jitter, stutter and input lag](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/rendering/jitter_stutter.html)); 120 Hz doubles the drive-frequency headroom from 30 to 60 Hz; and, decisively for a first-person body, bone-following nodes force physics interpolation OFF since [PR #119509](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/119509) (`scene/3d/bone_attachment_3d.cpp:375`, `spring_bone_collision_3d.cpp:210`) because skeleton children move by deferred processing, and skeleton bone poses are not `Node3D` transforms, so the skinned `MeshInstance3D` is expected to step at tick rate whatever the project setting says; at 120 Hz that is likely invisible, at 60 Hz it is not (source and docs inference, to be observed on the first rendered build). The measured cost makes 120 Hz cheap for the character (2.5 % of the tick on four threads) and a community Jolt active-ragdoll addon reports the same practical range ("Stiff drives want 90 to 120 physics ticks per second. The addon works at 60 but the drive is softer") ([emre-f/godot-active-ragdoll README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emre-f/godot-active-ragdoll/main/README.md)). So the project keeps **120 Hz**, `max_physics_steps_per_frame 16`, `physics/common/physics_interpolation` on (interpolation disables the jitter fix, so `jitter_fix` reads 0), and a fixed tick at runtime because the hinge motor converts max impulse to torque through `estimate_physics_step()` (`jolt_hinge_joint_3d.cpp:49-56, 195-196, 239-240`) and Baumgarte tuning is step-dependent. The remaining Jolt settings to set deliberately are solver iterations (10 velocity / 2 position, friction needs ≥ 2), sleeping (0.03 m/s, 0.5 s), `continuous_cd` per fast body (`LinearCast`), and the three limits that bite at the top tiers: `limits/max_linear_velocity` **500 m/s**, `limits/max_angular_velocity` **2700°/s**, and `limits/world_boundary_shape_size` **2000 m** (`modules/jolt_physics/jolt_project_settings.cpp:37-73`; `doc/classes/ProjectSettings.xml:2758-2781`; the linear cap is applied to every body at creation, `jolt_body_3d.cpp:110`). Determinism, replay and the frame budget against the renderer are design requirements of their own, treated with destruction below.

### The behaviour layer: Euphoria's parameters as a checklist, with three attributions fixed

Euphoria's GTA V message schema, re-verified property by property this pass, remains the architecture to copy: every behaviour scales a shared per-region stiffness and damping and sets targets ([SHVDN Euphoria class list](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_euphoria.html)). `ConfigureBalance` steps 0.1 m high, predicts 0.2 s ahead, aborts above `BalanceAbortThreshold` **0.6** lean or when the COM comes within `GiveUpHeight` **0.5 m** of the lowest foot, calls balance regained below 0.3 m/s and 0.3 rad/s, budgets 100 steps and 50 s, falls by `RampDownStiffness`, and runs `OpposeGravityLegs/Ankles` 1.0 as gravity compensation; `LegStiffness` 12 sits on the 6–16 scale ([SHVDN ConfigureBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_configure_balance_helper.html)). `BodyBalance` throws the arms out above `AngVelThreshold` (1.2, 3.0, 1.2) rad/s with hand delays of 0.3–0.7 s, and its bracing is **off by default** because `BraceDistance` is −1 until set ≥ 0, a detail the first edition dropped; its parameter comments also record NaturalMotion's own drunk preset ("Unless drunk - DO NOT EXCEED 0.8" on `SomersaultAngleThreshold`, "Mmmmdrunk = 0.7 shoulder lean2 offset", "Mmmmdrunk 1.1 shoulder lean1 offset (+ve frankenstein)", shoulder twist 0.0), which is the closest primary drunk-balance data in either pass ([SHVDN BodyBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_body_balance_helper.html)). `HighFall` transitions to landing 0.3 s before impact, windmills at 7.9 rad/s, uses `LegStrength` 12, caps the orientation torque at 300 and calls falls recoverable only below **15 m/s** with bracing from 10 m/s; `BraceForImpact` reaches at 0.5 m, predicts 0.5 s, absorbs over 0.2 s and grabs with `GrabStrength` 40 for 2.5 s; `CatchFall` runs torso 9, legs 6, arms 15 ([SHVDN HighFallHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_high_fall_helper.html); [SHVDN BraceForImpactHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_brace_for_impact_helper.html); [SHVDN CatchFallHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_catch_fall_helper.html)). NaturalMotion's patents describe a per-part control frame pulled toward a target frame by a resilient connection or a virtual wrench with per-part permitted DOFs, and a DAG of nodes in which "physics nodes" may sit anywhere ([US9177409B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9177409B2/en); [US8730245B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8730245B2/en)); Reil's earlier CPG work (hips actuated, force-limited velocity-constraint actuators preferred, 60 evolved parameters) is the lineage, not an engineering source ([US7685081B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7685081B2/en); [Reil & Husbands 2002 PDF](https://users.sussex.ac.uk/~philh/pubs/reilhusbandsIEEE.pdf)). No primary source states Euphoria's simulation rate or per-character CPU budget; the second pass documented that negative across every reachable GTA IV, Backbreaker and Force Unleashed article ([Game Developer, GTA IV product piece](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/product-i-grand-theft-auto-iv-i-using-naturalmotion-s-euphoria); [Computer Graphics World, Sept 2008](https://www.cgw.com/Publications/CGW/2008/Volume-31-Issue-9-Sept-2008-/Force-To-Be-Reckoned-With.aspx)).

Three attributions change. EA's ragdoll patent schedules strength **per supervisor state**, not per collision: "when falling, a doll is restrained to about half the maximum strength of when running", "when staying down, the doll uses only a small percentage", "when getting up, the doll has nearly all of its available strength on standby", and its balance test is a support circle from shin or foot centres of mass against the averaged pelvis-and-torso COM with an animation-agreement sanity check ([US7403202B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7403202B1/en)). That is also what Overgrowth's per-type `ragdoll_strength` and Euphoria's per-behaviour stiffness do, so strength is scheduled by behaviour state (balance / fall / stay-down / get-up), not gated on contact events. Zordan et al. 2005 hold kp and kv fixed "for the entire transition" and apply the **0.1–0.2 s** delay to the switch of the desired pose toward the reaction clip; the temporary gain reduction belongs to Zordan & Hodgins 2002 via the survey and to SIMBICON's feedback-error-learning section (gain lowered, then raised "after a reaction-time delay (150ms)"); and Zordan 2005 is an offline pipeline (~1 minute per reaction, the motion search ≈ 70 % of compute), so only the delay and blend ideas transfer ([Zordan 2005 PDF mirror](http://strangepaths.com/forum/files/Zordan2005.pdf); [Geijtenbeek & Pronost 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03189.x)). Overgrowth's fall layer stands as the cheapest shipped design (three-pose blend from a 4 m proximity probe; `ragdoll_strength = max(0, min(0.8, 0.1·|v|) − ragdoll_limp_stun)`, with the lower clamp applied *after* the stun; injured strength `clamp(2 − 0.3·speed, 0.2, 1)·(12 − t)·0.1` capped at 0.9 and LIMP at 12 s; recovery after 1 s once `|v|² < 20`; sleep below 0.4 m/s), but its Bullet ERP/CFM ramp (`STOP_ERP = 0.2·s`, `CFM = 1 − s` on the angular rows of a D6 constraint) has **no Jolt equivalent as an angular soft limit**: Jolt's `SixDOFConstraint` keeps `mLimitsSpringSettings` for translation axes only and the module warns that angular limit softness, damping, restitution and ERP "will be ignored" (`SixDOFConstraint.h:81-83`; `jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:425-445, 724-726`), so the limp-to-active ramp is the `Position` motor's frequency, damping or torque limit, the same knob as the main drive ([aschar.as](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/aschar.as); [bulletworld.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Physics/bulletworld.cpp)). Falls and get-ups remain a Faloutsos chain (`balance → fall → default → rollover → prone-to-standing → balance`) ([Faloutsos 2001 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2001-siggraph-composable.pdf)), and the second pass adds the complete published landing verb: Ha, Ye and Liu 2012's airborne inertia planner and landing controller with a constant-deceleration virtual force `c̈_y = ½(ċ̄_y² − ċ_y²)/(c̄_y − c_y)` toward c̄_y = 0.4 m, a 3000 N cap, PID tracking with contacting limbs as viscous dampers, a roll law toward the leading foot, per-joint gains (hip 90/20, knee 60/13, ankle 15/6, spine 300/60 and 180/40, shoulder 120/27, elbow 60/13.5), tested from heights up to 10.6 m and 6 m/s, in which a passive ragdoll "caused much more damage on the neck and the spine" measured as joint constraint force ([Ha, Ye, Liu 2012 PDF](https://www.dropbox.com/s/6qh29mlcty5xqqb/2012_landing.pdf)). Hämäläinen's sampling controllers show hands-on-wall support emerging from a balance cost with a head-contact damage term ([Hämäläinen et al. 2015 preprint](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf)), and Shiratori's trip responses give the elevating (5–25 % of swing) versus lowering (55–75 %) rule for a foot that catches a ledge ([Shiratori et al. 2009 PDF](http://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/2009/8/sca2009_trip.pdf)). Believability in Euphoria came from asymmetry parameters (`HandsDelayMin/Max`, `LegAsymmetry`, `PredictionTimeVariance`), which cost nothing to copy.

## The opening: one toddler signature, weak and slow, with an added delay

### What the precedents teach, re-read

The clumsy-locomotion canon still splits into three families, and only the indirect one maps onto a PD ragdoll. Direct per-limb actuation (QWOP's thigh and calf keys; Baby Steps' one trigger per leg, "Pull a trigger tight to lift and bend one of his knees, and release it bit by bit to swing out his lower leg"; Manual Samuel; Octodad, whose players "would forget which mode they were in") gives the purest embodiment but demands concentration "in the game's first few minutes" ([Engadget on Baby Steps](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/baby-steps-preview-serious-gameplay-in-a-silly-walking-sim-150008737.html); [Octodad post-mortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/octodad-dadliest-catch-post-mortem-pt-3-design); [QWOP, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QWOP)). Which stick does what in Baby Steps is disputed between Engadget (left stick supplies momentum), Reverse Shot (right stick positions the lifted foot) and Eurogamer, so no single mapping should be quoted until verified in-game ([Reverse Shot on Baby Steps](https://reverseshot.org/features/3411/baby_steps); [Eurogamer review](https://www.eurogamer.net/baby-steps-review)). Indirect control of a self-balancing rig is the family this design uses: Sumotori Dreams, where input "nudges it off-balance" and Game Developer magazine's 2007 column diagnosed the look precisely, the wrestlers "are always just a tad too slow to compensate for uneven footing or an awkward pose", a designer-grade description of delay-induced clumsiness ([Fun-Motion on Sumotori](https://www.fun-motion.com/physics-games/sumotori-dreams/); [Game Developer, Sept 2007, archive.org OCR](https://archive.org/stream/GDM_September2007/GDM_September2007_djvu.txt)); and GTA IV's drunk state, which Torsten Reil (co-founder of NaturalMotion, mislabelled "Rockstar Games CEO" by TechCrunch) described as "all synthesized on the CPU … a completely different outcome every time you play" ([TechCrunch 2008](https://techcrunch.com/2008/02/21/gta-iv-drunkenness-to-use-dynamic-physics-engine/); [NaturalMotion, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NaturalMotion)). The third family, a normal controller with a separate balance channel (Death Stranding: "If Sam is tilting left you must press R2 to pull him further right", hold both to brace; the "above 60 kg" tilt threshold is a PowerPyx guide figure, the official beginner's guide gives only white/yellow/red bands), is the expert layer that survives into the competent game ([PowerPyx](https://www.powerpyx.com/death-stranding-how-to-balance-cargo/); [Death Stranding Director's Cut Beginner's Guide, Kojima Productions](https://www.kojimaproductions.jp/en/death-stranding-directors-cut-beginners-guide)). Two design findings recur: Cuzzillo found that "manually controlling the lifts gave you more control and felt better" than automatic stepping, yet Baby Steps refuses upgrades because the team believed "the core gameplay was deep enough", keeps "the required stuff pretty easy" and leans into getting lost ("By design, it's very easy to walk in a complete circle") ([Game Rant, Baby Steps interview](https://gamerant.com/baby-steps-interview-gamble-walking-gameplay-only/); [Game Rant on refusing systems](https://gamerant.com/baby-steps-developers-systems-simple-limit-why/); [GamesRadar on Cuzzillo's GDC 2026 talk](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/open-world/ignoring-valves-half-life-lessons-baby-steps-dev-says-trolling-players-with-level-design-is-important-actually/)). The cautionary datum stays Metal Gear Solid V's prologue, about 45 minutes by a forum author's estimate and unskippable ([Steam discussion on the MGSV prologue](https://steamcommunity.com/app/287700/discussions/0/527273983050716645/)); Fallout 3's toddler section is the contrast (playpen, no Pip-Boy, no third-person view, ends in a choice), but **no source gives its duration**, so the first edition's "a few minutes" is withdrawn ([GameBanshee walkthrough](https://www.gamebanshee.com/fallout3/walkthrough/growingup.php)). Impairment that removes agency reads as padding; impairment that demands skill reads as the game.

The grammar therefore keeps one mapping whose meaning never switches: WASD and mouse are always intent, and competence changes the body's *response*. At s = 0 the pelvis authority is near zero and the stick is a Sumotori nudge to a weak, slow, late balance controller; by the end of the opening the authority reaches its human-nominal value and the same inputs produce ordinary first-person walking. A Death-Stranding lean pair on Q/E is offered as an optional expert channel, never required, because Jusant's playtests found extra limb controls "too complicated" and Human: Fall Flat rejected independent per-arm sticks as "really messy" ([Game Developer on Jusant](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/unlocking-the-catharsis-of-climbing-in-meditative-puzzler-jusant); [TheGamer, Sakalauskas interview](https://www.thegamer.com/human-fall-flat-interview-tomas-sakalauskas/)).

### The competence table, rebuilt from one signature

The first edition's table mixed drunk, ataxic, toddler and reinforcement-learning signatures and inverted its torque source. The second edition draws the opening from the **toddler** literature alone, because it is the one with a learning arc, and keeps drunk sway and fatigue as separate channels. The toddler evidence, re-verified from the PMC full texts: novice 12-month walkers take 1,456 steps and fall **31.5 times per hour**, are in motion 33.1 % of the time, and take a per-infant mean of 69.2 steps before a fall (the ratio of group means is 46 steps per fall; the pooled 12–19-month ratio is about 136), with 46 % of bouts only 1–3 steps long ([Adolph et al. 2012, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3591461/)). Step width narrows **0.19 → 0.15 → 0.12 m** over the first 12 weeks of walking (the source table mislabels the unit as cm), upper-limb elevation falls from 78–87° ("high guard") to **47–49°**, left-hip flexion range falls 42.5° → 29.1° (right hip 40.5° → 33.4°, not significant), while stride time (1.02–1.05 s) and step length (0.18 m) do not change ([Gimunová et al. 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8744759/)). Reciprocal arm swing and heel strike are present "in most children by the age of eighteen months" ([Sutherland 1980 PDF](http://www.analisedemarcha.com/papers/historia/The%20development%20of%20mature%20gait%20-%201980.pdf)). Knee-to-knee distance falls 29.3 → 25.6 → 23.7 % of height across 13–37 months and is the last parameter still maturing; hand height drops 12.7 → 6.1 % of height ([Tsuyuki et al. 2023, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9941512/)). Toddlers under six months of walking show "virtually no power … at the ankle joint prior to foot-off" and balance with hip and knee extensors ([Hallemans et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2004.07.010)); the inverted-pendulum energy exchange "is not implemented at the onset of unsupported locomotion" and is present in all infants after about one month ([Ivanenko et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.01214); [Bisi & Stagni 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2014.11.017)); dimensionless velocity follows β(t) = 0.45·(1 − e^(−0.05 t)) with t in months ([Vaughan et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-003-1635-x)). Sensorimotor delay is the cleanest deterministic wobble: with an *added* feedback delay of 20 ms everyone balances, at 100 ms one participant reached the limit once, at **≥ 200 ms every participant exceeded the balance limits**, at 400 ms people stayed upright 64 % of the first minute rising to 97 % after 100 minutes (time constant ≈ 28–33 min), and the inherent loop is about 100–160 ms, for a learnable total up to 500–560 ms ([Rasman et al. 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8480973/)). The perceptual anchor for torque comes from Learning to Get Up: "get-up strategies ending with torque limits ranging from **40 % to 60 %** are usually perceived as more natural than those terminating with high torque limits (above 70 %)", trained with kp = T and kd = kp/10 at 40 Hz over an 800 Hz simulation on a 1.5 m, 38.3 kg character, with a retiming stage κ ∈ [0.2, 0.8] making motions "up to 5× slower" ([Learning to Get Up PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00307)). The SI anchor comes from SIMBICON: the basic walk collapses below a **90 N·m** torque limit, 370 N·m suffices for every motion but the fast run, on a 90 kg planar model ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)).

Three rows of the first edition are gone or moved. The "1.10 → 1.43 m/s" speed row paired ten SCA14 patients with healthy *elderly* (69 ± 5 y) and 12-month bout statistics; the "stride-time CoV 0.07 → 0.03" row was Kroneberg's stride-*velocity* CoV (0.069 vs 0.028; stride-length CoV 0.046 vs 0.019; no stride-time CoV is reported); and the "arms abducted 80° → reciprocal swing 30°" row joined toddler elevation to elderly arm-swing range ([Kroneberg et al. 2024, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11174553/)). Ataxia remains the right reference for a *coordination* impairment (wide base, prolonged double support, temporal variability of intra-limb coordination), and if a pathological variability endpoint is ever wanted it should be cited as such ([Buckley 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2017.11.024); [Ilg 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awl376)). The "drunk" channel is separate: alcohol at 0.11 % BrAC barely changes forward gait (stride time 1.12 → 1.13 s, cadence unchanged) but multiplies standing sway area 3.8× once BAC passes about 0.6 mg/mL, biased medial-lateral with unchanged frequency ([Sci Rep 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9637089/); [Kubo 1989](https://doi.org/10.3109/00016488909139056); [Noda 2004](https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.98.3.873-887)); its controller form is a low-pass ML bias on the COM proxy plus Euphoria's drunk `BodyBalance` preset, and no adult intoxication study reports stride-time variability against BAC, so "drunk walk" in games is exaggerated relative to the literature. The fatigue channel is the Three-Compartment Controller's state- and time-dependent torque bounds ([Cheema et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08583)). Kingdom Come's "sprint disabled while drunk" is not in the cited wiki page and is dropped ([KCD wiki, Drunkenness](https://kingdom-come-deliverance.fandom.com/wiki/Drunkenness)).

What "incompetent" should look like has two sourced faces from robotics that the toddler data lacks: policies trained without a delay model showed "violent shaking of the limbs, probably due to not accounting for various delays properly", and over-penalised policies "stand still" ([Hwangbo et al. 2019, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08652)); Adolph names three novice archetypes, "steppers" who take small conservative steps, "fallers" who lurch forward while their feet hurry to catch up, and "twisters" who swing each leg around like a compass, and gives load and slope thresholds (a 15 % body-weight load makes infants take slower, smaller steps; a 6° slope is "barely an impediment", 24° changes gait, the most skilled traverse 40° slopes and 28 cm drop-offs) that the consequence layer can reuse ([Adolph & Hoch 2019, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6320716/)). Skill acquisition "stutter[s] into infants' repertoires", oscillating between expression and non-expression, which argues for an s(t) that is noisy at the minute scale rather than a smooth ramp ([Adolph, Hoch & Cole 2018, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6145857/)). Krakauer's split of skill into selection speed and execution precision maps onto "aim your step early, steady your step late" ([Krakauer et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/cphy.c170043)). Bril & Brenière's claim that velocity gains after five months come from cadence is contested by Bisi & Stagni's IMU cohort, which found step length dominant after two months, so the first edition's "phase two unlocks speed via cadence" is presented as one of two readings ([Bril & Brenière 1992](https://doi.org/10.1080/00222895.1992.9941606); [Bisi & Stagni 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2014.11.017)).

### Falls cost nothing here, and the assist path is a requirement

The opening's falls are free by design and by evidence. Of 563 infant falls in free play, 4 % produced fussing and 2 % medical attention, infants were "back at play within seconds" and falling "did not alter infants' subsequent behavior" ([Han & Adolph 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.13069)); in a simulated robot trained on infant paths, "zero penalty for errors led to the best performance for both learning and generalization" ([Ossmy et al. 2024, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10922068/)); Embark shows a Leaper failing a landing, tumbling and getting up as the thing that "make[s] the robot look real" ([4Gamer NDC26 report](https://www.4gamer.net/games/652/G065211/20260616016/)). So the opening has no death and no retry cost: a fall is an Overgrowth-style ragdoll, a 1 s recovery timer and a get-up, and progress is legible in the body (narrowing base, arms coming down, fewer falls per hundred steps, a steadier camera), not a bar. What advances s remains a design decision with two sourced options, use-based growth (Kingdom Come, Dying Light 2's parkour XP) with a gate against grinding, or a scripted curve tied to distance walked ([Stevivor on Dying Light 2 skill trees](https://stevivor.com/news/dying-light-2-details-new-skill-trees/); [Gravity Rush Wiki, wiki-only](https://gravityrush.fandom.com/wiki/Upgrades)). The opening must satisfy Foddy's flavours (aim for "Nearly There But Not Quite", avoid "Start Over" and "Going Nowhere") and George Fan's rules (teach by doing, at most eight words on screen, adaptive prompts only after N falls) ([foddy.net, Eleven Flavors of Frustration](https://www.foddy.net/blog/2017/01/eleven-flavors-of-frustration/); [Game Developer, George Fan's ten tips](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan)). Because the opening deliberately impairs control, an **assist path is a requirement, decided before Milestone 2**: an "authority assist" slider that raises pelvis authority and shortens the added delay, in the manner of Celeste's Assist Mode ("a couple of days' work"), Ghostrunner 2's block and stamina "which allow less experienced players to stop, take a breath", Skate's Flick-It accessibility toggle (snippet-only) and Meta's three-preset structure (Recommended / Comfortable / Advanced) ([Celeste, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_(video_game)); [Game Developer, Ghostrunner 2 deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/ghostrunner-2-deep-dive); [Meta Horizon, Locomotion Best Practices](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/locomotion-best-practices/)). Shipping RL checkpoints as skill levels still has no shipped precedent: early policies are seed-sensitive, and the one stable interpolation result (Rocca et al. 2025, PACMCGIT) needs a graph-regularised training scheme ([Heess et al. 2017, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.02286); [Rocca et al. 2025 project page](https://michelerocca.github.io/projects/policy-space_linterp/)); the detuned classical controller is deterministic, tunable per parameter, and the recommendation.

## Powers gated by consequences the rig can actually measure

### The biomechanics that set the thresholds, re-verified

All numbers use m = 70 kg and g = 9.81 m/s². A trained team-sport adult approaches a stop at **7.35 m/s** and, from the start of braking, needs 6.86 m and 1.50 s with an average deceleration of −4.44 m/s² (−3.88 early, −5.99 late) and a peak of −8.48 m/s², peak horizontal braking force −8.44 N/kg and average −4.36 N/kg ([Harper et al. 2022, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474351/)). Usain Bolt's instantaneous maximum was **12.34 m/s at 67.9 m** in Berlin 2009 (12.32–12.33 m/s at 52.5 m in an independent re-analysis), his 0–10 m speed was 5.77 m/s once the 0.146 s reaction time is subtracted (the 5.26 m/s in the first edition included it), and a constant-force model bounds his first-step acceleration at 9.5 m/s² with 2.62 kW of peak power; anything above about 12.5 m/s is superhuman ([Graubner & Nixdorf 2011, World Athletics PDF](https://worldathletics.org/download/downloadnsa?filename=31514203-6724-455e-906c-ef931384c656.pdf&urlslug=biomechanical-analysis-of-the-sprint-and-hurd); [Maćkała & Mero 2013, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3661886/); [Bioengineering 2023, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10669785/); [Hernández Gómez et al. 2013, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.3947)). Top speed is limited by the ground-contact time available to apply force, not by maximum force: at top speed forward running F_peak = 3.62 BW per leg with T_c = 0.108 s, and removing the muscle force–velocity limit in a forward-dynamics model buys only 15–22 % more speed, so superhuman speed needs changed physics (more force per contact or shorter contacts), which justifies keeping speed tiers separate from strength tiers ([Weyand et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20093666/); [Miller et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22035638/)). With a 0.22 s simple reaction time and `d = v·t_react + v²/(2a)`, stopping distance at Harper's *average* braking (a = 4.4 m/s², the constant the first edition named but did not use) and peak braking (8.5) is 7.7 / 4.8 m from 7.35 m/s, 13.5 / 8.1 m from 10, 19.9 / 11.7 m from 12.34, 28.6 / 16.5 m from 15, 49.4 / 27.9 m from 20 and 108.0 / 59.5 m from 30 m/s ([Woods et al. 2015, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4374455/)). Harper's 6.86 m contains no reaction distance and implies 3.9 m/s² of uniform deceleration, so it is consistent with the average column rather than a validation of it. A fit adult's countermovement jump raises the centre of mass **0.4 m** (Bobbert's 82 kg model, 49 W/kg), traceurs reach 0.59–0.65 m and power athletes 0.53 m, competitive female team-sport players 0.26–0.30 m, and standing long jumps run 1.95–2.12 m for ordinary young men against 2.83 m for traceurs; the first edition's non-primary "0.7 m is elite" is replaced by these peer-reviewed anchors ([Bobbert 2013, PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0071209); [Grosprêtre & Lepers 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26267256/); [Sampaio et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41562740/); [García-Sánchez et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41764994/)).

The square–cube argument survives and gains its principled statement. For a fixed-size body, "jump N times higher" multiplies take-off velocity by sqrt(N) and landing energy and leg force by N; "run N times faster" multiplies kinetic energy and stopping distance by N²; bone, tendon and pulley strengths are cross-section properties that do not change unless buffed. Mammalian skeletons operate at 25–50 % of failure stress, a safety factor of **2–4** held roughly constant from 0.1 to 300 kg by posture, so a superhuman whose forces rise N-fold without N-fold tissue strength has a safety factor of (2–4)/N and fractures once N exceeds 2–4, consistent with the 3–5 m fracture band below ([Biewener 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2251499/)). The Achilles already runs at its material limit in maximal jumping (in-vivo loads to 9 kN = 12.5 BW against a quasi-static failure stress of 71–86 MPa), so tendon strength must scale with the power tier as bone does ([Komi 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2081741/); [Wren et al. 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11240060/)). This reasoning is dimensional, cited to Bobbert's "Borelli's law" discussion and Biewener, not to a "square-cube law" encyclopaedia page.

### The damage model, rebuilt on quantities that exist on the rig

The first edition read contact normal impulses through `PhysicsDirectBodyState3D.get_contact_impulse` on a `RigidBody3D` with `contact_monitor` enabled. The rig is `PhysicalBone3D`, which has no `contact_monitor` or `max_contacts_reported` member (`doc/classes/PhysicalBone3D.xml`; `physical_bone_3d.cpp:1199-1270` sets only mass, friction, bounce, gravity and damping), and under Jolt contact impulses "are estimated ahead of time … only … accurate in cases where the two bodies in question are not colliding with any other bodies", a condition a fifteen-body ragdoll on a floor never meets ([Godot docs, Using Jolt Physics](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html)). Whether `PhysicsServer3D.body_set_max_contacts_reported` on a bone RID delivers contacts to `_integrate_forces` is untested (`godot_runtime_verification.md` §7). The model therefore uses three inputs that exist. First, each body's **per-tick velocity change**: `Δv = v_n − v_{n−1}` read from the `PhysicalBone3D` node each physics tick (the node exposes `linear_velocity` and `angular_velocity`; the runtime test read `angular_velocity` every tick), giving `a_mean = |Δv| / Δt` per tick and, summed over a contact window, the head's and chest's deceleration history. Second, **joint constraint forces and torques** from `get_applied_force/torque` (PR #123875): the hip, knee and ankle lambdas are the leg loads, the neck lambda is the head-on-body load, and the grab joint's lambda against its force limit is grip failure; Ha, Ye and Liu define joint stress exactly this way, as joint constraint force, when they show a passive ragdoll landing "caused much more damage on the neck and the spine" than a controlled one ([Ha, Ye, Liu 2012 PDF](https://www.dropbox.com/s/6qh29mlcty5xqqb/2012_landing.pdf)). Third, contact impulses, exact or not, locate the hit (which body, which surface, which normal).

Classification is duration-aware. Whole-body deceleration is compared with the Eiband boundary rather than a flat 46 g: the voluntary, uninjured limit is **45 g for a 0.044 s plateau, 40 g at 0.1 s, 25 g at 0.2 s, 13 g at 0.6 s, 10 g at 1.6 s** (full harness, ≈ 500 g/s onset; onset above 1,000 g/s produces shock above 30 g; a building-fall survivor was estimated at ≈ 200 g with moderate injury), so "Stapp's 46 g over 1.4 s" in the first edition conflated the momentary peak with the total stop and overstated sustained tolerance three- to fourfold ([Eiband 1959, NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19980228043/downloads/19980228043.pdf)). Chest deceleration is compared with FMVSS 208's 60 g "except for intervals whose cumulative duration is not more than 3 milliseconds" and its 63 mm deflection limit (unmodelled); head injury with HIC15 ≤ 700 computed from the head body's a(t), with the Zhang 25 / 50 / 80 % MTBI thresholds of 66 / 82 / 106 g and 4,600 / 5,900 / 7,900 rad/s² (confirmed through two citing papers, not the paper itself) and with Rowson & Duma's combined probability `CP = 1/(1 + exp(−(−10.2 + 0.0433·a + 0.000873·α − 0.00000092·a·α)))` for a smooth dazed state, where α is the head body's angular acceleration from its per-tick `angular_velocity` change ([49 CFR 571.208](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2023-title49-vol6/xml/CFR-2023-title49-vol6-sec571-208.xml); [Xiong et al. 2013, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3820257/); [Rowson & Duma 2013, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3624001/)). Leg loads are compared **per leg** with the tier's tissue limits: foot/ankle 50 % fracture at 3.7–8.3 kN (calcaneus first; frail female specimens at the low end), plantar impact 50 % at 6.8 kN neutral, acetabulum 5.7 kN, femoral neck 7.6 kN, tibia 7.5–11.3 kN, the 10 kN regulatory femur limit, and a mid-shaft femur bending moment of 240–562 N·m ([Funk et al. 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12596644/); [Gallenberger et al. 2013, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3861818/); [Rupp et al. 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17096226/); [Zain Ul Abidin et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41769460/); [Kennedy et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17230260/)). Death is a function of computed head and chest deceleration and HIC, with the pedestrian curves kept only as a **km/h sanity check**: Rosén & Sander's `P = 1/(1 + exp(6.9 − 0.090·v_kmh))` gives 1.5 % at 30 km/h, 8.3 % at 50, 35.4 % at 70, 57.4 % at 80 and 89.1 % at 100 km/h (50 % at 76.7 km/h) and the pooled meta-analysis of twenty studies gives 5 % at 30, 50 % at 59 and 90 % at 80 km/h, so the two bracket lethality; both describe a car front, a population and a second ground impact, and neither can respond to the compliance distance s, which is why the first edition's use of Rosén as the death draw made braced and rigid stops equally lethal ([Rosén & Sander 2009](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/pedestrian_fatality_risk_function_car_impact_speed_rosen.pdf); [Hussain et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31176144/)). Secondary impacts are summed: high-rise bodies are deflected up to 1.5 m and strike twice ([Bell et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40133676/)). Surface and orientation multiply the outcome from the clinical series (hard surface OR 2.7, head-first OR 16.7, anterior 10.6, lateral 11.1; soft ground lowers ISS by 4.8 points) ([Lapostolle et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15942337/); [Fujii et al. 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8324820/)). Two shipped formulas still bracket the *presentation*: Source's linear damage above a safe speed with HL2 constants 526.5 / 922.5 u/s (20 / 60 ft at 600 u/s² gravity; every non-HL2 Source game uses 580 / 1024 at 800), and Minecraft's elytra rule `damage = 10·Δv_horizontal − 3` for vertical surfaces only (wiki-only; velocity units unconfirmed in code), generalised as `damage = k·max(0, |v_rel·n| − v_safe)`; Tribes: Ascend adds a trajectory-angle test above 50 km/h and Halo's Acrophobia skull a speed-gated bump death (both wiki-only) ([shareddefs.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/shareddefs.h); [Minecraft Wiki, Elytra](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Elytra); [Tribes Depot](https://wiki.tribesdepot.com/wiki/Tribes:_Ascend/Standard_Equipment); [Halopedia, Health](https://www.halopedia.org/Health)). Copying Source's *height intent* (safe 6 m) is withdrawn: it put zero damage at the height the biomechanics call fracture-likely.

The roll is no longer a rule multiplier. The landing verb is Ha, Ye and Liu's controller: an airborne inertia planner choosing intermediate poses so the landing condition `θ(T) = a·v_y + b·v_z + c·ω_x + d` (feet-first a = −0.01, b = −0.06, c = −0.03, d = 0.45) is met, then a constant-deceleration virtual force toward c̄_y = 0.4 m capped at 3000 N with contacting limbs as viscous dampers and a roll toward the leading foot, tested from heights up to 10.6 m ([Ha, Ye, Liu 2012 PDF](https://www.dropbox.com/s/6qh29mlcty5xqqb/2012_landing.pdf)); Ha & Liu's later contact planner chooses "from a single step to recover a gentle nudge, to a rolling motion to break a high-speed fall" ([Ha & Liu 2015 PDF](https://www.dropbox.com/s/s50almtargco17c/2015_falling.pdf)). The force reduction then comes from the longer stopping path the physics computes, and Puddle & Maulder's 0.57× peak force and −63 % loading rate are the *validation targets*, not the discount. A two-phase leg (ankle-dominated spring setting the peak, then a knee-dominated damper dissipating the energy) is the right abstraction for the landing controller ([Toussaint & Schepens 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38422773/)).

### The grab: a 2 m/s catch, a slipping constraint, and a level rule

Maximum grip is **480.6 N per hand** for men aged 20–40 and 292.8 N for women (n = 558), experienced climbers hang two-handed for 77 s, the A2 pulley carries about 3× fingertip force in a crimp with a rupture threshold near 400 N (secondary), and Vigouroux 2006 owns the "36× higher in crimp than slope" figure the first edition attributed to Schweizer ([Bohannon & Magasi 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24729356/); [Yu et al. 2023, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10433161/); [Schweizer 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11165286/); [Vigouroux et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18267349/)). A body caught by the arms over d metres of shoulder give needs `F = m·v²/(2d) + m·g`: at 2 m/s (a 0.20 m drop) 0.97–1.15 kN for d = 0.5–0.3 m, at 3 m/s (0.46 m) 1.32–1.74 kN, at 4 m/s (0.82 m) 1.81–2.55 kN, at 5 m/s 2.4–3.6 kN, at 10 m/s 7.7–12.4 kN. Two maximal grips total **0.96 kN**, so a hard catch at human strength is possible only at about **2 m/s**, within 0.2 m of the apex; the first edition's 3–4 m/s rested on an uncited "1–2 BW eccentric arm capacity" and on harness forces (2.5–4 kN through a rope system) that describe a rope, not a hand ([Petzl, forces in a real fall](https://www.petzl.com/US/en/Sport/Forces-at-work-in-a-real-fall)). No study measures the hand forces of a parkour arm jump; the ledge-catch table is derived, and a one-arm hang has no duration norm and should be modelled as a strength gate (≈ 1 BW on one hand, above the 480 N single-hand maximum) rather than a timer ([Kozin et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32307465/)).

The design consequence is a redesigned verb. The grab attaches each hand body to the surface with a 6DOF joint whose linear `Position` drive has `PARAM_LINEAR_DRIVE_FORCE_LIMIT` equal to the grip budget (0.96 kN at V0, scaled with the durability tier); when the load exceeds the budget the motor saturates and the constraint **yields rather than breaks**: the hands stay attached while the body decelerates at `(F_limit/m − g)` ≈ 4.5 m/s², so a 2 m/s arrival stops in 0.45 m, 3 m/s in 1.0 m, 4 m/s in 1.8 m, provided a graspable face continues below the ledge (a rough wall, a pipe, a stepped façade) along which the hand bodies can slide; when a hand leaves the graspable volume the joint is released. That is what the harness data actually models (a rope that stretches and slips through a belay device), and it has shipped analogues in Celeste's `ClimbSlipSpeed = 30` (a tired climber slides rather than falls) and Human: Fall Flat's sticky hands ([Player.cs, Celeste](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NoelFB/Celeste/master/Source/Player/Player.cs); [Worthplaying on Human: Fall Flat](https://worthplaying.com/article/2017/2/23/reviews/102561-pc-review-human-fall-flat/)). The level rule follows: in the V1 areas, where 3 m ledges are reachable, every ledge that can be jumped for has either a graspable face of ≥ 1 m below it or an intermediate ledge within 0.5 m, so "grab or land soft" is honest; the rescue for a 3 m *drop* is the landing controller, not the grab, until the durability tier raises the grip budget (a 7.7 m/s arrival with d = 0.5 m needs 4.8 kN, five human grips). A Celeste-style shared stamina meter (110 units; climbing up burns 100 in 2.2 s, hanging still 10 per second, a climb-jump costs 27.5) drains while hanging with the 77 s two-hand hang as the human anchor; Jusant's playtests rejected per-arm gauges and non-regenerating chunks ([Game Informer on Jusant, snippet](https://gameinformer.com/2023/11/29/the-challenging-climb-to-make-jusant)).

### Strength: rigid impactors break brick, bodies do not

Half-inch gypsum board fails at 489 N in flexure and is penetrated by a 67 J soft impact (12 in drop of the ASTM E695 body), two layers of 5/8-inch Type X need 305–408 J (54–72 in), and a residential Grade 10 door assembly is rated to survive two 80 J blows from a rigid 150 mm ram, the top grade two 200 J blows ([Gypsum Association GA-235](https://www.americangypsum.com/sites/default/files/2022-01/ga-235_gypsum_board_typical_mechanical_and_physical_properties.pdf); [ASTM F476-84, archive.org OCR](https://archive.org/stream/gov.law.astm.f476.1984/astm.f476.1984_djvu.txt)). A 72.9 kg granite boulder breaks through a single-leaf 1 m × 1 m brick wall at translational kinetic energies "as low as 500 J" (3.7 m/s, 270 N·s) by local shear ([Mavrouli et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences14090226)). The first edition compared that boulder threshold with whole-body momentum and thereby had an unpowered sprinter (514 N·s, 1,891 J) breaking brick at tier zero while its own text said a human "cannot break masonry at all". The resolution is physical: the boulder is rigid and fails the wall over a small contact patch; a body is compliant (0.1–0.3 m of chest or arm compression), distributes load, and the rigid mass actually in contact is small (2.9 kg for a fist). So the bond graph's `Damage = Impulse / Hardness` test reads the contact impulse the *wall shard* receives from the striking part, whose effective mass is a game parameter (a few kg for a fist, 5–10 kg for a shoulder): a 7 kg shoulder at 7.35 m/s delivers about 51 N·s, a fifth of brick's 270 N·s, and a 2.9 kg fist at 9 m/s about 26 N·s; the 500 J / 270 N·s threshold applies to thrown objects, ground pound and an armoured fist. Effective mass grows with joint stiffness, because a stiffer chain transmits more of the body's momentum through the shoulder joint into the contact, so the durability tiers that lock the rig at impact are also what lets a body count as a boulder; in the limit a rigid 70 kg body at 7.35 m/s is 514 N·s, above brick. Blast's own recipe for a body that should pass through rather than bounce, "Kinematics Max Contact Impulse … set it somewhere around Hardness × Health", is the engine-side form of the top tier ([Blast UE4 settings](https://archive.docs.nvidia.com/gameworks/content/gameworkslibrary/blast/1.1/authoring_docs/BlastUe4_BlastSettings.html)). The hand is gated separately: no cadaver number for an intact fifth metacarpal was reached, fixation constructs fail at 365–598 N in bending, and a boxer's 3.4 kN punch already exceeds those by an order of magnitude, so a **hand-durability** tier precedes masonry ([Allen et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41584052/)).

| V5 | 20 m + | landing is an attack; ground cracks | world reacts | **Ground pound**; consequence begins to move to the world | Prototype shockwaves (fan wiki); Saints Row IV landing shockwaves (Wikipedia) |
| V6 | sustained boost / flight | any height | none to the body | consequence lives in the world | Crackdown 3 Escape Velocity (Shacknews) |

### Ladder H, horizontal: speed against stopping and impact

Braking is friction- and strength-limited, so it is bought separately; otherwise stopping distance grows as the square of speed and ordinary rooms become lethal, which has a level-design counterpart: Doom Eternal's rooms had to grow with the player's speed ("Too big, you actually feel slow. Too small, you're frustrated") ([Game Developer, Doom Eternal Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-evolving-the-combat-design-of-id-software-s-i-doom-eternal-i-)).

The first edition's H2 "capture-step stop" is withdrawn: at 12.34 m/s with h ≈ 1 m the Coros formula gives an 8.7 m single step, Coros caps steps at 0.6 L, and the capture point is a balance concept ([Pratt et al. 2006 PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cga/legs/Pratt_Goswami_Humanoids2006.pdf)). Self-arrest is a braking-force budget per stance (Harper's −4.36 N/kg average and −8.44 N/kg peak scaled by tier, with the braking-step GRF of 40–60 N/kg in the first 50 ms as the ceiling of what a leg delivers), plus a raised k_V ([Harper et al. 2022, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474351/)). The inertia asymmetry is now stated once: Mirror's Edge chose "slow to build, fast to lose" and removed stopping inertia because it "would make the gameplay irritating" (Wikipedia citing IGN 2008); the brief's "speed before brakes" is the reverse, fast to build and slow to lose, and Celeste's shipped constants are the analogue, `RunAccel = 1000` against `RunReduce = 400` so excess speed decays at 40 % of the rate it builds, while Wipeout 2097 softened wall grinding because instant stops "w[ere] considered too harsh" ([Mirror's Edge, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror%27s_Edge); [Player.cs, Celeste](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NoelFB/Celeste/master/Source/Player/Player.cs); [Wipeout 2097, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wipeout_2097)). Titanfall's pilots reach "up to 30 mph, well over double a pilot's sprint speed" (13.4 m/s), which places it at H1, not at the 30 m/s H4 row where the first edition cited it ([Game Developer, Titanfall controls interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designer-interview-getting-i-titanfall-i-s-controls-just-right)). Apex Legends' 25.1 m/s skydive cap and 10.3 m/s super-glide are the ballpark of "superhuman but no damage" speeds a Respawn game tolerates without a damage model (wiki-only) ([Apex Legends Wiki, Movement](https://apexlegends.wiki.gg/wiki/Movement)).

### Ladder S, strength: load against balance and world reaction

S0 grabs and lifts small objects and punches through drywall (489 N flexure, 67 J penetration; a 2.9 kg fist at 9 m/s is 26 N·s). S1 lifts heavy loads that shift balance and lengthen recovery: Adolph's 15 % body-weight rule for slower, smaller steps, Dark Souls' 25 / 50 % load tiers with frame-counted rolls (Fextralife, provenance unattributed), Death Stranding's tilt (guide-derived 60 kg; official bands only) ([Adolph & Hoch 2019, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6320716/); [Fextralife, Equip Load](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Equip_Load); [PowerPyx](https://www.powerpyx.com/death-stranding-how-to-balance-cargo/)). S2 shoulder-charges double drywall (≈ 400 J at 3.4 m/s) and breaks residential doors (two 80 J blows at Grade 10, a compliant shoulder delivering less than the rigid ram), and throws objects that raise the world-response state. S3 breaks masonry by a *rigid* impactor at 500 J / 270 N·s, with the hand-durability tier before it. S4 is impact tolerance for self-inflicted knockback from ground pound and charge (the first edition's "Resilience" label is dropped: Crackdown 3's Strength L4 Resilience "improves resistance to effects of Fire, Cold, Electricity and Chimera", elemental resistance, not impact tolerance) ([Shacknews, Crackdown 3 skills](https://www.shacknews.com/article/109880/skills-abilities-and-leveling-fast-in-crackdown-3)). S5–S6 lift vehicles and buildings and leave permanent world-state changes ([Wikipedia, Hulk: Ultimate Destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Incredible_Hulk:_Ultimate_Destruction)). In the engine every S tier is the same Blast-style hardness comparison, but the impulse is the striking part's, and hardness values come from the drywall, door and brick anchors above so the tiers fall out of the same numbers.

### The top tier: consequence moves to the world, on its own precedents

Once V6/H6/S6 make the body unhurtable, the design's own proposition is that the world becomes the thing that can be hurt, measured by what the player breaks, whether or not an enemy caused it. The precedents are shipped superhero games, not Megaton Rainfall's mission structure, which the brief excludes: Superman Returns (2006) gave Metropolis a damage meter ("if too much damage is done to the city during a mission … then the game ends") while keeping Superman invincible, and Hulk: Ultimate Destruction built its threat meter on collateral (both wiki-only) ([Superman Returns, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman_Returns_(video_game)); [Hulk: Ultimate Destruction, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Incredible_Hulk:_Ultimate_Destruction)). Battlefield 6's design statements are the current AAA version of destruction as a systemic consequence: "damage to players beneath destroyed buildings", "We wanted the result of destruction to stay, creating interesting ruins", and players and world assets acting as "chaos agents" ([EA, How Battlefield 6 redefined destruction](https://www.ea.com/news/how-battlefield-6-redefined-destruction)). Landing shockwaves that "kill most humans nearby and send objects as large as cars flying" (Prototype, fan wiki) and Saints Row IV's shockwaves on landing (Wikipedia) are the mechanical form ([Prototype Wiki, Alex Mercer](https://prototype.fandom.com/wiki/Alex_Mercer); [Saints Row IV, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saints_Row_IV)). What this design takes from Megaton Rainfall is only what the player can do and how the engine survives it, treated below.

### Economy, teaching, and the death loop

Shipped superhero ladders are short (4–6 tiers) and fed by an over-supplied visible economy: Crackdown's 500 agility orbs (corroborated by the SDA knowledge base; "at least two-stars in agility to complete the game") and Crackdown 3's 750, Saints Row IV's 1,255 clusters with 1,065 needed (wiki-only), and the "recommended level" labels are most plausibly Crackdown 2's, not the original's ([Crackdown, SDA Knowledge Base](https://kb.speeddemosarchive.com/Crackdown); [Crackdown Wiki, Agility Orb](https://crackdown.fandom.com/wiki/Agility_Orb); [Saints Row Wiki, Clusters](https://saintsrow.fandom.com/wiki/Clusters)). The "raw capability, then safety" ordering has one clean precedent and one counter-example: Prototype prices survivability far above movement (Health Boost 36,000 EP rising to 550,000; Regen 80,000 to 475,000; Airdash 2,500; Glide 15,000; Air Recovery 30,000), whereas Saints Row IV sells "Sprint – Reduced Damage" for 15 clusters at rank 1, the opposite order, and its `super_lateral_velocity 130 %` style parameter lines are Rift medal rewards transcribed by the wiki from `unlockables.xtbl`, not cluster purchases with the names printed in the UI ([Altered Gamer, Prototype](https://www.alteredgamer.com/prototype/39802-powers-movement-and-survivability-guide/); [Saints Row Wiki, Super Powers](https://saintsrow.fandom.com/wiki/Super_Powers_in_Saints_Row_IV); [saintsrowmods.com thread](https://www.saintsrowmods.com/forum/threads/changing-glide-terminal-velocity.3816/)). Sucker Punch's three rules are quoted from Bridges via Graft: "Make sure the player immediately has a chance to use a new power and understands what makes it fun and useful", each power must make the game "feel fresh again", and "the most effective way to play is also the most enjoyable way to play" ([Game Developer, The Meticulous Pacing of Infamous](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-the-meticulous-pacing-of-i-infamous-i-)). Cook supplies the burnout warning with a primary text: "The player only experiences the joy of mastery for an atom only once", and a skill with no downstream use is "burnout", so a tier that adds only a multiplier must feed a new verb; consequence-gated powers are *loops* (repeatable, mastery-building), story unlocks are *arcs* people "rarely desir[e] to experience … more than once" ([Cook, Chemistry of Game Design](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-chemistry-of-game-design); [Cook, Loops and Arcs](https://lostgarden.com/2012/04/30/loops-and-arcs/)). Cook's "three skill types" from the first edition are unsourced and dropped. Dormans' deadlock rule, Anthropy and Clark's safe areas and Schell's "understood and preventable" punishment remain the ordering constraints ([Dormans 2011](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/dormans2011design.pdf); [First Person Scholar on A Game Design Vocabulary](https://www.firstpersonscholar.com/a-game-design-vocabulary/); [Schell notes](https://notesbylex.com/the-art-of-game-design-a-book-of-lenses-2nd-edition-by-jesse-schell)). Hollow Knight's designers describe the same lock-and-key structure "submerge[d] … in favour of a more naturalistic world" ([ACMI, Team Cherry interview](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/)).

Each tier gets a consequence tutorial: a contextual gauge shown only above the safe threshold (the "appears only during effort" pattern the first edition attributed to Breath of the Wild is the Skyward Sword paragraph of the wiki page, so BotW's behaviour needs its own citation), a highlight of what tames it in Runner Vision's style, a proprioceptive telegraph before impact (Titanfall's view tilt "as a player starts to jump towards a wall but before they begin to run"), and one forced safe first use before the first lethal instance ([Zelda Wiki, Stamina Wheel](https://zelda.fandom.com/wiki/Stamina_Wheel); [Game Developer, Titanfall controls interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designer-interview-getting-i-titanfall-i-s-controls-just-right)). Because inertia is the fun here, telegraph and highlight get stronger with tier, not weaker.

The **death and respawn loop** is a design requirement the first edition omitted while designing a game that kills. The precedents agree that lethal consequence needs a cheap retry: Team Meat "included infinite lives, quick restarts of levels, obvious goals, and short levels" and "felt the replay feature transformed death into a form of reward"; Celeste is single-screen challenges with checkpoints between screens; Ghostrunner's one-hit kills cost "never … more than 30 seconds' worth of progress"; Getting Over It is the deliberate opposite ("there are no checkpoints") and is aimed at "a certain kind of person, to hurt them" ([Super Meat Boy, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Meat_Boy); [Celeste, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_(video_game)); [PCGamesN, Ghostrunner review](https://www.pcgamesn.com/ghostrunner/review); [Getting Over It, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getting_Over_It_with_Bennett_Foddy)). Meier's persistence criterion argues for a lasting cost (a limp, a damaged suit, a slower get-up) rather than an instant reset, but the retry loop itself must stay short ([Game Developer, Sid Meier GDC 2012](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-sid-meier-on-how-to-see-games-as-sets-of-interesting-decisions)). The rule set: a checkpoint at every consequence gate and every safe area; death rewinds no more than about 30 s; injury persists across the rewind as a durability debuff (Overgrowth's injured pose loses strength over 12 s to `LIMP`, a shipped shape for "hurt but not dead") until a rest point; state-transition cancels are designed against, because CD Projekt had to patch out fall damage cancelled "by performing a slide action" (Patch 1.2) ([aschar.as](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/aschar.as); [Cyberpunk 2077 Patch 1.2](https://www.cyberpunk.net/en/news/37801/patch-1-2-list-of-changes)); and lethal fall damage is exposed as a toggle the way Spider-Man 2 exposes it ("fall damage defaults to Off"), here defaulting *on* with the assist path described above ([PlayStation LifeStyle on Spider-Man 2](https://www.playstationlifestyle.net/2023/09/22/marvels-spider-man-2-ps5-fall-damage-feature-explained/)).

## Control scheme: right-mouse grab, six verbs, a gamepad map and a ledge algorithm

### Verbs and mapping

The scheme serves two masters, first-person intuition and explicit, timed commitment to each self-rescue verb so the consequence gates bite. The parkour lineage (Mirror's Edge, Dying Light, Apex's auto-mantle) uses one context-sensitive "up" button and auto-grabs from a real-time scan; the physics-body lineage maps one grip per hand, hold to grip and release to let go, which Ubisoft describes as "a very physical connection to the grabbing and the climbing" and which Human: Fall Flat implements with the mouse buttons ("Using your mouse buttons or triggers, you can independently stick out your arms and have them grab onto just about any surface, so long as those buttons are pressed"; "your right stick is responsible for your camera and where your arms are pointing"), Gang Beasts with left and right click (or bumpers), and Jusant with a trigger per arm ([Ubisoft News on Grow Home](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/7fDmgKCCwWJQtyoVoOvvGM/get-ready-to-grow-home); [Worthplaying on Human: Fall Flat](https://worthplaying.com/article/2017/2/23/reviews/102561-pc-review-human-fall-flat/); [Gang Beasts PC controls, keyboardcontrols.com](https://keyboardcontrols.com/shortcuts/games/gang-beasts/); [DON'T NOD on Jusant](https://dont-nod.com/en/scaling-new-heights-learn-more-about-jusants-innovative-climbing-system/)). The first edition cited Wikipedia for the Human: Fall Flat mapping; Wikipedia says only "using both his arms and looking with his head", so the citation is now Worthplaying. The recommended map is unchanged: **WASD** intent, **mouse** look, **RMB** grab (hold to hold; both hands reach toward the look direction and grip what the hand bodies touch), **LMB** brace/push (arms forward; later shove and strike), **Space** jump (a deliberate step commit in the opening), **Ctrl** crouch (arms the landing roll; starts a slide at speed), **Shift** sprint, **Q/E** optional lean. Grab stays off the jump button so that "high jump before fall tolerance" produces a real choice under time pressure, and it stays one button because Jusant found leg controls "too complicated" ([Game Developer on Jusant](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/unlocking-the-catharsis-of-climbing-in-meditative-puzzler-jusant)). The gamepad map the first edition lacked follows the same lineage: **RB/R1** grab, **LB/L1** brace, **RT/LT** as the optional per-hand grip pair for climbing (Gang Beasts' bumpers, Human: Fall Flat's triggers, Grow Home's triggers) or, in the opening's optional expert mode, Baby Steps' one-trigger-per-leg lift ([Game Rant, Gang Beasts Xbox controls](https://gamerant.com/gang-beasts-xbox-controls-guide/); [Engadget on Baby Steps](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/baby-steps-preview-serious-gameplay-in-a-silly-walking-sim-150008737.html)).

### The intent capsule's math, with Godot's defaults and HL2's real speeds

The capsule's locomotion is the Quake lineage, reproducible from released source. Acceleration projects velocity onto the wish direction and adds `accel × wishspeed × dt`, never more than the shortfall; friction decays speed exponentially above `stopspeed` and linearly below it. id's own verdict on the "correct" alternative is in the file: `bg_pmove.c` keeps the projection rule under `#if 1` and the vector-difference rule under `#else` labelled "proper way (avoids strafe jump maxspeed bug), but feels bad", primary evidence that the strafe-jump exploit was a knowing feel decision and that unrealism belongs in input interpretation rather than in the simulation ([Quake III bg_pmove.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/id-Software/Quake-III-Arena/master/code/game/bg_pmove.c)). Constants: `pm_accelerate 10`, `pm_airaccelerate 1`, `pm_friction 6`, `pm_stopspeed 100`, `JUMP_VELOCITY 270`, `STEPSIZE 18`, `MIN_WALK_NORMAL 0.7` (45.6°); a flat jump lands at 270 u/s and `270² × 10⁻⁴ = 7.29 > 7`, so `EV_FALL_SHORT` fires on every ordinary jump, the opposite of the first-pass inference. Source's `sv_accelerate 10`, `sv_friction 4`, `sv_stopspeed 100`, air-speed cap 30, jump `sqrt(2·g·21)` with a clipped 50 % forward boost stand, but `sv_maxspeed 320` (8.1 m/s) is HL2's **sprint**: `hl2_walkspeed 150`, `hl2_normspeed 190` (4.8 m/s), `hl2_sprintspeed 320`, so the first edition overstated baseline Source locomotion by 68 % and the H0 human sprint of 7.35 m/s is close to HL2's sprint, not its run ([movevars_shared.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/movevars_shared.cpp); [gamemovement.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/gamemovement.cpp); [hl2_player.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/server/hl2/hl2_player.cpp)). Celeste's ratios remain the feel targets (`RunAccel 1000` ≈ 11× `MaxRun 90` per second, `RunReduce 400`, `AirMult .65`, `JumpHBoost 40`, `VarJumpTime .2`, `JumpGraceTime 0.1`) ([Player.cs, Celeste](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NoelFB/Celeste/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)). The capsule runs on Godot's `CharacterBody3D` with its own defaults (45° floor, 0.1 m snap, six slides, no stair step), not on Jolt's `CharacterVirtual` numbers, unless Phase 0 binds the latter; Catto's GDC 2014 session covers the swept, iteratively solved kinematic controller design if one is written from scratch ([Catto, Understanding Constraints, GDC 2014](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1021116/Physics-for-Game-Programmers-Understanding)). Holden, Longmire and Potter's 2026 "Smooth Walking Mode" for Unreal's Mover (constant acceleration toward a desired velocity, a directional-acceleration mix, a turn-strength term, an optional spring stage tracking ahead in time) is a current production reference for the capsule's velocity model ([Holden, New Movement Model](https://theorangeduck.com/page/new-movement-model)). This design inverts Mirror's Edge's trade: the capsule accelerates and turns like Quake so intent is responsive, and the physics rig follows it with real inertia limited by authority; what the player feels as momentum is the body catching up with the capsule, and that lag is the fun.

Forgiveness lives in the input layer: Celeste's coyote time, jump buffering, half gravity at the apex, and corner correction of 4 pixels ("the game tries to wiggle you to the side around it"), Dying Light's deliberately delayed parkour button ("players were using the parkour button a little too soon … we adjusted the response of the button to be slightly delayed … adjusting it back and forth in microseconds"), and two roll schemes accepted at once (a 0.1–0.2 s pre-landing buffer, or armed any time while airborne) ([Thorson thread via ThreadReader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1238338574220546049.html); [MCV/Develop on Dying Light](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/the-vaulting-dead-implementing-first-person-parkour-in-dying-light/)). A grab-like verb rationed per airtime and refilled on landing (A Story About My Uncle's grapple: once per airtime at first, upgraded later) is the shape of a consumable rescue budget that grows with progression ([Game Wisdom on A Story About My Uncle](https://game-wisdom.com/analysis/a-story-about-my-uncle)).

### The ledge-detection algorithm (design requirement)

The first edition promised "a handful of `ShapeCast3D` and `intersect_ray_into` probes"; the verb owns Milestone 1, so it needs criteria, a cost cap and a hand-target rule. The sourced facts: Techland abandoned 50,000 hand-placed hooks for a real-time geometric analysis of "height, speed and position" costing "over 200 [raytraces] per frame" and needed "3 type of assist algorithms" because "some tend to jump to soon, some tend to jump too late"; its exact criteria are in Kulon's GDC 2018 video, unviewed ([Speaker Q&A: Bartosz Kulon, GDC](https://gdconf.com/news/speaker-qa-bartosz-kulon-explains-turning-nba-dreams-game-dev-expertise); [GDC Vault 1025208](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025208/Parkour-How-to-Improve-Freedom)). Overgrowth's shipped constants are readable: `_height_under_ledge = 1.05 m` (target distance from the leg-sphere origin to the ledge), `_ledge_move_speed = 10`, `_ledge_move_damping = 0.95` per step, grip weight following `1 − sin(progress·π)` while a hand moves, grip targets leading the body by `velocity × 0.1`, hand IK offsets re-projected onto the surface with rays along `down + ledge_dir·0.5`, and the body offset as the grip-weighted average of the four IK targets flattened against the ledge plane ([ledgegrab.as](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/ledgegrab.as)). The algorithm this design specifies, as inference from those sources and Godot's API: each physics tick, predict the hand-reach point from the capsule velocity `t_reach` seconds ahead (Kulon's early/late problem is solved by predicting rather than by waiting for contact); cast one `ShapeCast3D` forward at chest height for a wall within arm reach; from a point above and slightly beyond the wall hit, cast one ray down with `intersect_ray_into` to find a top surface whose normal is within the floor angle (45°) and whose height above the feet is within the tier's reach envelope (V0 ≈ 2.2 m standing reach, plus jump); reject ledges whose top surface is narrower than a hand or whose overhead is blocked; select the nearest such point per hand within an elbow-width of the look direction; and run the same probe against **graspable faces** (a material tag on rough walls, pipes and façades) below the ledge to decide whether the slipping catch is available. Budget: at most eight queries per tick with the non-allocating `_into` variants (PR #113970), well under Dying Light's 200 because the capsule predicts instead of scanning. Contact labelling for the hands copies Holden's foot rule: a contact is a velocity below 0.1–0.5 m/s within 0.1 m of the surface, majority-filtered over five frames at 60 Hz ([Holden, Inverse Kinematics and Foot Locking](https://theorangeduck.com/page/inverse-kinematics-foot-locking)). Hands visibly reach before contact so the grab teaches itself; the parkour studios' finding that limbs, not camera motion, stop the game feeling like "a floating camera" applies ([MCV/Develop on Dying Light](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/the-vaulting-dead-implementing-first-person-parkour-in-dying-light/)).

### The input-latency chain (design requirement)

No note carries an input-to-photon budget, so the chain is stated as a requirement to sum and measure in Milestone 1. Input is sampled at most one physics tick late (8.3 ms at 120 Hz); the capsule responds within the tick; the body follows the capsule with the authority spring's lag, which is deliberate and tier-dependent; the camera anchor follows the head with a half-life of 0.05–0.12 s, i.e. a lag of `halflife/ln 2` = 0.07–0.17 s ([Holden, Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)); physics interpolation adds up to one tick to node transforms while the skinned mesh is not interpolated at all (PR #119509); then render and display. Valve's own value for smoothing a discontinuity is `cl_smoothtime 0.1` s ([c_baseplayer.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/client/c_baseplayer.cpp)); Techland tuned a *deliberate* delay on the parkour button ([MCV/Develop on Dying Light](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/the-vaulting-dead-implementing-first-person-parkour-in-dying-light/)). The rule: look (yaw and pitch) bypasses every spring and reaches the camera the same frame; intent-to-capsule latency is one tick; the body's lag behind the capsule is the only latency the player is meant to feel, and it must read as weight, not as input delay, which is a Milestone 1 measurement with a stated target rather than a number this report can source.

## Procedural animation: physics is the animator, poses are only targets

### Generating the target pose without clips

Authored animation is allowed only as an assisting target, and the production-proven procedural designs are exactly that. Overgrowth's talk (slides verified from the archive.org OCR of all 66; the spoken explanation remains unavailable) states "13 keyframes" for movement, "Start with physics, Bumper and lifter", "**Bicubic** interpolation" (not "cubic"), "Acceleration tilt", "Impact compression", and a "Recoil spring" ([Rosen GDC 2014 slides, OCR XML](https://archive.org/download/GDC2014Rosen/GDC2014-Rosen_djvu.xml)). Spore's gait generator assigns each leg group a duty factor and step trigger offset, authors the flight-phase foot arc in a normalised space scaled by leg length, and interpolates parameter sets by speed; its cost is **0.2 ms per frame for the whole 25-body procedurally animating character on a 1.7 GHz Pentium-M, about 35 % of it in the IK solver** (≈ 0.07 ms), not "25-body IK at 0.2 ms on a 2004 laptop" ([Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)). The coupled-oscillator gait clock the first edition attributed to Fu's thesis is the CPG literature as summarised in that thesis's background chapter (phase form `φ̇i = ωi + Σ wij sin(φj − φi − φ⁰ij)`, a sigmoid phase bias on speed, `θhip = A·xi(t) + θoffset`), introduced there with "might be defined as"; the thesis's own implementation uses "a network of coupled nonlinear oscillators" with φ = π for walking and ≈ π/2 for running, plus a star-shaped multi-raycast foot probe and 13 artist parameters, and its 29-person study found rotation parameters far more impactful than displacement ones ([Fu 2025, LUP full text](https://lup.lub.lu.se/luur/download?func=downloadFile&recordOId=9206764&fileOId=9206770)). These fit together: the CPG supplies phase, SIMBICON's state machine and Coros's pendulum supply where the swing foot goes, Rosen-style sparse-pose interpolation (Holden's cubic quaternion interpolation gives the math for continuous angular velocity between sparse poses) supplies the shape between, and the physics rig makes it real ([Holden, Cubic Interpolation of Quaternions](https://theorangeduck.com/page/cubic-interpolation-quaternions)).

Human data fixes how gait changes with speed and therefore how to scale it. Running cadence rises slowly (169.2 → 177.9 steps/min from 2.68 → 3.83 m/s) while stride length grows 0.98 → 1.30 m, stance time shortens 0.28 → 0.24 s, step time 0.37 → 0.34 s, impact peak rises 1.51 → 1.88 BW and loading rate 36.8 → 48.0 BW/s ([Leacox et al. 2025, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12222555/)); the walk–run switch clusters at 1.98–2.16 m/s (Kram, Hreljac, Hansen, Segers) at a *measured* Froude number of **0.45–0.48**, with 0.5 as the textbook value, and is triggered by ankle dorsiflexor overexertion rather than energy minimisation ([Kram, Domingo & Ferris 1997](https://spot.colorado.edu/~kram/walk-run.pdf); [Hansen et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-01972-1); [Hreljac 1995](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=AUTH:%22Hreljac%20A%22%20AND%20PUB_YEAR:1995&resultType=core&format=json); [Segers et al. 2007](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Segers%202007%20transition%20walking%20running%20gradually%20changing%20speed&resultType=core&format=json)); across runners from 6.2 to 11.1 m/s support force rose 1.26× while swing time "did not vary (P = 0.18)" ([Weyand et al. 2000](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1152/jappl.2000.89.5.1991&resultType=core&format=json)); Bolt's Berlin 60–80 m split ran at 4.49 Hz with 0.091 s contacts, step lengths of 2.63–2.77 m, vertical stiffness 355.8 kN/m and leg stiffness 21.0 kN/m ([Taylor & Beneke 2012](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Taylor%20Beneke%202012%20spring%20mass%20fastest%20men&resultType=core&format=json)). The human envelope is therefore cadence 2.8 → 4.5 Hz, step length 1.3 → 2.8 m, contact 0.24 → 0.09 s; **above 4.5 Hz or 12.3 m/s there is no human data**, so the design rule (hold swing time, shorten contact, lengthen flight, so superhuman running reads as bounding with larger vertical force rather than a sped-up jog) is an extrapolation, stated as such. Blending gaits on an effective Froude number `Fr = v²/(g_eff·L)` with `g_eff = s·g` has an empirical analogue only in the hypogravity direction: Kram's table shows the Froude rule predicting the transition within 11 % down to 0.4 g and failing at 0.2–0.1 g, explained "if one replaced the term g … with the summed contribution of gravity and downward acceleration due to swinging limbs"; for g_eff > g there is no measurement ([Lacquaniti et al. 2017, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5682019/)). McMahon and Cheng's dimensionless horizontal and vertical Froude numbers with leg stiffness K_LEG ≈ 15 give the principled way to scale gait with power, holding U and V constant so stride length and contact time scale with sqrt(L) and leg stiffness with weight over leg length ([McMahon & Cheng 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2081746/)).

IK closes the loop between pose and ground and writes the *target* pose on the intent skeleton. Analytic two-bone IK for legs and arms (Holden's form: clamp the target distance to `[eps, l_ab + l_cb − eps]` with eps = 0.01, law of cosines, pole-stabilised bend axis), iterative solvers only for longer chains; cost orderings are consistent (Deglorie: Jacobian transpose 1,250 CPU ticks per frame versus CCD 300 at 0.53 versus 0.72 cm error, so CCD ≈ 4× cheaper at ≈ 36 % higher error; FABRIK ≈ 10× faster than CCD and three orders faster than Jacobian methods on a 10-joint chain; Buss's Jacobian-transpose update 1.1 µs versus DLS 2.2 µs on a 2.8 GHz Pentium) ([Holden, Simple Two Joint](https://theorangeduck.com/page/simple-two-joint); [Deglorie et al. 2015](https://www.scitepress.org/papers/2015/53106/53106.pdf); [Aristidou & Lasenby 2011, FABRIK](http://www.andreasaristidou.com/publications/papers/FABRIK.pdf); [Buss 2004/2009 survey](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/ikmethods/iksurvey.pdf)). Godot 4.6+ ships the family as `IKModifier3D` subclasses (`TwoBoneIK3D` by "intersection of two circles" with a required pole target; `ChainIK3D` → `SplineIK3D`, `IterateIK3D` → `CCDIK3D`, `FABRIK3D`, `JacobianIK3D` with defaults of 4 iterations, 1 mm tolerance and a 2° angular delta limit, which is Buss's "clamp the maximum angle change" advice), plus `LookAtModifier3D` (4.4), the `BoneConstraint3D` family (4.5), `SpringBoneSimulator3D` (4.4 per the release notes; the 4.6 blog says 4.5) and `LimitAngularVelocityModifier3D` and `BoneTwistDisperser3D` (4.6), with `BoneSpaceAdjuster3D` in 4.8 dev ([Godot 4.6 release](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.6/); [Godot 4.4 release](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.4/); [Godot 4.5 release](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.5/); [Inverse Kinematics Returns to Godot 4.6](https://godotengine.org/article/inverse-kinematics-returns-to-godot-4-6/)). Foot locking follows Holden's July 2026 recipe, which post-dates every first-edition source: heel target from toe target plus the input pose's heel–toe offset, two-bone IK with a soft clamp on maximum extension, heel rotated to orient the toe, a per-contact lock state with a **cubic** inertialization over a fixed blend time, lock when `inputContact && distance < lockDistance`, unlock when `!inputContact || distance > unlockDistance`, contacts labelled by toe velocity ("between 0.5 m/s and 0.1 m/s") with a 0.1 m height check and a five-frame majority filter at 60 Hz, because "Foot sliding is about velocity" and "The toe is what needs locking" ([Holden, Inverse Kinematics and Foot Locking](https://theorangeduck.com/page/inverse-kinematics-foot-locking)). Kinematic joint limits as projection functions (rectangular, elliptical, k-DOP, swing–twist) keep IK targets inside anatomical ranges before the physics rig tries to reach them ([Holden, Joint Limits](https://theorangeduck.com/page/joint-limits)).

### Continuity: inertialization, half-life springs, and the hand-back from ragdoll

Every discrete switch in the target pose is inertialized rather than cross-faded. Bollo's quintic stands as written in the first edition (slide form; the supplemental moves the ÷2 into its final equation and nothing was "dropped"), with the overshoot guards `t₁ = min(t₁, −5x₀/v₀)` and `a₀` used only when positive, and its cost is **per frame while a transition is active**: 12 µs against 20 + 10 µs for source evaluation plus blend on Xbox One, not per transition ([Bollo GDC 2018 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/bollo_david_inertialization_high_performance.pdf); [Bollo SIGGRAPH 2017 talk](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2017-Talks-Bollo_High-Performance-Animation-in-Gears-of-War-4.pdf)). Where the target keeps moving, Holden's exact critically damped spring with `halflife_to_damping(h) = 4·ln2/(h + eps)` (the 4 is "a bit of a fudge factor") and `lag = halflife/ln 2`; the open motion-matching controller's defaults (blend half-life 0.1 s, simulation velocity and rotation half-lives 0.27 s, IK blend 0.1 s, gait change 0.1 s) are sane starting values, and the "0.1 s blend" is that controller's default, not a Learned Motion Matching value ([Holden, Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call); [controller.cpp](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching)). Two of Holden's 2023 tools fit this architecture precisely: "Perfect Tracking with Springs" reproduces an input signal exactly while removing its discontinuities (gains 1.0 / 0.2 / 0.01 on acceleration, velocity and position at 60 fps; skip the derivative terms on discontinuity frames), the right filter for feeding a physics-rig pose into visible bones; and "Dead Blending" extrapolates the source pose by its velocity and cross-fades with smoothstep, needing only the current pose and velocity, which is exactly what is known when the ragdoll hands control back ([Holden, Perfect Tracking with Springs](https://theorangeduck.com/page/perfect-tracking-springs); [Holden, Dead Blending](https://theorangeduck.com/page/dead-blending)). Secondary motion uses `SpringBoneSimulator3D` fed the skeleton's acceleration through `external_force`, noting the open framerate-dependence issue, or Jakobsen's Verlet chain implemented directly ([godot issue #103229](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/103229); [Jakobsen 2001](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/academic/class/15462-s13/www/lec_slides/Jakobsen.pdf)). Motion matching stays secondary: its database holds human-speed clips; the two Godot add-ons (GuilhermeGSousa's GDExtension for 4.4+, V-Sekai's 4.2 implementation) are Holden-derived and assume root-motion clip databases; and basic motion matching at 50–100 MB and 10–20 µs per frame is cheaper in CPU than Learned Motion Matching's 5 MB at ~200 µs, so LMM pays only when memory is the constraint ([godot-motion-matching README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GuilhermeGSousa/godot-motion-matching/master/README.md); [Learned Motion Matching PDF](https://theorangeduck.com/media/uploads/other_stuff/Learned_Motion_Matching.pdf)). If used, Clavet's coupling is right: the rigid body is the "simulation object" predicted by the real integrator and the visual body is clamped within about 15 cm of the physics root ([Clavet GDC 2016 slides, OCR](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Clavet/GDC2016-Clavet_djvu.txt)).

### First-person arms and legs

Only arms, upper chest and (looking down) legs are visible, so the consequence cues of growing power ride on them. Duffin's principle, first-person motion as a HUD for "pain, strain, exertion and weight", stands ([Wolfire summary of Duffin's GDC 2013 talk](https://www.wolfire.com/blog/2013/04/GDC13-Summary-Animation-Bootcamp-Part-5-6/)). Piquet's UE4 "true first person" attaches the camera to a head socket of a *hand-animated* skeleton, which is acceptable because an animator authors a smooth head path; a *simulated* head cannot be authored, which is why the next section keeps the camera off the physics head ([Game Developer, True First Person Camera in UE4](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/true-first-person-camera-in-unreal-engine-4)). Here the arms are physics bodies on the rig whose targets are 2–4 authored arm poses per locomotion state blended by measured velocity and acceleration, with Rosen's recoil spring fed by the physical impulse so weapon kick and strain scale with strength; breathing and idle sway live on the arms only, never on the camera.

## The camera: body motion on the arms, a corrected history, and rate-based comfort

### The three-layer stack

The camera never binds rigidly to the simulated head. The first edition's claim that "every studio that tried a head-mounted camera reports the same failure" is corrected: Rockstar shipped GTA V's first person from a head-bone camera with three opt-out toggles ("First Person Ragdoll", "First Person Combat Roll", "First Person Head Bobbing"; primary Rockstar text unreachable, toggles sourced to a fan guide), Warhorse shipped Kingdom Come's without an opt-out and collected the predicted complaints ("Even when standing still … your head waves back and forth like you're at sea"), DICE's finding is Jonathan Cooper's paraphrase of the GDC 2009 talk ("It's interesting to see someone else thought of attaching a camera to a mocapped head, though it didn't work out"), and Cloud Imperium's "bobble head" fix was a look-at constraint toward a distant focus point, which is why it failed near walls ([GTA BOOM guide](https://www.gtaboom.com/gta-5-first-person-guide-bc4b); [Kingdom Come forum thread](https://forum.kingdomcomerpg.com/t/head-bobbing-motion-sickness/39868); [gameanim.com on Mirror's Edge](https://www.gameanim.com/2010/11/05/creating-first-person-movement-for-mirrors-edge/); [Kotaku on Star Citizen](https://kotaku.com/star-citizens-first-person-view-is-based-on-birds-1787021141)). Respawn's rule is the flat-screen statement: "Any time we move the view without your input that can cause motion sickness"; their landing reaction *reuses the weapon-recoil view spring* ("The depth of the spring is relative to the height fallen … the movement code, was identical"), Titanfall does have view bob, and a fixed crosshair with bob made the character seem "constantly looking left-right-left-right" ([Game Developer, Titanfall controls interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designer-interview-getting-i-titanfall-i-s-controls-just-right)).

Layer one, the base view, is player yaw and pitch only; body orientation never writes into it ([Oculus Best Practices PDF](https://static.oculus.com/documentation/pdfs/intro-vr/latest/bp.pdf)). Layer two, the anchor position, is Holden's exact critically damped spring toward the simulated head body's position with a half-life of about **0.05–0.12 s** (lag 0.07–0.17 s; a design inference, not a sourced value), the anchor–head offset clamped to about 10–15 cm as a safety limit in Opsive's sense ("Use them as safety limits, not as the main way to shape the motion"), with the visible neck and head mesh absorbing the residual; the camera reads the interpolated transform in `_process` while the body moves in `_physics_process` ([Holden, Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call); [Opsive Springs](https://opsive.com/support/documentation/ultimate-character-controller/animation/springs/)). Layer three is the additive impact layer with Source's exact constants: `punch += vel·dt; vel *= max(0, 1 − 9·dt); vel −= punch·clamp(65·dt, 0, 2)` (k = 65, c = 9, "UNDONE: Per-axis spring constant?"; `ViewPunch` adds `angleOffset × 20` to the velocity), which is ω₀ = 8.06 rad/s (1.28 Hz) at ζ = 0.56, damped frequency 1.07 Hz, overshoot gone after about 0.67 s ([gamemovement.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/gamemovement.cpp); [baseplayer_shared.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/baseplayer_shared.cpp)). Landing **sets roll only**, `PunchAngle[ROLL] = fallVelocity × 0.013`, uncapped, and the "8°" is a clamp on a *pre-existing pitch punch* (a weapon kick during the fall), not a landing pitch; the constants are Half-Life 1's (1998) carried into Source unchanged, with only the threshold lowered from 350 to 303 u/s for HL2's 600 u/s² gravity ("at least a 76 inch fall", `sqrt(2·600·76)`) ([pm_shared.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/halflife/master/pm_shared/pm_shared.c); [shareddefs.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/shareddefs.h)). At **one inch per unit**, the convention Valve's own 76-inch comment fixes, 0.013° per u/s is **0.51° per m/s** of impact speed (the first edition's 0.68 used an unsourced 0.75 in per unit), the 303 u/s threshold is 7.7 m/s (a 3 m drop), and the fatal 922.5 u/s gives 12.0° of roll; the design's "clamp the summed punch to about 12°" is this report's inference (Source clamps at ±89°/±179°). Wall hits use Quake's direction-dependent kick `roll = count·(hitDir·right)·0.6`, `pitch = count·(hitDir·forward)·0.6` decaying linearly over `v_kicktime 0.5` s, and the impulse magnitude comes from the head and torso bodies' per-tick Δv, so superhuman speed produces bigger reactions inside the clamp ([Quake view.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/id-Software/Quake/master/WinQuake/view.c)). Sustained trauma uses Eiserloh's scalar (trauma ∈ [0, 1], linear decay, shake = trauma² or trauma³, rotation-only Perlin shake in 3D because translation is "VERY BAD"); the exponent is cited to Eiserloh (2 or 3), not to Roystan's Unity port, whose final tuned scene uses exponent 1 with recovery 1.5/s, frequency 25 and maxima (15, 15, 10)° ([Eiserloh GDC 2016 slides](http://www.mathforgameprogrammers.com/gdc2016/GDC2016_Eiserloh_Squirrel_JuicingYourCameras.pdf); [Roystan camera shake](https://roystan.net/articles/camera-shake/)).

When the ragdoll takes over, the anchor's follow target switches from the intent head to the ragdoll head through an inertialization offset, ragdoll head *rotation* blends in at reduced gain and clamped, the camera decelerates before a wall in Meta's soft-collision manner ("camera slows down before stopping"), and a head inside geometry fades the view to a colour rather than teleporting, which is what Half-Life: Alyx does (yellow means "Your head is inside of a wall", player-reported) and what its developer commentary describes as narrowing or dominating the field of view to reduce sickness ([Meta Horizon, Locomotion comfort](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/locomotion-comfort-usability/); [Steam Community, Alyx support thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/546560/discussions/0/3435626476281651816/); [Combine OverWiki, Alyx developer commentary](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Developer_commentary/Half-Life:_Alyx)). Recovery blends back with Overgrowth's exponential (speeds 2 stand, 10 fall/roll, 100 flip) ([aschar.as](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/aschar.as)). "First-person ragdoll" ships as a toggle separate from head bob, as GTA V does.

### Comfort limits, now framed by rate

The Oculus guidance is numeric: avoid visually presented acceleration, avoid vertical oscillation near **0.2 Hz** and off-vertical rotation near **0.3 Hz**, keep speeds near 1.4 m/s walking and 3 m/s jogging (the 2.8 m/s running figure is Meta's web page, not the PDF), do not head-bob, default to the lowest-intensity experience ([Oculus Best Practices PDF](https://static.oculus.com/documentation/pdfs/intro-vr/latest/bp.pdf); [Meta Horizon, Locomotion comfort](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/locomotion-comfort-usability/)). Laboratory work pins the worst band for visually induced sickness at **0.2–0.4 Hz** of fore-aft oscillation ([Diels & Howarth 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720812469046); [Golding et al. 2001, PMID 11277284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11277284/)), which is the argument against body-following half-lives above about 0.3 s: they turn every stop into a 0.3–1 s oscillation in the danger band, while gait-rate bob (1.5–2.5 Hz) and Source's 1.28 Hz punch sit above it. The second pass adds a roll literature that disagrees with itself: an HMD survey of 154 participants found roll scenes worse than yaw and pitch, translation acceleration 19.7 % worse than constant velocity and user control 22.5 % better; a staircase onset study found rotational thresholds (roll ≈ 0.36, yaw ≈ 0.47 rad/s if the paper's units hold, about 21–27°/s) far below translational ones with **roll versus yaw not significant**; a 20-minute oscillation study at 30°/s found no significant axis effect; dual-axis rotation is reliably worse than single-axis; and the 2025 meta-analysis of 97 studies names visual oscillation, multidimensional motion and off-vertical rotation as aggravators and peripheral FOV restriction as a mitigator ([Oh & Son 2022, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8963115/); [Terenzi & Zaal 2020, NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200000787/downloads/20200000787.pdf); [Lo & So 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0003-6870(00)00059-4); [Bonato et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.3357/asem.2394.2009); [Allison & Palmisano 2025](https://doi.org/10.1163/22134808-bja10181)). None is a flat-screen study and none gives a safe static roll angle; the defensible rule is about *rate and duration*: body-induced rotation is opt-in, clamped to the 8–12° landing ceiling, decayed within 0.5–1 s, kept single-axis where possible, and foreshadowed by the visible body ("an avatar that … anticipates and foreshadows the visual motion"). Two more findings shape settings: above about **10 m/s** further speed did not add nausea in a 30-minute head-steered study (ratings rose from 3 to 10 m/s and then plateaued), so the risk concentrates in the 3–10 m/s band the early game lives in and in accelerations ([So, Lo & Ho 2001](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872001775898223)); and a velocity-driven soft FOV restrictor that stays above about 90° was unnoticed by 11 of 15 participants while lowering discomfort (Wilcoxon Z = −2.75, p = 0.003), the peer-reviewed basis for an optional speed vignette ([Fernandes & Feiner 2016 PDF](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/2016/combating-vr-sickness/images/combating-vr-sickness.pdf)). Distance-graded haze raises *perceived* speed while uniform contrast loss lowers it, so atmospheric fog is a free speed cue ([Pretto et al. 2012, eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.00031)). The TNO finding that matched internal and external FOV was the *sickest* condition comes with its authors' caveat about the large FOV differences used and no significant effect of absolute FOV, and it conflicts with the HMD restrictor literature, so the two are display-specific rather than one rule ([de Vries et al., VIMS2007 PDF](https://www.ieda.ust.hk/dfaculty/so/pdf/Pages89-95-VIMS2007.pdf)). Flat-screen prevalence is not negligible (42–56 % of students sick within 50 minutes of console games; controller-based locomotion has the highest locomotion-type SSQ in a 55-study meta-analysis) ([Stoffregen et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872008x250755); [Saredakis et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2020.00096)).

The history is corrected too. Quake ships `cl_rollangle 2.0` and `cl_rollspeed 200`, so strafing rolls the view 2° by default, and it bobs **the eye** (`r_refdef.vieworg[2] += cl.viewheight + bob`) as well as the gun; Half-Life 1 still adds the full bob to the camera origin (`pparams->vieworg[2] += ( bob )`) and to the viewmodel; only HL2/Source removed bob from the eye (`sv_rollangle 0`; viewmodel bob `0.3·b + 0.7·b·sin(cycle)` clamped to [−7, 4] at 40 % scale, continuing in the air "because it snaps badly without it"); idle sway (`v_idlescale`) defaults to zero in all three ([Quake view.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/id-Software/Quake/master/WinQuake/view.c); [Half-Life view.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/halflife/master/cl_dll/view.cpp); [basehlcombatweapon_shared.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/hl2/basehlcombatweapon_shared.cpp)). The natural bob for a physics body is therefore not a sine: on each foot-contact event, add a downward velocity impulse to the vertical camera spring proportional to the foot's impact speed (and a lateral impulse signed by foot), half-life 0.1–0.2 s, which keeps bob energy at gait frequency, stays continuous through take-off and landing, and makes superhuman running produce larger bob without a hand-tuned curve; Nesky's list names "Bouncing the camera with the avatar's walk cycle" and "Translating or rotating up and down when the avatar jumps" among the fifty mistakes ([shermanrose.uk notes on Nesky's 50 Camera Mistakes](https://shermanrose.uk/knowledge/programming/games/50-game-camera-mistakes/)).
