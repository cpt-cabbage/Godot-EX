# Learn to Walk, Then Break the World

A first-person game in which a clumsy body learns to walk and then grows into a Megaton-Rainfall-scale superbeing is buildable on this Godot 4.8-dev fork with Jolt 5.6.0 today, provided the character is an **active ragdoll driven by Jolt's solver-side joint motors rather than by script-applied torques**, the physics tick is raised to **120 Hz**, and every power is expressed as a physical budget (joint torque limits, a pelvis "authority" force, tissue-failure thresholds) rather than as a scripted flag. The single most important engine fact is that this fork already contains the missing piece that community active-ragdoll builds still lack: `Generic6DOFJoint3D.set_angular_target_rotation()` plus `PARAM_ANGULAR_DRIVE_TORQUE_LIMIT`, merged upstream in June 2026 for milestone 4.8, which gives an implicit PD orientation drive per joint without any hand-integrated torque ([godot PR #118997](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/118997); `doc/classes/Generic6DOFJoint3D.xml:67-74,434-443`). The classical control literature supplies every gain and law needed for a hand-authored balance and fall controller (SIMBICON's swing-hip feedback `θd = θd0 + cd·d + cv·v` with cd 0.5, cv 0.2, kp 300 N·m/rad, kd 0.1 kp; Coros's inverted-pendulum step `d = v·sqrt(h/g + v²/4g²)`), and Euphoria's shipped parameter set (balance abort at lean 0.6, give-up when the COM is within 0.5 m of the lowest foot, catch-fall 0.3 s before impact) shows how to layer behaviours on top of it ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [SHVDN ConfigureBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_configure_balance_helper.html)). Human biomechanics fixes the ladder's thresholds: a fit adult sprints at about 7.4 m/s and stops in about 7 m, falls above roughly 6 m start breaking tibiae (50 % fracture risk at 3.7–8.3 kN), a wall stop at 7.35 m/s is 18.9 kN if rigid and 6.3 kN if the arms crumple, and two maximal grips hold only about 1 kN, which caps a ledge catch near 3–4 m/s of downward speed ([Harper 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474351/); [Funk 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12596644/); [Bohannon 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24729356/)). Digital Molecular Matter is proprietary but fully published; reproducing it is a multi-month C++ FEM project that the player-facing fantasy does not need, because a Blast-style connection graph over pre-fractured convex chunks delivers the same "the wall gives way" reaction in one to three weeks of script ([Parker & O'Brien 2009](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Parker-RTD-2009-08/Parker-RTD-2009-08.pdf); [Blast SDK docs](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/blast-sdk/latest/docs/api/introduction.html)). Houdini Engine for Godot exists only as a community GDExtension that requires a commercial SideFX licence, so Houdini's role is offline authoring through glTF, and learned controllers should wait until a hand-authored PD base exists, because even the cheapest learned policies cost 30–140 CPU-hours per skill and the only shipped example took a 5–10 person team years ([HEGo README](https://github.com/peterprickarz/hego); [AMP PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.02180); [80.lv on ARC Raiders](https://80.lv/articles/inside-the-magic-of-machine-learning-that-powers-enemy-ai-in-arc-raiders)). The first milestone is a locally testable grey-box prototype: a 15-body PhysicalBone3D ragdoll standing on Jolt joint drives, pushable with DeepMimic-style 0.2 s pelvis shoves, walkable with a detuned SIMBICON controller, viewed from a stabilised head-anchored camera, with a right-mouse ledge grab.

## Executive summary: what to build, in what order, and why it will hold together

The design resolves into one controller architecture, one scalar that drives the opening, three power ladders, and a destruction plan graded by cost. The controller is a two-rig system in the pattern Boneworks and Ubisoft's motion-matching work both converged on: an intent rig (a kinematic capsule that integrates player input into a desired root trajectory and a target pose) and a physics rig (a Jolt ragdoll of roughly 15 rigid bodies whose joints are pulled toward the target pose by solver-side spring drives and whose pelvis is pulled toward the intent capsule by a force-limited virtual spring) ([BONEWORKS Wiki snippet on the Controller Rig and Physics Rig](https://boneworks.fandom.com/wiki/BONEWORKS_(2019)); [Clavet GDC 2016 slides on the "simulation object" clamped to 15 cm](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Clavet/GDC2016-Clavet_djvu.txt)). Balance is not faked: a SIMBICON/Coros stepping controller writes the leg targets, so when its gains are low the character genuinely falls, and when they are high the character genuinely recovers. The **authority** of the pelvis spring and the **torque limits** of the joints are the two numbers that the game varies over its whole arc: near zero and about 40–50 % of human torque at minute one (the toddler), nominal by the end of the opening (competent walking), and far above human at the top tiers (the superbeing), which is exactly the strength curriculum the animation literature uses in the opposite direction ([Learning to Get Up, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00307)).

Every power is then a change to a physical budget with a consequence the same physics produces for free. Jump height raises take-off velocity; landing speed is `sqrt(2gh)`; a landing above about 3 m (7.7 m/s) risks fracture and above about 6 m (10.9 m/s) risks death unless the player rolls, lands on a soft surface, or grabs a ledge, because the leg-force model and the clinical fall data both cross their thresholds there ([Niu 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4160626/); [Hsieh 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7312001/)). Sprint speed raises kinetic energy quadratically while stopping distance also grows quadratically, so a runner who has speed but not self-arrest runs out of corridor; a wall stop is scored from the contact impulse against 46 g (blackout), 60 g / HIC15 700 (serious injury) and the Rosén–Sander speed-to-death curve `P = 1/(1+exp(6.9 − 0.090·v_kmh))` ([49 CFR 571.208](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2023-title49-vol6/xml/CFR-2023-title49-vol6-sec571-208.xml); [Rosén & Sander 2009 preprint](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/pedestrian_fatality_risk_function_car_impact_speed_rosen.pdf)). The self-rescue verbs (grab, roll, brace/self-arrest) arrive one tier behind each capability, which is the "raw capability, then safety" order that Crackdown 3, Prototype and Saints Row IV all use, except that those games cap the consequence at zero and this design lets it kill ([Shacknews on Crackdown 3 skills](https://www.shacknews.com/article/109880/skills-abilities-and-leveling-fast-in-crackdown-3); [Altered Gamer on Prototype upgrades](https://www.alteredgamer.com/prototype/39802-powers-movement-and-survivability-guide/)). At the top, when the body is unhurtable, the consequence moves to the world in Megaton Rainfall's manner: a city health bar measured in casualties regardless of who caused them ([Hardcore Gamer review](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-megaton-rainfall/276013/)).

The world reacts on a graded budget. Tier one is free dressing (sleeping RigidBody3D clutter, decals, dust with heightfield collision, reverb zones, per-instance foliage bending); tier two is a pre-fractured swap on an impulse threshold; tier three is a Blast-style bond graph with `Damage = Impulse / Hardness` and island flood-fill in script; tiers four to six (runtime cutting, voxels, FEM) are C++ and are not needed for round one ([Blast UE4 settings](https://archive.docs.nvidia.com/gameworks/content/gameworkslibrary/blast/1.1/authoring_docs/BlastUe4_BlastSettings.html); [Godot docs, Using decals](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/3d/using_decals.html)). Houdini authors the fracture pieces, the constraint graph and the ragdoll shapes offline and ships them through glTF with extras metadata that Godot 4.4+ imports into node metadata ([RBD Material Fracture docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/rbdmaterialfracture.html); [godot PR #86183](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/86183)). The rest of this report gives the laws, gains, APIs, thresholds and sources for each of those claims, then the phased plan, the assessment of the sources the user supplied, and the open questions.

## Jolt's solver-side springs make a first-person active ragdoll feasible in this fork today

### What the fork exposes and what it hides

The repository is upstream Godot master as of 2026-09-22 (`version.py:3-6` reports 4.8.0 dev) with fork-local commits confined to rendering, and it vendors **Jolt 5.6.0** (`thirdparty/README.md:553-561`). Because the fork's own work does not touch `modules/jolt_physics`, `scene/3d` or `servers/physics_3d`, engine-side additions for this project can be made without conflicting with the ray-tracing work (`git log --oneline -n 15 -- modules/jolt_physics scene/3d servers/physics_3d scene/animation`). The character-control primitives are all upstream behaviour. `CharacterBody3D.move_and_slide` under Jolt runs through Godot's generic `body_test_motion` path, tunable via `physics/jolt_physics_3d/motion_queries/*` (`modules/jolt_physics/jolt_project_settings.cpp:58-60`); Jolt's own `CharacterVirtual` is compiled into the binary (`modules/jolt_physics/SCsub:55`) but **not wrapped or exposed** anywhere in the module, so the sliding, stair-stepping and moving-platform behaviour Jolt documents for it is unavailable from script ([Jolt Architecture page](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html)). `RigidBody3D` gives full per-tick control through `custom_integrator` and `_integrate_forces` with `PhysicsDirectBodyState3D` (`doc/classes/RigidBody3D.xml:188-190`), and the new non-allocating typed space queries (`intersect_ray_into`, `cast_motion_into`, `collide_shape_into` and their result classes) landed via upstream PR #113970, which matters because a Dying-Light-scale ledge scan of 200 traces per frame would otherwise allocate every tick (`doc/classes/PhysicsDirectSpaceState3D.xml:119-126`; [Game Developer on Dying Light's 200+ raytraces](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-dying-light-i-s-natural-movement-system)).

The joint API is where this fork is ahead of the community. `Generic6DOFJoint3D` under Jolt supports limits, velocity motors and stiffness/damping springs; its motor state machine maps motor+spring to Jolt's `PositionAndVelocity`, spring-only to `Position`, motor-only to `Velocity` (`modules/jolt_physics/joints/jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:112-121`). The June 2026 addition wires a **body-space quaternion target** through `set_angular_target_rotation` → `SetTargetOrientationBS(to_jolt(q))` (`jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:213-224`; `scene/3d/physics/joints/generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:56-59`; server API `servers/physics_3d/physics_server_3d.cpp:435-436`) and adds `PARAM_LINEAR_DRIVE_FORCE_LIMIT = 22` and `PARAM_ANGULAR_DRIVE_TORQUE_LIMIT = 23`, which "apply in both spring and motor modes" and supersede the deprecated `PARAM_ANGULAR_MOTOR_FORCE_LIMIT` (`servers/physics_3d/physics_server_3d_enums.h:231-232`; `generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:286-288`). The PR's own motivation names active ragdolls and PD drives, and a reviewer summarises the usage exactly: "you just calculate the desired relative transform between the two bodies and pass it to set_angular_target_rotation" ([godot PR #118997](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/118997)). Inside Jolt the Position motor is `force = stiffness × (target − current) − damping × current_velocity`, solved implicitly in the constraint solver (`thirdparty/jolt_physics/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/MotorSettings.h:18-21`), which is the same "soft constraint = implicit-Euler spring, unconditionally stable" identity Catto proved for Box2D ([Catto, Soft Constraints, GDC 2011](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_SoftConstraints_GDC2011.pdf)). That is why the September 2026 forum thread in which a user asks "Where is the motor parameters in the 6DoF PhysicalBone3D node?" and is told to "simulate the motor in script" is already obsolete on this build ([forum topic 144270](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/144270)).

Three limitations remain and shape the rig. First, hinge and cone-twist joints only get velocity motors from script; Jolt's `SwingTwistConstraint` has full swing/twist `Position` motors and `SetTargetOrientationBS`, but Godot's cone-twist wrapper never enters Position mode and its motor parameters live in `JoltPhysicsServer3D::ConeTwistJointParamJolt`, whose `_bind_methods()` is empty (`jolt_cone_twist_joint_3d.cpp:91-101`; `modules/jolt_physics/jolt_physics_server_3d.h:91-105,120`). Second, the hinge motor converts Godot's max-impulse to torque assuming a constant timestep, so changing the tick rate at runtime silently changes hinge strength (`jolt_hinge_joint_3d.cpp:195-197,235-238`). Third, applied-force readback (`GetTotalLambda` divided by the step) exists in the module but is not bound (`jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:~665-695`; [godot-proposals #13422](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/13422)). The practical consequence is to build every driven joint as a `Generic6DOFJoint3D` (or PhysicalBone3D `JOINT_TYPE_6DOF`) with angular limits standing in for cone-twist and hinge ranges, and to add a small Phase 0 engine patch (below) for the rest.

### The recommended rig: intent capsule plus a 15-body physics rig

`PhysicalBone3D` is strictly kinematic-or-simulated: when simulating it is `BODY_MODE_RIGID`, otherwise kinematic or static, and it writes its transform back to the skeleton through `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D.set_bone_global_pose` (`scene/3d/physics/physical_bone_3d.cpp:805-836`). It already stores a 6DOF joint with `angular_spring_enabled/stiffness/damping` and `angular_equilibrium_point` (`scene/3d/physics/physical_bone_3d.h:127-160`) but no quaternion target; the target must be supplied each tick by script through `get_joint_rid()` and `PhysicsServer3D.generic_6dof_joint_set_angular_target_rotation(rid, q)`, with the torque cap via `generic_6dof_joint_set_param(rid, axis, G6DOF_JOINT_ANGULAR_DRIVE_TORQUE_LIMIT, v)` (`servers/physics_3d/physics_server_3d.cpp:404-405,435-436`). The engine note flags that this chain is unverified at runtime but that every link exists in source; verifying it is the first task of milestone one. The target `q` for each joint is the animation's desired child-relative-to-parent rotation in body A's frame, `q = basis_A.inverse() × basis_B_desired`, because `SetTargetOrientationBS` is body-space (`thirdparty/jolt_physics/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/SixDOFConstraint.h:169-193`). This reproduces Jolt's own unexposed `Ragdoll::DriveToPoseUsingMotors(pose)` one joint at a time, which the Jolt sample uses each step after sampling an animation (`thirdparty/jolt_physics/Jolt/Physics/Ragdoll/Ragdoll.h:180-193`; [PoweredRigTest.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/master/Samples/Tests/Rig/PoweredRigTest.cpp)).

The tracking pose comes from the intent rig, which runs first each physics tick: `AnimationMixer.callback_mode_process = PHYSICS` and `Skeleton3D.modifier_callback_mode_process = PHYSICS` so the procedural pose is generated before the simulator modifier runs (`doc/classes/AnimationMixer.xml:305,323-325`; `doc/classes/Skeleton3D.xml:395,445-453`), and the design article's rule that "if the node uses the physics process outside of Skeleton3D and it affects SkeletonModifier3D, the property must be set to Physics" is followed ([Design of the Skeleton Modifier 3D](https://godotengine.org/article/design-of-the-skeleton-modifier-3d/)). The intent capsule itself is a `CharacterBody3D` running Quake-lineage acceleration (below) purely to produce a desired root position and velocity; it never renders and it does not collide with the ragdoll. The physics rig's pelvis is tied to it by a linear spring drive on a 6DOF joint to a kinematic anchor (or, equivalently, a `custom_integrator` virtual force `F = k_V (V_d − V)` clamped by a force limit), which is Coros's velocity-tuning virtual force and Euphoria's "cheat force" in one object ([Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [SHVDN ConfigureBalanceHelper on `ResistAcc` "cheat force added to resist floorAcceleration"](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_configure_balance_helper.html)). The force limit is the **authority** scalar: at zero the body must balance on its own legs; at human-nominal it steadies like Euphoria's balancer; at superhuman values it carries the body through anything, which is what Saints Row IV's top sprint tier ("knocks them over but you can't be knocked over") amounts to ([Saints Row Wiki, wiki-only](https://saintsrow.fandom.com/wiki/Super_Powers_in_Saints_Row_IV)).

Body layout follows the official ragdoll tutorial and the Overgrowth/Jolt conventions: cone-type limits at shoulders, hips and neck, hinge-type at elbows and knees, all implemented as 6DOF joints with angular limits, collision layers set so the player capsule does not collide with its own ragdoll ([Ragdoll system, Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/ragdoll_system.html)). Masses should follow the SIMBICON 2D model (70 kg trunk, 5 kg thighs, 4 kg shanks, 1 kg feet) or the SIMBICON-CEF humanoid link masses (12.9, 22.5, 5.2, 2.2, 1.7, 6.6, 3.2, 1.0, 0.2 kg) for a ~70 kg total, since every published gain below assumes that scale ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [simbicon_cef](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/)). The DReCon ragdoll (23 bodies, 54 DOF, 90 kg at 985 kg/m³ from capsule fitting) and Boneworks' 82 kg player are the shipped-game anchors for the same choice ([DReCon PDF](https://static-wordpress.ubisoft.com/montreal.ubisoft.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/13214229/DReCon.pdf); [Game Developer on Boneworks](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-classical-game-mechanics-and-physics-converge-in-vr-hit-i-boneworks-i-)).

### Control laws and gains, transcribed from the sources

The joint layer is a PD servo toward the target pose. SIMBICON writes it as `τ = kp(θd − θ) − kd·θ̇` and, for its 3D biped in ODE, uses **kp = 300 N·m/rad or less on all joints except the waist (kp = 1000), with kd = 0.1 kp**, torque magnitudes limited to kp, and a 0.005 s step; its 2D biped uses kp 300 / kd 30 everywhere with limits of 1000 N·m that can be lowered to 370 N·m for everything but the fast run, and the basic walk survives down to 90 N·m before it "becomes weak-kneed and falls" ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)). The released SIMBICON-CEF controller files give a per-joint table for the humanoid: root 3000/300, pelvis–torso 200/30, hips 300/30, knees 300/30, torso–head 200/20, shoulders 20/5, elbows 5/1, ankles 75/10, toes 10/0.5, with `stanceHipDamping 25` and `stanceHipMaxVelocity 4` ([simbicon_cef README and source](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/)). Coros scales kp by the mass ratio M′/M and uses **kd = 2·sqrt(kp)** (critical damping) on every joint, with optional limits of 200 N·m at hips and knees and 100 N·m elsewhere that "did not have any visible impact" ([Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)); the survey's rule of thumb is the same critical-damping relation `kv = 2 sqrt(kp)` as a starting point ([Geijtenbeek & Pronost 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03189.x)). In Jolt terms these become the 6DOF `angular_spring_*/stiffness` and `/damping` values in `StiffnessAndDamping` mode (N·m/rad and N·m·s/rad), because that is the only spring mode reachable from script (`jolt_generic_6dof_joint_3d.cpp:176-190`; `thirdparty/jolt_physics/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/SpringSettings.h:17-19,51-69`), and the torque limit becomes `PARAM_ANGULAR_DRIVE_TORQUE_LIMIT`. Jolt's own guidance for tuning in frequency terms is a stiff drive at about **20 Hz** (reaching target in ~0.0125 s undamped) and a soft one at about 2 Hz, damping ratio in [0, 1] with 1 critical, and "if your motor doesn't seem to do anything, chances are that you have set the value too low", sized as force = mass × acceleration ([Jolt Physics docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/)). Euphoria's shipped scale is a dimensionless stiffness of 6–16 and damping 0–2 per body region, with balance legs at 12, catch-fall arms at 15 and torso 9, which is the ratio pattern (legs stiff, arms stiffest when reaching, torso softest) to keep when translating to SI ([SHVDN CatchFallHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_catch_fall_helper.html); [SHVDN BodyBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_body_balance_helper.html)).

The balance layer is SIMBICON's finite-state machine plus swing-hip feedback, written into the target pose rather than into torques. Walking uses four states (two mirrored steps; state 1 lifts the swing foot for a fixed dwell of **0.3 s**, state 2 drives it down until foot contact), running two; joints track per-state target angles, the torso and swing hip are servoed in the world frame, and the stance-hip torque is made physically realisable as `τ_A = −τ_torso − τ_B` ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)). The feedback law on the swing-hip target is **`θd = θd0 + cd·d + cv·v`**, with d the horizontal distance from stance ankle to COM and v the COM velocity, cd = 0.5 and cv = 0.2 in both sagittal and coronal planes for the basic 3D walk, the hip midpoint serving as "a simple and effective proxy" for the COM; the generalised form `θd = θd0 + F·[d v]ᵀ` adds stance-ankle feedback for quiet standing ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)). SIMBICON's Table 1 supplies the target angles in radians for each state (3D walk states 0,2: torso 0, swing hip 0.5, swing knee −1.1, swing ankle 0.6, stance knee −0.05; states 1,3 on foot contact: swing hip −0.1, swing knee −0.05, swing ankle 0.15, stance knee −0.1) and for a fast run (one state, dwell 0.15 s, swing hip 1.08, swing knee −2.18) and a crouch walk ([SIMBICON PDF, Table 1](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)). The released CEF controller uses cd 0.55 / cv 0.30 sagittally and −0.30 / −0.30 coronally with 0.7 s states transitioning on `footDown` ([simbicon_cef](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/)).

For foot placement and speed control, Coros's generalised controller replaces the linear law with a **predictive inverted pendulum**: with COM height h and speed v, the step that brings the COM to rest is `d = v·sqrt(h/g + v²/(4g²))`, shortened by `d′ = d − α·V_d` with α = 0.05 to hold a desired speed V_d, applied independently in the sagittal and coronal planes, recomputed every step and capped at 0.6 L so the character may need several steps to recover; the swing foot follows a three-segment height spline with linear x/z interpolation to the target, solved by IK and tracked by low-gain PD plus gravity compensation ([Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)). Two more virtual forces make low gains sufficient: gravity compensation `τ_i = J_iᵀ(−m_i g)` on every link except the stance leg, and the velocity force `F_V = k_V (V_d − V)` applied to the COM through the stance-leg Jacobian transpose, with the ankle component zeroed when the stance foot is not well planted ([Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); virtual model control lineage in [Pratt et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1177/02783640122067309)). The same pendulum constant appears in robotics as the capture point, `C = sqrt(g/h)`, which humanoids step to in order to stop, and N-step capturability formalises how many steps a push demands ([Missura et al., Capture Steps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02793); [Koolen et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364912452673)). With these laws SIMBICON withstood 0.1 s pushes of **600 N forwards and 500 N backwards** and Coros's 70.4 kg character omnidirectional 600 N pushes for 0.1 s at 0.6 m/s, which are the acceptance numbers for milestone one ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)).

### Tick rate: 120 Hz with interpolation, and why explicit torques are ruled out

Classical PD controllers only worked at 0.1–0.5 ms steps: SIMBICON 2D at 0.0001 s, Coros at 0.0005 s, Wang at 2400 Hz, Geijtenbeek at 0.0003 s, Zordan at ~0.0005 s ([Wang 2009 PDF](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fleet/research/Papers/optwalkACMTransGraph.pdf); [Geijtenbeek 2013 PDF](https://www.goatstream.com/research/papers/SA2013/SA2013.pdf)). LocoTest measured the cartwheel walker falling once the step exceeded **0.9–1.6 ms** regardless of engine (ODE, PhysX, Bullet, Vortex), even though the simulations themselves stayed stable to 18–150 ms ([LocoTest PDF](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~kkyin/papers/locotest.pdf)), and an Unreal port of cartwheel-3d needed 2000 updates per second and dropped below 30 fps with three characters ([UE forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/cartwheel-generalized-biped-walking-control-test-with-source-code/62555)). Godot's default 60 Hz tick is therefore an order of magnitude too coarse for script-side explicit PD at kp ≈ 300, which is exactly what the Godot forum reports: script Hooke's-law drives "randomly flop around" at 30 ticks and are only acceptable "above 45 ticks" after moving to server calls with a fixed delta and physics-mode skeleton callbacks ([forum topic 143292](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/143292)). Two remedies exist and this plan uses both. Solver-side springs are implicit and stable at any step (Catto), so the joint drives go through Jolt's motors as above. Where script must apply torques or forces (virtual forces, gravity compensation, the balance law's feed-forward), use Stable PD, `τ = −kp(q + Δt·q̇ − q̄_{n+1}) − kd(q̇ + Δt·q̈)`, which tracks with arbitrarily high gains at 33 ms steps under explicit Euler provided **kd ≥ kp·Δt** ([Tan, Liu, Turk 2011 PDF](https://www.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)); at 120 Hz a kp of 300 needs only kd ≥ 2.5, comfortably below either damping rule.

Jolt in Godot runs exactly one collision step per tick (`physics_system->Update(p_step, 1, …)` in `modules/jolt_physics/spaces/jolt_space_3d.cpp:196`) and exposes no sub-stepping setting, so the only fidelity knob is `physics/common/physics_ticks_per_second`, whose docs say CPU cost scales roughly linearly and that `max_physics_steps_per_frame` (default 8) should be raised with it (`doc/classes/ProjectSettings.xml:2717-2741`). Jolt caps a frequency-mode spring at half the simulation frequency ([Jolt Architecture page](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html)), Catto's semi-implicit rule wants at least four steps per oscillation period, and Jolt's "stiff" 20 Hz drive therefore needs ≥ 80 Hz; **120 Hz** is the defensible choice, with `max_physics_steps_per_frame` raised to 16 and `physics/common/physics_interpolation` enabled so rendering stays smooth at any frame rate (3D interpolation shipped in 4.4 and moved to SceneTree in 4.5; move interpolated objects only in `_physics_process` and call `reset_physics_interpolation()` on teleports) ([Godot 4.4 release](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.4/); [Using physics interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/interpolation/using_physics_interpolation.html)). The other Jolt project settings to set deliberately are the solver iterations (default 10 velocity / 2 position; friction needs ≥ 2), sleeping (0.03 m/s, 0.5 s), `continuous_cd` per fast body (Jolt `LinearCast`), and `limits/max_linear_velocity`, whose **500 m/s default** must be raised long before a Mach-8 tier (`modules/jolt_physics/jolt_project_settings.cpp:37-73`; `modules/jolt_physics/objects/jolt_body_3d.cpp:1192-1202`). Keep the tick fixed at runtime, since hinge limits and Baumgarte tuning are step-dependent. No measured Jolt cost at 120 or 240 Hz inside Godot exists; profile with the "Physics Process" monitor and Jolt's body-pair counters. For scale, LocoTest measured 0.1 ms per 0.5 ms step for one character in ODE/Bullet on 2011 hardware, and Jolt's 3680-body ragdoll benchmark scales 4.9× on 8 threads ([LocoTest PDF](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~kkyin/papers/locotest.pdf); [Jolt multicore scaling PDF](https://jrouwe.nl/jolt/JoltPhysicsMulticoreScaling.pdf)).

### The behaviour layer: Euphoria's parameters as a checklist for reactions

Euphoria was never published, but its GTA V message schema is, through the ScriptHookVDotNet wrapper, and it reads as an architecture: every behaviour scales a shared per-region stiffness/damping and sets targets, and behaviours stack by message ([SHVDN Euphoria class list](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_euphoria.html)). Its balancer is the piece to copy first. `ConfigureBalance` steps 0.1 m high, with `PredictionTime` 0.2 s ("Bigger values = fewer, bigger steps"), gives up when `BalanceAbortThreshold` lean exceeds **0.6** or the COM comes within `GiveUpHeight` **0.5 m** of the lowest foot, counts balance as regained below 0.3 m/s and 0.3 rad/s, budgets `MaxSteps` 100 and `MaxBalanceTime` 50 s, and on failure uses `FallType = RampDownStiffness` rather than a hard switch; `OpposeGravityLegs/Ankles` 1.0 is gravity compensation ([SHVDN ConfigureBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_configure_balance_helper.html)). `BodyBalance` throws the arms out on a push above 1.2 rad/s of somersault (with the memorable note "Unless drunk - DO NOT EXCEED 7.0"), delays the hands asymmetrically by 0.3–0.7 s, and braces with `BraceStiffness` 12 ([SHVDN BodyBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_body_balance_helper.html)). `HighFall` transitions to landing **0.3 s before impact** (`Catchfalltime`), decides between land and bail-out by an uprightness cut-off of 0.9, windmills arms and pedals legs at 7.9 rad/s, uses `LegStrength` 12 at touchdown, caps the orientation torque at 300 ("will orientate the character softly"), and declares falls recoverable only below **15 m/s** with bracing starting at 10 m/s horizontal ([SHVDN HighFallHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_high_fall_helper.html)). `BraceForImpact` reaches at 0.5 m, predicts 0.5 s ahead, absorbs over 0.2 s and grabs with `GrabStrength` 40 for 2.5 s ([SHVDN BraceForImpactHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_brace_for_impact_helper.html)). Whole-character knobs `SetCharacterStrength` and `SetCharacterHealth` run 0..1 on a "dead-granny-to-healthy-terminator scale" ([SHVDN Euphoria](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_euphoria.html)). NaturalMotion's patent describes the same runtime as a "control frame" per part pulled toward a "target frame" by a connection with stiffness and damping, or by an explicit virtual wrench, with per-part permitted degrees of freedom ([US9177409B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9177409B2/en)), and the 2013 interview confirms behaviours are combined "like LEGO bricks", that developers "can cheat the system with various forces", and that Euphoria "is not a replacement for inverse kinematics" (IK still does doors and ladders) ([MCV/DEVELOP 2013](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/ecstatic-movement-naturalmotion-on-euphoria/)). Believability came from deliberate asymmetry and randomisation rather than fidelity, which is cheap to copy.

Impacts follow Zordan's recipe: on contact, drop the tracking gains for a **0.1–0.2 s** reaction delay "in the range of real human response delays", then ramp back toward tracking of a reaction target; too short a delay "appears to be anticipating the contact", too long "as if stunned" ([Zordan et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1073204.1073249)); EA's ragdoll patent halves per-part force and torque limits during collisions and minimises them on ground contact ([US7403202](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7403202)); Unity's open active-ragdoll repos reduce drive coefficients while colliding and interpolate them back ([EggyStudio active ragdoll](https://github.com/EggyStudio/Unity.Humanoid.ActiveRagdoll)). Falls and get-ups are a Faloutsos chain of controllers with pre-conditions, `balance → fall → default → rollover → prone-to-standing → balance`, where the fall controller's pre-conditions are the complement of the balance controller's and its "main action is to absorb the shock of the impact using mostly the hands" ([Faloutsos 2001 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2001-siggraph-composable.pdf)). Overgrowth's shipped version of the same idea is the cheapest: while ragdolled it drives a soft weld toward a blend of three poses (flail base, plus fetal and catch-fall-front layers whose opacities come from a 4 m sliding-sphere proximity probe and the dot of torso-forward with the hazard direction), with `ragdoll_strength = clamp(0.1·|v|, 0, 0.8)`, strength mapped onto Bullet's `STOP_ERP = 0.2·strength`, `CFM = 1 − strength`, damping accumulating to sleep after 0.4 m/s stillness, and wake-up to stand, roll or flip after a 1 s recovery timer once `|v|² < 20` ([aschar.as](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/aschar.as); [bulletworld.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Source/Physics/bulletworld.cpp)). In Jolt the ERP/CFM ramp is a 6DOF constraint with zero angular limits and soft-limit spring settings scaled by strength, a cheaper alternative to motors for the limp-to-active transition ([Catto GDC 2011](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_SoftConstraints_GDC2011.pdf)).

Bind the Jolt-only joint parameters and flags in `JoltPhysicsServer3D::_bind_methods` (currently empty at `jolt_physics_server_3d.h:120`), which exposes `FrequencyAndDamping` springs, hinge `MOTOR_MAX_TORQUE`, and cone-twist swing/twist motors (`jolt_physics_server_3d.h:68-118`; `jolt_physics_server_3d.cpp:1808-1935`). ## A single competence scalar turns a toddler into a walker in the opening

### What the precedents teach about clumsy control

The clumsy-locomotion canon splits into three families, and only one of them is safe for a first-person game that must stay playable. Direct per-limb actuation (QWOP's thigh and calf keys; Baby Steps' one trigger per leg, "Pull a trigger tight to lift and bend one of his knees, and release it bit by bit to swing out his lower leg", with the left stick supplying momentum; Manual Samuel; Octodad's mouse-button-per-leg) produces the purest embodiment but demands concentration "in the game's first few minutes" and, in Octodad's case, players "would forget which mode they were in" ([Engadget on Baby Steps](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/baby-steps-preview-serious-gameplay-in-a-silly-walking-sim-150008737.html); [Octodad post-mortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/octodad-dadliest-catch-post-mortem-pt-3-design); [QWOP, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QWOP)). Indirect control of a self-balancing rig (Sumotori Dreams, where "your input doesn't control the rig directly, but rather nudges it off-balance" and winners use "small, controller movements, or sometimes not move at all"; GTA IV's drunk state, "fully simulated" by Euphoria with the stick reduced to a directional bias) is the family that maps onto a PD ragdoll ([Fun-Motion on Sumotori](https://www.fun-motion.com/physics-games/sumotori-dreams/); [TechCrunch quoting Reil on GTA IV drunkenness](https://techcrunch.com/2008/02/21/gta-iv-drunkenness-to-use-dynamic-physics-engine/); [Grand Theft Wiki on Euphoria control](https://www.grandtheftwiki.com/Euphoria)). The third family, a normal controller with a separate balance channel (Death Stranding: "If Sam is tilting left you must press R2 to pull him further right", hold both to brace), is the expert layer that survives into the competent game ([PowerPyx, Death Stranding cargo balance](https://www.powerpyx.com/death-stranding-how-to-balance-cargo/)). Two design findings recur. Baby Steps' Cuzzillo found that "manually controlling the lifts gave you more control and felt better" than automatic stepping, yet the same game refuses upgrades entirely because "the core gameplay was deep enough", and Foddy keeps "the required stuff pretty easy" while optional content goes "extremely spicy" ([Game Rant, Baby Steps interview](https://gamerant.com/baby-steps-interview-gamble-walking-gameplay-only/); [Game Rant on Baby Steps refusing systems](https://gamerant.com/baby-steps-developers-systems-simple-limit-why/)). The cautionary datum is Metal Gear Solid V's prologue: roughly 45 minutes, about 15 with cutscenes skipped, unskippable as a whole, remembered as "crawl around on the ground for a very long time, slowly" with scripted trips the player cannot influence ([Steam discussion on the MGSV prologue](https://steamcommunity.com/app/287700/discussions/0/527273983050716645/); [Steam discussion on skipping it](https://steamcommunity.com/app/287700/discussions/0/371918937262241404/)). Fallout 3's toddler section is the opposite: a few minutes of ordinary controls at low camera height that end in a meaningful choice ([GameBanshee walkthrough](https://www.gamebanshee.com/fallout3/walkthrough/growingup.php)). Impairment that removes agency reads as padding; impairment that demands skill reads as the game. Nothing shipped literally grows controller competence over an opening; that is white space, and the numbers below fill it.

The recommended grammar therefore keeps one consistent mapping whose meaning never switches: WASD and mouse are always intent, and competence changes the body's **response** to that intent, not the inputs. At competence zero the intent capsule's authority over the pelvis is near zero and the stick is a Sumotori nudge to a weak balance controller; by the end of the opening the authority reaches its human-nominal value and the same inputs produce ordinary first-person walking. A Death-Stranding-style lean pair on Q/E is offered as an optional expert channel throughout, never required, because Jusant's playtests found extra limb controls "too complicated" and Human: Fall Flat rejected independent per-arm sticks as "really messy" ([Game Developer on Jusant](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/unlocking-the-catharsis-of-climbing-in-meditative-puzzler-jusant); [TheGamer, Sakalauskas interview](https://www.thegamer.com/human-fall-flat-interview-tomas-sakalauskas/)).

### Sourced endpoints for the competence parameterisation

"Drunk", "ataxic" and "toddler" are different signatures, and the brief's "early-RL" look is closest to the toddler with an erratic high-torque quality; pick one deliberately. Alcohol at 0.11 % BrAC barely changes forward gait (women's velocity 4.17 → 4.33 km/h, step width 9.26 → 9.57 cm, cadence 107.6 → 106.8, none significant) but multiplies standing sway once BAC passes 0.5–0.8 mg/mL, with sway biased medial-lateral and its frequency unchanged ([Sci Rep 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9637089/); [Kubo 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0196-0709(90)90121-b); [Noda 2004](https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.98.3.873-887)). Cerebellar ataxia is the "cannot coordinate" model: walking speed **1.10 vs 1.43 m/s**, stride length 70.8 vs 85.9 % of stature, stride-velocity CoV **0.069 vs 0.028**, arm-swing range 20.6 vs 32.0°, wide base, prolonged double support, and specifically increased temporal variability of intra-limb coordination ([Kroneberg 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11174553/); [Buckley 2018 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2017.11.024); [Ilg 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awl376)). Infant walking is the "learning" model: novice 12-month walkers fall **31.5 times per hour**, about one fall per 69 steps, with 46 % of walking bouts only 1–3 steps long; step width narrows from **0.19 m to 0.15 m to 0.12 m** over the first 15 weeks while hip-flexion range falls 42.5° → 29.1° and upper-limb elevation drops from 78–87° (arms abducted, elbows extended) to 47–49° before reciprocal swing appears; the speed range triples and step width halves over six months, with early velocity gains coming from step length and later ones from cadence ([Adolph 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3591461/); [Gimunová 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8744759/); [Bril & Brenière 1992](https://doi.org/10.1080/00222895.1992.9941606); [Sutherland 1980 PDF](http://www.analisedemarcha.com/papers/historia/The%20development%20of%20mature%20gait%20-%201980.pdf)). Sensorimotor delay is the cleanest deterministic wobble: with an added feedback delay of 20 ms everyone balances, at 100 ms there is minimal difficulty, at **≥ 200 ms every participant exceeded the balance limits at least once**, at 400 ms people stayed upright 64 % of the first minute rising to 97 % after 100 minutes of practice, and the natural loop is about 100–160 ms ([Rasman et al. 2021, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/65085)).

The animation literature supplies the knobs that convert those endpoints into controller settings. SIMBICON's cd = 0.5, cv = 0.2 are the nominal balance gains; Learning to Get Up shows that a torque-limit curriculum in 0.95× steps toward **40–60 % of human strength** produces motions perceived as natural, that full-strength training yields "highly dynamic and erratic" motion, and uses kp = T (the torque limit), kd = kp/10, 40 Hz control over 800 Hz simulation ([SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [Learning to Get Up, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00307)); Reda et al. show control frequency far from its optimum "is usually harmful" ([Reda 2020, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.04304)); Yu et al. train with "modulated physical assistance" that is "gradually relax[ed]" ([Yu 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08093)); human motor learning removes gross location errors first and execution noise last ([Cohen & Sternad 2009](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00221-008-1596-1)). The table below is the resulting schedule for a competence scalar s ∈ [0, 1]; every endpoint is sourced above and every knob exists in the controller of the previous section.

| Knob (owner) | s = 0 (first minute) | s = 1 (competent walking) | Source of endpoints |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pelvis authority force limit (intent spring) | ≈ 0 N (nudge only) | human-nominal, sized so a 0.1 s 600 N push is absorbed | Sumotori/GTA IV nudge model; SIMBICON push tolerance |
| Joint torque limits (6DOF drive cap) | 40–50 % of human T, kp = T, kd = kp/10 | 100 % of human T | Learning to Get Up curriculum |
| Balance feedback cd / cv | ≈ 0.15–0.25 / 0.05–0.10 (under-corrects) | 0.5 / 0.2 | SIMBICON nominal |
| Sensory delay on balance-state input (ring buffer) | 200–300 ms | 0 ms | Rasman 2021 |
| Balance/step decision rate | low action-repeat (chunky, late steps) | tuned rate (≈ 30–40 Hz) | Reda 2020; Get Up 40 Hz |
| Step width target | 0.19 m | 0.12 m, then adult 0.09–0.12 m | Gimunová 2021; Sci Rep 2022 |
| Step-time coefficient of variation (seeded jitter) | 0.07 | 0.03 | Kroneberg 2024 |
| Hip-flexion range / arm pose | 42° / arms abducted ≈ 80° | 29° / reciprocal swing ≈ 30° range | Gimunová 2021; Kroneberg 2024 |
| Lateral COM-proxy offset (ML-biased, low-pass) | large, slow | none | Noda 2004 |
| Fall rate target | ≈ 1 per 70 steps | ≈ 0 | Adolph 2012 |
| Walking speed | 1.10 m/s (bouts of 1–3 steps) | 1.43 m/s continuous | Kroneberg 2024; Adolph 2012 |

Bril and Brenière's two-phase result (postural integration first, cadence tuning after about five months) argues for two sub-phases inside the opening: phase one unlocks *not falling* (authority, delay, base width), phase two unlocks *speed* via cadence, which is the seam where the later "running faster" power attaches ([Bril & Brenière 1992](https://doi.org/10.1080/00222895.1992.9941606)). Adolph's bout statistics say novice locomotion should be sold as many short bouts with frequent stops and immediate get-ups, not slow continuous walking; a fall every ~70 steps in the first minutes, tapering to near zero, is the defensible target ([Adolph 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3591461/)). Because the delay is a ring buffer and the jitter is seeded, the wobble is fully reproducible for a given input stream, which the QA and replay plan needs.

What advances s is a design decision the notes leave open, with two sourced options. Use-based growth in the Kingdom Come / Skyrim / Dying Light 2 pattern ("skills improve organically through use"; parkour XP for "more complex and skillful movements") is the most natural pacing for a body that is fictionally learning, but it needs Kingdom Come's "initial training" gate or Gravity Rush's reputation cap to stop grinding ([Kingdom Come Wiki, wiki-only](https://kingdom-come-deliverance.fandom.com/wiki/Skills); [Stevivor on Dying Light 2 skill trees](https://stevivor.com/news/dying-light-2-details-new-skill-trees/); [Gravity Rush Wiki, wiki-only](https://gravityrush.fandom.com/wiki/Upgrades)). The alternative is a short scripted curve tied to distance walked, on Fallout 3's model. Either way the opening must satisfy Foddy's flavours of frustration (aim for "Nearly There But Not Quite", avoid "Start Over" and "Going Nowhere", so input always visibly moves the body even badly) and George Fan's tutorial rules (teach one leg-lift by doing, at most eight words on screen, adaptive prompts only for players who have fallen N times) ([foddy.net, Eleven Flavors of Frustration](https://www.foddy.net/blog/2017/01/eleven-flavors-of-frustration/); [Game Developer, George Fan's ten tutorial tips](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan)). Progress should be legible in the body rather than a bar: narrowing base, arms coming down, fewer falls per hundred steps, a steadier camera. Keep the impaired phase short on the required path and move the long tail of clumsy mastery into optional content, as Baby Steps does ([Kill Screen, Foddy interview](https://www.killscreen.com/bennett-foddy-baby-steps-interview/)).

Shipping RL checkpoints as skill levels, the brief's "early-RL" metaphor taken literally, has no shipped precedent: early policies are seed-sensitive and "entertaining but visually unsatisfactory" in uncontrolled ways, Ubisoft tuned Roller Champions bot difficulty by a penalty multiplier rather than by checkpoint choice, and the one stable interpolation result (Rocca et al. 2025, linear interpolation of network weights) requires a graph-regularised training scheme ([Heess 2017, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.02286); [Iskander et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06031); [Rocca et al. 2025 project page](https://michelerocca.github.io/projects/policy-space_linterp/)). The detuned classical controller above is deterministic and tunable per parameter, and it is the recommendation.

## Each power arrives one tier before the body can survive it

### The biomechanics that set the thresholds

The ladder's numbers come from human performance ceilings, injury thresholds and six formulas, all with m = 70 kg and g = 9.81 m/s². An ordinary trained adult sprints at about **7.35 m/s**, brakes at −4.4 m/s² on average and −8.5 m/s² at peak, and needs 1.5 s and 6.86 m to stop from that speed; Usain Bolt's best 10 m segment was **12.34 m/s**, so anything above about 12.5 m/s is superhuman ([Harper 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474351/); [Maćkała & Mero 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3661886/)). With a 0.22 s simple reaction time and `d = v·t_react + v²/(2a)`, stopping distance at average and peak braking is 7.0/4.8 m from 7.35 m/s, 12.2/8.1 m from 10 m/s, 17.9/11.7 m from 12.34 m/s, 25.8/16.5 m from 15 m/s, 44.4/27.9 m from 20 m/s and 96.6/59.5 m from 30 m/s; Harper's measured 6.86 m sits between the two model rows, which validates the constants ([Woods 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4374455/); [Harper 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474351/)). A good adult countermovement jump raises the centre of mass **0.4 m** (an 82 kg musculoskeletal model, peak power 49 W/kg), 0.7 m is elite, and the design's superhuman tiers begin above about 1 m ([Bobbert 2013](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0071209); [Topend Sports vertical-jump norms, non-primary](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/norms/vertical-jump.htm)).

Falls are scored by impact speed `v = sqrt(2gh)` (1 m → 4.4 m/s, 2 m → 6.3, 3 m → 7.7, 5 m → 9.9, 6 m → 10.9, 10 m → 14.0, 20 m → 19.8) and by leg force. The pooled two-leg landing model is **PvGRF [BW] = 0.49·sqrt(h_cm) + 0.37**, valid to 1.03 m, and a rigid landing that stops the body over 0.5 m of knee flexion averages `a = g·h/s`, i.e. 6 g and 4.8 kN at 3 m, 10 g and 7.6 kN at 5 m, 20 g and 14.4 kN at 10 m, with real peaks 1.5–2.5× the mean ([Niu 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4160626/)). Against that, the tibia fractures at about 7.5–11.3 kN, the foot/ankle complex reaches 50 % fracture risk at **3.7–8.3 kN**, and the regulatory femur limit is 10 kN ([Zain Ul Abidin 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41769460/); [Funk 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12596644/); [49 CFR 571.208](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2023-title49-vol6/xml/CFR-2023-title49-vol6-sec571-208.xml)). The clinical series agree on where it turns lethal: falls over 6 m carry an adjusted odds ratio of 10 for death, 50 % mortality sits near 10.5 m with head or chest injury and 22 m without, or 14.8 m in modern US trauma data, a hard surface roughly triples the odds of death, and head-first contact multiplies them 16.7-fold relative to feet-first ([Hsieh 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7312001/); [Dickinson 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22860503/); [Smith 2017, snippet only](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1460408616689807); [Lapostolle 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15942337/)). Technique is worth a tier by itself: from 0.75 m, a parkour roll cuts peak force from 5.2 to **2.9 BW (−43 %)** and loading rate by 63 %, precision landing −38 % and −54 % ([Puddle & Maulder 2013](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-12-122.xml%3EFulltext)). Euphoria's own `MaxSpeedForRecoverableFall` of 15 m/s (an 11.5 m drop) is the shipped-game version of the same boundary ([SHVDN HighFallHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_high_fall_helper.html)).

Running into a wall is scored by `a = v²/(2s)` over a compliance distance s. For a rigid chest-first stop (s = 0.1 m) versus an arms-first crumple (s = 0.3 m), a 70 kg body sees 27.5 g / 18.9 kN versus 9.2 g / 6.3 kN at 7.35 m/s, 78 g / 53 kN versus 26 g / 18 kN at 12.34 m/s, 204 g / 140 kN versus 68 g / 47 kN at 20 m/s, and 459 g versus 153 g at 30 m/s (computed from `a = v²/(2s)`; the 0.1 m and 0.3 m compliance distances are engineering assumptions, since no study measures a human running into a fixed barrier). The anchors are Stapp's voluntary **46 g** over 1.4 s, the FMVSS chest limit of **60 g** and HIC15 ≤ **700** (about 5 % probability of AIS 4+ head injury), the 10 kN femur limit, the 8 kN OSHA harness cap, and Bräck's survived 214 g with multiple fractures ([NM Museum of Space History on Stapp](https://nmspacemuseum.org/inductee/john-p-stapp/); [49 CFR 571.208](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2023-title49-vol6/xml/CFR-2023-title49-vol6-sec571-208.xml); [HIC mini review](https://biomedgrid.com/fulltext/volume5/head-injury-criterion-mini-review.000957.php); [OSHA 1926.502](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.502); [Guinness on Bräck](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/67617-highest-g-force-endured-non-voluntary)). So an unpowered sprinter hitting concrete is at fracture level if rigid and at bruising level if the arms absorb it; a head that stops over 2 cm from 7.35 m/s sees about 138 g, above the 95–105 g concussion cluster, so unprotected head-first wall contact should daze by default ([Rowson & Duma 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3624001/); [Broglio 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2943536/)). The pedestrian curves give a smooth speed-to-death probability in m/s already: **P = 1/(1 + exp(6.9 − 0.090·v_kmh))** yields 1.5 % at 30 km/h, 8 % at 50, 35 % at 70, 57 % at 80 and 89 % at 100 km/h; Tefft's US curve reaches 50 % death at 65 km/h (18 m/s) ([Rosén & Sander 2009](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/pedestrian_fatality_risk_function_car_impact_speed_rosen.pdf); [Tefft 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22935347/)). A car front is more compliant than a wall, so these are lower bounds on lethality.

Grabbing has the hardest ceiling. Maximum grip is about **480 N per hand** for men and 293 N for women, experienced climbers hang two-handed for 77 s, the A2 finger pulley sees ~3× fingertip force in a crimp and fails near 400 N, and a body caught by the arms over d metres of shoulder give needs `F = m·v²/(2d) + m·g`: 1.3–1.7 kN at 3 m/s, 2.4–3.6 kN at 5 m/s, 7.7–12.4 kN at 10 m/s ([Bohannon & Magasi 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24729356/); [Yu 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10433161/); [Schweizer 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11165286/)). Two maximal grips total about 1 kN and real climbing falls put 2.5–4 kN through a harness, so the human ceiling for a ledge catch is roughly **3–4 m/s of downward speed**, a drop of 0.5–0.8 m below the ledge; above that the hands slip or tissue fails ([Petzl, forces in a real fall](https://www.petzl.com/US/en/Sport/Forces-at-work-in-a-real-fall)). Strength is bounded by the same logic from the other side: an Olympic boxer delivers 3.4–4.1 kN at 9 m/s with an effective mass of about 3 kg (~121 J), half-inch gypsum board fails at 489 N in flexure and is penetrated by a 67 J soft impact, two layers of 5/8-inch Type X need about 400 J, a 73 kg boulder breaks through a single-leaf brick wall at **500 J**, and brick averages 78 MPa in compression against a 13.8 MPa loadbearing block minimum, so a human punches through drywall at tier zero, shoulder-charges double drywall at about 3.4 m/s, and cannot break masonry at all ([Walilko 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16183766/); [Gypsum Association GA-235](https://www.americangypsum.com/sites/default/files/2022-01/ga-235_gypsum_board_typical_mechanical_and_physical_properties.pdf); [Mavrouli 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences14090226); [BIA Technical Note 3A](https://www.gobrick.com/media/file/TN_3A_Brick_Masonry_Material_Properties.pdf)). Kinetic energy makes the point about speed: 1,891 J at 7.35 m/s, 3,500 J at 10, 5,330 J at 12.34, 7,875 J at 15, 14,000 J at 20, 31,500 J at 30 m/s, so a sprinter already carries 15–45 boxers' punches and a superhuman runner is a demolition tool ([Engineering ToolBox concrete properties](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/concrete-properties-d_1223.html)).

The square-cube law is what makes the separate durability tiers a physical necessity rather than a design conceit: for a fixed-size body, "jump N times higher" multiplies take-off velocity by sqrt(N) and landing energy and leg force by N, "run N times faster" multiplies kinetic energy and stopping distance by N², while bone, tendon and pulley strengths are properties of tissue cross-section that do not change unless buffed separately ([Bobbert 2013](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0071209); [Square–cube law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square%E2%80%93cube_law)). Each durability tier therefore multiplies the tissue thresholds (tibia ~8 kN, femur 10 kN, HIC15 700, chest 60 g, grip ~0.5 kN per hand, pulley ~0.4 kN) by the same factor as the power tier's force gain, one tier late.

### The damage model that the physics produces for free

Read the contact normal impulse J on the head, torso and pelvis bodies (Jolt reports it through `PhysicsDirectBodyState3D.get_contact_impulse`, exact only for isolated pairs, and `RigidBody3D` needs `contact_monitor` and `max_contacts_reported > 0`) and the closing speed along the normal; compute `a_mean = J/(m·Δt)` and `F = J/Δt`; then classify: above 46 g blackout, above 60 g or an equivalent HIC15 of 700 serious injury, leg force above the tier's tibia/femur limit fracture, and a Rosén–Sander draw for death at the impact speed; use the Rowson–Duma combined probability `CP = 1/(1 + exp(−(−10.2 + 0.0433a + 0.000873α − 0.00000092aα)))` for a smooth dazed state ([Godot docs, Using Jolt Physics](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html); `doc/classes/RigidBody3D.xml`; [Rowson & Duma 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3624001/)). Multiply by technique and surface: roll 0.57× peak force, soft surface 1/2.7 odds, head-first 16.7× odds, dorsal or lateral first contact survival odds 0.11–0.17 ([Puddle & Maulder 2013](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-12-122.xml%3EFulltext); [Lapostolle 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15942337/); [Fujii 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8324820/)). Two shipped formulas bracket the presentation. Source computes fall damage linearly above a safe speed, `damage = (v − v_safe)·100/(v_fatal − v_safe)`, with safe about 20 ft and fatal about 60 ft ("so damage will be based on speed beyond that, not the entire fall"), subtracts a platform's velocity and a 173 u/s allowance for floating objects, and rolls the view by `0.013° per u/s` capped at 8° pitch ([Source SDK shareddefs.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/shareddefs.h); [singleplay_gamerules.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/singleplay_gamerules.cpp)); under real gravity the height intent (safe 6 m ≈ 10.9 m/s, fatal 18 m ≈ 18.9 m/s) is the thing to copy, and it matches Minecraft's 3-block safe / 23.5-block fatal rule ([Minecraft Wiki, Damage](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Damage)). Minecraft's elytra is the only shipped horizontal-impact formula found, `damage = 10·Δv_horizontal − 3` for vertical surfaces only, harmless at sprint speed and lethal near 46 m/s, which is exactly the shape the brief wants ([Minecraft Wiki, Elytra](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Elytra)). Generalise both as `damage = k·max(0, |v_rel·n| − v_safe)` on any contact normal, and copy Quake III's quadratic solve for the true impact speed inside a fixed step so damage does not depend on tick phase ([Quake III bg_pmove.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/id-Software/Quake-III-Arena/master/code/game/bg_pmove.c)). Rolls should shift the safe threshold by a fixed allowance rather than zero the damage, since no shipped roll cancels a lethal fall (Dying Light: building-height falls "will still kill you regardless of attempting to roll") ([Dying Light Wiki, snippet](https://dyinglight.fandom.com/wiki/Forward_Roll)).

### The three ladders

Shipped superhero games use short ladders of 4–6 tiers fed by a large visible collectible economy (Crackdown 500 orbs for 4 tiers with "+5 feet each level" from 15 to 30 ft; Crackdown 3 750 orbs for 6; Saints Row IV 1,255 clusters with 1,065 needed), over-supplied so completion is optional, and they order each ladder "raw capability, then the ability that makes it safe": Crackdown 3's Boosters (L1) precede Air Dash (L2) and Ground Pound (Strength L2) precedes Resilience (L4); Prototype's Airdash (2,500 EP) precedes Glide (15,000) and Air Recovery (30,000); Saints Row IV requires Air Dash before Glide and sells stamina cost in 75 % → 50 % → unlimited steps with its tuning parameters printed in the upgrade text (`super_lateral_velocity` 130 %, `glide_terminal_velocity` 75 %, `super_sprint_buildup_time` 50 %) ([Crackdown Wiki, wiki-only](https://crackdown.fandom.com/wiki/Agility); [Shacknews, Crackdown 3](https://www.shacknews.com/article/109880/skills-abilities-and-leveling-fast-in-crackdown-3); [Altered Gamer, Prototype](https://www.alteredgamer.com/prototype/39802-powers-movement-and-survivability-guide/); [Saints Row Wiki, wiki-only](https://saintsrow.fandom.com/wiki/Super_Powers_in_Saints_Row_IV)). The games that keep real consequences pair each with a self-rescue verb: Dishonored's Blink to a ledge cancels fall damage, Just Cause 3's wingsuit could only stop by crashing, parachuting or a purchased air-brake mod that the sequel made standard, Breath of the Wild's stamina wheel appears only during effort and its exhaustion drops you, Dark Souls doubles roll length above 50 % load while keeping i-frames fixed, Death Stranding's cargo tilts Sam ([Dishonored Wiki, wiki-only](https://dishonored.fandom.com/wiki/Blink); [Just Cause Wiki, wiki-only](https://justcause.fandom.com/wiki/Wingsuit); [Zelda Wiki, wiki-only](https://zelda.fandom.com/wiki/Stamina_Wheel); [Fextralife, Equip Load](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Equip_Load)). Schell's rule that punishment be "understood and preventable", Dormans' deadlock rule (never lock a key behind the lock it opens), Anthropy and Clark's "safe areas" and inFamous's rule that a new power be usable "immediately after they got it" are the constraints on ordering ([Schell notes](https://notesbylex.com/the-art-of-game-design-a-book-of-lenses-2nd-edition-by-jesse-schell); [Dormans 2011](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/dormans2011design.pdf); [First Person Scholar on A Game Design Vocabulary](https://www.firstpersonscholar.com/a-game-design-vocabulary/); [Infamous, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infamous_(video_game))). The ladders below combine those precedents with the thresholds above; no shipped game both grows movement powers gradually and keeps lethal consequences throughout, so playtest validation is mandatory.

**Ladder V, vertical (jump height against landing).** Fall damage uses the 6 m safe / 18 m fatal intent at V0 and the durability multiplier thereafter; the grab is available from minute one because a ledge catch is the always-available rescue, but it is only physically possible below 3–4 m/s of descent.

**Ladder S, strength (load against balance and world reaction).** S0 grabs and lifts small objects and punches through drywall (489 N, 67 J); S1 lifts heavy loads that shift balance and lengthen recovery (Death Stranding tilts above 60 kg; Dark Souls 25/50 % thresholds); S2 shoulder-charges double drywall (~400 J at 3.4 m/s) and throws objects, raising a world-response meter; S3 breaks masonry (20–50× a boxer's 3.4 kN) but the hand is gated separately because metacarpal constructs fail at 0.4–0.6 kN in bending and boxers already fracture at 3–4 kN, so a **hand-durability** tier must precede it; S4 Resilience covers self-inflicted knockback from ground pound and charge; S5–S6 lift vehicles and buildings and leave permanent world-state changes ([PowerPyx](https://www.powerpyx.com/death-stranding-how-to-balance-cargo/); [Allen 2026, fixation constructs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41584052/); [Wikipedia, Hulk: Ultimate Destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Incredible_Hulk:_Ultimate_Destruction)). In the engine every S tier is a Blast-style hardness comparison: `Damage = Impulse / Hardness`, with a brick wall's hardness set so ~270 N·s (73 kg at 3.7 m/s) breaks it, making an unpowered sprinter (≈ 480 N·s) marginal and a 15 m/s runner (1,200 N·s) decisive ([Blast UE4 settings](https://archive.docs.nvidia.com/gameworks/content/gameworkslibrary/blast/1.1/authoring_docs/BlastUe4_BlastSettings.html); [Mavrouli 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences14090226)).

**The top tier is the Megaton Rainfall state.** Once V6/H6/S6 make the body unhurtable, adopt the city/casualty bar as the mission health bar regardless of who caused the damage ("it doesn't really matter if it's the attacks of the invaders or the defending hero"), communicate it diegetically (collapsing buildings, mass screams), score on time plus casualties, keep adding powers that raise collateral risk rather than personal safety, tune the bar so a careful player can win (reviewers found it "often simply impossible"), add braking assistance at speed ("easy to overshoot"), and budget mission variety because the loop wore thin after the first hour once personal danger was gone ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-megaton-rainfall/276013/); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/megaton_rainfall); [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/megaton-rainfall-review-google-earth-meets-man-steel/); [PlayStation Blog, Aug 2017](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/08/09/first-person-superhero-sim-megaton-rainfall-flies-to-ps4-ps-vr-september-26/)).

**Economy and teaching.** Feed the ladders with about 100–150 pickups per tier over-supplied by 15–20 % and labelled with a recommended tier (Crackdown's orbs), or with use-based growth where a stat grows through its own risky use (Crackdown's "kill from height → Agility"), capped so a tolerance tier never lags more than one behind its capability tier ([Crackdown Wiki, wiki-only](https://crackdown.fandom.com/wiki/Agility_Orb); [Crackdown, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crackdown_(video_game))). Each tier gets a "consequence tutorial": a contextual gauge that appears only above the safe threshold (a kinetic-energy or impact-speed arc in the BotW style), a highlight of what tames it (ledges, soft landings, brake surfaces, in Runner Vision's style, disableable as skill grows), a proprioceptive telegraph before impact (Titanfall's camera tilt before a wall-run; Dark Souls' longer roll), and one forced safe first use (inFamous core relays, Titanfall's Gauntlet) before the first lethal instance ([Game Developer, Titanfall controls interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designer-interview-getting-i-titanfall-i-s-controls-just-right); [Mirror's Edge, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror%27s_Edge); [Infamous Second Son, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infamous_Second_Son)). DICE judged full stopping inertia "irritating" even with Runner Vision, so since inertia is the fun here, the telegraph and the highlight should get stronger with tier, not weaker ([Mirror's Edge, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror%27s_Edge)).

## Right-mouse grab and six verbs carry the whole control scheme

The scheme has to satisfy two masters: it must stay intuitive to a first-person player, and it must make each self-rescue verb an explicit, timed commitment so that the consequence gates bite. The parkour lineage (Mirror's Edge, Dying Light, Apex's auto-mantle) uses one context-sensitive "up" button and auto-grabs from a real-time geometry scan, which maximises flow but hides the decision; the physics-body lineage (Grow Home, Jusant, Human: Fall Flat, Boneworks) maps one grip per hand or per mouse button, hold to grip and release to let go, which Ubisoft describes as giving "a very physical connection to the grabbing and the climbing" ([Game Developer, Mirror's Edge interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-philosophy-of-faith-a-mirror-s-edge-interview); [Ubisoft News on Grow Home](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/7fDmgKCCwWJQtyoVoOvvGM/get-ready-to-grow-home); [DON'T NOD on Jusant](https://dont-nod.com/en/scaling-new-heights-learn-more-about-jusants-innovative-climbing-system/)). Human: Fall Flat is the closest analogue to mouse-look plus a grab button: each hand on a mouse button, arms raised by the look direction ([Human: Fall Flat, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human:_Fall_Flat)). The recommended map is: **WASD** intent, **mouse** look, **RMB** grab (hold to hold, release to drop; both hands reach toward the look direction and grip whatever the hand bodies touch), **LMB** brace/push (arms forward, the crumple that turns a 0.1 m stop into a 0.3 m one, later a shove and a strike), **Space** jump (early in the opening, a deliberate step commit), **Ctrl** crouch, which also arms the landing roll and starts a slide at speed, **Shift** sprint, and **Q/E** as the optional Death-Stranding lean channel. The grab is not context-mapped to jump precisely so that "high jump before fall tolerance" produces a real choice under time pressure, and it remains one button because Jusant found leg controls "too complicated" ([Game Developer on Jusant](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/unlocking-the-catharsis-of-climbing-in-meditative-puzzler-jusant)).

The intent capsule's locomotion math is the Quake lineage, which is fully reproducible from released source and is what Titanfall, Apex and the surf culture Neon White cites all descend from. Acceleration projects velocity onto the wish direction and adds `accel × wishspeed × dt`, never more than the shortfall; friction decays speed exponentially above `stopspeed` and linearly below it, which is why Quake characters snap to a halt rather than creep ([Quake III bg_pmove.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/id-Software/Quake-III-Arena/master/code/game/bg_pmove.c)). With `pm_accelerate = 10` the ground reaches top speed in 0.1 s; Source's defaults are `sv_accelerate 10`, `sv_friction 4`, `sv_stopspeed 100`, `sv_maxspeed 320` (8.1 m/s at one unit per inch), an air-speed cap of 30 units that is what makes air-strafing gain speed, and a jump of `sqrt(2·g·21 units)` with a clipped forward boost of up to 50 % of max speed ([Source SDK movevars_shared.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/movevars_shared.cpp); [gamemovement.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/gamemovement.cpp); [Apex Legends Wiki on unit conversions](https://apexlegends.wiki.gg/wiki/Movement)). Celeste's constants give the same ratios in 2D and are the tuning targets for feel: ground acceleration about 11× max speed per second, deceleration 4.4×, air control 65 % of ground, jump horizontal boost about 44 % of run speed, a 0.2 s variable-jump window ([Celeste Player.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NoelFB/Celeste/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)). Mirror's Edge is the deliberate outlier, taking 8 s to reach top speed but stopping instantly because inertia was judged "irritating" ([CritPoints, Mirror's Edge review](https://critpoints.net/2020/10/31/mirrors-edge-review/); [Mirror's Edge, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror%27s_Edge)). This design inverts that trade: the intent capsule accelerates and turns like Quake so the player's *intent* is responsive, but the physics rig follows it with real inertia limited by authority, so what the player feels as momentum is the body catching up with the capsule, and the lag is the fun. Every studio quoted spent its "unrealism" in input interpretation rather than in the simulation, and this design does the same.

Forgiveness lives in the input layer, not the physics. Copy Celeste's coyote time (`JumpGraceTime = 0.1 s`) and jump buffering, and Dying Light's deliberately delayed parkour button, added because players "were using the parkour button a little too soon" when the ledge edge was hidden by the first-person view ([Thorson thread via ThreadReader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1238338574220546049.html); [MCV/Develop on Dying Light](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/the-vaulting-dead-implementing-first-person-parkour-in-dying-light/)). The roll accepts two shipped schemes at once: press within a 0.1–0.2 s pre-landing buffer (Mirror's Edge's "about 10 feet") or arm it any time while airborne (Dying Light's Forward Roll) ([Steam Community Mirror's Edge guide, secondary](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails?id=658340379); [Dying Light Wiki, snippet](https://dyinglight.fandom.com/wiki/Forward_Roll)). Ledge detection is a runtime scan keyed to speed, not level markup: Techland abandoned 50,000 hand-placed hooks for a real-time geometric analysis of "height, speed and position" costing 200+ raytraces per frame, and DICE's vaulting went from "fixed metrics" to "fully dynamic" selection by "height, speed and obstacle shape" ([Game Developer, Dying Light deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-dying-light-i-s-natural-movement-system); [DICE GDC slides via SlideServe](https://www.slideserve.com/miron/creating-first-person-movement-for-mirror-s-edge-powerpoint-ppt-presentation)). In Godot that is a handful of `ShapeCast3D` and `intersect_ray_into` probes ahead and above the capsule each tick, predicting time-to-ledge from velocity so the hands start reaching before contact (`doc/classes/ShapeCast3D.xml`; `doc/classes/PhysicsDirectSpaceState3D.xml:119-126`). The grab itself is a hand-body contact that spawns a constraint: at human tiers a 6DOF joint whose linear drive force limit is the grip budget (≈ 1 kN for two hands, ≈ 0.4 kN per crimped finger), so a body arriving faster than 3–4 m/s tears free by physics rather than by rule; a Celeste-style stamina meter (110 units, climbing up burns 100 in 2.2 s, hanging still 10/s, a climb-jump costs 27.5) drains while hanging, with the two-handed hang baseline of about 75 s as the human anchor ([Bohannon & Magasi 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24729356/); [Celeste Player.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NoelFB/Celeste/master/Source/Player/Player.cs); [Yu 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10433161/)). Jusant's playtests rejected per-arm gauges and non-regenerating chunks as punitive or hard to read, so one shared meter with visible resting recovery is right ([Game Informer on Jusant, snippet](https://gameinformer.com/2023/11/29/the-challenging-climb-to-make-jusant)). Player-facing feedback follows the parkour studios' finding that visible limbs, not camera motion, stop the game from feeling like "a floating camera": hands visibly reach, arms visibly brace, the leg mesh is in view when looking down ([MCV/Develop on Dying Light](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/the-vaulting-dead-implementing-first-person-parkour-in-dying-light/)).

## Procedural animation: physics is the animator, poses are only targets

### Generating the target pose without clips

The brief allows authored animation only as an assisting target pose, and the production-proven procedural designs are exactly that. Overgrowth achieved "responsive character animation with great looking results using only 13 keyframes in total for everything" by treating the character as a Newtonian vehicle ("Start with physics, Bumper and lifter"), interpolating between sparse poses with synchronised blends and cubic interpolation, adding "Acceleration tilt" and "Impact compression" from the vehicle's dynamics, then refining with IK, head/torso look and secondary physics ([OCR of Rosen's GDC 2014 slides](https://archive.org/download/GDC2014Rosen/GDC2014-Rosen_djvu.xml); [Game Anim summary](https://www.gameanim.com/2018/03/04/indie-approach-procedural-animation/)). Spore's gait generator assigns each leg group a duty factor ("the fraction of the duration of the overall gait cycle time that the foot is on the ground") and a step trigger offset, authors the flight-phase foot arc in a normalised space scaled by leg length, and interpolates parameter sets by speed ([Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)). Fu's 2025 thesis gives a drop-in gait clock: coupled Hopf oscillators, `ẋi = γ(μ − ri²)xi − ωi yi + Σ wij(xj cos φij − yj sin φij)`, phase-only form `φ̇i = ωi + Σ wij sin(φj − φi − φ⁰ij)`, walk-to-run phase bias scheduled by a sigmoid on speed `φ⁰ij(v) = φlow + (φhigh − φlow)/(1 + e^(−k(v − v0)))` with coupling rising linearly in v, joints mapped as `θhip = A·xi(t) + θoffset`, plus a star-shaped multi-raycast foot-target probe scored by balance, energy and style; its 29-person study found rotation-angle parameters far more impactful for style than displacement ones ([Fu 2025 full text](https://lup.lub.lu.se/luur/download?func=downloadFile&recordOId=9206764&fileOId=9206770)). These three fit together: the CPG supplies phase, SIMBICON's state machine and Coros's pendulum supply where the swing foot goes, Rosen-style two-pose interpolation (or Spore's foot arc) supplies the shape between, and the physics rig makes it real.

Human data fixes how the gait must change with speed, and therefore how to scale it past human speed. Running cadence rises only slowly (169 → 178 steps/min from 2.68 → 3.83 m/s) while stride length grows 0.98 → 1.30 m, stance time shortens 0.28 → 0.24 s, and impact peak and loading rate rise 1.51 → 1.88 BW and 36.8 → 48 BW/s ([Leacox 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12222555/)); the walk–run switch happens near 2.1 m/s and at a Froude number of about 0.5 (`Fr = v²/(g·L)`), at which bipeds of any leg length change gait ([Hansen 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-01972-1); [Kram, Domingo & Ferris 1997](https://spot.colorado.edu/~kram/walk-run.pdf)); and top sprint speed is limited by ground force, not leg-swing rate: across runners from 6.2 to 11.1 m/s, support force rose 1.26× while swing time "showed no significant variation" ([Weyand 2000](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1152/jappl.2000.89.5.1991&resultType=core&format=json)). The design rules follow directly. Keep swing time roughly human, shorten ground contact and add flight time as speed grows, so superhuman running looks like bounding with larger vertical ground force rather than a sped-up jog; and blend gaits on an effective Froude number `Fr = v²/(g_eff·L)` with `g_eff = s·g` for strength multiplier s, so the same tables keep working as powers grow and dynamically similar poses appear at speeds scaled by sqrt(s) ([Alexander & Jayes 1983](https://zslpublications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1469-7998.1983.tb04266.x)). The Leacox trend also gives the "gating by consequence" signal: extrapolating impact peak and loading rate with speed is the landing-force input to the damage model.

IK closes the loop between pose and ground. Use analytic two-bone IK for legs and arms (Holden's 3D two-joint form: clamp the target distance to `[eps, l_ab + l_cb − eps]`, solve hip and knee by the law of cosines, rotate about a pole-stabilised bend axis) and iterative solvers only for longer chains; measured cost orderings are consistent across three sources (CCD about 4× cheaper than Jacobian transpose at ~35 % higher error; Spore's whole 25-body particle IK at ~0.2 ms on a 2004 laptop) ([Holden, Simple Two Joint IK](https://theorangeduck.com/page/simple-two-joint); [Deglorie 2015](https://www.scitepress.org/papers/2015/53106/53106.pdf); [Hecker 2008](https://www.chrishecker.com/images/c/cb/Sporeanim-siggraph08.pdf)). Godot 4.6+ ships all of it as `IKModifier3D` subclasses (`TwoBoneIK3D` by "intersection of two circles" with a pole target; `FABRIK3D`, `CCDIK3D`, `JacobianIK3D`, `ChainIK3D`, `SplineIK3D`; iterative defaults 4 iterations, 1 mm tolerance, 2° angular delta) plus `LookAtModifier3D`, the `BoneConstraint3D` family and `LimitAngularVelocityModifier3D`, all running after `AnimationMixer` with per-modifier `influence` (`doc/classes/TwoBoneIK3D.xml`; [Godot 4.6 release](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.6/); [Godot IterateIK3D class reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_iterateik3d.html)). Foot placement drops the pelvis by the lowest foot's offset and flattens the sole to the surface normal, and Holden's foot-locking switch (feed the fixed contact point with zero velocity, inertialize the switch with a 0.1 s half-life) keeps feet planted ([salivity foot-placement IK](https://salivity.github.io/game-development/article/foot-placement-inverse-kinematics-for-uneven-terrain); [Holden, Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)). Note that in this architecture IK writes the *target* pose; the physics rig decides whether the foot actually lands there.

### Continuity: inertialization and half-life springs

Every discrete switch in the target pose (a fall layer engaging, a grab attaching, a state-machine step) should be inertialized rather than cross-faded. Bollo's method cuts immediately to the target and decays the offset `x₀ = x_source − x_target` with velocity `v₀` along a quintic `x(t) = At⁵ + Bt⁴ + Ct³ + (a₀/2)t² + v₀t + x₀`, with `A = −(a₀t₁² + 6v₀t₁ + 12x₀)/(2t₁⁵)`, `B = (3a₀t₁² + 16v₀t₁ + 30x₀)/(2t₁⁴)`, `C = −(3a₀t₁² + 12v₀t₁ + 20x₀)/(2t₁³)`, `a₀ = (−8v₀t₁ − 20x₀)/t₁²` used only when positive, and the overshoot guard **`t₁ = min(t₁, −5x₀/v₀)`**; vectors are inertialized by magnitude along `x̂₀` and quaternions by the angle of the offset rotation, at 12 µs per transition versus 30 µs for an evaluated blend ([Bollo GDC 2018 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/bollo_david_inertialization_high_performance.pdf); [Bollo SIGGRAPH 2017 supplemental](https://cdn.gearsofwar.com/thecoalition/publications/SIGGRAPH%202017%20-%20High%20Performance%20Animation%20in%20Gears%20ofWar%204%20-%20Supplemental.pdf)). Where the target itself keeps moving, use Holden's exact critically damped spring with a single half-life parameter: `y = halflife_to_damping(h)/2` with `halflife_to_damping(h) = 4·ln2/(h + eps)`, `j0 = x − x_goal`, `j1 = v + j0·y`, `x ← e^(−y·dt)·(j0 + j1·dt) + x_goal`, `v ← e^(−y·dt)·(v − j1·y·dt)`; the tracking lag is `halflife/ln 2`, and the open motion-matching controller's defaults (blend half-life 0.1 s, simulation velocity/rotation half-life 0.27 s, IK blend 0.1 s, gait change 0.1 s) are sane starting values ([Holden, Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call); [controller.cpp](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching)). The half-life should shrink as strength grows: a stronger character recovers pose faster, and that is one parameter. Secondary motion (hair, cloth, holster, weapon sway) uses Godot's `SpringBoneSimulator3D` (4.4+, VRM logic, `stiffness`, `drag`, `gravity` as a constant velocity, `external_force`), fed the skeleton's acceleration through `external_force` so it responds to superhuman accelerations; for physically consistent behaviour implement Jakobsen's Verlet chain directly (`x′ = 2x − x* + a·Δt²`, distance-constraint projection by relaxation) ([Godot SpringBoneSimulator3D class reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_springbonesimulator3d.html); [Jakobsen 2001](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/academic/class/15462-s13/www/lec_slides/Jakobsen.pdf)). Motion matching is deliberately not the primary path: its database only contains human-speed clips, so beyond human speed it would need authored exaggerated clips tagged by tier or Witkin–Popović warps `θ′(t) = a(t)θ(t) + b(t)`; if it is used at all, Clavet's coupling is the right one, with the rigid body as the "simulation object" predicted by the real integrator and the visual body clamped within about 15 cm of the physics root ([Clavet GDC 2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Clavet/GDC2016-Clavet_djvu.txt); [Witkin & Popović 1995](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/warpage/warpage.pdf)).

### First-person arms and legs

Only arms, upper chest and (looking down) legs are ever visible, so the consequence cues for growing power must be carried by those parts. Duffin's first-person principle is that FP motion works like a HUD, conveying "pain, strain, exertion and weight", and that FP animations look "bizarre" in third person while third-person animation fails in first ([Wolfire summary of Duffin's GDC 2013 talk](https://www.wolfire.com/blog/2013/04/GDC13-Summary-Animation-Bootcamp-Part-5-6/)). The true-first-person rig is one skeleton with the camera on the head socket, the head mesh hidden for the owner, the spine and neck driven by aim offsets by pitch and yaw, and full-body reactions (landing) overriding the aim offset ([Game Developer, True First Person Camera in UE4](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/true-first-person-camera-in-unreal-engine-4)). Here the arms are physics bodies on the rig whose target poses are 2–4 authored arm poses per locomotion state blended by measured velocity and acceleration, with Rosen's "recoil spring" (the same object as Holden's spring) fed by the physical impulse so weapon kick and strain scale with strength ([Rosen slides 42–46](https://archive.org/download/GDC2014Rosen/GDC2014-Rosen_djvu.xml)). Keep breathing and idle sway (12–18 cycles per minute) on the arms only, never on the camera, for the comfort reason in the next section ([MoCap Online FP animation guide, secondary](https://mocaponline.com/blogs/mocap-news/first-person-animation-guide)).

## Keep body motion off the camera and on the arms

### The three-layer camera stack

No shipped game in the sources binds the camera rigidly to a simulated head, and every studio that tried a head-mounted camera reports the same failure: DICE "thought of attaching a camera to a mocapped head, though it didn't work out and good old animator talent was the solution", and Cloud Imperium's lead animation engineer called the raw head camera "like controlling a bobble head" ([gameanim.com on Mirror's Edge](https://www.gameanim.com/2010/11/05/creating-first-person-movement-for-mirrors-edge/); [Kotaku on Star Citizen](https://kotaku.com/star-citizens-first-person-view-is-based-on-birds-1787021141)). CIG's fix is the reusable one: eye counter-rotation removed about 80 % of the bounce but caused sickness near walls, so the shipped solution copies birds, whose long necks counter-translate body motion, keeping the head (camera) position steady while the body absorbs the difference ([Kotaku 2016](https://kotaku.com/star-citizens-first-person-view-is-based-on-birds-1787021141)). Respawn's rule is the flat-screen statement of the same finding: "Any time we move the view without your input that can cause motion sickness", and their landing reaction is purely a camera spring "with intensity proportional to fall height" on top of unchanged movement code ([Game Developer, Titanfall controls interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designer-interview-getting-i-titanfall-i-s-controls-just-right)). The recommended stack has three layers.

Layer one, the base view, is player yaw and pitch only; body orientation never writes into it, in line with Oculus's "User and camera movements should never be decoupled" and "Have accelerations initiated and controlled by the user whenever possible" ([Oculus Best Practices PDF](https://static.oculus.com/documentation/pdfs/intro-vr/latest/bp.pdf)). Layer two, the anchor position, is Holden's exact critically damped spring toward the simulated head body's position with a half-life of about **0.05–0.12 s** (lag 0.07–0.17 s), the anchor–head offset clamped to roughly 10–15 cm as a safety limit in the manner of Opsive's Min/Max Value clamps ("Use them as safety limits, not as the main way to shape the motion"), with the visible neck and head mesh absorbing the residual as in Star Citizen; the camera reads the interpolated transform in `_process` while the body moves in `_physics_process` ([Holden, Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call); [Opsive Springs](https://opsive.com/support/documentation/ultimate-character-controller/animation/springs/); [Using physics interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/interpolation/using_physics_interpolation.html)). Layer three is the additive impact layer with Source's exact constants: a punch angle driven by a damped torsional spring `punch += vel·dt; vel *= max(0, 1 − 9·dt); vel −= punch·clamp(65·dt, 0, 2)` (k = 65, c = 9; `ViewPunch` adds `angleOffset × 20` to the velocity), which is ω₀ ≈ 8.06 rad/s (about 1.28 Hz) at ζ ≈ 0.56, deliberately underdamped to overshoot once and settle in about half a second ([Source SDK gamemovement.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/gamemovement.cpp); [baseplayer_shared.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/baseplayer_shared.cpp)). Landing feeds it a roll of `fallSpeed × 0.013°` per unit per second above a threshold of 303 u/s ("at least a 76 inch fall"), with pitch hard-capped at **8°**, which at one inch per unit is about 0.68° per m/s of impact speed and never exceeds about 12° in the shipped game ([gamemovement.cpp `PlayerRoughLandingEffects`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/gamemovement.cpp); [shareddefs.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/master/src/game/shared/shareddefs.h)). Wall hits use Quake's direction-dependent kick, `roll = count·(hitDir·right)·0.6`, `pitch = count·(hitDir·forward)·0.6`, decaying linearly over 0.5 s ([Quake view.c](https://github.com/id-Software/Quake/blob/master/WinQuake/view.c)). The impulse magnitude comes from Jolt's contact impulse on the head and torso bodies, so superhuman speed automatically produces bigger reactions, but the summed punch is clamped to about 12° total. For sustained trauma, Eiserloh's model is a trauma scalar in [0, 1] that decays linearly, shake = trauma² or trauma³, and rotation-only Perlin-noise shake in 3D (`yaw = maxYaw·shake·noise(seed, t)`), because translational shake is "VERY BAD" in 3D; the common Unity port uses exponent 2, recovery 1.5/s, noise frequency 25 and maxima of (15, 15, 10) degrees ([Eiserloh GDC 2016 transcript](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Eiserloh/GDC2016-Eiserloh_djvu.txt); [Roystan camera shake](https://roystan.net/articles/camera-shake/)).

When the ragdoll takes over on a knockdown, switch the anchor's follow target from the intent head to the ragdoll head through an inertialization offset (Holden's `inertialize_update`: decay an offset spring and add it to the new input) so there is no snap, blend ragdoll head *rotation* in at reduced gain and clamped (CIG's counter-rotation removed about 80 %), decelerate the camera before a wall in Meta's "soft collision" style, and if the head is pushed inside geometry fade to black rather than teleport, which is standard VR practice ("if the user puts their head where it shouldn't be, then fading to a colour will make the user realise they've done something wrong") ([Holden, Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call); [Meta locomotion comfort](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/locomotion-comfort-usability/); [VRTK HeadsetCollisionFade](https://vrtoolkit.readme.io/v2.2.1/docs/vrtk_headsetcollisionfade)). On recovery blend back with Overgrowth's exponential, `cam = mix(cam, ragdoll_cam, t); t −= dt·speed` with speeds of 2 (stand) to 10 (fall/roll) per second ([aschar.as](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/aschar.as)). Ship "first-person ragdoll" as a toggle separate from head bob, as GTA V does with "First Person Ragdoll", "First Person Combat Roll" and "First Person Head Bobbing" ([GTA BOOM first-person guide](https://www.gtaboom.com/gta-5-first-person-guide-bc4b)).

### Comfort limits with numbers

The Oculus guidance is numeric: avoid visually presented acceleration (any change of velocity, including turning, stopping, bobbing), avoid vertical oscillation near **0.2 Hz** and off-vertical rotation near **0.3 Hz**, keep speeds near 1.4 m/s walking and 2.8–3 m/s running for comfort, do not head-bob, and let players lower intensity with settings that "default to the lowest-intensity experience" ([Oculus Best Practices PDF](https://static.oculus.com/documentation/pdfs/intro-vr/latest/bp.pdf)). Laboratory work pins the worst band for visually induced sickness at **0.2–0.4 Hz** of fore-aft oscillation (physical translation peaks at 0.2 Hz too), which means any slow camera drift, breathing sway or "inertia" lag whose energy lies between roughly 0.1 and 0.5 Hz is the most provocative motion possible, while gait-rate bob (1.5–2.5 Hz) and short impact springs above 1 Hz (Source's 1.28 Hz punch) sit above the band ([Diels & Howarth 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720812469046); [Golding 2001 via Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22motion%20sickness%20maximum%20around%20the%200.2%20Hz%20frequency%22&format=json&resultType=core)). That is the argument against body-following half-lives longer than about 0.3 s: they turn every stop and start into a 0.3–1 s oscillation in the danger band. Flat-screen prevalence is not negligible (42–56 % of students sick within 50 minutes of console games; 2.5–5 % of Steam VR-FPS reviews mention sickness), so the low-intensity default matters ([Stoffregen 2008](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872008x250755); [Guzsvinecz 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-026-01353-y)). Two further findings shape the settings: the matched internal/external field of view was the *sickest* condition in the TNO study, so expose a wide FOV range and do not tie bob amplitude to FOV, and players repeatedly ask for a way to disable "involuntary camera movement" ([de Vries et al., VIMS2007 PDF](https://www.ieda.ust.hk/dfaculty/so/pdf/Pages89-95-VIMS2007.pdf); [ResetEra thread](https://www.resetera.com/threads/first-person-and-motion-sickness.295178/page-2)). Historically Valve moved *away* from camera motion (idle sway and view roll default to zero in Quake, Half-Life and Source) and onto the viewmodel, whose bob is `0.3·b + 0.7·b·sin(cycle)` clamped to [−7, 4] units at 40 % scale ([Half-Life view.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/halflife/blob/master/cl_dll/view.cpp); [Source basehlcombatweapon_shared.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/master/src/game/shared/hl2/basehlcombatweapon_shared.cpp)). The natural bob for a physics body is therefore not a sine at all: on each foot-contact event, add a downward velocity impulse to the vertical camera spring proportional to the foot's impact speed (and a lateral impulse signed by foot), half-life 0.1–0.2 s, which keeps bob energy at gait frequency, stays continuous through take-off and landing (Half-Life 2 lets the bob phase continue in the air "because it snaps badly without it"), and makes superhuman running produce larger bob without a hand-tuned curve. Body-induced rotation is the highest-risk element of the whole design: make it opt-in, clamp it to the 8–12° landing ceiling, decay it within 0.5–1 s, and foreshadow it with the visible body, since Oculus notes that an avatar that "anticipates and foreshadows the visual motion" reduces discomfort ([Oculus Best Practices PDF](https://static.oculus.com/documentation/pdfs/intro-vr/latest/bp.pdf)).

## Grade destruction by cost; DMM is reproducible but not worth reproducing

### Six options from free to months of C++

Every shipped large-scale destruction system separates what the physics simulates from what the player sees, and every one caps real bodies: Rainbow Six Siege ships instanced, recycled, non-self-colliding box debris and budgets "roughly 6 ms for a wall" with 0.33–23 ms per event by platform; Müller's runtime fracture spawns no rigid bodies below a size threshold and stays under 50 ms per event; The Force Unleashed faded debris once "no longer needed" so it would not "interfere with the kinematic motion of the player" ([L'Heureux, GDC 2016 PDF](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/LHeureux_Julien_Art_Of_Destruction.pdf); [Müller, Chentanez, Kim 2013](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/fractureSG2013.pdf); [Parker & O'Brien 2009](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Parker-RTD-2009-08/Parker-RTD-2009-08.pdf)). The fork already has the two hardest ingredients: Jolt 5.6 with a raisable 10,240-body ceiling (`modules/jolt_physics/jolt_project_settings.cpp`) and V-HACD as an engine module exposed through `MeshInstance3D.create_multiple_convex_collisions()` and `Mesh.convex_decompose()` (`modules/vhacd/register_types.cpp`). The graded options, each traceable to a shipped or published system, are:

1. **Free, days: reactive dressing.** Sleeping `RigidBody3D` clutter (Jolt sleeps islands at 0.03 m/s after 0.5 s), `Decal` impact marks and footprints (Forward+ budget of 512 clustered elements), `GPUParticles3D` dust with heightfield collision, `Area3D` reverb zones and Doppler, a per-instance foliage bending vector in the Crysis manner (`fBF = z·bendScale; fBF = fBF² − fBF; pos.xy += wind.xy·fBF`) that can also carry a fast player's wake, and camera impulses on landings ([Godot docs, Using decals](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/3d/using_decals.html); [Godot docs, 3D particle collisions](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/3d/particles/collision.html); [GPU Gems 3 ch. 16](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis)). Control's lesson is to span three visual scales (rigid chunks, mid-scale mesh particles and decals, sprite particles) with per-material rules ([Game Developer on Control](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/using-procedural-destruction-to-unleash-chaos-in-i-control-i-)).
2. **Days: pre-fractured swap on an impulse threshold.** Fracture offline (Blender Cell Fracture, now an Extensions add-on, or the MIT VoronoiShatter plugin for Godot 4.6), 5–20 shards each with one convex hull, swap on `contact_monitor` impulse above a threshold, fade after N seconds ([Jummit destruction plugin](https://github.com/Jummit/godot-destruction-plugin); [VoronoiShatter](https://github.com/robertvaradan/voronoishatter); [Cell Fracture on Blender Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/cell-fracture/)).
3. **One to three weeks, script: connection graph with islands (the Siege/Blast pattern).** Bonds from shard adjacency, per-material Hardness with **`Damage = Impulse / Hardness`**, health with min/max damage-threshold fractions, world anchors, flood-fill island detection so unsupported islands become compound rigid bodies, and a capped cosmetic `MultiMesh` debris pool (ShatterKit's 48 physics chunks plus 2,048 shards is a shipped Godot example); optionally Blast's iterative stress solver to collapse overhangs ([Blast Introduction](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/blast-sdk/latest/docs/api/introduction.html); [Blast UE4 settings](https://archive.docs.nvidia.com/gameworks/content/gameworkslibrary/blast/1.1/authoring_docs/BlastUe4_BlastSettings.html); [ShatterKit](https://indiedevwilliam.itch.io/shatterkit)). This is the tier that round one needs, because it is where the strength ladder's thresholds live.
4. **Weeks, C++: runtime cutting.** Smash Hit's plane-splitting of convex compounds with capped contact impulses and up to three re-solves per step, Siege's 2D polygon clipping of planar walls with collision rebuilt from a simplified convex set, or NVIDIA Blast itself (BSD-3, physics-agnostic) compiled as a module driving Jolt bodies ([Gustafsson GDC 2015 PDF](https://archive.org/download/GDC2015Gustafsson/GDC2015-Gustafsson.pdf); [NVIDIA-Omniverse/PhysX](https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/PhysX)).
5. **Months, C++: voxels (Teardown).** Voxel volumes per object, deterministic cut commands, connectivity split into per-island bodies; `godot_voxel` provides editable terrain but not per-island rigid bodies ([Voxagon, Teardown multiplayer](https://blog.voxagon.se/2026/03/13/teardown-multiplayer.html); [Zylann/godot_voxel](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel)).
6. **Months, C++ plus research skills: DMM-class FEM**, discussed next.

Godot's own guidance applies at every tier: primitives and few convex hulls on dynamic bodies, no trimesh, Continuous CD for fast bodies, sleeping on, and awareness that Jolt's `get_contact_impulse()` is exact only for isolated pairs and that kinematic-vs-static contacts are opt-in ([Godot docs, Troubleshooting physics](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/troubleshooting_physics_issues.html); [Godot docs, Using Jolt Physics](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html)). Determinism for replays is not guaranteed by Godot's Jolt integration even though Jolt is deterministic, so do not design around lockstep ([godot-jolt README](https://github.com/godot-jolt/godot-jolt)).

### The DMM verdict

Digital Molecular Matter is real, published and console-proven, but it is proprietary middleware whose public footprint stops at a Maya 2019 plugin. Pixelux (Geneva, founded 2003; CTO Eric Parker) developed it over 6.5 years with LucasArts; it shipped in The Force Unleashed I and II and Quantum Break, integrations existed for Gamebryo, Vision, Irrlicht and OGRE, and Parker, O'Brien and Cole received a 2015 Academy Sci-Tech award; the company still advertises "evaluation and licensing" for a DMM Engine on PS4/Xbox One/PC, sells the Maya plugin at $499 per seat with a free version capped at 2,500 elements, but its news page is gone ([Digital Molecular Matter, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Molecular_Matter); [Pixelux DMM Engine](https://www.pixelux.com/DMMengine.html); [Pixelux DMM Plugin](https://www.pixelux.com/DMMplugin.php); [Oscars Sci-Tech 2015](https://www.oscars.org/sci-tech/ceremonies/2015)). Nothing in the record says it was open-sourced or acquired; assume no active support. The method, however, is fully described in Parker and O'Brien's SCA 2009 paper: a corotational tetrahedral FEM with linearized backward Euler, lumped mass and Rayleigh damping, `(M + Δt C + Δt² K) v⁺ = Δt f + M v − Δt K (x − u)` solved by block conjugate gradient to a relative error of 0.001 with a designer-set iteration cap; additive plasticity with yield, creep and a clamp; fracture by per-node separation tensor, replicating the node and splitting on the plane normal to the eigenvector when the largest eigenvalue exceeds the material toughness, with tet splitting disabled so element counts stay bounded; artist-authored "splinters" re-associated on fracture; islands that sleep, wake on impact and merge transiently; a coarse tet cage deforming the render mesh by barycentric coordinates in a vertex shader ([Parker & O'Brien 2009 PDF](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Parker-RTD-2009-08/Parker-RTD-2009-08.pdf)). Its ancestors are O'Brien and Hodgins' brittle fracture (1999) and Müller's stable real-time deformations (2002) ([O'Brien & Hodgins 1999 record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1145/311535.311550); [Müller et al. 2002 PDF](http://graphics.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2002/p_Mue02.pdf)). The paper's only disclosed cost is PC-side: the glass-explosion scene of about **2,400 tetrahedra** on a 2.4 GHz Core 2 Q6600 plotted on a 0–16 ms axis across four cores, console timings withheld by contract, and TFU's first demo was a tower of 300–400 tets ([Parker & O'Brien 2009](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Parker-RTD-2009-08/Parker-RTD-2009-08.pdf); [fxguide, DMM: FEA for VFX](https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/dmm-fea-for-vfx/)).

So the honest answer to "proprietary or reproducible" is both: the algorithm is reproducible from the paper and its citations, but no open-source library does real-time FEM *fracture*. Vega FEM (BSD-3) has corotational and nonlinear FEM with implicit integrators but lists no fracture; PhysX 5's FEM soft bodies are GPU-only and leave "tearing/cutting" to the application; Jolt's and Bullet's soft bodies are position-based and do not fracture (Bullet has link cutting) ([VegaFEM mirror](https://github.com/starseeker/VegaFEM); [PhysX 5.3.1 Soft Bodies](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.3.1/docs/SoftBodies.html); [Jolt Physics docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/)). A DMM-class reproduction therefore means writing the fracture and island manager on top of one of those solvers, plus a tet-meshing pipeline (fTetWild, MPL-2.0, or TetGen, AGPL) and a GPU barycentric skinning path — a several-month C++ project with solver-stability, collision and content-pipeline risk ([fTetWild](https://github.com/wildmeshing/fTetWild); [TetGen](https://wias-berlin.de/software/index.jsp?id=TetGen&lang=1)). The player-facing fantasy does not need it: the Blast/Siege bond graph or voxel connectivity delivers "I hit the wall hard and it gives way with debris and dust" on the existing Jolt path in days to weeks. DMM's unique property, stored elastic energy released at fracture ("rigid bodies joined by constraints produce deadened motions where the objects look like they simply fell apart"), matters for wood, glass and metal beams and can be faked for hero objects with a plastic-deformation shader or a short XPBD soft-body swap ([Parker & O'Brien 2009](http://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Parker-RTD-2009-08/Parker-RTD-2009-08.pdf)). TFU also shows how the fantasy was tuned: not by scripting but by editing physical parameters, making "the column a little weaker" and the thrown stormtrooper "weigh 5,000 pounds" ([fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/dmm-fea-for-vfx/)).

### Megaton Rainfall's scale lessons: capabilities and engineering only

Megaton Rainfall is the reference for the ceiling, and its engineering is the opposite of physics-first. One programmer built it from January 2012 on an engine inherited from a previous employer, extended with "planet rendering, which allows seamless transitions from outer space to ground level", cities "as big as the real Los Angeles (more than 200 square miles)", CPU physics and GPU particles, and it shipped on PS4 at 1080p60 (PSVR at 60 reprojected to 120) with a 1.5 GB install ([Wccftech interview 2016](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-interview-real-sized-earth-save/); [Wccftech on PS4 target](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-targeting-1080p60fps-medium-settings-ps4-release-q3q4/); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/430210/Megaton_Rainfall/)). Its destruction contains no rigid-body debris at all: buildings exist as a hidden zero-thickness "paper" mesh and a displayed "inflated" mesh; an explosion removes paper triangles near the blast and re-inflates; continuity is preserved by taking "a snapshot of the house just as it's seen from the camera. Yes, a snapshot. A bitmap" and using it as the texture of GPU billboard particles ("The PS4 can handle bazillions easily"); pre-fractured meshes were rejected because at that city scale they "would exhaust memory in one fell swoop" ([PlayStation Blog, The Tech That Powers Megaton Rainfall](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/10/17/the-tech-that-powers-megaton-rainfall-launching-today-on-ps-vr/); [PlayStation Blog EU on the paper mesh](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2017/10/17/how-procedurally-destructable-cities-work-in-ps-vr-superhero-sim-megaton-rainfall-out-today/)). Flight is look-direction thrust with an automatic, context-dependent speed scale that del Cerro called "the most difficult power to implement", reaching Mach 8 in about five seconds and later superluminal speeds; reviewers uniformly reported overshoot ("flying ice skates") and no lock-on as the price ([AP2HYC interview](https://ap2hyc.com/2015/04/interview-alfonso-del-cerro-lead-developer-of-megaton-rainfall/); [Gamecritics review](https://gamecritics.com/daniel-weissenberger/megaton-rainfall/); [UploadVR review](https://www.uploadvr.com/megaton-rainfall-review-google-earth-meets-man-steel/)). Comfort was handled by control choice rather than by slowing down: six VR control interfaces, snap turns by default for the most sensitive players, a peripheral vignette, quarter-turn and teleport alternatives, and head-aligned thrust so the view and velocity vector stay aligned ([Road to VR 2017](https://roadtovr.com/battle-aliens-map-big-earth-first-person-superhero-game-megaton-rainfall-coming-psvr/); [PlayStation Country review](https://www.playstationcountry.com/megaton-rainfall-psvrps4-review/)). A launch bug tied simulation speed to vsync, a reminder to integrate flight in `_physics_process` with a fixed delta ([Steam announcements](https://steamcommunity.com/app/430210/allnews/)).

For Godot, the precision foundation is the `precision=double` build ("large world coordinates"): single precision is acceptable only to about 2–4 km from the origin for first-person 3D, double remains acceptable beyond 262,144 units, rendering uses an emulated approach with known triplanar and particle artefacts, and custom export templates are required ([Godot docs, Large world coordinates](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/large_world_coordinates.html)). A real-size Earth (radius 6.4 × 10⁶ m) therefore needs the double build plus origin rebasing or nested frames above that for anything interplanetary, which is the standard split: Star Citizen moved physics and positioning to 64-bit world space, Kerbal Space Program made the active craft the origin so "the entire universe moves around it", and Outer Wilds shrank the world instead ([GamersNexus on Star Citizen, snippet](https://gamersnexus.net/gg/2622-star-citizen-sean-tracy-64bit-engine-tech-edge-blending); [KSP 1.2 changelog](https://wiki.kerbalspaceprogram.com/wiki/1.2); [Outer Wilds, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Wilds)). Del Cerro never disclosed his precision scheme. The reproducible pieces need no engine change: the comfort layer, a flight controller with context-scaled max speed and a hard mid-air stop, runtime mesh editing via `ArrayMesh`/`SurfaceTool`, a `SubViewport` capture as a particle texture, and `MultiMesh` instancing with texture arrays (del Cerro's own draw-call strategy); the deep work is the planet renderer, city generation and streaming, and damage-aware collision rebuilds in Jolt ([Wccftech on PS4 target](https://wccftech.com/megaton-rainfall-targeting-1080p60fps-medium-settings-ps4-release-q3q4/)). Elite Dangerous' split of extreme speed into discrete regimes (supercruise versus hyperspace) is the same idea as Megaton's supersonic/superluminal/dash tiers and is how the H6 tier should be structured ([Elite Dangerous, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Dangerous)). Del Cerro's order of operations (destruction rendering first in 2012, flight and planet transitions next, comfort last) is a reasonable template: the expensive systems were prototyped before the game existed ([PlayStation Blog EU](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2017/10/17/how-procedurally-destructable-cities-work-in-ps-vr-superhero-sim-megaton-rainfall-out-today/)).

## Houdini fits as an offline authoring tool, not a runtime

Houdini Engine for Godot exists only as a community project, and the licence makes it an editor-time tool at most. SideFX's official hosts are Unreal, Unity, Maya, 3ds Max and a Maxon-maintained Cinema 4D plugin; Godot appears nowhere on the product page, the FAQ or the Labs index, and the FAQ states that custom HAPI integrations "require a commercial Houdini Engine license" (Workstation $525/year, floating from $795/year) while "Houdini Engine Indie will work with the plug-ins listed here" only ([Houdini Engine product page](https://www.sidefx.com/products/houdini-engine/); [Houdini Engine FAQ](https://www.sidefx.com/faq/houdini-engine-faq/)). **HEGo** by Peter Prickarz is the one integration found: a C++ GDExtension for Godot 4.4+ and Houdini 22.0.429+, MIT-licensed, with a GDScript API for sessions, HDA nodes, geometry/curve/heightfield inputs, off-main-thread cooking and a Terrain3D bridge, whose README states that "An active commercial SideFX license (FX, Core, or Houdini Engine) is required for runtime use"; its author says SideFX "seem very open" to an Indie exception but SideFX has made no statement ([HEGo README](https://github.com/peterprickarz/hego); [HEGo releases](https://github.com/peterprickarz/hego/releases); [SideFX forum, Houdini Engine for Godot Release](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/101121/)). For an indie-budget team the practical route is offline export, which needs only Houdini Indie ($299/year) and no Engine licence ([Houdini Indie](https://www.sidefx.com/products/houdini-indie/)). Shipped builds could never depend on HEGo, since the licensing model does not contemplate end-user machines.

The offline contract is glTF with metadata. Houdini's generic glTF ROP exports hierarchies (built from a `path` attribute), materials, transform animations, lights and custom attributes and extras, but **not skinning or morph targets**, and Draco must stay off because Godot refuses `KHR_draco_mesh_compression`; skinned characters go through the KineFX **ROP GLTF Character Output** SOP (H19+), which exports skin from `boneCapture`, joints, clips and morph targets ([glTF ROP docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/out/gltf.html); [ROP GLTF Character Output docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/kinefx--rop_gltfcharacteroutput.html); [godot issue #73738](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/73738)). Godot applies node-name suffixes at import (`-col`, `-convcol`, `-colonly`, `-convcolonly` for V-HACD convex decomposition, `-rigid`, `-navmesh`, `-occ`, `-noimp`, `-loop`), and since 4.4 lands glTF `extras` in node, mesh and material metadata readable as `get_meta("extras")` by an `EditorScenePostImport` script ([Godot docs, node type customization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/node_type_customization.html); [godot PR #86183](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/86183); [Godot docs, import configuration](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html)). Two gaps need a post-import script: Godot does not turn `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing` into `MultiMeshInstance3D` (proposal open since 2024), so scatter must ship as points or a JSON sidecar and be instanced on import; and Labs Vertex Animation Textures 3.0 ships shaders only for Unreal and Unity, so VAT playback uses the community MIT port Godot_VAT3 with uncompressed 16/32-bit EXR textures ([godot-proposals #8787](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/8787); [Labs VAT 3.0 docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/out/labs--vertex_animation_textures-3.0.html); [Godot_VAT3](https://github.com/G4ND44/Godot_VAT3)).

Houdini earns its seat on exactly the systems this design needs. For destruction, **RBD Material Fracture** produces per-material pieces (Concrete, Glass, Wood, Metal, Custom) plus a constraint network as polyline primitives between piece centroids and optional convex proxy geometry; **RBD Constraint Properties** writes `constraint_name`, `constraint_type` (glue, hard, soft, cone twist, slider, spring), `strength` (−1 = unbreakable, i.e. a world anchor), `half_life` and `propagation_rate` ([RBD Material Fracture docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/rbdmaterialfracture.html); [RBD Constraint Properties docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/rbdconstraintproperties.html)). That graph is a Blast-style support graph already (nodes = pieces by `name`, edges = constraint primitives, weight = `strength`); serialise it with a few lines of hython or as glTF extras on the destructible's root, export per-piece hulls from the Convex Decomposition SOP as `<piece>-convcolonly` children, and use the RBD Bullet Solver only to *tune* strengths offline via its `broken` group or to bake cinematic collapses to VAT rigid mode ([RBD Bullet Solver docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/rbdbulletsolver.html); [Convex Decomposition SOP docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/convexdecomposition.html); [hython command-line docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/hom/commandline.html)). Only topology and `strength` transfer one-to-one; Houdini's glue propagation is Bullet-specific. For the ragdoll, KineFX's **Configure Joints** stores per-joint angle ranges and twist/up axes in a dictionary attribute and **Ragdoll Collision Shapes** generates convex hulls or capsules per joint from skin weights, all as packed primitives with `name`; a post-import script maps cone-twist limits to `PhysicalBone3D` `JOINT_TYPE_CONE`, single-axis ranges to `HINGE`, and asymmetric ranges to `6DOF` (0–5 in Godot's enum) ([Configure Joints docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/kinefx--configurejoints.html); [Ragdoll Collision Shapes docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/kinefx--ragdollcollisionshapes.html); [Godot PhysicalBone3D class reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_physicalbone3d.html)). For terrain, HeightField Output writes 16/32-bit float EXR that Terrain3D and HTerrain both import ("Only use 16 or 32-bit height data"), and Labs Building Generator, Lot Subdivision, OSM tools and Physics Painter cover the semi-procedural city and set dressing offline ([HeightField Output docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/heightfield_output.html); [Terrain3D import docs](https://terrain3d.readthedocs.io/en/stable/docs/import_export.html); [SideFX Labs docs index](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/labs/)). Should the team ever pursue FEM, Tet Embed and FEM Validate make Houdini a strong tet-mesh generator, exported via hython to TetGen `.node`/`.ele` files ([Tet Embed docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/tetembed.html); [TetGen file formats](https://wias-berlin.de/software/tetgen/fformats.html)). Blender's bundled glTF exporter reaches Godot equally well for modelling and skinning, and Cell Fracture produces pieces, but the authored constraint graph, per-piece proxies and strength values would have to be scripted in Blender Python, which is the decisive Houdini advantage for this game's destruction ([glTF-Blender-IO](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO); [Cell Fracture on Blender Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/cell-fracture/)).

## Hand-author the controller first; a learned policy is a later residual

The learned-control field has converged on one runtime interface that a hand-authored controller can share: a policy queried at 20–40 Hz (30 Hz in DeepMimic, AMP, ASE, CALM, PHC, PULSE, MaskedMimic, PARC and Motivo) outputs PD targets or target offsets for every joint of a ragdoll simulated at 60–1,200 Hz ([DeepMimic PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02717); [MaskedMimic PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.14393)). Inference is cheap — DReCon's 2×128 MLP costs **60 µs** of a 340 µs frame on one Xeon core and SuperTrack's 5×1024 network about 1.8 ms with 20 MB of weights — so runtime is not the obstacle ([DReCon PDF](https://static-wordpress.ubisoft.com/montreal.ubisoft.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/13214229/DReCon.pdf); [SuperTrack PDF](https://theorangeduck.com/media/uploads/other_stuff/SuperTrack.pdf)). Training is. The cheapest policies cost DeepMimic "about 2 days on an 8-core machine" per skill and AMP 30–140 hours on 16 cores; from ASE onward everything assumes GPU simulators with 4,096–16,384 parallel environments (ASE 10 days on a V100, MaskedMimic two weeks on four A100s, PARC a month on an A6000, SONIC 21,000 GPU-hours), and only DReCon and SuperTrack were ever trained in a game-class engine (Bullet, PhysX/Havok) ([AMP PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.02180); [ASE PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.01906); [PARC PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04002); [SONIC, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07820)). The strongest models still report the artefacts this game would show in first person: ASE's "jittering, sudden jerks, and overly energetic recovery behaviors", MaskedMimic's "unnatural jittering" and naive terrain footwork, Motivo's "unnatural movements, particularly for behaviors like falling or standing", CALM's mode collapse and failure on stairs and uneven terrain ([ASE PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.01906); [Motivo PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11054); [CALM PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.02195)). Transfer is fragile: a PhysX-trained tracker moved to MuJoCo but the hierarchical AMP policy on top "collapsed within 0.9–2 seconds", and PolySim needed training across three simulators to gain 52.8 % success in a fourth ([IsaacLab issue #7750](https://github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab/issues/7750); [PolySim](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01708)). Godot's tooling is thin: Godot RL Agents drives the engine over a TCP socket at about 12k interactions per second across four processes, two to three orders of magnitude below Isaac Gym, and its ONNX path requires the .NET build; the native ONNX GDExtension is unmaintained since February 2024, so a small custom C++ MLP or ncnn in a GDExtension is the practical inference route ([Godot RL Agents paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.03636); [godot-native-rl comparison](https://github.com/minigraphx/godot-native-rl/blob/main/docs/ncnn_vs_onnx.md)).

The one verified shipped title with learned physics locomotion is ARC Raiders (Embark, UE5, 2025), and its lessons are sobering: machine learning is "strictly for locomotion, such as how the robot places its feet and moves", behaviour trees decide intent, a 5–10 person team worked "for years", and the system "was almost cut multiple times" ([80.lv, ARC Raiders ML interview](https://80.lv/articles/inside-the-magic-of-machine-learning-that-powers-enemy-ai-in-arc-raiders)). SuperTrack's authors themselves note that RL's "unpredictability of the algorithm's success can make it a risky choice in a production setting" ([SuperTrack PDF](https://theorangeduck.com/media/uploads/other_stuff/SuperTrack.pdf)). Two lessons from the learned literature transfer directly to the hand-authored design: torque limits are the quality knob ("Poorly designed torque limits can lead to unnatural motion"), and the Three-Compartment Controller fatigue model that turns constant torque bounds into state- and time-dependent limits is the closest published analogue to this game's consequence gating, usable without any learning ([Learning to Get Up PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00307); [Cheema et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08583)). The learned controllers also supply the acceptance tests: DeepMimic's 0.2 s pelvis pushes increased by 10 N until the character falls (run survives 720 N forward, walk 240 N), DReCon's projectiles while running at 3 m/s, SuperTrack's 5 m/s cubes once per second, and DeepMimic's early-termination rule (torso or head touching the ground) as the fall detector ([DeepMimic PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02717); [DReCon PDF](https://static-wordpress.ubisoft.com/montreal.ubisoft.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/13214229/DReCon.pdf)). Ledge catching emerged in PARC only after a month of GPU time, so an explicit hand-contact grab constraint is both cheaper and consistent with the framing; wall collision appears in the literature only as an error to remove ([PARC PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04002)). If learning is ever added, the smallest-risk shape is DReCon's: a 2×128 residual policy that outputs corrective offsets to the hand-authored PD targets, trained in a headless C++ harness driving Jolt directly so there is no sim-to-sim gap, and reserved for NPC bodies before the player body, because in first person every artefact is on camera and latency is felt directly.

## Milestone one is a standing, pushable ragdoll you can walk and grab with

The plan is ordered so that every later system consumes numbers the earlier one measured, and so that the first deliverable runs locally in the editor with no content pipeline. Del Cerro's history (expensive systems prototyped before the game existed) and Mirror's Edge's process ("we spent a lot of time in white box", 2-week cycles of 2–3 moves, "most moves iterated on 5–6 times") are the process references ([PlayStation Blog EU](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2017/10/17/how-procedurally-destructable-cities-work-in-ps-vr-superhero-sim-megaton-rainfall-out-today/); [Game Developer, Mirror's Edge interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-philosophy-of-faith-a-mirror-s-edge-interview); [DICE GDC slides via SlideServe](https://www.slideserve.com/miron/creating-first-person-movement-for-mirror-s-edge-powerpoint-ppt-presentation)).

**Phase 0 — engine verification and patches (days).** Add an `upstream` remote and diff `modules/jolt_physics`, `scene/3d`, `servers/physics_3d` to confirm the fork carries no local physics changes (`git remote add upstream https://github.com/godotengine/godot`). Write a ten-line GDScript test that puts two `RigidBody3D`s on a `Generic6DOFJoint3D`, enables the angular spring, calls `set_angular_target_rotation()` each tick and confirms the child tracks a rotating target with the torque cap honoured; repeat through `PhysicalBone3D.get_joint_rid()` and `PhysicsServer3D.generic_6dof_joint_set_angular_target_rotation()`, which the engine note flags as unverified at runtime (`doc/classes/Generic6DOFJoint3D.xml:67-74`; `servers/physics_3d/physics_server_3d.cpp:435-436`). Check whether the spring counts as active with zero damping (`HasStiffnessOrDamping` in `SpringSettings.h:44`). Set the project to 120 ticks, `max_physics_steps_per_frame` 16, physics interpolation on, and profile an empty 15-body ragdoll at 60/120/240 Hz. Land the Phase 0 patches listed earlier (bind Jolt-only params, cone-twist Position motor, applied-force readback) in `modules/jolt_physics`.

**Milestone 1 — the locally testable prototype (weeks).** A grey-box room with a floor, a 1 m step, a 2 m and a 4 m ledge, a wall, and a slope. The 15-body `PhysicalBone3D` rig with 6DOF drives at the SIMBICON-CEF gains (root 3000/300, hips/knees 300/30, ankles 75/10, shoulders 20/5, elbows 5/1), the intent capsule with Quake acceleration, the pelvis authority spring, the SIMBICON four-state walk with cd 0.5 / cv 0.2 and Coros's pendulum step, gravity compensation, the Euphoria-style abort rule (lean 0.6, COM within 0.5 m of the lowest foot) ramping stiffness down into the Overgrowth three-layer fall pose, and the 1 s recovery to stand when `|v|² < 20` ([simbicon_cef](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/); [SIMBICON PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf); [Coros 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [SHVDN ConfigureBalanceHelper](https://nitanmarcel.github.io/shvdn-docs.github.io/class_g_t_a_1_1_natural_motion_1_1_configure_balance_helper.html); [aschar.as](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/aschar.as)). The three-layer camera with the Source punch constants, the RMB grab as a hand-contact 6DOF constraint with a 1 kN force limit, and the contact-impulse damage read-out on head, torso and legs. Acceptance tests are the published ones: absorb a 0.1 s 600 N forward / 500 N backward push while walking (SIMBICON), find the DeepMimic push limit in 10 N steps, survive a 5 m/s cube per second (SuperTrack), catch the 2 m ledge from a standing jump and fail to catch the 4 m ledge at 6.3 m/s (grip ceiling 3–4 m/s), and confirm the camera never exceeds 12° of punch or a 15 cm anchor offset. Cartwheel-3d (Apache-2.0) is the license-friendly reference implementation to consult while porting; SIMBICON-CEF (GPLv3) is for its controller files only ([Banbury/cartwheel-3d](https://github.com/Banbury/cartwheel-3d); [simbicon_cef](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/simbicon_cef/)).

**Milestone 2 — the competence scalar and the opening (weeks).** Expose every knob in the competence table as a curve of s, add the 200–300 ms ring-buffer delay, the seeded step jitter, the ML-biased COM offset and the 40–50 % torque start, and playtest the fall rate against the infant baseline of one fall per ~70 steps tapering to zero; audit against Foddy's flavours and Fan's rules; keep the required path under the MGSV threshold and measure minutes-to-competent-walking ([Adolph 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3591461/); [Rasman 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/65085)).

**Milestone 3 — ladders V and H to tier 3, with the damage model (weeks).** Jump and sprint multipliers, the roll (0.57× force) and its two input windows, brace as a compliance-distance change, self-arrest as a raised braking force and capture-step stop, durability multipliers, Source-style linear damage above the safe threshold and the Rosén draw for death; consequence tutorials per tier (contextual gauge, highlight, telegraph, forced safe first use). Validate that stopping distance and landing forces match the tables in this report within the tick's resolution.

**Milestone 4 — destruction tiers 1–3 (weeks).** Reactive dressing, pre-fractured swap, then the bond graph with `Damage = Impulse / Hardness`, island flood-fill and a `MultiMesh` debris pool; hardness values from the drywall (489 N / 67 J), double Type X (~400 J) and brick (500 J / ~270 N·s) anchors so ladder S's tiers fall out of the same numbers ([Blast UE4 settings](https://archive.docs.nvidia.com/gameworks/content/gameworkslibrary/blast/1.1/authoring_docs/BlastUe4_BlastSettings.html); [Gypsum Association GA-235](https://www.americangypsum.com/sites/default/files/2022-01/ga-235_gypsum_board_typical_mechanical_and_physical_properties.pdf); [Mavrouli 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences14090226)).

**Milestone 5 — Houdini pipeline (weeks, parallel with 3–4).** RBD Material Fracture → constraint graph → glTF extras → `EditorScenePostImport`; Ragdoll Collision Shapes and Configure Joints → `PhysicalBone3D` builder; HeightField Output → Terrain3D; scatter points → `MultiMesh`.

**Milestone 6 — upper tiers and scale (months).** Wall-run, ground pound, Immovable, the world-response meter and the casualty bar; a `precision=double` build; a flight controller with Elite-style speed regimes and context-scaled caps; runtime mesh editing and snapshot-billboard collapses for large structures; raise `limits/max_linear_velocity` ([Godot docs, Large world coordinates](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/large_world_coordinates.html)). Only after Milestone 3 is stable should a learned residual policy be considered, and then for NPCs first.

## Conclusion

The research changes the problem's shape in two ways. First, what looked like a research-grade control problem (Euphoria-class reactions in a first-person body) is, on this fork, mostly an integration problem: Jolt's implicit joint motors plus the June 2026 quaternion-target API remove the tick-rate fragility that has stalled every Godot active-ragdoll attempt to date, and the balance, fall and impact laws are thirty years of published, gain-annotated work that runs at 120 Hz on one core. Second, the brief's central conceit turns out to be physically true rather than merely thematic: because tissue strength scales with cross-section while kinetic energy scales with the square of speed, a body given power without durability *must* injure itself, so "every power gated by its own consequence" is what a faithful simulation produces by default, and the design's job is to make that legible (contextual gauges, telegraphs, a cheap rescue verb one tier behind each capability) rather than to invent it. The same two scalars, pelvis authority and joint torque budget, carry the character from toddler to superbeing, which means the opening and the endgame are one system tuned along one axis.

The risks are concentrated in three places that no source resolves: whether players tolerate real inertia in first person once the telegraphs are in (DICE decided they would not, and the brief bets the other way); whether body-driven camera motion can be made comfortable at superhuman speeds with the clamps and toggles proposed; and whether the casualty-bar endgame stays interesting once personal danger is gone, where Megaton Rainfall's reviews are a warning. All three are answerable only by the Milestone 1 grey-box, which is why the plan puts a standing, pushable, grabbable ragdoll ahead of every content system, and why the first destruction tier is a bond graph in script rather than the finite-element system that made The Force Unleashed famous.
